# A CHANGING LANDSCAPE 风云变幻的图景

Jo Salas & Jonathan Fox (2020)

乔·萨拉斯与乔纳森·福克斯（2020）

## What was the cultural context in which Playback Theatre emerged? 一人一故事剧场是在怎样的文化背景下诞生的？

THE MID-SEVENTIES, when Playback Theatre¹ was born, was an era of enormous turmoil and expansive social change on every front, with the repercussions of the Vietnam War, the ongoing struggles for civil rights and women's rights, and the rise of the counterculture which swept up large numbers of our generation. Bold new ideas took hold in the arts and technology. Many, especially young people, questioned long-held assumptions about social and family structures and often tore them down, experimenting with new patterns of connection and power.

七十年代中期，一人一故事剧场¹应运而生，那是一个各领域都充满巨大动荡和广泛社会变革的时代：越南战争的余波未平，民权与妇女权利的斗争仍在持续，而席卷我们这一代人的反主流文化浪潮也正蓬勃兴起。艺术与科技领域涌现出许多大胆的新思想。许多人，尤其是年轻人，对社会和家庭结构中长期存在的假设提出质疑，并常常将其推翻，尝试建立新的联结与权力模式。

This was the atmosphere in which we, Jonathan and Jo, found ourselves in our young adulthood, and which was the context for Playback's emergence. Each of us was directly shaped, in our different ways, by the social transformations of these years.

这就是我们——乔纳森和乔——在青年时期所身处的氛围，也是一人一故事诞生的背景。我们每个人都以各自不同的方式，直接受到了这些年社会变革的影响。

JONATHAN: As I emerged from university in the mid-1960s, I was not especially politically minded. Nevertheless, I strongly opposed the US fighting in Vietnam, as well as other foreign incursions by the US, that seemed no more than the whims of an overpowered bully. I turned away from the privileged path that had been set for me—that choice, of course, a privilege in itself. I accepted an opportunity as far away from New York and the corridors of power and wealth as I could—in the small, beautiful city of Wellington, New Zealand.

乔纳森：20世纪60年代中期，我刚从大学毕业时，并没有特别强烈的政治意识。尽管如此，我坚决反对美国在越南的战争，以及美国其他对外干预行为——这些行为在我看来，不过是某个势力过强的霸凌者的一时兴起罢了。我背离了为我铺就的优越之路——当然，这种选择本身就是一种特权。我接受了一个机会，前往离纽约以及权力与财富中心尽可能远的地方——新西兰那座美丽的小城惠灵顿。

Living in New Zealand for a year was like going back in time. Life was simpler, slower, and more convivial. This feeling was intensified by the two years that I subsequently spent living in a tiny village in Nepal, volunteering with the US Peace Corps, to avoid being sent to Vietnam. My life there was hard, and it was lonely.² But again, my ideas of what was necessary for life transformed. After Nepal, as Jo and I began our married life together and soon had our first daughter, material wealth seemed so much less important to life than US culture insisted, with its relentless promulgation of commerce and capitalism.

在新西兰生活的一年，仿佛是一次时光倒流。生活更简单、更悠闲、更融洽。随后两年，为了避免被派往越南，我作为美国和平队志愿者在尼泊尔的一个小村庄生活，这种感觉变得更加强烈。那里的生活既艰辛又孤独。²但我的生活观再次发生了转变。离开尼泊尔后，当乔和我开始新婚生活并很快迎来了我们的第一个女儿时，物质财富在生活中的重要性似乎远不如美国文化所宣扬的那样重要——那种文化无休止地鼓吹商业和资本主义。

² See Chapter 24, "Garland of Flowers."

² 参见第24章《花环》。

As I sought to find my calling in those post-university and post-Nepal years, I shifted from "writer" to "theatre . . . something." In my search I was drawn to what we then called the experimental theatre. I did not embrace all that these avant-garde groups stood for, but their commitment to social liberation was stirring, as was their dedication to developing a deep ensemble and experimentation. What excited me now were crude homemade pieces developed improvisationally, where the social interactions between players were as important as the product made. I seemed to want a theatre that was personal and intimate, but how could I define it, and where would it lead me? What kind of theatre person did that?

在大学毕业和尼泊尔归国后的那些年里，当我试图寻找人生使命时，我的身份从“作家”转变为“做……的戏”。在这段探索中，我被当时所谓的“实验戏剧”所吸引。我并非完全认同这些前卫团体所代表的一切，但他们对社会解放的承诺令人振奋，他们致力于培养深厚的集体默契和进行艺术实验的精神同样如此。当时让我兴奋的是那些即兴创作的粗糙自制剧目，其中演员之间的社会互动与最终呈现的作品同样重要。我似乎渴望一种个人化且亲密的戏剧，但我该如何定义它，它又会将我引向何方？什么样的戏剧人会做这样的事？

Jo was away for several weeks when some people invited me to direct the group that became our first theatre company, It's All Grace. Once she returned and met this new group (the members all soon became good friends), we invited her to play music with us. Jo and I did not imagine that it would become a professional project—and it didn't—but we were both trying to fashion our lives as independent artists and we were ready to explore this project as a couple. Two and a half years later when I resolved to start Playback Theatre, this time with the hope that it would indeed become a vocation, it felt natural to do it together. It was a time of breaking boundaries, creating new configurations. I knew I did not want to spend my working life in an office hierarchy, as my father had. Jo and I were happy to blend work and work and pin our hopes on a collective, untried experiment.

乔外出数周期间，有人邀请我执导一个剧团，这个剧团后来成为了我们的第一家剧团——“一切皆恩典”（It's All Grace）。等她回来并见到了这个新团队（成员们很快都成了好朋友）后，我们便邀请她和我们一起演奏音乐。乔和我当时都没想到这会成为一个专业项目——事实上也没有——但我们都在努力将生活塑造成独立艺术家的模样，并且准备好作为伴侣共同探索这个项目。两年半后，当我下定决心创办一人一故事剧场（Playback Theatre）时——这次是怀着希望它能真正成为一份事业的愿望——与乔携手同行显得顺理成章。那是一个突破界限、创造新格局的时代。我知道自己不愿像父亲那样，在办公室的等级制度中度过职业生涯。乔和我乐于将工作与生活融为一体，并将希望寄托于一项集体性的、未曾尝试过的实验。

JO: I grew up in New Zealand. In my very early teens, I was involved first in the youth campaign for nuclear disarmament (YCND) and then in the protest movement against the Vietnam War, strenuously opposing the deployment of NZ troops for which the US was pressing our government with Clinton threats and promises. When Jonathan and I met in New Zealand in 1966, the US political situation became more personal for me. The looming draft meant that he had to leave after a year, despite our wish to be together. Our forced two-and-a-half-year separation ended after our stints as volunteers in Asia (myself in the tiny nation of Sarawak, on the island of Borneo; Jonathan in the lowlands of Nepal). We arrived as newlyweds in the United States in April 1970. Two weeks later, Nixon bombed Cambodia. Campuses and communities across the country exploded in protest. National Guard troops killed four white student protesters at Kent State and eight Black students at Jackson State University. My introduction to the US was through the lens of the shock, grief, and rage caused by this violence.

乔：我在新西兰长大。十几岁时，我先是参与了青年核裁军运动（YCND），随后又加入了反越战抗议运动，强烈反对新西兰政府向越南派遣军队——当时美国正通过克林顿的威胁与承诺，向新西兰政府施压。1966年，当乔纳森和我相遇在新西兰时，美国的政治局势对我而言变得更加切身相关。迫在眉睫的征兵令意味着，尽管我们渴望在一起，他一年后还是不得不离开。在亚洲完成志愿服务后（我是在婆罗洲岛上的小国沙捞越，乔纳森则在尼泊尔低地），我们被迫分离两年半的时光才告结束。1970年4月，我们作为新婚夫妇抵达美国。两周后，尼克松下令轰炸柬埔寨。全国各地的校园和社群爆发了抗议浪潮。国民警卫队在肯特州立大学枪杀了四名白人学生抗议者，在杰克逊州立大学又枪杀了八名黑人学生。我初识美国，正是透过这场暴力事件所引发的震惊、悲痛与愤怒的棱镜。

It was not until 1972, when we returned to the US with our baby girl after a year and half in rural England, that we engaged fully with the new youth culture that had taken hold. The values and lifestyle of the counterculture echoed our own, evolved through our separate experiences of living in remote developing countries as well as our pared-down existence on a farm in the West Country of England—wild beauty all around, but no telephone and rarely electricity in the ancient cottage we rented, miles from any town. Communication with our distant families and friends was solely through letters, handed to us by the postman after his slow scooter ride along our potholed dirt road.

直到1972年，我们在英格兰乡村生活了一年半后，带着刚出生的女儿回到美国，我们才真正融入了当时已然兴起的新青年文化。反主流文化的价值观和生活方式与我们自己的不谋而合——这些观念源于我们各自在偏远发展中国家的生活经历，以及在英格兰西部乡村农场的那段简朴生活：四周是原始的自然美景，但我们租住的那座古老小屋远离城镇数英里，既没有电话，也极少通电。与远方亲友的联系完全依赖书信，邮差骑着踏板车沿着布满坑洼的土路缓缓驶来，将信件交到我们手中。

Our new American life was not isolated. But we and the friends we soon found—some in It's All Grace—lived outside "the system," with artistic interests and multiple part-time jobs instead of careers, very little participation in consumer culture, and the fierce espousal of left-wing political and social ideals. I encountered the feminist movement and began to explore what it meant to be a woman in a different mold from my mother's generation. Feminist insights reverberated deeply for me, splintering my assumptions about marriage and motherhood. I took part in a consciousness-raising group, a phenomenon of the time: five or six women sitting in a quiet room, telling stories about what was true for us, speaking what had not been spoken before, listening, not judging.

我们在美国的新生活虽不至于与世隔绝，但我和很快结识的朋友们——其中一些来自“It's All Grace”团体——都生活在“体制”之外：我们热衷艺术，从事多份兼职而非专职工作，几乎不参与消费文化，并坚定地拥护左翼的政治和社会理想。我接触到了女权运动，并开始探索作为一名女性，如何以与母亲那一代截然不同的方式生活。女权主义的见解在我心中产生了深远的共鸣，彻底颠覆了我对婚姻和母职的固有观念。我参加了一个“意识觉醒小组”——那是当时的一种社会现象：五六个女人坐在一间安静的房间里，讲述属于我们自己的真实故事，说出那些从未被言说的话语，倾听彼此，不作评判。

Jonathan and I shared a house with other people in a deliberate attempt at communal living. We helped to create and then taught at a new alternative high school. I made most of our clothes, often modeled on the loose traditional clothes that we'd become used to wearing in Nepal and Malaysia. It led to a small design business for a while, "Clothes for the Global Village."

乔纳森和我与其他人合住一屋，这是我们刻意尝试的集体生活。我们协助创办了一所新的另类高中，并在那里任教。我亲手缝制了我们大部分的衣服，款式往往借鉴了我们在尼泊尔和马来西亚习惯穿的宽松传统服饰。这还一度催生了一家名为“全球村服饰”的小型设计公司。

With my newfound feminist courage, I insisted on some respite from constant childcare. I would lock myself in a room and read: Germaine Greer, women's poetry, Abraham Maslow, Carl Rogers, Abbie Hoffman, Doris Lessing, Eldridge Cleaver, Jane Roberts, the anthology Sisterhood is Powerful. At the local university's famed performing arts festival, I was exhilarated by the music of pioneers like Meredith Monk and Robert Wilson. I practiced Bach and Beethoven on my violin and gingerly tried jazz idioms, not yet ready to improvise or compose.

凭借新获得的女性主义勇气，我坚持要从无休止的育儿中抽身休息片刻。我会把自己锁在房间里读书：杰尔曼·格里尔、女性诗歌、亚伯拉罕·马斯洛、卡尔·罗杰斯、阿比·霍夫曼、多丽丝·莱辛、埃尔德里奇·克利弗、简·罗伯茨，以及选集《姐妹情深，力量无穷》。在当地大学著名的表演艺术节上，梅雷迪思·蒙克和罗伯特·威尔逊等先驱者的音乐让我心潮澎湃。我用小提琴练习巴赫和贝多芬的作品，还小心翼翼地尝试爵士乐的表达方式，虽然当时还无法即兴演奏或创作。

It was music that connected me to It's All Grace. At our outdoor performances I stood at the side, playing pre-chosen melodies on my violin. By the time a breakaway group began exploring the new vision that became Playback Theatre, I was brave enough to step onto the stage as an actor. When we moved to New York and started the original company, I acted for a while and then returned to the musician's spot, finally ready to leap into the world of improvisation.

正是音乐将我与"一切皆恩典"联系在了一起。在我们的户外演出中，我站在一旁，用小提琴演奏事先选好的旋律。当一个分裂出来的小组开始探索后来发展为一人一故事剧场的新愿景时，我终于鼓起勇气作为演员走上舞台。当我们搬到纽约并创立创始剧团时，我演了一段时间戏，随后又回到了乐师的位置，终于准备好投身即兴表演的世界。

## What were the vision and influences at Playback Theatre's inception? 一人一故事剧场创立之初的愿景和受到的影响是什么？

The stage on which the original Playback Theatre company created our first experiments consists of boards well worn by those who came before, our mentors. On this stage, we can imagine a five-pointed guiding star.

创始剧团进行首次实验的舞台，是由我们的前辈——那些导师们——长期使用而磨损的木板构成的。在这个舞台上，我们可以想象一颗五芒星作为引路之星。

The star's first point represented the **oral tradition**, which centers around stories. Traditionally, stories helped pass the time during the long evenings. They also contained important information. In the absence of permanent records, these stories preserved the prevailing history and moral precept of the tribe. Even though these stories, like the literature that would later emerge from them, dealt with setting, character, plot, emotion, they were always more than entertainment. In the same spirit, Playback Theatre would also be based on stories, avoiding scripts and pre-rehearsed pieces. And as in the oral tradition, the stories would portray the collective wisdom of the community. Although it was a form of improvisation—nothing planned, nothing written—Playback Theatre would have a much more serious purpose than the modern improv at a comedy club, which aimed primarily to entertain. And even more than the traditional context, which privileged the established moral order, Playback Theatre would give space for the people's voice.

这颗星的第一芒代表**口述传统**，其核心是故事。传统上，故事有助于打发漫长的夜晚时光，同时也承载着重要信息。在缺乏永久性记录的情况下，这些故事保存了部落的主流历史和道德准则。尽管这些故事——就像后来从中衍生出的文学作品一样——涉及场景、人物、情节和情感，但它们绝不仅仅是娱乐。秉承同样的精神，一人一故事剧场也将以故事为基础，避免使用剧本和预先排练的片段。与口述戏剧传统一样，这些故事将展现社群的集体智慧。尽管这是一种即兴表演——没有任何预先规划，也没有书面剧本——但一人一故事剧场的目的远比喜剧俱乐部中以娱乐为主的现代即兴表演更为严肃。而且，与更重视既定道德秩序的传统语境相比，一人一故事剧场将为民众的声音提供更多表达空间。

The bards, griots, and singers of the oral tradition usually used an instrument to help them string together their memorized lines. So stories in the oral tradition usually had a rhythm to them, and the performance was often part of a ceremony. We would eventually work long and hard on adapting this ritual element to our work.

口述传统中的吟游诗人、格里奥和歌手通常会借助乐器来串联他们背诵的台词。因此，口述传统中的故事通常具有节奏感，而表演往往是仪式的一部分。我们最终花费了大量时间与精力，将这种仪式元素融入我们的创作中。

Another point of the star was the **creative dramatics** movement out of England, an educational approach that focused on young children in school. The pioneers of creative dramatics, such as Peter Slade and Brian Way, preferred devised (or self-developed) pieces to more commonly used written plays. Those plays inevitably starred only a few, relegating everyone else to minor roles. Slade and Way valued instead safety and inclusivity, and they cherished the inherent creativity in each child. They tended to resist the pressure of large auditorium performances and prized an intimate workshop-type approach.

该星形的另一个支点是源自英国的**创意戏剧**运动，这是一种专注于学龄前儿童的教育方法。“创意戏剧”的先驱们，如彼得·斯莱德和布莱恩·韦，更倾向于创作（或自主开发）剧目，而非使用更常见的现成剧本。那些剧本不可避免地只由少数人担任主角，将其他人降格为配角。斯莱德和韦则更重视安全感和包容性，并珍视每个孩子与生俱来的创造力。他们往往抵制大型礼堂演出的压力，而推崇亲密的工作坊式方法。

**Experimental theatre**, a major source of inspiration, was, in many ways, the opposite of creative dramatics. It was adult, confrontational, and rarely safe—following in the footsteps of the seminal Living Theater, founded in 1947. We eagerly adopted the credo of many groups seeking to liberate theatre from conventional theatre spaces, preferring to stage our work in community settings. We followed the advice of Jerzy Grotowski of Poland and built a "poor theatre," needing nothing but our bodies and a space to mark out as the stage. We studied the work of many groups whose productions grew out of months of intense collaboration. We aspired to the objective of Richard Schechner, an influential US performance studies professor and director of the Performance Group, who advocated a theatre of "transformation" (stimulating lasting change in the identity of the observers—i.e. taking them somewhere from which there was no going back) rather than mere "transportation" (an entertaining experience leaving the observer unchanged). We would act out stories in the community itself, and we would have the courage to act out any story, including those that revealed personal or societal injustice.

**实验戏剧**作为重要的灵感来源，在许多方面与创意戏剧截然相反。它面向成人、充满对抗性，且鲜有安全感——这正是追随1947年创立的开创性剧团“生活剧团”（Living Theater）的脚步。我们热切地采纳了许多剧团的信条，致力于将戏剧从传统的剧场空间中解放出来，更倾向于在社群环境中呈现我们的作品。我们遵循波兰剧作家耶日·格罗托夫斯基的建议，打造了一座“贫穷剧场”，除了我们的身体和一块可划定为舞台的空间外，别无他求。我们研究了许多剧团的作品，这些作品都源于数月来的密集合作。我们以美国有影响力的表演研究教授、表演团体（Performance Group）导演理查・谢克纳（Richard Schechner）的目标为志向，他倡导一种“转化”的戏剧（在观众的身份认同中激发持久的改变——即带他们前往一个无法回头的境地），而非单纯的“转移”（一种让观众保持原样的娱乐体验）。我们要在社群中直接演绎故事，并勇于演绎任何故事，包括那些揭露个人或社会不公的故事。

We were influenced by **psychodrama**, the creation of J.L. and Zerka Moreno. This approach gave us a concept to understand a key objective of our improvisation—spontaneity. Spontaneity encouraged creativity, but also appropriateness, finding the right action at the right moment. It also provided a theory and method of inclusion (sociometry) that would grow in importance as our work progressed. About half of the original company came to Playback Theatre from the psychodrama network, and Zerka Moreno actively supported Playback's beginnings. (Playback's affiliation to psychodrama has led to some confusion about whether Playback Theatre is a therapeutic modality or not; it isn't, but can be adapted and applied in therapeutic contexts.)

我们受到了**心理剧**的影响，这是J.L.和哲卡·莫雷诺（Zerka Moreno）创立的。这种方法为我们提供了一个概念，帮助我们理解即兴表演的一个关键目标——自发性。自发性不仅能激发创造力，还能确保恰如其分，即在恰当的时刻做出恰当的行动。它还提供了一种包容性理论和方法（社会计量法），随着我们工作的推进，其重要性日益凸显。创始剧团成员中约有一半是从心理剧网络加入一人一故事剧场的，哲卡·莫雷诺也曾积极支持一人一故事剧场的起步。（一人一故事剧场与心理剧的关联曾导致一些困惑，即人们不确定它是否属于一种治疗方式；其实并非如此，但它可以在治疗情境中加以改编和应用。）

The writings of the Brazilian educator, Paolo Freire, helped draw another point of the star, which we can call **social activism**. It would take years of performing in the community to refine our activist approach, but from the start we emulated a bottom-up (rather than elitist) approach to culture that emphasized embodied listening rather than presentation. We also recognized the tremendous potential of Freire's concept of critical consciousness, which argues that a sense of our own story is a prerequisite for revolutionary change. (Augusto Boal's seminal *Theatre of the Oppressed*, also inspired by Freire, was not published until 1973 and first brought out in English in 1979, by which time we had already been working for four years.) It reinforced many of our ideas.

巴西教育家保罗·弗莱雷的著作帮助勾勒出这颗星的另一条边，我们可以称之为**社会活动**。虽然需要经过多年的社群演出才能完善我们的行动主义方法，但从一开始，我们就效仿了一种自下而上（而非精英主义）的文化方法，强调具身倾听而非单纯的表演。我们还认识到弗莱雷批判性意识概念的巨大潜力——该概念主张，对自身故事的认知是革命性改变的先决条件。（奥古斯都·波瓦同样受弗莱雷启发的开创性著作《受压迫者剧场》直到1973年才出版，1979年才由英国首次推出英文版，而那时我们已经开展工作四年了。）这进一步巩固了我们的许多理念。

This original mix—the oral tradition, psychodrama, and social activism—five points of light complementing and at times contrasting with each other, was significant because it was the ground we stood upon when the original idea for Playback Theatre came to life. It was perhaps an unusual combination, allowing for a simple idea to develop into a method rich in complexity. What emerged was a theatre devoted to embodying truth; to the personal accounts of ordinary people, including from those usually unheard; to an emergent creativity demanding a deep ensemble; to rhythm and ritual; and to an animating approach combining workshop and performance; to an allegiance to safety without avoiding risk; and finally to a way of communicating that lies at the core of what it is to be human—our stories, which at once entertain us, enlighten us, and connect us to each other.

这种独特的融合——口述传统、心理剧和社会活动——这五道光芒既相互补充，又时而形成对比，之所以意义重大，是因为当一人一故事剧场的最初构想应运而生时，这正是我们立足的根基。这或许是一种不同寻常的组合，它让一个简单的构想发展成为一种充满复杂性的方法。由此诞生了一种致力于体现真实的戏剧；致力于普通人的个人叙述，包括那些通常不被倾听之人的声音；致力于一种需要深度集体协作的涌现式创造力；致力于节奏与仪式；致力于一种将工作坊与表演相结合的充满活力的方法；致力于在不回避风险的前提下坚守安全；最后，它还致力于一种沟通方式，这正是人类本质的核心——我们的故事，它们既娱乐我们、启迪我们，又将我们彼此联结。

FOR ALL THE RICHNESS of our "guiding star," there were also disadvantages. Playback Theatre seemed not to fit anywhere. Was this because it was innovative and boundary-crossing? Arts councils rejected our applications because there was no product and no text. Our weekly 3-hour rehearsals were a part of our year-round lives, in contrast to other innovative theatre companies who often rehearsed intensively for a series of performances and then stepped back for a while. Mental health institutions dismissed our overall lack of therapeutic credentials (some of us were credentialed, others not), our public positioning of private narrative, and our welcoming of highly emotional, and therefore in their eyes, unsafe, material. Theatre of the Oppressed came the closest to the kind of theatre we embraced. But there were basic differences, including Playback's much stronger identity as an art form. Clearly, if Playback were to succeed, we would have to establish our own ground, not quite the same as anyone else's.

尽管我们的“引路之星”如此丰富多彩，但也存在弊端。一人一故事剧场似乎无处容身。这是因为它具有创新性且跨越了界限吗？艺术委员会驳回了我们的申请，理由是既没有成品，也没有剧本。我们每周3小时的排练已成为全年生活的一部分，这与其他创新剧团截然不同——后者通常为一系列演出进行密集排练，之后便会暂作休整。心理健康机构则以我们整体缺乏治疗资质（其中部分成员持有资质，部分则没有）、将私人叙事公开呈现，以及我们接纳高度情绪化（因此在他们眼中被视为不安全）的素材为由，拒绝了我们。受压迫者剧场最接近我们所秉持的戏剧理念。但两者仍存在根本差异，其中包括一人一故事作为一种艺术形式的身份认同更为鲜明。显然，若要让一人一故事取得成功，我们就必须开辟属于自己的道路，而非简单复制他人的模式。

## Since those early years, what else has emerged as a fundamental value? 自那些早期岁月以来，还涌现出哪些其他核心价值观？

We learned that it was essential for us to listen with full attentiveness and compassion to each teller: that without such listening, it was not possible to enact their story in a way that carried meaning for them and for the audience. When we were able to listen fully, on the other hand, each telling became part of a profound, enriching dialogue³ as story responded to story in the process we now recognize as narrative reticulation.⁴

我们认识到，全神贯注且充满同理心地倾听每位讲述者至关重要：若缺乏这种倾听，就不可能以一种对讲述者和观众都富有意义的方式演绎他们的故事。反之，当我们能够全神贯注地倾听时，每一次讲述都会成为一场深刻而富有启发性的对话³的一部分——在这个我们如今称为“叙事网络”⁴的过程中，故事与故事相互呼应。

³ See Chapter 10, "What We Mean by 'Dialogue' in Playback Theatre."

³ 参见第10章，“我们在一人一故事剧场中对‘对话’的理解”。

⁴ See Chapter 13, "The Theory of Narrative Reticulation."

⁴ 参见第13章，“叙事网络理论”。

This kind of listening is rare, and difficult to achieve—rare *because* it is difficult to achieve. But it is the greatest gift that Playback has to offer. (It has been troubling, in recent years, to see that some Playback teams have apparently given up on trying to listen, instead taking the teller's story as a kind of prompt for artistic flight.)

这种倾听方式十分罕见，且难以实现——之所以罕见，*正是因为*它难以实现。但这正是一人一故事剧场所能给予的最珍贵的礼物。（近年来，令人不安的是，一些一人一故事剧团似乎已经放弃了倾听的尝试，反而将讲述者的故事视为一种激发艺术创作的提示。）

In our ordinary lives most people neither fully listen or are fully listened to. Our days move too fast. Everyone is preoccupied. People listen through a veil of opinion or distraction, thinking about the story they want to tell in response.

在我们的日常生活中，大多数人既没有全神贯注地倾听，也没有被全神贯注地倾听。我们的生活节奏太快。每个人都心事重重。人们总是透过主观意见或分心的面纱去倾听，脑海中想着自己想要讲述的回应故事。

The promise of Playback is that we, the performing team, will listen to your story and, to the best of our ability, hear and embody its important meanings. It sounds simple. But it is not. The philosopher Simone Weil wrote, "Attention consists of suspending our thought, leaving it detached, empty, and ready to be penetrated by the object."⁵ The "object" in this case is the teller's story. As actors and musicians we must listen in this empty, egoless way, not planning, not worrying, not judging. For conductors, the listening is even more demanding: while we listen with the same open ears as the actors, we are also responsible for eliciting key information and keeping an eye on the wellbeing of the teller, the actors, and the audience.

一人一故事的承诺在于：我们——表演团队——将倾听您的故事，并竭尽所能去领会并诠释其中的重要含义。这听起来很简单，但实际上并非如此。哲学家西蒙娜·韦伊曾写道：“注意力在于暂停我们的思考，让思维保持超然、空灵，并准备好被对象所渗透。”⁵此处的“对象”即讲述者的故事。作为演员和乐师，我们必须以这种空无、无我的方式去倾听，不预设、不担忧、不评判。对于领航员而言，这种倾听的要求更为严苛：虽然我们像演员一样以开放的心态去倾听，但同时还肩负着询问关键信息、并关注讲述者、演员及观众状态的责任。

⁵ Simone Weil, Waiting for God. New York: Harper Torchbooks, 1973, p. 62.

⁵ 西蒙娜·韦伊，《等待上帝》。纽约：哈珀·托奇图书公司，1973年，第62页。

Compassion, openness, and attentiveness require maturity and self-knowledge: we have to know our own story and be able to set it aside while we listen. Our listening, ideally, is also informed by knowledge of the social context of the story we are hearing. Confidence about Playback techniques is also necessary. It's hard to listen with openness if we are trying to remember the choreography of the Playback form we're about to use.

同情心、开放性和专注力需要成熟和自我认知：我们必须了解自己的故事，并在倾听时能够将其暂且搁置。理想情况下，我们的倾听还应基于对所听故事的社会背景的了解。对一人一故事技巧的自信也是必要的。如果我们正忙于回忆即将使用的一人一故事形式的编排，就很难保持开放的心态去倾听。

When a performing team achieves this kind of listening, the promise of Playback can be fulfilled. Each enactment is built around a clear sense of what the story means, allowing subsequent stories to respond to it with spontaneity and authenticity.

当表演团队达到这种倾听境界时，一人一故事的承诺便能得以实现。每次演绎都建立在对故事含义的清晰理解之上，从而使后续的故事能够以自发而真实的的方式对此作出回应。

## How did Playback develop its current worldwide presence? 一人一故事是如何发展到如今遍布全球的？

Playback began as a single ensemble in upstate New York. Forty-five years later, there are Playback practitioners in about seventy countries, on every inhabited continent. It has been welcomed and adapted in many cultures, despite the skepticism of some who considered public self-disclosure to be too "American" to work in countries that valued restraint and privacy. People everywhere have stories and the desire to tell them.⁶

一人一故事最初始于纽约州北部的一个表演团体。四十五年后，在每个有人居住的大陆上，约有七十个国家都有一人一故事的实践者。尽管有人持怀疑态度——认为公开自我披露过于“美国化”，在重视克制与隐私的国家行不通——但一人一故事仍被许多文化所接纳并加以本土化。世界各地的人们都有自己的故事，也都有讲述这些故事的愿望。⁶

⁶ See Chapter 19, "Burnout Journal."

⁶ 参见第19章《倦怠日记》。

The original company's first rehearsal space was a church hall in Beacon, NY, a Hudson River town an hour and a half north of New York City.⁷ The hall was just down the road from the Moreno Institute, a magnet for psychodrama students from all over the world. Sometime during that first year, we began to hold open rehearsals and some of those international students attended them. A few fell in love with this brand-new theatre, taking home with them the rudiments and experimenting further in their own countries.

创始剧团最初的排练场地位于纽约州比肯市的一间教堂大厅，这座位于哈德逊河畔的小镇距离纽约市以北一个半小时车程。⁷该大厅紧邻莫雷诺研究所，那里吸引了来自世界各地的心理剧学生。在成立的第一年里，我们开始举办公开排练，一些国际学生前来观摩。其中几位爱上了这种崭新的戏剧形式，将基础要领带回了各自的国家，并在那里进行了进一步的探索。

⁷ The original company's history is described at more length in our books, including Jonathan's 2015 memoir Beyond Theatre, which also discusses the choice to start our theatre in upstate New York, not the city.

⁷ 关于创始剧团的早期历史，我们在多部著作中都有更详细的描述，包括乔纳森2015年的回忆录《超越戏剧》（Beyond Theatre），书中还探讨了为何选择在纽约州北部而非纽约市创立剧团。

In 1980, an Australian academic saw us perform at a psychodrama conference in New York City and invited four of us to Melbourne. Word spread, and Sydney and New Zealand were added to the itinerary. In Melbourne, Sydney, Wellington, and Auckland, we led workshops and carried out public performances, joined by workshop participants who soon founded companies in those four cities. It was a turning point, expanding our horizons far beyond the Hudson Valley. Meanwhile, our training workshops at home, also starting in 1980, attracted people from other countries as well as around the US, leading soon to invitations to teach in Europe, Eastern Europe, and the former Soviet Union, then in Latin America, Asia, Africa, and the Middle East.

1980年，一位澳大利亚学者在纽约市的一场心理剧会议上观看了我们的演出，并邀请我们四人前往墨尔本。消息传开后，悉尼和新西兰也被加入了行程。在墨尔本、悉尼、惠灵顿和奥克兰，我们主持了工作坊并进行了公开演出，参与工作坊的学员们随后在上述四座城市相继成立了剧团。这成为一个转折点，将我们的视野拓展到了哈德逊河谷之外。与此同时，我们于1980年在家乡开设的培训工作坊，不仅吸引了来自美国各地的参与者，还吸引了其他国家的学员，这很快促使我们受邀前往欧洲、东欧和前苏联地区授课，随后又扩展至拉丁美洲、亚洲、非洲和中东地区。

BY THE TIME the original company was approaching the end of its active life in the late '80s, there were Playback companies in about fifteen countries. Some of us, within and beyond our company, recognized the need for a means of connecting them. A new entity came into being in 1990: the International Playback Theatre Network, to which the original company donated its legal nonprofit structure and logo. The IPTN was consciously conceived as a network rather than a professional association: we saw ourselves as artists linked by common ideals and practices rather than, say, as helping professionals bound by strict training requirements and uniform standards.⁸

到80年代末，当创始剧团即将结束其活跃期时，全球已有约十五个国家成立了“一人一故事剧场”剧团。我们中的一些人，无论是在剧团内部还是外部，都意识到需要一种将这些团体联系起来的途径。1990年，一个新的组织应运而生：国际一人一故事剧场网络（IPTN），创始剧团将其非营利法律结构和徽标捐赠给了该组织。IPTN被有意识地设计为一个网络，而非专业协会：我们视自己为因共同理想和实践而联结的艺术家，而非受严格培训要求和统一标准约束的“助人专业人士”。⁸

⁸ The IPTN carries out its mission of connection in three ways: sponsoring international conferences—the first in Sydney in 1992; a newsletter (formerly *InterPlay*, now called *IPTN Journal*); and a membership directory. Worldwide conferences now take place every three or four years, alternating with regional gatherings. Regional networks, representing 12 or 15 countries guide the IPTN's direction. Regional gatherings in a rotating selection of the world and hold their own gatherings. See www.iptn.info.

Parallel with the development of the IPTN was the founding of the other main Playback institution, the Centre for Playback Theatre, a training organization founded in 1993 (originally called the Jonathan Fox School of Playback Theatre, later the School of Playback Theatre). Training had been available for years through our own workshops and those of other experienced practitioners. Many people simply learned by apprenticeship, joining a company and absorbing the basics until they were ready to perform. But along with the growth of Playback there had developed a need for more comprehensive training for those who wanted to deepen and strengthen their work, often with the purpose of building a professional presence in their communities.⁹

与IPTN的发展并行的是另一家主要一人一故事剧场机构——“一人一故事剧场中心”（Centre for Playback Theatre）的成立，该培训机构成立于1993年（最初名为乔纳森·福克斯一人一故事剧场学校，后更名为一人一故事剧场学校）。多年来，我们一直通过自身的工作坊以及其他经验丰富的实践者的工作坊提供培训。许多人只是通过学徒制学习，加入一个剧团并掌握基础知识，直到他们准备好登台表演。但随着一人一故事剧的发展，那些希望深化和加强自己工作的人对更全面的培训产生了需求，他们往往旨在在各自的社群中建立专业的形象。⁹

⁹ At this point, there are CPT-affiliated schools in seventeen countries carrying out a coordinated curriculum, adapted for each cultural context. Students who complete the training receive a diploma. In 2013, the Centre established an accreditation system for qualified trainers, identifying benchmarks for training recognized as competent by already-established trainers. See www.playbackcentre.org.

⁹ 目前，已有十七个国家的CPT附属学校在实施一套协调一致的课程体系，该体系已根据各文化背景进行了调整。完成培训的学生将获得结业证书。2013年，该中心建立了合格培训师的认证体系，由资深培训师确定了培训的评估标准，以确保培训质量达到专业水平。详见 www.playbackcentre.org。

We had also slowly come to recognize the unique ethical demands of the Playback Theatre idea. It was certainly different from traditional theatre where "the show must go on," no matter the cost for the performers. It was equally different from therapy and the modern helping professions, which create a cocoon of privacy around personal narrative. Playback was public, and it aimed to be constructive for all involved. Without proper attention, we learned from hard experience, tellers, audience members, and even actors could be harmed. Our increasing emphasis on training grew in part as a way to instill in new practitioners the necessary sense of responsibility.

我们也逐渐认识到一人一故事剧场理念所提出的独特伦理要求。它显然不同于传统戏剧中“演出必须继续”的原则——无论表演者要付出多大代价。它同样不同于心理治疗和现代助人职业，后者会在个人叙事周围构建一个隐私的“茧”。一人一故事剧场是公开的，其目标是让所有参与者从中获益。我们从痛苦的经历中认识到，若不加以适当重视，讲述者、观众甚至演员都可能受到伤害。我们日益重视培训，部分原因正是为了向新实践者灌输必要的责任感。

These two organizations, the IPTN and the CPT (both now run by volunteer boards), each serve a large, active, international, and amorphous community of people, the IPTN through connection and the CPT through training. Each seeks to ensure that the fundamental values and practices of Playback Theatre are understood and upheld. Playback practitioners are artists applying their art in service to communication and a safer, fairer world. The inclusiveness that is inherent in Playback extends to its practitioners: it is a tenet of Playback that anyone can do this work. You do not have to be outstandingly talented, you do not have to be an expert. This deeply-held ideal exists in tension with a contrasting reality: offering Playback Theatre on a professional basis in the community requires considerable training and experience. Our field does not need the kinds of rules and regulations that are found in other disciplines—Playback is not the law, nor medicine, nor psychology. But we acknowledge that it is adequate to do well or poorly, and work that is adequate in one context may not be adequate in another (as in, for example, the field of music: anyone can learn to play an instrument and find great joy in doing so alone or with others, but to engage a public audience you have to train and practice for years). In the end, the need for training is about serving the community with effectiveness and integrity, which in turn requires a sense of standards and ethical responsibility.¹⁰

这两个组织——IPTN和CPT（目前均由志愿者董事会运营）——各自服务于一个庞大、活跃、国际化和形态多变的社群：IPTN通过建立联系，CPT则通过培训。两者都致力于确保一人一故事剧场的基本价值观和实践得到理解与恪守。一人一故事剧场实践者是艺术家，他们运用自己的艺术来促进沟通，并致力于创造一个更安全、更公平的世界。一人一故事剧场固有的包容性也延伸至其实践者：一人一故事剧场的一项基本信条是，任何人都可以从事这项工作。你不必才华横溢，也不必是专家。这一深植于心的理想与截然不同的现实之间存在着张力：要在社群中以专业水准开展一人一故事剧场演出，需要相当程度的培训和经验。我们的领域不需要其他学科中常见的那些规则和条例——一人一故事剧场既不是法律，也不是医学，更不是心理学。但我们承认，表现有优有劣，而在某种情境下表现尚可的作品，在另一种情境下可能就不够格了（例如在音乐领域：任何人都可以学习演奏乐器，并从中获得独自或与他人共乐的巨大喜悦，但若要面向公众演出，则必须经过多年的训练和练习）。归根结底，培训的必要性在于以高效和诚信的方式服务社群，而这反过来又需要具备标准意识和伦理责任感。¹⁰

¹⁰ See Chapter 4, "What is 'Good' Playback Theatre?"

¹⁰ 参见第4章，“什么是‘好的’一人一故事剧场？”

The enormous diversity of culture, resources, and language to be found in Playback's worldwide presence brings variation and richness. There is no single "correct" way of doing Playback Theatre, although its basic values, structures, and forms remain consistent and recognizable whether you are in San Francisco or Havana, Dublin or Johannesburg, Singapore or Cairo, Moscow or Bangkok. Published writing has played a part. From the earliest years, we have each chronicled and explored Playback through the written word. Some of our work has been translated (*Improvisando Real Life* is in ten languages at this point!), making our experiences and precepts directly accessible to people in many parts of the world. Between us we have published five books about Playback Theatre as well as numerous articles and contributions to edited collections.¹¹ We have also encouraged writing by others, including through the essay requirement for the CPT Leadership course, which has led to a body of valuable documentation and research, and the website Playback Theatre Reflects, which publishes thoughtful, professional-quality writing about Playback.¹² Books by other Playback authors, in English and other languages, have also contributed significantly to our field.

一人一故事剧场在全球范围内的广泛传播，带来了文化、资源和语言的巨大多样性，从而造就了丰富多彩的呈现形式。无论是在在旧金山、哈瓦那、都柏林、约翰内斯堡、新加坡、开罗、莫斯科或曼谷等地，一人一故事剧场的基本价值观、结构和形式始终保持一致且易于辨识，但并不存在唯一“正确”的一人一故事剧场实践方式。出版物在其中发挥了重要作用。从最初的岁月起，我们每个人都通过文字记录并探索一人一故事剧场。其中部分作品已被翻译（*Improvisando Real Life*目前已有十种语言版本！），使世界各地的人们能够直接了解我们的经验和原则。我们共同出版了五本关于一人一故事剧场的书籍，以及大量文章和收录于文集中的贡献。¹¹我们还鼓励他人进行写作，包括通过CPT领导力课程的论文要求——这催生了一批宝贵的文献和研究成果——以及“一人一故事剧场 Reflects”网站，该网站发表了关于一人一故事剧场的富有洞见且具有专业水准的文章。¹² 其他一人一故事剧场作者用英语及其他语言撰写的书籍，也为我们的领域做出了重要贡献。

## As a theatre movement, Playback has developed a strong though not universal focus on social change. What were the steps in this evolution? 作为一种戏剧运动，一人一故事在社会变革方面形成了鲜明的关注点，尽管这种关注并非普世性的。这一演变过程经历了哪些阶段？

As young adults we were firmly identified with progressive values—anti-war, non-violence, feminist, critical of the capitalist order, strongly skeptical about mainstream politics. Although not at that time very attuned to environmental issues, we chose to live quite lightly on the earth, with few material possessions. We were staunch supporters of civil rights, though with a superficial awareness of the full complexities of racism—likewise our knowledge of the struggles of gay and lesbian people. (The term LGBTQ did not exist then.)

作为年轻一代，我们坚定地秉持着进步价值观——反战、非暴力、女权主义、批判资本主义秩序，并对主流政治持强烈怀疑态度。尽管当时我们对环境问题关注尚浅，但我们选择以极简的方式生活，物质财富极少。我们是民权运动的坚定支持者，尽管对种族主义的全部复杂性仅有肤浅的认识——同样，我们对同性恋群体的抗争也了解甚少。（当时“LGBTQ”这一术语尚未出现。）

It was with this sociopolitical background that we launched Playback Theatre in 1975. Like It's All Grace, in our all-white educational backgrounds. With little explicit discussion, we expected that our theatre would in some way contribute to a more just and peaceful society. After all, key to the Playback vision was the profoundly egalitarian idea that everyone has a story and deserves a place to tell it, and to be heard with compassion and respect. But our sense of Playback as a force for change was vague, not much more than an orientation for the focus on artistic realization that was in the foreground.¹³ For the first few years, our attention was more on finding the rituals and aesthetic forms that would bring our vision to fruition, to render it a viable and powerful form of theatre, and to have it be recognized as such. We also strove to deepen our skills in hearing and responding to any story no matter how complex or sensitive. We experimented with many forms and structures for enacting stories, discarding most of them after a few rehearsals. A few took hold and remain basic to most Playback performing: fluid sculptures, pairs, and the five-part sequence of a Playback scene.¹⁴

正是在这样的社会政治背景下，我们于1975年创立了一人一故事剧场。与“一切皆恩典”一样，我们的教育背景都完全是白人主导的。虽然鲜少进行明确的讨论，但我们期待我们的剧场能在某种程度上为一个更公正、更和平的社会做出贡献。毕竟，一人一故事剧场愿景的核心，是一种深刻的平等主义理念：每个人都有自己的故事，都值得拥有一个讲述故事的场所，并以同情和尊重的态度被倾听。但我们对一人一故事剧场作为变革力量的认知尚显模糊，它不过是作为一种导向，引导我们聚焦于当时处于首要地位的艺术实现。¹³在最初的几年里，我们的注意力更多地集中在寻找能够使愿景得以实现的仪式和美学形式上，旨在将其打造成一种可行且有力的戏剧形式，并使其获得相应的认可。我们还致力于深化倾听与回应任何故事的能力，无论这些故事多么复杂或敏感。我们尝试了多种演绎故事的形式与结构，但在经过几次排练后，便舍弃了其中大部分。其中少数形式得以保留，并成为大多数一人一故事表演的基础：流动塑像、一对对，以及一人一故事自由演绎（scene）的五步序列。¹⁴

Over time, many of us (by now including practitioners beyond the original company) came to realize that the idealistic view upon which Playback was based—that everyone's story had value—contained within it a call to social justice. Voices of the poor, of people of color, of immigrants, of women, of children—in fact, of all who do not belong to the traditional holders of power and visibility—have been actively suppressed for most of US history. Other cultures hold similar patterns. The long struggle for equity is a struggle to be heard: to tell one's story and know that it has been comprehended and remembered; for cumulative voices to burst out of the silence and compel change. Our Playback stage was a place where the unheard voices could be heard, where stories told—if our awareness, historical knowledge, and interactive skills were robust enough.

随着时间的推移，我们中的许多人（如今已包括创始剧团之外的实践者）逐渐意识到，一人一故事剧所基于的理想主义观点——即每个人的故事都具有价值——本身就蕴含着对社会正义的呼唤。在美国历史上绝大多数时期，穷人、有色人种、移民、妇女、儿童——事实上，所有不属于传统权势阶层和公众视野中的人群——的声音都一直遭到积极压制。其他文化中也存在类似的模式。这场争取公平的漫长斗争，本质上是一场争取被倾听的斗争：讲述自己的故事，并确信它已被理解和铭记；让汇聚的声音冲破沉默，从而推动变革。我们的一人一故事舞台正是让那些未被听见的声音得以被倾听的地方，也是讲述故事的地方——前提是我们的觉察力、历史认知和互动技能足够扎实。

There have been inflection points throughout Playback's history, often at gatherings, which have jolted our community into anxiousness. At the international conference in Olympia, Washington, in 1995, DC Playback—a multicultural company from Washington, DC—presented a stark analysis of racism within the Playback world. Uncomfortable as it was, it launched many of us on the unending journey of learning and changing, of commitment to building an ethos that fully acknowledges the realities of privilege and injustice, and the imperative to do all we can to address them. Later gatherings—notably a regional conference in the northeast US in 2000—continued this process of education and discovery, not without stormy confrontations and tears. By no means everyone welcomed the uncompromising focus of the organizers. For us, Jo and Jonathan, it seemed salient and necessary and we stood with the courageous people of color who insisted on making explicit the patterns of injustice so often—out of white obliviousness or weariness and wariness on the part of those who are oppressed—go unacknowledged.

在一人一故事的发展历程中，曾出现过若干转折点，往往发生在聚会场合，这些事件曾让我们的社群陷入焦虑。1995年在华盛顿州奥林匹亚市举行的国际会议上，来自华盛顿特区的多元文化剧团“DC Playback”对一人一故事领域内的种族主义进行了犀利的剖析。尽管这令人感到不适，但它促使我们许多人踏上了永无止境的学习与改变之旅，致力于构建一种充分承认特权与不公现实的伦理精神，并认识到必须竭尽所能去解决这些问题。此后的聚会——尤其是2000年在美国东北部举行的一次区域会议——延续了这一教育与探索的过程，期间不乏激烈的冲突与泪水。绝非所有人都欢迎组织者这种毫不妥协的关注点。但对我们——乔和乔纳森——而言，这显得尤为重要且必要；我们与那些勇敢的有色人种站在一起，他们坚持要将不公正的模式公之于众——这些模式往往因白人的无知，或是受压迫者自身的疲惫与戒备，而长期被忽视。

As civil rights leaders always remind us, the path to racial justice is long and maddeningly circuitous, impeded by the forces of inertia and amnesia. For example, at a more recent US gathering, an African American woman told a story in which the central point about racism—clearly present though obliquely expressed in this lyrical story—was ignored in the enactment. Another Black woman spoke up, furious at the performing team and at the mostly white audience for not intervening. Passionate discussions ensued about the importance of addressing race and racism in Playback. It became clear that almost none of the 100 or so people present knew about the intense explorations that had taken place in our community years before and had led to significant changes we thought were incredible. They weren't. It was a lesson in the need for constant attention and vigilance.

正如民权领袖们总是提醒我们的那样，通往种族正义的道路漫长而令人抓狂地曲折，且受到惯性与遗忘力量的阻碍。例如，在最近的一次美国聚会上，一位非裔美国女性讲述了一个故事，其中关于种族主义的核心观点——尽管在这个抒情故事中隐晦地表达出来，却清晰可见——在演绎时却被忽略了。另一位黑人女性愤然发声，既对表演团队，也对未予干预的以白人为主的观众表示愤怒。随后，关于在“一人一故事剧”中探讨种族与种族主义重要性的热烈讨论随之展开。很明显，在场的约100人中，几乎无人知晓多年前我们社群曾进行过深入探讨，并由此引发了我们曾认为不可思议的重大变革。但事实并非如此。这让我们深刻认识到，必须时刻保持关注与警惕。

Slow as our progress might be, these ongoing disruptions to the majority-white, heterosexual, middle-class culture of Playback Theatre and the stories that go beyond the inward-looking personal stories that had been our initial focus.¹⁵ Many in the Playback world began to use Playback in a more concerted way to address inequities, injustices, and major fault lines both within and between our societies: international conferences and even regional gatherings can include people from 30 or 40 countries, a microcosm of our troubled and unjust world order. Political divisions and oppressions come into focus, insensitivity and injustice are rightfully challenged. At the 1997 international conference in Perth, Australia, gay and lesbian playbackers shared stories in an open workshop-performance (non-gay participants were welcome to join "us perverts," as one of the instigators said in her ironic Kiwi way). They emerged from their session to challenge the rest of the conference participants to "meet us halfway on the bridge." The European regional gathering in Amsterdam in 2014 saw friction between Russians and Ukrainians, Palestinians and Israelis, and among Dutch participants bitterly divided about a beloved Christmas tradition now seen as racist.

尽管我们的进步可能缓慢，但这些持续的颠覆正在改变一人一故事剧场中以白人为主、异性恋、中产阶级的文化，同时也拓展了故事的边界——不再局限于我们最初关注的、内省式的个人故事。¹⁵一人一故事剧界中的许多人开始更有组织地运用一人一故事，以应对社会内部及社会之间存在的不平等、不公正以及重大分歧：国际会议甚至地区性聚会都可能汇聚来自30或40个国家的参与者，这恰似我们这个动荡且不公正的世界秩序的缩影。政治分歧与压迫日益凸显，而缺乏同理心与不公正现象也理所当然地受到了挑战。在1997年澳大利亚珀斯举行的国际会议上，男同性恋和女同性恋“一人一故事”参与者在一场开放的工作坊表演中分享了故事（一位发起人以她特有的新西兰式反讽语气说道，欢迎非同性恋参与者加入“我们这些变态”的行列）。他们结束环节后，向其他与会者发出挑战，要求大家“在桥上与我们相向而行”。2014年在阿姆斯特丹举行的欧洲区域聚会上，俄罗斯人与乌克兰人、巴勒斯坦人与以色列人之间出现了摩擦；而荷兰参与者之间也因一项曾深受喜爱的、如今却被视为种族主义的圣诞传统而产生了尖锐分歧。

即便拥有一人一故事坚韧而包容的仪式，即便具备我们所拥有的倾听技巧，要在经历挫伤和沮丧之后重新站起来，也绝非易事。

One outcome of this expanded focus on stories with societal resonance has been the use of Playback in the turmoil following natural disasters—in which inequity often plays a part.¹⁶ After the Indian Ocean tsunami in 2004, local Playback groups offered shows for displaced people whose homes and livelihood had been lost and who were in further danger of losing their stories, silenced by shock-doctrine capitalism which swooped in to build high-rise hotels where fisherfolk used to live and work. When Hurricane Katrina hit New Orleans in 2005, there was again a need for real people's stories to be heard and shared, in contrast to the mainstream media's story. With the majority of those affected in New Orleans being people of color, it was not a fitting project for a white-led group. Guided by Pamela Freeman from Philadelphia, eleven other practitioners of color from around the US met for a preliminary workshop to share and strategize before traveling to New Orleans. At the invitation of the group, Jonathan was also present, as a witness rather than either leader or participant.

Our own—Jo and Jonathan's—personal involvement in social justice, including antiracism, has evolved over time. In the spirit of what is now called applied theatre, the original company performed in senior centers, group homes for people with disabilities or troubled teens, and prisons. We were willing to work in very difficult conditions. At the same time, we had only minimal awareness of race, class, and privilege. At a high school in Brooklyn, for instance, where the only white faces were teachers, the lack of congruence between the mostly Caribbean and African American children and us, with our older, white-skinned faces, was stark. We felt it but did not yet know what to do about it.

我们——乔和乔纳森——在社会正义（包括反种族主义）方面的个人参与，随着时间的推移而不断演变。秉承如今被称为“应用戏剧”的精神，创始剧团曾在老年人活动中心、残疾人或问题青少年的集体之家以及监狱中演出。我们愿意在极其艰难的条件下工作。与此同时，我们对种族、阶级和特权的认知却十分有限。例如，在布鲁克林的一所高中，那里唯一的白人面孔是教师，以加勒比裔和非裔美国人为主的学生与我们——这些年长且皮肤白皙的人——之间的格格不入显得格外鲜明。我们感受到了这种差异，却尚不知该如何应对。

JO: In the early 1990s, I joined a study group on race and racism with six or seven other local residents, white, Black, and Latinx, Jonathan soon became a member as well. We met weekly for several years. It was an extraordinary opportunity to listen to people of very different backgrounds in response to readings and videos that we took turns suggesting—an education we have never forgotten.

JO：20世纪90年代初，我与另外六七位当地居民——包括白人、黑人和拉丁裔——加入了一个关于种族与种族主义的研究小组，乔纳森很快也成为了成员。我们每周聚会，持续了好几年。这是一个难得的机会，让我们能够倾听背景迥异的人们对我们轮流推荐的阅读材料和视频的回应——这是一段我们永生难忘的学习经历。

As someone who did not grow up in the US, the depth and perniciousness of racism in this culture was hard to comprehend. At the 2000 Playback gathering, I was shocked as I listened to stories from participants in a caucus for white people only, understanding viscerally for the first time how white Americans have been saturated with racist myths since infancy. My experience had been different: Racism and racial injustice endure in New Zealand, with its colonial history, but there has never been slavery and its insidious, poisoned, and all-pervasive consequences. Even so, as someone who now lives in the US, I am tainted by its racism. I carry unearned privilege granted to me by my whiteness. I share the responsibility to learn, grow, and take action to build justice. For me, that means using Playback with humanity and awareness to invite and honor stories from seldom-heard voices. It means standing on the street to protest oppression and corruption. It means speaking up even when it is controversial to do so, both in North American and international contexts.

作为一个并非在美国长大的人，我很难理解这种文化中种族主义的深度和危害性。在2000年的一人一故事聚会上，当我聆听一个仅限白人参加的小组会议中参与者的故事时，我深受震撼，第一次切身体会到美国白人自幼便被种族主义神话所浸透。我的经历则有所不同：尽管新西兰有着殖民历史，种族主义和种族不公依然存在，但这里从未有过奴隶制及其隐蔽、毒害且无处不在的后果。即便如此，作为如今生活在美国的人，我也受到了美国种族主义的侵染。我享有因白人身份而获得的、不劳而获的特权。我有责任去学习、成长并采取行动，以构建正义。对我而言，这意味着以人文关怀和觉知之心运用一人一故事形式，倾听并致敬那些鲜少被听到的声音。这意味着走上街头抗议压迫与腐败。这意味着即使在北美乃至国际背景下，即便发声会引发争议，我也要勇于发声。

JONATHAN: In 1993, six years after the end of the original company, and inspired by the study group on race and racism that Jo and I belonged to, I began to teach a series of Playback Theatre courses called "Social Change." I remember having an acute sense of inadequacy in offering to explore this subject, but feeling, at the same time, that a first step—for myself and as a leader of the Playback Theatre community—had to be taken. In the next few years, I facilitated Playback workshops explicitly on this theme in the US, Germany, Japan, and Australia. Some of our colleagues felt that these kinds of workshops, with their emphasis on justice and fairness, took Playback Theatre in an unfortunately "political" direction, weakening the core value of respect for all. But Jo and I did not accept this dichotomy. To the contrary, practicing Playback with a naive or uninformed analysis regarding social issues virtually ensured the perpetuation of a status quo where the powerful and privileged shared their stories while others stayed in the shadows. With my own privileged background, I was no doubt not the best person to lead these workshops, and yet in them I did hear and embody stories from Aboriginal Australians, Korean Japanese, Maori New Zealanders, Jewish Germans, and African Americans.

乔纳森：1993年，在创始剧团解散六年后，受乔和我所在的关于种族与种族主义的研究小组的启发，我开始教授一系列名为“社会变革”的一人一故事剧场课程。我记得当时在提出探讨这个主题时，内心曾有过强烈的无力感，但与此同时，我也觉得——无论是对我个人而言，还是作为一人一故事剧场社群的领导者——都必须迈出这一步。在接下来的几年里，我在美国、德国、日本和澳大利亚主持了专门围绕这一主题的一人一故事剧场工作坊。一些同侪认为，这类强调正义与公平的工作坊，将一人一故事剧场带向了一个令人遗憾的“政治化”方向，削弱了“尊重所有人”这一核心价值。但乔和我并不认同这种二分法。恰恰相反，如果在对社会问题缺乏深刻理解或分析的情况下进行一人一故事剧场实践，几乎注定会延续一种现状：即权势者和特权阶层讲述他们的故事，而其他人则始终处于阴影之中。鉴于我自身的特权背景，我无疑并非主持这些工作坊的最佳人选，然而在这些工作坊中，我确实倾听了并演绎了澳大利亚原住民、日裔韩国人、新西兰毛利人、德国犹太人和非裔美国人的故事。

As performers and teachers, we had to acquire skills in responding to the often unconscious behavior of audience members and students—the high-status white man who has no idea that he is dominating the workshop discussion; the woman whose hand flies up first at every performance; the native English-speakers who cannot remember to speak more slowly so that international students can understand them; the middle school students who parrot anti-immigrant or homophobic sentiments they've heard at home. It's a complex, delicate task to address such moments constructively and kindly in the heat of a performance or training, even harder when you are the visiting outsider in another country.

作为表演者和教师，我们必须掌握应对观众和学生那些往往无意识行为的技巧——比如那位完全没意识到自己正在主导工作坊讨论的高地位白人男性；那位在每场演出中总是第一个举手的女性；那些忘记放慢语速以便国际学生听懂的英语母语者；以及那些复述在家中听到的反移民或恐同言论的中学生。在演出或培训的紧张氛围中，以建设性且友善的方式应对这些时刻是一项复杂而微妙的任务，而当你作为外来访客身处异国他乡时，这更是难上加难。

JO: In a workshop in Germany a participant—a psychotherapist and the respected supervisor of several others in the room—pushed up the corners of her eyes with her fingers as she played an Asian person. Trying to help my American language stereotypes are hurtful, I told her about my American language teacher who mined a Heil Hitler salute to indicate the German. The woman objected. "Not everyone here was a Nazi," she said. "But all Chinese people have slanted eyes." Others in the workshop understood my point, even if she did not, especially when we re-enacted the scene portraying the nature of the Chinese person, not her physical appearance. Sometimes, reaching the others who are present, whether workshop participants or an audience, is the best we can do.

JO：在德国的一次工作坊上，一位参与者——一位心理治疗师，也是在场几位同行的受人尊敬的督导——在扮演亚洲人时，用手指向上拉了拉眼角。为了帮助她认识到“美国式语言刻板印象”的伤害性，我向她讲述了我的美国语言老师曾通过“希特勒万岁”的敬礼动作来刻画德国人的例子。这位女士表示反对。“这里并非所有人都曾是纳粹，”她说，“但所有中国人都有眯缝眼。”尽管她不理解，但工作坊中的其他人却明白了我的观点，尤其是当我们重新演绎那个场景时——该场景刻画的是中国人的本质，而非其外貌特征。有时，触动在场的其他人——无论是工作坊参与者还是观众——就是我们所能做的最好之事。

With Playback now thriving in so many countries and cultures, sociopolitical issues might include class, caste, religious intolerance, gender bias, or political exclusion. In some places it is impossible for Playback companies to not attend to social justice topics: these are the stories that people come to tell.¹⁹ In others, the political climate is so repressive that stories of injustice have to go underground.

随着一人一故事如今在众多国家和文化中蓬勃发展，社会政治议题可能涉及阶级、种姓、宗教排斥、性别偏见或政治排斥。在某些地方，一人一故事团体无法回避社会正义话题：这些正是人们前来讲述的故事。¹⁹在其他地方，政治氛围如此压抑，以至于关于不公的故事不得不转入地下。

¹⁹ See Chapter 23, "Stories by Firelight."

¹⁹ 参见第23章《篝火旁的故事》。

## As a long-married couple who created this work together, what can you say about gender balance in Playback Theatre? 作为一对结婚多年的夫妻，你们共同创作了这部作品，对于一人一故事剧场中的性别平衡，你们有什么看法？

Jo: This is the most personal topic that we've ever written about. We are doing so here because our very private journey as cofounders who are also a male-female partnership has implications that go beyond us, especially as gender equity and sexism in Playback Theatre have come under discussion in recent times.

乔：这是我们迄今为止写过的最私人的话题。我们之所以在此探讨，是因为作为联合创始人，同时又是一对男女搭档，我们这段极其私人的经历所蕴含的意义已超越了我们自身，尤其是近年来一人一故事剧场中的性别平等与性别歧视问题已引发广泛讨论。

When Playback began, Jonathan was thirty-two years old and I was twenty-six. We had met nine years before, at twenty-three and seventeen. On the deepest level we had quickly recognized each other as soulmates and equals. But in worldly and psychological terms, we did not feel ourselves to be equals, with discrepancies in age, family wealth, nationality, and education (he a Fulbright scholar from Harvard, myself a first-year student at the small university in New Zealand where he came to study) as well as the pervasive assumption that men's lives mattered more. The disparity only grew stronger when I became an immigrant to the United States, cut off from all that was familiar.

一人一故事刚起步时，乔纳森三十二岁，我二十六岁。我们九年前相识，那时他二十三岁，我十七岁。在最深层的层面，我们很快就认定对方是灵魂伴侣和平等的伙伴。但在世俗和心理层面上，我们并不觉得自己是平等的——年龄、家庭财富、国籍和教育背景（他是哈佛大学的富布赖特学者，而我只是他来新西兰求学时，那所小型大学里的一名大一新生）都存在差异，加之当时普遍认为男人的生活更重要。当我作为移民来到美国，与一切熟悉的事物断绝联系时，这种差距变得更加明显。

TODAY, AFTER MORE THAN fifty years of marriage and forty-five years of Playback, this asymmetry has transformed completely, at least in our personal lives. But at the time that Playback began, the imbalance in our sense of ourselves prevailed. On the path toward professional identity, Jonathan was much further along than I was. It was his vision and initiative, not mine, that led to Playback's creation. But, looking back, I contributed my own considerable creativity in the ferment of those first years when the basic concept took on flesh and grew into a viable practice. I also supported from behind the scenes, a comfortable place for me—organizing, handling publicity, writing, creating our graphics, and sewing our performing outfits. Much of the early documentation is in my voice, even my handwriting. I did not question that the work itself should be promoted as Jonathan's work, leading inevitably to the inaccurate impression that he built Playback Theatre alone. The truth is that not only did I share in its crucial early development: the other members of the original company did as well. It was a genuinely collective effort—in which Jonathan's role as leader and inspirer and the one who carried the largest burden of responsibility was nevertheless fundamental. One could not have taken place without the other.

如今，在经历了五十多年的婚姻和四十五年的一人一故事实践之后，这种不对称已彻底转变，至少在我们的个人生活中是如此。但在一人一故事刚刚起步之时，我们对自我的认知中仍充斥着这种失衡。在职业身份的形成之路上，乔纳森比我走得要远得多。正是他的远见和主动，而非我，促成了一人一故事剧场的创立。但回首往事，在最初那几年充满活力的岁月里，当基本理念逐渐成形并发展为一种可行的实践时，我也贡献了自己的相当大的创造力。我还从幕后提供支持——这对我来说是一个舒适的位置——负责组织、处理宣传、写作、设计我们的视觉元素，以及缝制我们的演出服装。早期的大部分记录都以我的口吻书写，甚至还有我的手写字迹。我从未质疑过这项工作应以乔纳森的名义推广，这不可避免地导致了人们产生他独自创建一人一故事剧场的错误印象。事实是，不仅我参与了其至关重要的早期发展，创始剧团的其他成员同样如此。这是一项真正的集体努力——尽管如此，乔纳森作为领导者、启发者以及承担最大责任的人，其作用依然至关重要。没有一方，另一方便无法存在。

Our private relationship was not static. Over the long years of loving and parenting and working, we learned how to face our personal wounds, self-delusions, and weaknesses and build a more mindful, egalitarian marriage. Our professional life gradually caught up, not always smoothly, but eventually enabling us to become mutually respectful and appreciative partners. The hurtful experiences that used to arise where my role was openly devalued or dismissed—by women as well as men—now happen far less frequently. But I can still be derailed by unthinking disrespect—arriving, for example, for a workshop that we've been invited to co-lead and have carefully prepared together, only to find a large sign on the door saying "Workshop with Jonathan Fox." Or having our academic host excitedly introduce Jonathan to his (male) colleagues while ignoring me as I stand awkwardly beside him. There is no graceful solution in such situations: one can either speak up and interrupt the bonhomie, or stay silent like a clod of earth.

我们的私人关系并非一成不变。在漫长的相爱、育儿和工作的岁月里，我们学会了如何面对各自的内心创伤、自我欺骗和软弱，从而建立起一段更加觉知、平等的婚姻。我们的职业生活也逐渐跟上了这一步伐，虽然过程并非一帆风顺，但最终使我们成为了相互尊重、彼此欣赏的伙伴。过去那些曾让我深受伤害的经历——无论来自女性还是男性，我的角色都被公开贬低或忽视——如今已少之又少。但我仍会因不经意的轻视而感到失落——例如，当我们受邀共同主持并精心准备了一个研讨会，抵达现场却发现门上挂着一块大牌子，上面写着“乔纳森·福克斯研讨会”。又或者，当我们的学术东道主兴致勃勃地向他的（男性）同事介绍乔纳森时，却对我视若无睹，而我只能尴尬地站在他身旁。在这种情况下，没有体面的解决办法：要么挺身而出打破这种融洽的氛围，要么像一块泥巴一样保持沉默。

Playback Theatre has probably attracted more women than men as practitioners. At a rough guess, women lead more than half of the companies worldwide, alone or in a leadership team. Whether woman-led or not, there are, in fact, a number of integral aspects of Playback itself that seem female in nature, at least as femaleness is traditionally understood.²⁰ It is a gentle, flexible form, with receptiveness at its core. But when I hear about groups led by powerful and not always sensitive men, or about a confident but inexperienced man who has claimed more stature than his knowledge justifies, or pushed aside a more qualified woman, I am reminded that Playback Theatre takes place in a world still dominated by patriarchy. Disrupting this pattern of power, as with any form of power, takes intentionality and courage.

一人一故事剧场的实践者中，女性可能比男性更多。粗略估计，全球超过半数的剧团由女性领导，无论是单独领导还是作为领导团队的一员。无论是否由女性领导，事实上，一人一故事剧场本身确实存在许多本质上具有女性特质的方面，至少从传统对“女性特质”的理解来看是如此。²⁰这是一种温和、灵活的形式，其核心在于接纳。但当我听说有些团体由强势且未必敏感的男性领导，或者某个自信但缺乏经验的男性自诩地位高于其知识水平所能支撑的程度，甚至排挤了更合格的女性时，我不禁意识到：一人一故事剧场所处的世界依然由父权制主导。要打破这种权力模式——正如打破任何形式的权力一样——需要有意识的努力和勇气。

²⁰ See Chapter 10, "Is Playback Female?"

²⁰ 参见第10章，“一人一故事是女性的吗？”

JONATHAN: In my thirties, while profoundly influenced by the counterculture movement, I remained a product of the male-dominant cultural education of my childhood, which produced a strong compulsion to stand out and to succeed. There is no question that in my striving, I took Jo's contribution for granted, and I shined in what attention I received. I remember our company members teasing me that I was going to be famous one day. They meant, I *wanted* to be famous one day, and they were right.

乔纳森：三十多岁时，尽管深受反主流文化运动的影响，但我依然是童年时期那种男性主导的文化教育的产物，这种教育让我产生了强烈的脱颖而出和取得成功的冲动。毋庸置疑，在追逐成功的过程中，我理所当然地将乔的贡献视为理所当然，并沉醉于自己所获得的关注之中。我记得剧团成员们常开玩笑说，我总有一天会成名的。他们的意思是，我*渴望*有朝一日成名，而他们说得没错。

But the fact is, I failed to succeed in the manner I initially had hoped for—to be recognized and respected in New York avant-garde theatre circles. Other company members did not share my aspirations. I tried too hard on my own, ending up disappointed and burned out. In retrospect, it would have been better for the company and myself if I had shared leadership with Jo and the other company members, who of course were helping and supporting me all the time.

但事实是，我未能以最初期望的方式取得成功——即在纽约前卫戏剧圈获得认可和尊重。其他剧团成员并不认同我的抱负。我独自拼命努力，最终却感到失望并精疲力竭。回过头来看，如果当时能与乔以及其他剧团成员（他们当然一直都在帮助和支持我）共同分担领导职责，对剧团和我自己来说都会更好。

When I finally began to intellectually accept Jo's seminal and co-equal role, to shift in my heart, I think the price I had paid made it even harder. What was all this sacrifice for, I said to myself, if I admit it was misconceived in the first place? Jo not only performed many of the essential "behind the scenes" tasks, as she writes. She also helped us always to deal with issues of society and to honor the importance of aesthetics. Later, in many real ways, she gained more experience than I, as founder, director, and now a performer with Hudson River Playback Theatre (which does share leadership), for thirty years, while I directed the original company for only twelve.

当我终于在理智上接受乔那至关重要且地位平等的角色，并在内心做出转变时，我想，我付出的代价让这一切变得更加艰难。我暗自思忖：如果我承认这一切从一开始就是误判，那么所有的牺牲究竟是为了什么？正如乔在文中所写，她不仅承担了许多至关重要的“幕后”工作，还始终帮助我们应对社会议题，并重视美学的重要性。后来，在许多实际层面上，她积累的经验甚至比我还要丰富——作为创始人、导演，以及如今在哈德逊河一人一故事剧场（该剧场确实实行领导层共享）担任表演者已长达三十年，而我执导创始剧团的时间仅为十二年。

It was a long road from our beginnings, where we both had one foot in the tradition of our parents (husband works, wife looks after the house and children). Gradually, gradually, I came to welcome our publicly standing together as equals... and to genuinely cringe when someone spoke to me with shining eyes while ignoring Jo. At a certain point, we began to refer to ourselves as cofounders and to ask our colleagues to acknowledge us as such. Most have readily accepted this change, giving recognition both to gender equity and collective creative process.

从我们起步之初算起，这是一段漫长的历程——那时我们俩都还半只脚踩在父母那代人的传统里（丈夫工作，妻子操持家务、照顾孩子）。渐渐地、渐渐地，我开始乐见我们以平等的身份共同站在公众面前……也开始在有人眼睛发亮地只跟我说话、却无视乔时，由衷地感到难堪。到了某个阶段，我们开始称自己为“联合创始人”，并请同事们也这样认可我们。大多数人欣然接受了这一变化，既认可了性别平等，也认可了集体创作过程。

Tensions remain. One of the trials of being cofounders of what has become a worldwide movement is that inevitably there are projections. Jo and I (and to some degree our daughter Hannah Fox, an international Playback trainer who co-runs the New York School of Playback Theatre) can be both idealized and targeted in ways that have little to do with who we actually are or what we have actually done.

但紧张关系依然存在。作为这一已发展成全球性运动的联合创始人，我们面临的考验之一是不可避免地会成为他人投射的对象。乔和我（以及在某种程度上，我们的女儿汉娜·福克斯——一位国际一人一故事剧场培训师，共同运营着纽约一人一故事剧场学校）既可能被理想化，也可能成为攻击目标，而这些与我们真实的身份或实际所为往往毫无关系。

It is a relief to have reached old age, where I can let go of the ambitiousness inculcated in my childhood, and genuinely want others to take the limelight in my stead, including my partner and co-creator.

能活到这个年纪，我感到如释重负，终于可以放下童年时期被灌输的野心，发自内心地希望他人——包括我的伴侣和共同创作者——能代替我站在聚光灯下。

## How has the vision of Playback Theatre changed since the early days? What might you have done differently in retrospect? 自早期以来，一人一故事剧场的理念发生了哪些变化？回过头来看，你们可能会做哪些不同的选择？

We recently came across a very early statement of the original Playback Theatre company's values:

我们最近发现了一份关于创始剧团价值观的早期声明：

> WE BELIEVE *in the richness and wisdom of the human story; and we will devote ourselves to acting out people's stories with all of our inventiveness, sympathy, and courage.*
>
> 我们*相信人类故事的丰富性与智慧；我们将倾注全部的创造力、同理心和勇气，致力于将人们的故事搬上舞台。*
>
> *We believe that any story—no matter what it is—can be of importance and is capable of being rendered with meaning and beauty if we are skilled enough.*
>
> *我们相信，任何故事——无论是什么——都可能具有重要意义，只要我们技艺精湛，就能将其演绎得富有深意且充满美感。*
>
> *We believe in a theatre which serves.*
>
> *我们相信一种服务于人的戏剧。*
>
> *We believe in a theatre that tries to reach everyone in the audience, and every type of audience, including the meek, the dependent, and the different.*
>
> *我们相信一种致力于触及每位观众、每类观众的戏剧，包括温顺者、依赖者以及与众不同者。*
>
> *We believe in a theatre of neighbors.*
>
> *我们相信一种邻里间的戏剧。*
>
> *We believe in a theatre that is for the good of the actors as well as the spectators.*
>
> *我们相信一种既造福演员也造福观众的戏剧。*
>
> *We believe in the spontaneity approach.*
>
> *我们相信自发性创作的方法。*
>
> *We believe in finding a balance between collective concerns and individual personhood... and between being and doing.*
>
> *我们相信在集体关切与个人人格之间……以及在“存在”与“行动”之间寻求平衡。*
>
> *And we believe in artistic standards, which requires education, criticism, and the constant reach for perfection.*

The understanding and practice of Playback Theatre has been deepened, refined, and complicated in the intervening years, thanks to the work of countless practitioners as well as to our own continuing learning. A similar statement written now would refer explicitly to the commitment to social justice, embedded by implication in this proclamation. But in general, these values remain the foundation of what we do.

在过去的岁月里，得益于无数实践者的努力以及我们自身的持续学习，一人一故事剧场的理解与实践得到了深化、精炼和丰富。若在当下撰写类似的声明，会明确提及对社会正义的承诺——这一承诺在本宣言中已隐含其中。但总体而言，这些价值观依然是我们一切工作的基石。

Throughout our own years of performing and teaching we have observed, documented, and researched how Playback Theatre creates impact for its audiences and for the community. We continue to share our discoveries in our writing and teaching, offering our understanding of elements that will make the work more effective for more people—a pressing task in a world that seems to be unraveling in the face of climate catastrophe, nationalist extremism, the chokehold of white supremacy, economic breakdown, and the Covid-19 pandemic. This volume includes Jonathan's essay on the theory of narrative reticulation, a rationale that he has developed in recent years for how and why Playback works and its potential impact in counteracting isolation and despair;²¹ and another essay on the Listening Hour, a variation created and used widely online during the pandemic when in much of the world face-to-face performances are impossible.²² Jo's essays on climate-focused performances, one of which is included here, are intended to support companies wishing to play a role in climate action in their own communities.²³

DO WE HAVE REGRETS? Might we have taken different decisions, in hindsight? Yes and yes. Individually and together we've made choices based on what we were aware of at the time, but that perspective can look different over one's shoulder. One decision with far-reaching implications was the philosophical choice to allow others to take our idea and use it, without remuneration for ourselves, in the spirit of Abbie Hoffman and the other generative pathbreakers of the '60s and '70s who shared their ideas freely. We still stand by this choice. It has led to a rich diversity in practice and has contributed to the flourishing of Playback in so many places. Looking back, however, it would have been wise to send it off with clearer guidelines and a few warnings. We could have said more emphatically in those early years that you have a responsibility to know what you're doing if you're going to do this work well and safely. We could have tried harder to ensure that would-be practitioners apprentice themselves to those who already had substantial experience. We could have emphasized formal training more than we did. We could have said by all means innovate, but not until you have actually learned the form. (In fact we have said this, but have not intervened to discourage ill-judged "improvements" based on an incomplete understanding of the work.) We could have done more to teach the pragmatic organizing skills a company needs to be a respected, effective presence in a community—and to make it clear that being a company leader is a major task and commitment that not everyone is suited for. We could have encouraged people to remain humble and honest about their own capacities and limitations, and to commit to carrying out the work with social awareness as well as respect and consideration for colleagues. Some of these precepts are now embedded in the Centre for Playback Theatre's Code of Ethics, as well as being folded into the teaching that we and others offer.²⁴

我们是否有遗憾？事后看来，我们是否本可以做出不同的决定？是的，确实如此。无论是个人还是集体，我们当时都是基于已有的认知做出选择的，但回首往事，这种视角可能会有所不同。其中一项影响深远的决定，是基于哲学层面的选择——我们允许他人采纳并使用我们的理念，而不索取任何报酬，这正是秉承了阿比·霍夫曼（Abbie Hoffman）以及60、70年代其他富有创造力的开拓者们自由分享思想的精神。我们至今仍坚守这一选择。它催生了实践中的丰富多样性，并促成了一人一故事在众多地区蓬勃发展。然而回首往事，若当初能附上更明确的指导方针和几条警示，或许更为明智。在那些早期岁月里，我们本可以更强调：若想将这项工作做好且确保安全，你就有责任弄清楚自己在做什么。我们本可以更加努力地确保准实践者向那些已有丰富经验的人士拜师学艺。我们本可以比实际做得更重视正规培训。我们本可以明确表示：尽管大胆创新，但务必先真正掌握这一形式。（事实上我们确实说过这一点，但并未介入阻止那些基于对工作理解不全面而做出的、不恰当的“改进”。） 我们本可以付出更多努力，传授剧团所需的务实组织技能，使其在社群中成为受人尊敬且高效的存在——并明确指出，担任剧团领导者是一项重大任务和承诺，并非人人都能胜任。我们本可以鼓励大家保持谦逊，诚实地面对自身的能力与局限，并承诺在开展工作时既具备社会意识，又能尊重和体谅同事。其中一些准则现已纳入一人一故事剧场中心的《道德准则》，同时也融入了我们及他人提供的教学内容中。²⁴

Human nature is what it is—loving, generous, idealistic, brave, and also at times fearful, arrogant, shallow, mean-spirited. We see all of it in the Playback Theatre community. Needless to say, we are fallible ourselves. But, dispiriting as it can be to witness or learn about feuds, unfairness, and unethical behavior among practitioners, we are enormously heartened that the great majority of practitioners do good work and strive sincerely to foster kindness and respect in relation to their audiences and colleagues. Our hope is that the aspirational values of Playback Theatre—its openness to outcome, its honoring of ordinary people's stories, its commitment to voices otherwise unheard, its call to the expansiveness of the artistic imagination—will always be a sort of magnetic north which all of us can steer by.

人性本如此——既充满爱、慷慨、理想主义、勇敢，有时却也充满恐惧、傲慢、肤浅和刻薄。在一人一故事剧场社群中，我们能看到人性的一切面向。毋庸赘言，我们自己也难免犯错。尽管目睹或得知实践者之间的纷争、不公和不道德行为可能会令人沮丧，但绝大多数实践者都致力于做好本职工作，并真诚地努力在与观众及同事的互动中培养善意与尊重，这一点让我们倍感鼓舞。我们希望，一人一故事剧场所倡导的理想价值观——对结果的开放态度、对普通人故事的珍视、对那些原本无法被听见的声音的关注，以及对艺术想象力无限拓展的呼唤——将永远成为我们所有人可以遵循的指南针。
