## SYNOPSIS 简介

At present, the Playback community is divided between seeing Playback as therapy or art. Because Playback's democratic vision of ordinary creativity found support in the group-oriented psychodrama community rather than in the individualist-oriented artistic culture, the psychodrama community's support has paradoxically contributed both to Playback's stability and erosion as a purely theatrical language. Playback is consequently termed the "stepchild" of psychodrama. The promulgation of story-centered therapeutic ideology and the "shadowless" influence of humanistic psychology now threaten to erode Playback's original vision of itself as a story-centered theater of creative enhancement and socio-political empowerment.

目前，一人一故事社群分为将一人一故事视为治疗还是艺术。因为一人一故事对普通创造力的民主愿景在以群体为导向的心理剧社区而不是以个人主义为导向的艺术文化中得到了支持，心理剧社区的支持矛盾地促进了一人一故事作为一种纯粹戏剧语言的稳定和侵蚀。因此，一人一故事被称为心理剧的“继女”。以故事为中心的治疗意识形态的传播和人本主义心理学的“无阴影”影响，现在有可能侵蚀一人一故事作为一个以故事为中心的戏剧的最初愿景，即创造性增强和社会政治赋权。

An exploration of the iconoclastic Jungian James Hillman's idea of psychology as "soulmaking" gives us a vocabulary which frees us from the identity of "stepchild" without sacrificing the important role psychodrama plays in the development of the Playback practitioner. Using Hillman's language, we can describe ourselves as awakening the aesthetic heart to ensoul the world, releasing us from the false conflict of "therapy" and "art" within our community and challenging us to expand our healing parameters beyond the self to include the world.

对反传统荣格学派的詹姆斯·希尔曼的“塑造灵魂”心理学思想的探索，为我们提供了一个将我们从“继女”的身份中解放出来的词汇，同时又不牺牲心理剧在一人一故事实践者的发展中所起的重要作用。使用希尔曼的语言，我们可以将自己描述为唤醒审美之心以赋予世界灵魂，将我们从我们社区内“治疗”和“艺术”的虚假冲突中释放出来，并挑战我们将我们的疗愈因子扩展到自我之外，以包括世界。

## PROLOGUE 开场白

Every theory is a remaking of the world so that the soul putting forth the theory can live in it. The theory put forth in this paper is no different. It arises from my personal story, the drama of my life's effort to reconcile art and therapy in both an intellectually vigorous and emotionally vibrant way.

每一种理论都是对世界的重塑，以便提出理论的灵魂能够活在其中。本文提出的理论也不例外。它源于我的个人故事，我一生努力调和艺术和治疗的戏剧，以一种智力上和情感上都充满活力的方式。

I am a published writer who is the daughter of a psychoanalyst who is herself the daughter of a poet. I have been in various forms of therapy, ranging from classic Freudian analysis on the couch to psychodrama, with maverick variations between. Some of the therapists I have seen have been superb; others, terrible. I have trained as a psychodramatist and have a masters degree in health education with a specialty in human sexuality. I have attempted to pursue graduate training in psychology three times. Every time, the creative artist in me has balked. Yet, my creative self continues to feel impoverished without the riches and insights from the field of psychology.

我是一位出版过作品的作家，是一位精神分析学家的女儿，而精神分析学家本人也是一位诗人的女儿。我接受过各种形式的治疗，从沙发上的经典弗洛伊德分析到心理剧，其间有特立独行的变化。我见过的一些治疗师非常出色；另一些，则糟糕透顶。我接受过心理剧的培训，还拥有健康教育硕士学位，专业是人类性学。我曾三次尝试攻读心理学研究生课程。每一次，我内心的艺术家创造力都止步不前。然而，没有心理学领域的财富和见解，我的创造性自我仍然感到贫乏。

The ideas set forth in this paper are my attempt at this juncture in my life of reconciling these various "mothers" in my personal story. In my personal struggle, I have bent my mind around some serious and sometimes mind-wrenching ideas t so that I could understand how it is that Playback contains my two passions, my many mothers.

本文中提出的观点是我在人生的这个关键时刻，试图在我的个人故事中调和这些不同的“母亲”。在我个人的挣扎中，我把我的思想集中在一些严肃的、有时令人揪心的想法上，这样我就能理解，一人一故事是如何包含了我的两种激情，我的许多母亲。

If Playback is a theater of membership, which I believe it is, then these are ideas which carry for me my own reasons for desiring membership in this unique theater form.

如果一人一故事是一个归属的剧场，我相信它是，那么这些想法对我来说就是我自己渴望成为这种独特剧场形式一员的原因。

The implications of my ideas for training and the practice of Playback challenge me and, I anticipate, will challenge others. For I am not asking the Playback community to choose between seeing Playback as art or therapy. I am asking the Playback community to allow the taxonomy of "therapy" and "art" to collapse, then steal, like Hermes (patron god of artists) from an unwitting psychologist, an idea. This idea--ensoulment -- is neither therapy as we would recognize it (despite its being the idea of a Jungian), nor is ensoulment art as we would customarily think of it. It is an idea which I believe can contain Playback as neither art nor therapy while at the same time preserving its debt to both. Ensouling is what we do; Ensoulers are what we are; and, finally, Playback itself is Ensoulment -- as I hope to persuade you in this chapter in my lifelong story about art and therapy, aesthetics and psychology.

我对训练和一人一故事实践的想法的含义对我提出了挑战，我预计也会对其他人提出挑战。因为我并不是要求一人一故事社区在将一人一故事视为艺术或治疗之间做出选择。我是在要求一人一故事社区允许“治疗”和“艺术”的分类学崩塌，然后像赫尔墨斯（艺术家的守护神）一样，从一个不知情的心理学家那里偷走一个想法。这个想法——赋予灵魂——既不是我们所认识的治疗（尽管它是荣格学派的想法），也不是我们习惯上认为的灵魂化艺术。我相信这是一种既不作为艺术也不作为治疗的理念，同时又保留了它对两者的债务。赋予灵魂是我们所做的；赋予灵魂者就是我们；最后，一人一故事本身就是一种赋予灵魂——正如我希望在我关于艺术和治疗、美学和心理学的一生故事的这一章中说服你们的那样。

If, by the end of my exhortation, I have failed to persuade you that the idea of Ensoulment reconciles therapy and art in the Playback community, I hope that I will at least have succeeded in persuading you that ideas about Playback are critical to the capacity for membership in each of us. At the very least, I hope I will have aroused other members to undertake their own serious thinkings on what this remarkable process called Playback is.

如果在我的劝勉结束时，我还没能说服你，赋予灵魂的想法调和了一人一故事社区的治疗和艺术，我希望我至少能成功地说服你，关于一人一故事的想法对我们每个人的成员能力至关重要。至少，我希望我能唤醒其他成员，让他们对这个被称为一人一故事的非凡过程进行自己的严肃思考。

### PLAYBACK'S ROOTS IN "EFFICACIOUS" THEATER 一人一故事的根源在于“效用”剧场

Ideas about Playback abound in Playback. They inform the many small choices we make during a performance. Ideas lead the Conductor to ask one question rather than another. Ideas shape the choices actors make in the enactment. Ideas subtly determine the story the Teller chooses to tell. Ideas form invisible contours within the audience members, orchestrating their emotional responses. Sometimes the ideas are overt, and we hold them up before our own minds. Sometimes the ideas hover at the threshold of our consciousness, and we are only dimly aware of them. And sometimes they are hidden, buried where we cannot know them directly, and we would be startled to discover what they are.

关于一人一故事的思想在一人一故事中比比皆是。它们告诉我们在表演中所做的许多小选择。思想引导领航员问一个问题而不是另一个问题。思想影响着演员在剧中所做的选择。思想微妙地决定了讲述者选择讲述的故事。思想在观众心中形成了无形的轮廓，精心策划着他们的情绪反应。有时这些想法是公开的，我们把它们举在自己的脑海里。有时，这些思想徘徊在我们意识的门槛上，我们只是模糊地意识到它们。有时它们被隐藏起来，埋在我们无法直接认识的地方，当我们发现它们是什么时，会大吃一惊。

Of these many informing ideas there are two large ones in the Playback community at the present time. The international Playback community is conflicted between those practitioners, in a number of countries, who want Playback to be a form of therapy, and those who desire to develop it as a theater art. This conflict is not manifested openly, according to Jonathan Fox, but it is "heated." People complain about bad performances, and yet resist delimiting Playback as a performance art form, saying the performance quality is secondary to the transformative and healing effects of the experience. Yet this "heat" is a clue to us that something happens which does not feel good, or healing, when the performances are bad. Somehow, the healing aspects of Playback are contained within and heightened by performance. It is as if the intensity of attention performance requires acts like a distillation process which concentrates the power of the "medicine." How? Why? And what word can we call our process which can articulate this intensification of healing through performance? Theater? Therapy? Before we can address these questions, let's step back from the heat -- assured we will return to it -- and ask ourselves an obvious but important question: How is it that a theater form, after twenty years of life, can even be considered a form of therapy? How did we get here?

在这众多的启迪思想中，目前在一人一故事社区中有两个大的。国际上的一人一故事社群存在着冲突，一些国家的实践者希望一人一故事成为一种治疗形式，而另一些人则希望将其发展为一种戏剧艺术。根据乔纳森·福克斯(Jonathan Fox)的说法，这种冲突并没有公开表现出来，但它是“激烈的”。人们抱怨糟糕的表演，却又不愿将一人一故事界定为一种行为艺术形式，认为表演质量是次要的，不如体验带来的变革和治愈效果。然而，这种“热度”给我们提供了一个线索，当表演不好的时候，就会发生一些不让人感觉好的事情，或者不让人治愈的事情。不知何故，一人一故事的疗愈性被包含在表演之中，并被表演所强化。就好像集中注意力的表演需要像蒸馏过程一样的行为来集中“药物”的力量。怎么做呢？为什么？我们可以用什么词来称呼我们的过程，这个过程可以通过表演来表达这种强化的治愈？戏剧？治疗？在我们讨论这些问题之前，让我们先退一步——确信我们会回到它——问我们自己一个明显但重要的问题：为什么一种戏剧形式，在经历了二十年的生命之后，甚至可以被认为是一种治疗形式？我们是如何走到这一步的？

Playback began as a form of community theater in 1975 during a remarkable period in theater history. In the mid 70s in America and Europe, theater was pulsing with the ideas and methods of Grotowski, Boal, The Living Theater, Schechner's Performance Group, and the genius of Peter Brook, to name a few. It was a period when performance was infused with ideas, many borrowed from psychology and anthropology, about how theater and life were essential to each other. Whether in Grotowski's hieroglyphics and his experiments to make the actor "holy," or in The Performance Group's open rehearsals of Mother Courage on the street, theater was engaged in a global quest for an "efficacious" theatrical form vitally connected to socio-political. process. Influenced by the theology of Paolo Friere and the political theatrical experiments of Boal, Jonathan Fox sought to create an "efficacious" theater which empowered the socially disenfranchised and politically oppressed through the spontaneous enactment of their stories. Hence the Playback expression, "a people without someone to tell their story to is an oppressed people."

1975年，在戏剧史上一个非凡的时期，一人一故事作为一种社群戏剧形式开始。在70年代中期的美国和欧洲，戏剧在格罗托夫斯基、波瓦、生活剧场、谢赫纳的表演团体和天才彼得·布鲁克等人的思想和方法的推动下蓬勃发展。那是一个表演被注入思想的时期，许多思想借鉴了心理学和人类学，关于戏剧和生活是如何相互不可或缺的。无论是在格罗托夫斯基的象形文字和他让演员变得“神圣”的实验中，还是在表演团体在街头公开排练的《勇气母亲》中，戏剧都参与了一种与社会政治密切相关的“效用”戏剧形式的全球追求。过程。受保罗·弗里埃神学和波瓦政治戏剧实验的影响，乔纳森·福克斯试图创造一种“效用”戏剧，通过自发地演绎他们的故事，赋予社会上被剥夺权利和政治上受压迫的人以权力。因此有了一人一故事(Playback)这个表达，“如果一个群体没有可以讲述自己故事的对象，那么这个群体就是被压迫的。”。

As defined by Schechner, "efficacious theater" is on a continuum with entertainment at the other end.1 Because efficacious theater shares with the ritual roots of theater the goal of transformation it is traditional, even when it breaks Western mimetic theatrical norms. Avant garde theater, with its aspirations to transform the participants in the event (e.g., Grotowski's trance state), paradoxically belongs to traditional theater. Using Schechner's model, Playback is a classic example of the combination of ancient and avant garde, which defines Schechner's efficacious theater.

正如谢赫纳所定义的那样，“效用戏剧”与娱乐处于一个连续体的另一端因为效用戏剧与戏剧的仪式根源有共同的转化目标，所以它是传统的，即使它打破了西方模仿戏剧的规范。前卫戏剧渴望改变事件参与者（如格罗托夫斯基的恍惚状态），矛盾的是它属于传统戏剧。运用谢赫纳的模式，一人一故事是古典与前卫相结合的经典范例，它定义了谢赫纳的效用戏剧。

Playback was a theater conceived to be vital to the community, in which the community participated on all levels -- creation, both through telling and through invitation to the audience to be actors; regular or ritualized attendance, as in the traditional monthly performance by the Original Playback Theater Company; and in the continuing life of the theater, through community support. Much like classic Indian theater, which inspired 70s American practitioners, Playback takes its actors from working members of the community. Playback's everyday, real life content resonates with The Living Theater's experiments to break down the fourth wall and fuse "living" with "theater." Also consistent with American theatrical experiments of the 70s, such as the open rehearsal, Playback reveals its process throughout. The process of creating the enactment is itself an enactment; and, as in the moment when the actors are selecting the cloth, stage business is revealed. Finally, the Playback event in which the participants see enactments of their lives played back effects one's perspective on one's life, thus fulfilling the objective of traditional theater to transform its participants in some way.

一人一故事是一个被认为对社群至关重要的剧场，社群在各个层面都参与其中——创造，既通过讲述，也通过邀请观众成为演员；常规或仪式化的出席，如始创一人一故事剧团的传统月度演出；以及在剧场的持续生命中，通过社群的支持。就像经典的印度戏剧，启发了70年代的美国实践者，一人一故事从社群的工作人员中挑选演员。一人一故事的日常，现实生活的内容与生活剧场的实验产生共鸣，打破第四堵墙，融合“生活”与“戏剧”。同样与美国70年代的戏剧实验相一致，比如开放式排练，一人一故事自始至终都在揭示它的过程。创造实景的过程本身就是一个实景；而且，就像演员选布的那一刻，舞台生意就显露出来了。最后，参与者看到自己生活中的场景被演绎的一人一故事事件影响了一个人对自己生活的看法，从而在某种程度上实现了传统戏剧改变参与者的目标。

Where Playback departs from Schechner is in recognizing that when urban modern Westerners try to create rituals and transformations, "and become shamans in their own time, [they] produce either artificially forced or grossly provocational pieces," according to Jonathan Fox.2 There is indeed something "forced" in Brooks' trip to Africa when his Caucasian company mimicked the native rituals perfectly--but at the same time insulted the tribal members, who were appalled that women and men were dancing their sacred dances together. The provocative acts of the Living Theater when audience members were invited up on stage to fornicate was shocking in New Haven, but once it got to Berkeley it became pornographic and narcissistic squalor. much to the consternation of Judith Malina. 3

一人一故事与谢赫纳的不同之处在于，他认识到，当现代城市西方人试图创造仪式和转化，“并在自己的时代成为萨满时，（他们）要么制造人为强迫的作品，要么制造严重挑衅的作品。”布鲁克斯的非洲之行确实有一些“强迫”，他的白人同伴完美地模仿了当地的仪式，但同时又侮辱了部落成员，他们对女人和男人一起跳神圣的舞蹈感到震惊。在纽黑文，生活剧场(Living Theater)邀请观众上台私通的淫乱性行为令人震惊，但一旦传到伯克利，它就变成了色情和自恋的肮脏。这让朱迪思·马里纳大为震惊。3

Jonathan Fox, aware of the milieu of ritual and the 70s mandate for theater to engage "life" with art, shunned these types of performance inspired by Artaud's "theater of cruelty"4 in favor of a celebrational mode. The model for effective drama that Playback took was one of "awakening in people a sense of their own creativity--not only for art, but for every day social life."5 Instead of forcing shamanisms, Jonathan sought to develop a method in which5 ordinary contemporary life could be passed through a sharnan-like person -the Conductor -- who did not have to invoke Balinese ideas about art or learn how to scream in order to authenticate or legitimize the process. Jonathan was in search for a way to use story in modern life which could carry the wisdom for the community as story traditionally carried the teachings of the tribe in cultures still based on oral communication, without imitation.

乔纳森·福克斯(Jonathan Fox)意识到仪式的环境，以及70年代要求戏剧将“生活”与艺术联系起来的要求，他避开了这种受亚陶(Artaud)的“残酷戏剧”(theater of cruelty)启发的表演形式，而倾向于一种庆祝模式。一人一故事所采用的效用戏剧模式是“唤醒人们对自己创造力的意识——不仅是对艺术，而且对日常的社会生活。”乔纳森没有强迫萨满教，而是寻求发展一种方法，使普通的当代生活可以通过一个类似于萨满教的人——领航员——来传递，他不必为了证实或使这一过程合法化而诉诸巴厘岛人的艺术观念或学习如何尖叫。乔纳森在寻找一种在现代生活中使用故事的方法，它可以为社区带来智慧，因为故事传统上承载了部落的教义，这些文化仍然基于口述故事交流，而不是戏仿。

After studying the oral tradition with Albert Lord at Harvard, Jonathan left for Nepal as a member of the Peace Corps. In a remote village, he drew upon his theater skills to perform his Peace Corps duties as an agricultural specialist. Keenly aware of his position as outsider, he never made the attempt to imitate the traditional culture, as did Brook in Africa. He never forgot that he was himself a "deracinated urban child,"6 recognizing that he could not deny his cultural roots without committing psychological and aesthetic fraud. Hence, despite the seminal influence of Nepal on his quest for an efficacious form of story-theater, Jonathan eschewed any replication of 'primitive cultures' in favor of enactments of our own. He remained faithful to his own story-performing as a seed salesman among traditional shamans--in his quest for a method for "story" to be vital to modern, Western culture.

在哈佛大学跟随阿尔伯特·洛德学习口头传统后，乔纳森作为和平队的一员前往尼泊尔。在一个偏远的村庄里，他运用自己的戏剧才能，履行着作为农业专家的和平队职责。他深知自己作为局外人的身份，因此从未像布鲁克在非洲那样试图模仿当地传统文化。他始终没有忘记自己是一个“失去根基的城市孩子”6，并意识到若否认自己的文化根源，便等同于在心理和美学上自欺欺人。因此，尽管尼泊尔对他探索一种有效的“故事剧场”形式产生了深远影响，乔纳森仍摒弃了对“原始文化”的任何复制，转而倾向于演绎我们自己的文化。在寻求一种使“故事”对现代西方文化至关重要的方法时，他始终忠于自己的故事——以种子推销员的身份在传统萨满之中进行表演。

Jonathan conceived of the actor not as a specially selected artist nor as a derivative tribal member; but as anyone and everyone, here and now, "selling seeds," as it were. "We are all potential actors: not that kind of performer who speaks other people's scripts under other people's direction, but persons capable of creative human action."7 To make this point, as a theater director, Jonathan never imposed certain actions on participants which he had previously deemed of high political, metaphysical, or performance value; never tapped the shoulder of a participant and took him or her off to the woods for five days of privileged screaming. Jonathan refused to be seduced by "the scream" as a symbol for a pure theatrical language. Instead, he sought to "create a context for communication between actor and audience."8 Playback also refused to go into the woods, to retreat into a specially designated "sacred space"; but chose instead to go right into its neighborhood where it looked to the community itself for the material which was then enacted. It did not seek holy martyrdom or primitive authenticity for its actors or itself; it trusted actors own life experiences to provide insight to the Tellers. Playback's role was close to "the jester in a king's court, with his special license for truth and his ability to provide group cohesion."9

乔纳森将演员既不视为经过特别挑选的艺术家，也不视为从属的部落成员；而是视为此时此地任何一个人，或者说，就像是在“兜售种子”一样。“我们都是潜在的演员：不是那种在他人指导下朗读他人剧本的表演者，而是能够进行创造性人类行动的人。”7 为了阐明这一点，作为一名戏剧导演，乔纳森从未将他事先认定具有高度政治、形而上学或表演价值的特定动作强加给参与者；也从未拍着某位参与者的肩膀，把他或她带到树林里进行为期五天的“特权式呐喊”。乔纳森拒绝被“尖叫”作为纯粹戏剧语言的象征所诱惑。相反，他致力于“为演员与观众之间的交流创造一个语境”。8一人一故事也拒绝深入森林，拒绝退隐到专门划定的“神圣空间”；而是选择直接走进社群，从社群本身中汲取素材，进而将其演绎出来。它既不追求演员或自身的“神圣殉道”，也不追求“原始真实性”；而是相信演员自身的生活经历能为“讲述者”提供洞见。一人一故事的角色近似于“王廷中的弄臣，他拥有说真话的特权，并能促进群体凝聚力”。9

Such theatrical experiments as The Living Theater, Grotowski, and the other disciples of Artaud frightened participants -- actors and audience alike -by their attitude of daring people to do the socially and oftentimes sexually unacceptable in order to be among 'the chosen.' This style of tyrannical machismo bullying combined with the metaphysics of stripping bare, denuding the actor and participants literally, emotionally, and intellectually was dangerous -- intentionally so. The end result of this combination of retreat, outrage, bullying, and denuding participants emotionally in order to attain membership among the select could be not only psychologically and physically damaging, but the ultimate realization of such an "impossible" theater, as Kott eloquently and chillingly explicates, is the nightmare of cultism and Jonestown.

像生活剧场、格罗托夫斯基和阿尔托的其他弟子这样的戏剧实验，使参与者——演员和观众——都感到害怕，因为他们的态度是，人们为了成为“被选中的人”而大胆地做社会上和通常在性方面不可接受的事情。这种暴虐的大男子主义的欺凌风格，加上把演员和参与者从字面上、情感上和智力上剥光的形而上学，是危险的——是故意这样做的。这种撤退、愤怒、霸凌和情感上剥夺参与者以获得精英成员身份的组合的最终结果，可能不仅是心理和身体上的伤害，而且是这种“不可能”戏剧的最终实现，正如科特雄辩而令人不寒而栗地解释的那样，是邪教和琼斯镇的噩梦。

In eschewing the tyranny of denuding ideology and membership by special selection in favor of an open democracy of ordinary creativity, Playback challenged the masculine ego of the solitary artist-creator. It became, metaphorically, a safe mother rather than the bullying, 'divine' father. And like a safe mother, though the feeling within her embrace was oceanic, the language she spoke to us was mundane and grounded in the every day. Consequently, in contrast to the other experiments of the time, Playback is intimate with daily life and feels safe. And it is safe when done well, a point I shall return to later. Its success depends upon trust between the actors and the audience. Hence, no specialized language of scream and gesture, no secret election, no unearthly metaphysics, since these promote anxiety. In choosing a salutary, life-enhancing direction, Playback turned away from the dismemberment of the Dyonisian body of 70s theater to a theater of membership. The challenge it gave itself was how to make "membership" theatrically powerful without any special criterion for belonging. This embrace through story became -- and remains -- the transformational task of our theatrical form.

一人一故事摒弃了通过特殊选拔以排斥意识形态和成员身份、从而建立开放民主的普通创造力的专制方式，挑战了孤独艺术家-创作者那具有男性气质的自我。它在隐喻上成为了一位安全的母亲，而非那种欺凌性的“神圣”父亲。如同一位安全的母亲，尽管她怀抱中的感受如海洋般深邃，但她与我们交谈的语言却是平实而贴近日常生活的。因此，与其他时代实验不同，一人一故事与日常生活亲密相联，让人感到安心。当它做得好时，便显得安全——这一点我稍后会再次强调。它的成功取决于演员与观众之间的信任。因此，它没有使用专门的尖叫或手势语言，没有秘密的选举，也没有超自然的形而上学，因为这些都会引发焦虑。在选择一种有益于生活、提升生命质量的方向时，一人一故事远离了70年代戏剧中迪奥尼西式身体的肢解，转向了一种归属感的戏剧。它所面临的挑战是：如何在没有任何特定归属标准的情况下，使“归属”变得戏剧化地强大。这种通过故事实现的拥抱，已成为我们戏剧形式的转化任务——并且至今仍如此。

### THE PARADOX OF DISSEMINATION: STABILITY AND EROSION 传播的悖论：稳定与侵蚀

The emphasis in the artistic culture upon the solitary ego of the creator has yet to yield to Playback's plural, feminine vision of creativity. Instead, Playback has found more welcome in the group-oriented community of psychodrama. At the most obvious level, the population of our membership has shaped our identity as a quasi-theatrical form of therapy rather than exclusively a form of theater. Playback has not attracted as many trained performance artists to its ranks as it has an eclectic mix of psychodramatists, therapists, trainers, and others associated with the human potential movement who circulate within the workshop milieu. Playback's 'go anywhere' portability has allowed it to flourish in a variety of therapeutic modalities, from the classic psychodramatist who may utilize Playback in a hospital setting to European existentialist psychotherapists who see in it an excellent example of situational reality. As a community, we cannot help but be influenced by the many therapeutic perspectives of our members and their applications of the form.

艺术文化中对创作者孤独自我的强调，至今尚未让位于一人一故事所倡导的、具有多元性与女性特质的创造力视野。相反，一人一故事在以群体为导向的心理剧（psychodrama）社群中受到了更广泛的欢迎。从最直观的层面来看，我们的成员构成塑造了我们作为一种准戏剧化治疗形式的身份，而非单纯的戏剧形式。一人一故事吸引的受过专业训练的表演艺术家并不多，但汇聚了来自心理剧、治疗师、培训师以及与“人类潜能运动”相关人士等多元背景的群体，他们活跃于工作坊的圈子里。一人一故事这种“无处不在”的灵活性，使其在多种治疗模式中蓬勃发展，从可能在医院环境中运用一人一故事的经典心理剧实践者，到将其视为情境现实绝佳范例的欧洲存在主义心理治疗师。作为一个群体，我们不可避免地会受到成员们多种治疗视角及其对该形式应用方式的影响。

The support of the psychodrama community has helped Playback to succeed at what the other similar theatrical experiments of that time have not: to separate the form and practice from the single mind of its masculine creator. The psychodrama community provided Jonathan with both a container and a strategy for Playback to thrive all over the world independently from him. His method of entrusting his theater to others has been consistent with his philosophy that each and every one of us has creative potential which society has dampened. He has practiced his democratic vision of ordinary creativity. Even more singular to the evolution of Playback as a theater form is that, with the exception of Judy Swallow's group in Poughkeepsie and Jo Salas's Hudson River Playback, companies thrive under leaders who were not necessarily members of the Original Playback Theater Company. This distribution of the form, followed by practicing the form without the presence of the founder, has given Playback a stability and longevity which is remarkable among theatrical languages. Even more astonishing is that this unique theatrical mode is vital after almost twenty years.

心理剧社群的支持帮助一人一故事做到了当时其他类似戏剧实验未能做到的：将这种形式和实践从其男性创作者的个人思维中分离出来。心理剧社群为乔纳森提供了既能让一人一故事在全球范围内蓬勃发展，又能使其独立于他本人之外的“容器”和“策略”。他将自己的戏剧托付给他人的做法，与他“我们每个人都拥有被社会压抑的创造潜能”这一哲学理念一脉相承。他践行了关于“平凡创造力”的民主愿景。一人一故事作为一种戏剧形式在演变过程中更为独特之处在于：除波基普西的朱迪·斯沃洛（Judy Swallow）剧团和乔·萨拉斯（Jo Salas）的哈德逊河一人一故事外，其他剧团都是在非“始创一人一故事剧团”成员的领导者带领下蓬勃发展的。这种形式的传播，以及随后在创始人缺席的情况下持续实践，赋予了一人一故事一种在戏剧语言中极为罕见的稳定性和持久性。更令人惊叹的是，这种独特的戏剧模式在近二十年后依然充满活力。

However, the idea of Playback as exclusively a form of theater has paradoxically been eroded by the psychodramatic community even as it supported Playback. It has been hidden behind the screen of psychodrama – the community and the process. Playback's geographical center near to the Moreno Institute, Jonathan Fox's personal and professional relation with Zerka Moreno, and Playback's honored place in the psychodramatic community, have conflated Playback's theatrical transformational vision with a therapeutic agenda. Consequently, at its best, Playback is described by the Australian psychodramatist Tony Williams as the most significant variation on the psychodrama mode since its inception. At the other end, Playback is sometimes derisively described as psychodrama's "stepchild."

然而，尽管心理剧社群一直支持一人一故事，但将一人一故事视为一种纯粹戏剧形式的观念，却悖论般地被该社群所削弱。它一直被隐藏在心理剧这块“屏幕”之后——无论是心理剧社群本身，还是其运作过程。一人一故事的地理中心毗邻莫雷诺研究所，乔纳森·福克斯与哲卡·莫雷诺的个人及职业关系，以及一人一故事在心理剧社群享有的崇高地位，这些因素将一人一故事的戏剧转化愿景与治疗议程混为一谈。因此，澳大利亚心理剧专家托尼·威廉姆斯将其评价为：即便在最佳状态下，一人一故事也是心理剧模式自诞生以来最具意义的变体。另一方面，一人一故事有时也被讥讽地称为心理剧的“继女”。

At the core of this paradox lies the role that psychodrama plays in training for Playback practitioners. As Jonathan was developing the theatrical language that would become Playback Theater, he became aware that more than theater training was required. Taken by a friend to a performance of psychodrama at the original Moreno Institute in Beacon, New York, Jonathan sensed that Moreno's philosophy of intimate membership resonated with his own ideas about story and theater and community. Moreno, then in the final year of his life, had a similar ambivalent relationship to the art forms of his youth (Dadaism and surrealism) as Jonathan had to Artaud and the forced shamanisms of his theater contemporaries. Moreno, like Jonathan, might have chosen to go exclusively into expressionist theater, but instead took the bolder course of seeking a salutary, transformational form of theater which extended the boundaries of stage art through the inclusion of nonprofessionals. Moreno's commitment to use artistic skills to create a method which could empower the politically and emotionally neglected spoke to Jonathan's own struggle with his larger vision for Playback, and he settled himself geographically near to psychodrama to learn what it had to offer him in his quest.

这一悖论的核心在于心理剧在一人一故事实践者培训中所扮演的角色。当乔纳森正在发展那种最终演变为一人一故事的戏剧语言时，他意识到仅靠戏剧培训是不够的。有位朋友带他去纽约比肯市最初的莫雷诺研究所观看了一场心理剧演出，乔纳森感到莫雷诺关于“亲密成员关系”的哲学理念，与他自己关于故事、戏剧和社区的观点产生了共鸣。当时已进入生命最后一年莫雷诺，对他青年时代所热衷的艺术形式（达达主义和超现实主义）抱有与乔纳森对阿尔托及其同时代戏剧家所推崇的“强制性萨满主义”相似的矛盾态度。莫雷诺和乔纳森一样，本可以选择专攻表现主义戏剧，但他却选择了更大胆的道路——寻求一种有益且具有转化性的戏剧形式，通过吸纳非专业人士来拓展舞台艺术的边界。莫雷诺致力于运用艺术技巧，创造一种能够赋予政治上和情感上被忽视者力量的方法，这与乔纳森在一人一故事这一宏大愿景上的挣扎不谋而合；因此，他特意在地理上靠近心理剧的发源地，以期从心理剧中汲取对自身探索有益的经验。

Jonathan came to realize the importance of psychodrama personally through his work as Conductor, when he would come up against emotional blocks which impeded his ability to conduct the stories. In psychodrama, he found a system in place which spoke to the psychological awareness required for his work. Here was a form of "insight training" which used action; hence, the translation of the psychodrama skills to Playback was a fluid one. The listening skills, the particular kind of, expansive acting skills which require using one's own story and one's own insight, the philosophy and practice of role reversal, the plurality of "healers" all supporting the protagonist's struggle, and the idea of the stage as a space of psychic process evolved, through Jonathan's own personal self scrutiny, into central components of the Playback practitioner's repertoire.

乔纳森通过担任指挥的工作，亲身领悟到了心理剧的重要性——当时他常常遭遇情感障碍，这些障碍阻碍了他讲述故事的能力。在心理剧中，他发现了一套与自己工作所需的心理觉察相契合的体系。这是一种通过行动进行的“洞察力训练”；因此，将心理剧的技巧应用到一人一故事中是一个自然而流畅的过程。倾听技巧、那种需要运用自身故事与洞察力的独特而富有表现力的表演技巧、角色互换的哲学与实践、多位“疗愈者”共同支持主角的挣扎，以及将舞台视为心理过程空间的理念——所有这些，都通过乔纳森对自身的深刻审视，逐渐演变为一人一故事实践者技能库中的核心要素。

Further, Jonathan came to realize that his theater was not only a theater of membership in performance, but a theater of membership for its practitioners. Group process became another aspect of his theater for which additional training was required. Even psychodrama's most scientized area, sociometry, thus found its way into the art form of Playback. Within group life was the understanding that the more members of a Playback group increased their emotional repertoire through "doing one's own work," the more the entire group's performance repertoire was increased.

此外，乔纳森逐渐意识到，他的戏剧不仅是一种以表演为纽带的戏剧，更是一种以实践者为纽带的戏剧。群体过程成为他所倡导的戏剧的另一个方面，这需要额外的培训。因此，即使是心理剧中最科学化的领域——社会计量学，也融入了一人一故事这一艺术形式之中。在群体生活中，人们认识到：一人一故事小组的成员越是通过“处理自身问题”来丰富自己的情感表现力，整个小组的表演表现力就越能得到提升。

In my own experience, the types of stories a group elicits are an accurate reflection of its emotional repertoire. There is an uncanny ability of Tellers to know what kind of story can be done by a group. This "reflection" of the group's level of knowledge in the types of stories the group engenders is one of the mysteries of the Playback process which demands from us respect and humility. We work to expand what we can give to Tellers, so that we can enact whatever story comes our way. In our art which seeks to include the disenfranchised, we recognize the importance of fulfilling Goethe's goal as an artist: "Nothing human should be alien to me." Psychodrama has been the primary (and sanctioned) medium for training Playback practitioners to expand our emotional repertoire to meet Goethe's goal."

根据我个人的经验，一个群体所激发出的故事类型，准确地反映了其情感表达的范围。讲述者有一种超乎寻常的能力，能够洞悉一个群体能够演绎出什么样的故事。这种通过群体所催生的故事类型来“映射”其知识水平的现象，正是一人一故事过程中的奥秘之一，它要求我们怀着尊重与谦逊的态度去面对。我们致力于拓展能给予“讲述者”的资源，以便能够演绎任何呈现在我们面前的故事。在这门致力于接纳被边缘化群体的艺术中，我们深知实现歌德作为艺术家的目标至关重要：“凡属人类之事，皆不可与我无缘。”心理剧一直是培训一人一故事实践者的主要（且被认可的）媒介，它帮助我们拓展情感表达的范围，从而实现歌德的目标。

Psychodramatists have also found a variety of applications and contexts for Playback, both performance and non-performance, which are specially suited to their populations. The key element which appears to make Playback more effective in some situations than classic psychodrama is what is called the "distancing effect" in Playback. For trauma survivors, for instance, being able to have emotional distance from their trauma is more therapeutic than immersion within it; and, at the same time, being actors in stories which resonate with their own appears to have a salutary effect.12 When psychodramatists use Playback as therapy successfully, and have evidence of its power to heal, it can become very difficult to make the translation back into Playback as an art form.

心理剧从业者还为一人一故事找到了多种应用场景和情境——包括表演型和非表演型——这些场景和情境特别适合他们的服务对象。在某些情况下，一人一故事之所以比经典心理剧更有效，关键因素似乎在于一人一故事中所说的“疏离效应”。例如，对于创伤幸存者而言，能够与创伤保持情感距离比沉浸其中更具治疗效果；与此同时，在与自身经历产生共鸣的故事中扮演角色，似乎也具有积极的疗效。12当心理剧治疗师成功地将一人一故事作为治疗手段，并获得了其治愈力量的证据时，要将其重新归类为一种艺术形式就变得非常困难。

The term "distancing" itself is a good example of the "stepchild;' problem. "Distancing" reflects the perspective of the psychodramatist rather than the Playback practitioner. It describes the Teller's experience in contrast to that of a protagonist in a psychodrama, as the Playback Teller is allowed to view his story rather than descend into it. From the Playback perspective, however, there is no increase in "distance" when Playback is used in a therapeutic context. We seek always to overcome distance, no matter the context. The dominant perspective and vocabulary of psychodrama in our community may allow us to speak of what we do in fairly impressive terms, yet without coming from our own experience in the work.

“疏离效应”这一术语本身就是“继女”问题的典型例证。“疏离效应”反映的是心理剧从业者的视角，而非一人一故事实践者的视角。它描述了讲述者的体验，将其与心理剧中主角的体验相对比——因为一人一故事中的讲述者被允许以旁观者的视角审视自己的故事，而非沉浸其中。然而，从一人一故事的角度来看，当一人一故事被用于治疗情境时，“距离”并不会增加。无论处于何种情境，我们始终致力于消除距离。我们这个群体中心理剧占主导地位的视角和术语，或许让我们能够用相当令人印象深刻的措辞来描述我们的工作，但这些表述却并非源于我们自身的工作经验。

We find similar kinds of language problems in finding our terminology when we try to describe how Playback is healing. We tend to say what we do not do rather than what we do do. We do not push the Teller toward insight. We do not diagnose when we listen. We don't quite listen the way that a therapist does, even though therapists and Conductors both use their intuition. We don't really know how to describe the healing effects on actor-patients, though we see evidence of it. We don't heal the same way therapy does. After a while, we sound like the adolescent who has yet to find himself, and can only protest to his parents that he is not like them.

在试图描述一人一故事如何发挥疗愈作用时，我们在确定术语方面也遇到了类似的语言问题。我们往往更倾向于说明“我们不做什么”，而不是“我们做什么”。我们不会强迫讲述者获得顿悟。在倾听时，我们不会做出诊断。我们的倾听方式与治疗师并不完全相同，尽管治疗师和领航员都会运用直觉。虽然我们看到了疗效的证据，却不知该如何描述这种疗法对“演员-患者”产生的疗愈效果。我们的疗愈方式与传统心理治疗不同。久而久之，我们听起来就像那个尚未找到自我的青少年，只能向父母抗议说自己并不像他们那样。

We now begin to come a bit closer to the "heat" of our conflict, the energy of our pair about "art" and "therapy." On the one hand, the support of the psychodrama community, the role psychodrama plays in our training, and the evidence of its powers to heal when used in therapeutic contexts all drive us further and further toward calling Playback therapy. We want to validate the power of our work. Calling Playback "therapy" seems to meet our desire for validation, for in the twenty years since Playback was founded, theater has lost its claim of efficacy as the Age of the Happening has evolved into the Age of Therapy. The transformational power of theater which Playback originally believed it possessed has been subsumed by therapy which has emerged in the past decades as the primary -- if not sole -- transformational vehicle which can enhance individual and group life.

现在，我们开始逐渐接近这场冲突的“焦点”——即我们这对关于“艺术”与“治疗”的组合所蕴含的能量。一方面，心理剧社群的支持、心理剧在我们的培训中所扮演的角色，以及它在治疗情境中展现出的疗愈力量，都不断推动着我们更坚定地将一人一故事称为“治疗”。我们希望证实我们这项工作的力量。将一人一故事称为“治疗”似乎满足了我们对认可的渴望，因为在一人一故事创立以来的二十年间，随着“偶发艺术时代”演变为“治疗时代”，戏剧已失去了其效用的主张。一人一故事最初所坚信的戏剧变革力量，已被治疗所取代——治疗在过去几十年间已成为能够提升个人和群体生活的主要（甚至可能是唯一的）变革载体。

On the other hand, somehow -- it is very "delicate," to coin one of Jonathan's favorite words -- we also know that our power lies in our fidelity to our method. We know that we "work" in certain therapeutic contexts- because we are not therapy. Though psychodrama has contained and supported us, allowing us to grow, we do not want to be seen as one of many (too many) therapeutic modalities. Hence, we engage in this reactive "not" way of defining ourselves. Our desire for validation lures us to call ourselves therapy, yet our soul, as it were, rebels. The adolescent craves recognition for his unique self even though that emerging self does not yet know who it is. Playback craves 11 that recognition, yet we do not yet possess a vocabulary of our own to engender it. And just as we are striving for recognition that our power is achieved through some other means than therapeutic, searching for our own vocabulary to describe what we are, we find ourselves not only living in a culture enamored of therapy, but our own language, the language of story, is now being usurped by therapy.

另一方面，不知为何——用乔纳森最喜欢的一个词来说，这非常“微妙”——我们也知道，我们的力量在于对自身方法的忠诚。我们知道，我们之所以能在某些治疗情境中“工作”，正是因为我们本身并非治疗。尽管心理剧包容并支撑着我们，让我们得以成长，但我们并不希望被视为众多（甚至过多）治疗模式中的一种。因此，我们采取了这种反应性的“否定”式自我定义方式。我们对认可的渴望诱使我们将自己称为治疗，然而我们的灵魂——姑且这么说——却在反抗。那个青少年渴望自己的独特自我得到认可，尽管那个正在形成的自我尚且不知自己是谁。一人一故事同样渴求这种认可，但我们尚未拥有能产生这种认可的专属词汇。就在我们努力争取认可——即我们的力量是通过非治疗手段实现的，并寻找属于自己的词汇来描述我们究竟是什么的时候，我们发现自己不仅生活在一个痴迷于治疗的文化中，而且我们自己的语言——故事的语言——如今正被治疗所篡夺。

Is it any wonder we are beginning to feel hot?

难怪我们开始感到焦灼了。

### THE SEED SALESMAN 种子推销员

In the context of the experimental theater of the 1970s, allowing people to tell their story on stage was a means to align theatrical performance with authentic, uncontaminated "life." The intact narratives of everyday life seemed like fresh, clear water compared to the dense metaphysics, strained shamanisms, brutal martyrdoms, and damaging denuded spectacles of the time. As we turn our attention to the evolution of the philosophy of story since then, we would do well to keep in mind Jonathan's experience as a seed salesman in Nepal. The seed salesman serves us as an emblem for Playback's commitment to the actual in a context of potential wonder. Not all practitioners of "story" working today have the same commitment, and it is important for our future practice to be discriminating in our influences.

在20世纪70年代的实验戏剧语境中，让人们在舞台上讲述自己的故事，是一种将戏剧表演与真实、未经玷污的“生活”相融合的手段。与当时那些晦涩难懂的形而上学、牵强附会的萨满教、残酷的殉道情节以及具有破坏性的赤裸裸的奇观相比，日常生活那些完整无缺的叙事仿佛清澈甘冽的泉水。当我们关注自那时以来故事哲学的演变时，不妨将乔纳森在尼泊尔担任种子推销员的经历铭记于心。这位种子推销员的形象，象征着一人一故事在充满潜在奇迹的语境中对真实的坚守。当今并非所有从事“故事”实践的人都秉持着同样的信念，因此，在选择影响我们的思想时保持明辨力，对于我们未来的实践至关重要。

In the decades since Playback's inception, "story" has developed in three contexts, each with its own ideology. They are: story as knowledge, story as art, and story as medicine/therapy. Each of these is obviously connected to the other; these are loose categories, meant to help us in the discussion.

自一人一故事诞生以来的数十年间，“故事”在三个各具独特意识形态的语境中发展：故事作为知识、故事作为艺术，以及故事作为疗法。这三者显然相互关联；这些只是宽泛的分类，旨在帮助我们展开讨论。

Story as knowledge asks the question, "What place do stories occupy in the development of the human mind?" In this area we find rich and interesting characters, many of them at the extreme edges of society. We find the neurologically impaired somnambulists and poets of Oliver Sacks' world. We find the chronic walkers and talkers of Robert Coles' experience in training as a psychoanalyst. What these diverse populations have in common is that the method of story reaches them. When the neurologist and the psychoanalyst give up the codified language of theory, and try, instead, to approach their "subjects" through story, their isolation is broken. As Coles asked himself, at issue was "what the nature of my attention ought to be. Was I to "treat," or was I to listen carefully, record faithfully, comprehend as fully as possible?"13 From paying attention to the stories of their subjects, these two men, indoctrinated in the language of medicine, have felt keenly the inadequacy of theory to help them fulfill their roles as healers. The act of listening to stories illuminated the human struggle, the drama, which caused the disease or the disorder; story humanized their approach. We might say that story has returned them to the soil, or the humus, from which the pain and disorders have grown. 14

“故事作为知识”这一概念提出了一个问题：“故事在人类思维的发展中占据着怎样的地位？”在这个领域，我们发现了一群丰富而有趣的人物，其中许多人处于社会的边缘地带。我们看到了奥利弗·萨克斯笔下那些患有神经系统疾病的梦游者与诗人。我们还发现了罗伯特·科尔斯在接受精神分析培训过程中所接触的那些喋喋不休、踱步不休的患者。这些不同群体有一个共同点，那就是故事这一方法能够触及他们。当神经学家和精神分析师摒弃理论中那些程式化的语言，转而尝试通过故事来接近他们的“研究对象”时，他们的孤立状态便被打破了。正如科尔斯自问的那样，关键在于“我的关注点应当是什么。我是要‘治疗’，还是要仔细倾听、如实记录、尽可能全面地理解？”13 通过关注受试者的故事，这两位深受医学语言熏陶的人，深刻地体会到理论在帮助他们履行治愈者职责方面的不足。倾听故事这一行为，照亮了人类的挣扎与戏剧性——正是这些导致了疾病或障碍；故事使他们的治疗方式更具人性。我们可以说，故事将他们带回了那片土壤，或者说那层腐殖质，而痛苦与障碍正是从中滋生的。14

Being attentive to story has forced these men to revise theories of how the brain and mind work within their respective disciplines. Sacks's "iconic" mode of processing neurological information joins with the cognitive psychologist Jerome Bruner's idea of a "story paradigm" as descriptions of mental events. In the field of education, Howard Gardner's inclusion of the "Intrapersonal" as a type of intelligence speaks particularly to Jonathan's view that Playback can be described as an art form which structures relationships in the moment.15 These intellectual endeavors have moved from theory to story, back to new theory as these men have recognized the role of story in the structure of the mind.

对故事的关注迫使这两位学者重新审视各自学科中关于大脑与心灵运作机制的理论。萨克斯处理神经学信息的“符号”模式，与认知心理学家杰罗姆·布鲁纳将“故事范式”视为心理事件描述的观点相契合。在教育领域，霍华德·加德纳将“自我认知”纳入智能类型之列，这尤其印证了乔纳森的观点：即一人一故事可以被描述为一种在当下构建人际关系的艺术形式。15随着这些学者认识到故事在心灵结构中的作用，这些学术探索已从理论转向故事，进而又回溯至新的理论。

As these men have been allowing stories from everyday life to inform and revise their theories of the mind, storytellers have revived the ancient art of story telling in performance and educational contexts. Some of these storytellers, most notably Bill Hadley from Massachusetts and the extraordinary autobiographical performance artist Kevin Kling, have expanded the revival of ancient stories to include original stories from contemporary life. As the renaissance in storytelling has blossomed, storytellers have expanded their primary role as performers in the educational field with enormous success, reaching into the management training field, the personal growth workshop merry-go-round, and into therapy as such.

正当这些学者们允许日常生活中的故事来启发和修正他们对心灵的理论时，讲故事的人也在表演和教育场合中复兴了这一古老的讲故事艺术。其中一些讲故事的人——最引人注目的是来自马萨诸塞州的比尔·哈德利（Bill Hadley）和杰出的自传体表演艺术家凯文·克林（Kevin Kling）——将古老故事的复兴拓展到了当代生活的原创故事领域。随着讲故事艺术的复兴日益蓬勃，讲故事的人在教育领域成功地拓展了其作为表演者的主要角色，并取得了巨大成功，其影响力已延伸至管理培训领域、个人成长工作坊的“旋转木马”，乃至心理治疗本身。

Within the storytelling community, there is a debate between the traditional role of storytelling as a way of imparting human wisdom and the uses of story in a human awareness context which borders on therapy. In the former, traditional mode, storytelling enhances wisdom generally. In the latter applied mode, storytelling carries an agenda to achieve a specific goal in personal growth. Some practitioners, have promoted stories as "the latest talking cure for all that ails us ... the key to a kinder and gentler America." A California storyteller describes storytelling as "one of the strongest modalities than one can use to heal, sometimes quite incidentally." She goes on to say that, "In personal stories, when you hear that someone else has a problem, you think, Oh, I'm not alone. I don't feel so bad, or you cry together." (Brecht should be turning over in his grave.) This reduction of Story to "problem" should alarm any storyteller, and it does at least one. "If storytellers get up with the intention of healing people, one, they're assuming they know what's best for other people ... and two, it's just a very refined kind of didacticism." 16 It is this didactic quality of applied prescriptions for the Self, as well as the inflation of all stories into the dimensions of myth, that Jo Salas seeks to separate from Playback when she dissociates our work from the type of storytelling Bly and others are using.17

在讲述故事的社群里，关于讲述故事作为传授人类智慧的一种方式的传统角色，以及故事在接近治疗的人类意识背景下的应用，存在着一场辩论。在前者的传统模式中，讲故事总体上增强了智慧。在后一种应用模式中，讲故事承载着实现个人成长特定目标的议程。一些实践者将讲故事宣传为“治愈我们所有病痛的最新谈话疗法……是让美国变得更善良、更温和的关键。”一位加州讲故事的人将讲故事描述为“一种最强大的治愈方式，有时是偶然的。”她接着说，“在个人故事中，当你听到别人遇到问题时，你会想，哦，我不是一个人。我不觉得那么难受，不然你们就一起哭。”（布莱希特应该在坟墓里翻来复去。）这种将“故事”还原为“问题”的做法，应该会让任何一个讲故事的人感到警惕，而且至少有一个人是这样做的。“如果讲故事的人一开始就带着疗愈别人的意图，第一，他们认为自己知道什么对别人是最好的……第二，这只是一种非常精致的说教。”正是这种为“自我”提供处方的说教性质，以及将所有故事膨胀成神话的维度，乔·萨拉斯试图将我们的作品与布莱和其他人正在使用的讲故事的类型分离开来，从而与一人一故事区分开来.17

Bly is a liminal figure, working at the threshold where the poet-bard meets the therapist. Together with Michael Meade, a superb storyteller, Bly has used poetry and story to alert the men in this country to their lost wildness and the tragedy of their lost ancestors. While I agree with Jo Salas that the method of applying ancient stories to psychological growth is fundamentally opposite to the release of wisdom within the quotidian we strive to achieve in Playback, Bly's recognition that white, middle class Americans (men and women) have lost their stories helps us to understand the attraction of that same population to Playback. There is in the storytelling tradition the notion of the bard 'singing over the bones of the ancestors' to awaken them.18 Bly builds on this exquisite metaphor to lament the literal absence of bones for most Americans whose ancestors are buried in ground we have never seen or touched or tilled. No bones--no song--no story, goes the iconic logic of Bly's view. In a sense, Bly has spoken out loud what Jonathan intuited twenty years ago: the deracinated urban child had lost his ancestral story wisdom -- no bones to sing over. Playback is a way for this population to finds its lost stories without embarking on a spurious journey for the bones.

布莱是一个临界人物，工作于诗人-吟游诗人与治疗师交汇的门槛之上。他与杰出的讲故事者迈克尔·米德（Michael Meade）合作，运用诗歌与故事，唤醒这个国家男性的野性，并让他们意识到失去祖先所带来的悲剧。虽然我同意乔·萨拉斯的观点，即把古代故事应用于心理成长的方法，与我们在一人一故事中力图实现的、从日常生活中释放智慧的做法在根本上是相悖的，但布莱对白人中产阶级美国人（无论男女）已失去自身故事这一现象的洞见，却帮助我们理解了这一群体为何会对一人一故事产生吸引力。在讲故事的传统中，有一种“吟游诗人唱响祖先骸骨”以唤醒他们的观念。18布莱以此精妙的隐喻为基础，哀叹大多数美国人祖先的骸骨在字面意义上已不复存在——他们的祖先被埋葬在我们从未见过、触摸过或耕种过的土地之下。“没有骸骨——就没有歌谣——就没有故事”，这正是布莱观点中标志性的逻辑。从某种意义上说，布莱将乔纳森二十年前的直觉大声说了出来：那些失去根基的城市儿童已经丧失了祖先故事中的智慧——没有骸骨可供吟唱。一人一故事正是这一群体找回失落故事的一种方式，而无需踏上一场虚妄的寻骨之旅。

In his Hermes position at the threshold, Bly has also aligned himself with two very different yet equally remarkable Jungian analysts -- James Hillman and Marion Woodman. One of the most striking examples of his work with Woodman can be found in their workshop on the Jungian concept of the Shadow, in which they combine performance, lecture, and process through the artful alternation of telling an ancient Nordic story with Jungian inspired analysis-19 This movement back and forth between a traditional tale and the Jungian Shadow manifestations in every day contemporary life, all contained by that most modern of rituals, the workshop,20 is at a distance from our story-as-knowledge thinkers. In contrast to Coles and Sacks, who found story to be an antidote to theory, Bly and Woodman use story as theory . That is, Coles and Sacks allow the apparent chaos of life events to help them understand the particular configuration of psyche in their tellers. Bly and Woodman, on the other hand, graft a story upon life events to structure the psyche, assuming the role of teller themselves.

作为伫立在门槛处的“赫尔墨斯”，布莱也与两位风格迥异却同样杰出的荣格派分析师——詹姆斯·希尔曼和玛丽昂·伍德曼——站在了同一阵线。他与伍德曼合作中最引人注目的例子之一，可见于他们关于荣格“阴影”概念的工作坊。在该工作坊中，他们通过巧妙交替讲述古老的北欧故事与受荣格启发的分析，将表演、讲座和心理过程融为一体19。这种在传统故事与当代日常生活中荣格“阴影”表现形式之间的往复穿梭，全都蕴含于那个最现代的仪式—— 即“工作坊”这一最现代的仪式所承载——20这与那些将“故事视为知识”的思想家们拉开了距离。与科尔斯和萨克斯将故事视为理论的解药不同，布莱和伍德曼将故事本身作为理论来运用。也就是说，科尔斯和萨克斯允许生活事件中看似混乱的表象，帮助他们理解讲述者内心世界的特定结构。而布莱和伍德曼则将故事嫁接到生活事件之上以构建心理结构，并亲自扮演讲述者的角色。

This pattern of imposing a traditional story upon life events at the service of an ideology is taken one (dangerous) step further by the Jungian analyst and contadora, Clarissa Pinkola-Estes. In her best-selling book, Women Who Run With the Wolves, she attempts to define a new "wild woman archetype" by uncovering the patterns of the archetype in ancient and familiar narratives (e.g., "The Red Shoes," "The Ugly Duckling"), and then translating them into contemporary lives. She accompanies each tale with a step-by-step correlation to psychological processes. In so doing, the wonder of the tale is reduced to a formula for awareness. The powerful images inside the stories are packaged as issues, with the implication that we should "get over them." In essence, Pinkola-Estes is erasing the icons (as Sacks would say) as she reifies images into therapeutic agendas. Story as an antidote to theory, as we find in Coles and Sacks, has passed through the art of storytelling to become story as a formula for self awareness.21

这种为了服务于某种意识形态而将传统故事强加于生活事件之上的模式，被荣格派分析师兼“故事讲述者”克拉丽莎·平科拉-埃斯特斯推向了一个（危险的）新高度。在她那本畅销书《与狼共舞的女人》中，她试图通过发掘古代及耳熟能详的叙事（例如《红舞鞋》、《丑小鸭》）中该原型的模式，进而将其转化为当代生活，从而定义一种新的“野性女性原型”。她为每个故事都配上了与心理过程逐一对应的关系说明。如此一来，故事的奇妙之处便被简化为一种觉察的公式。故事中那些富有力量的意象被包装成问题，暗示着我们应当“克服它们”。本质上，平科拉-埃斯特斯在将意象实体化为治疗议程的同时，正在抹去这些图腾（正如萨克斯所言）。正如我们在科尔斯和萨克斯的作品中所见，作为理论解药的故事，已通过讲故事的艺术，转变为一种自我觉察的公式。21

Let's now return to the seed salesman in order to understand where Playback fits into these story-based modalities. Metaphorically speaking, Jonathan's choice to honor the seed salesman he actually was rather than try to become a shaman himself implied a rejection of archetype and myth. No singing over the bones; the bones are lost. Like Coles, Jonathan tuned his ear to the actual, the every day, uncontaminated by theory -- his real-life situation as a seed salesman amidst shamans. For Coles, it was the theory of psychoanalysis that stood between him and his ability to attend to the human beings in his charge. In Sacks' experience, it was the theory of Western medicine and its allopathic orientation to symptom and disease which obscured the rich iconic world of his patients. I am arguing that it was the ideology of "shamanism" or, actor as shaman, which so powerfully influenced the theatrical milieu of the time, which Jonathan distrusted in the same way Coles and Sacks distrusted the allopathic ideology of Western medicine. Jonathan's fidelity to the here-and-now reality of being no shaman at all, but a seed salesman among shamans, is analogous to the fidelity of Coles to his patients' own words when he invited them to "tell a story," and Sacks' attention to the images in his patients' stories without pathologizing them as symptoms.

现在让我们回到那位种子推销员身上，以便理解一人一故事在这些基于故事的治疗模式中占据何种位置。从隐喻意义上说，乔纳森选择尊重自己作为种子推销员的真实身份，而非试图成为一名萨满，这暗示了他对原型与神话的拒绝。不再为骸骨吟唱；骸骨已然消逝。与科尔斯一样，乔纳森将耳朵倾听于现实、日常，不受理论污染——即他作为一名身处萨满之中的种子推销员的真实处境。对科尔斯而言，正是精神分析理论横亘在他与关注其照护下的人类之间。在萨克斯的经验中，正是西医理论及其针对症状和疾病的对症疗法取向，遮蔽了患者丰富而象征性的内心世界。我认为，正是“萨满主义”这一意识形态——或者说“演员即萨满”这一观念——对当时的戏剧界产生了如此强大的影响，而乔纳森对此的怀疑，恰如科尔斯和萨克斯对西医对症疗法意识形态的怀疑。乔纳森对“此时此刻”的忠实——即他根本不是萨满，而只是身处萨满群体中的一名种子推销员——这与科尔斯邀请患者“讲个故事”时对其原话的忠实，以及萨克斯关注患者故事中的意象而不将其病理化为症状的做法如出一辙。

I need to reinforce that it was a search for a context for story which could engender wonder and wisdom from everyday life which gave rise to the form we know as Playback. The Playback performance can be defined as more than an "intentional act," as Jonathan sometimes describes it. The Playback performance is also a commitment by performers to create a context -- or container -- which has the potential for wonder and wisdom which characterizes Playback's unique approach to story. The stories need not themselves have any claim to wisdom; they need not be variations on ancient, wise tales, or imitate the timeless architecture of archetype. Rather, the reification of the everyday by the intentional act of Playback has the power to engender connection, awareness, wonder and -- sometimes -- wisdom. We may do this foolishly and reverently, but always keeping our commitment to the actual before us. We never know what shape this actual will take, but we approach each story with humility, that is, grounded in the humus, the soil, hopeful that we can shape the story in a way that will give to those gathered a sense of life being delightful, or miraculous, or full of high significance.2-2 Life -- not "story." In short, Playback does not require that thestories reflect a preexisting codification of the collected wisdom of the culture into myth in order to work its effects. The event of Playback, and the intentional act of the community gathered together in the moment, are the reflectors of meaning.

我需要强调的是，正是为了寻找一种能够从日常生活中孕育出惊奇与智慧的故事语境，才催生了我们所知的一人一故事这一形式。一人一故事的表演可以被定义为不仅仅是一种“有意为之的行为”，正如乔纳森有时所描述的那样。一人一故事表演更是表演者的一种承诺，即创造一个具有孕育惊奇与智慧潜力的语境——或称“容器”——这正是一人一故事独特叙事方式的特征所在。这些故事本身未必宣称蕴含智慧；它们不必是古老智慧寓言的变体，也不必模仿原型那永恒不变的架构。相反，一人一故事这一有意为之的行为将日常具象化，从而具有激发联结、觉知、惊奇，以及——有时——智慧的力量。我们或许会以既天真又虔诚的态度去做这件事，但始终坚守着对眼前现实的承诺。我们永远无法预知这一现实将呈现何种形态，但我们始终以谦卑的态度面对每个故事——即扎根于腐殖质与土壤之中，满怀希望地期待能以某种方式塑造故事，让在场的人们感受到生活是令人愉悦的、充满奇迹的，或是意义非凡的。2-2生活——而非“故事”。简而言之，一人一故事并不要求故事必须反映出文化中已汇集的智慧被编纂成神话的既有模式，才能产生其效果。一人一故事这一事件本身，以及当下聚集在一起的社区所进行的有意为之的行为，才是意义的映射者。

This "seed salesman," or "humble" approach is, therefore, at odds with the Jungian style of Bly, Woodman and Pinkola-Estes. What structures the meaning of stories in Playback is not ideological.. When Playback, which originated as an art form, begins to base itself on ideology, we find ourselves'in the same dilemma as the storytellers; that "refined kind of didacticism" creeps into our work. The worth of stories is judged by their level of archetypal resonance, and "deep" stories are deemed more worthy, theatrically and therapeutically, than stories which are not laden with archetypal significance. Instead of meaning deriving from the story itself, reflected by the Playback performers, the meaning of the story is derived from an ideology. The goal of performance shifts from a commitment to engendering meaning from the soil of the everyday (being a seed salesman) to transforming the everyday into a mythical dimension.

因此，这种“种子推销员”式的，或称“谦逊”的方法，与布莱、伍德曼和平科拉-埃斯特斯所代表的荣格学派风格背道而驰。在一人一故事中，构成故事意义的并非意识形态。当一人一故事——这种源于艺术形式的表演——开始以意识形态为根基时，我们便发现自己陷入了与讲故事者相同的困境：那种“精致的说教主义”正悄然渗入我们的工作。故事的价值取决于其原型共鸣的程度，而那些不那么充满原型意义的故事，在戏剧性和治疗效果上，被认为不如“深刻”的故事有价值。故事的意义不再源自故事本身——由一人一故事表演者加以呈现——而是源自某种意识形态。表演的目标也随之转变：不再致力于从日常生活的土壤中孕育意义（例如“种子推销员”这一角色），而是将日常生活转化为神话般的维度。

What, one might ask, is wrong with asking people to see their everyday lives in the rich, meaningful dimensions of myths? Isn't the discovery of the mythical aspect of the everyday what Jonathan was after when he developed this form of theater which has been compared to the origins of theater itself? Seeking in the performance to direct the mundane toward the universal -- isn't that what Playback is?

有人可能会问：要求人们从神话中丰富而富有深意的维度来审视自己的日常生活，这有什么问题吗？当乔纳森创立这种被比作戏剧起源的戏剧形式时，他所追求的不正是发掘日常生活的神话性吗？在表演中力图将平凡引向普世——这不正是一人一故事的精髓所在吗？

Let me answer by way of a story I recently told at a workshop about going for a swim in a green lake. A friend had told me of this beautiful lake, and we went in search of it one hot day. It took us a long time to find it, with many wrong turns and winding roads. Once we finally found it, my friend talked and talked, and the wonder of this green lake was contaminated by her chatter. In the end, I did not really succeed at getting the wonder that I had hoped to find in the lake. In the workshop, the actors reflected my story as I told it without embellishment. My sense of frustration with my inability to access that wonder of green water was accurately reflected in the Playback performance (by novices). What this "meant" to me, personally, was never revealed or analyzed, and I was able to hold within myself the private meaning of the story for me, which was rich. 23

让我通过一个故事来回答这个问题——这是我最近在一次研讨会上讲过的，讲的是去一个绿色的湖里游泳的事。一位朋友曾向我提起过这个美丽的湖，于是我们在一个炎热的日子里动身去寻找它。我们花了很长时间才找到它，途中多次走错路，蜿蜒曲折。终于找到后，我的朋友喋喋不休地讲个不停，这片绿湖的奇妙之感被她的唠叨所冲淡。最终，我并没有真正从湖中获得我原本期待的那份惊奇。在工作坊上，演员们如实重现了我讲述的这个故事，没有添油加醋。我因无法触及那片碧水之美而感到的挫败感，在一人一故事（由新手演员表演）中得到了精准的呈现。这个故事对我个人而言“意味着什么”，从未被揭示或分析，而我得以将这个故事对我而言的私密意义——那份丰富的内涵——深藏于心。23

The workshop participants were then invited to explore the two predominant images of my story, along with other images from other stories, to see what meaning these images had for them. The imaginations and the personal experience of the participants were given free and full license, and the meaning of these images was presented to the group. These presentations were rich with personal significance and the innate poetry of private revelation. Throughout, the wonder of the green water grew. This icon of my story was amplified and augmented by the imaginations of the participants as they allowed the green water to reflect their own lives.

随后，工作坊参与者被邀请探索我故事中的两个主要意象，以及其他故事中的意象，以了解这些意象对他们而言意味着什么。参与者的想象力和个人经历得到了充分且完全的释放，他们将这些意象的含义呈现给整个小组。这些分享充满了个人意义，也蕴含着私人领悟所特有的内在诗意。整个过程中，“绿水”的奇妙感不断增强。随着参与者们让“绿水”映照出他们自己的生活，这个我故事中的象征意象被他们的想象力进一步放大并丰富了。

The meaning of the green water was never presumed, yet it very easily could have been. Without much intellectual strain, my simple story could be viewed as an example of the Narcissus myth. The winding roads are. symbols of Narcissus' futile search for love, and the dark green water of the lake is an ample and accurate image for the deep secluded waters of Narcissus' pool. The chatter of my friend is Echo's meaningless repetition of the Self, and the reverent silence I desired is what Thomas Moore eloquently describes as the deep reflection of SoUl.24 I can even make the intellectual leap and see how all of the explorations of the green water presented by the participants spoke to some desire to contact their souls, to reflect themselves in the depths, removed from the 'superficial' chatter of the Self. This analysis has its own richness and truth, and opens up for me certain ideas about the Self and the soul which tantalize my mind.

“绿水”的含义从未被预设，尽管这本是轻而易举之事。无需费力推敲，我的这个简单故事本可被视为纳西索斯神话的一个例证：蜿蜒的小路象征着纳西索斯对爱情徒劳的追寻，而湖中深绿色的水，正是纳西索斯池塘那深邃隐秘之水的恰当写照。我朋友的喋喋不休，正是回声（Echo）对“自我”的无意义重复；而我所渴望的虔诚静默，正是托马斯·摩尔（Thomas Moore）所精辟描述的“灵魂”的深刻反思。24我甚至可以进一步推演，看到参与者们对绿水的种种探索，其实都折射出一种渴望——渴望与自己的灵魂建立联系，在深处映照出真实的自我，从而摆脱“自我”那种“肤浅”的喋喋不休。这种分析自有其丰富内涵与真理，并为我开启了关于“自我”与“灵魂”的一些见解，这些见解令我心驰神往。

But in so doing, not only I have lost my sense of wonder in the lake; and what felt rich and spontaneous in the participants' presentations has lost its vitality, the unalloyed connection to life. But the poetic has been usurped by the didactic, and, instead of feeling like a person with a story rich in significance, I feel like an example of someone else's idea of 'how I should mean.' And, the green lake itself is gone, reflecting nothing. I am left with nothing my body can contain, adrift in theory. The unique capacity for story to carry symbols through imagery, the iconic strategy of the tale, has been sacrificed. Because my interpretation is inspired by Moore, my story about being "narcissistic" does not feel pathological. But an air of diagnosis hangs over it, squeezing the mystery out of the story, the poetry from its telling, and the wonder from my life.

但这样做不仅让我失去了对这片湖泊的惊奇感；而且参与者们在展示中原本那种丰富而自然的感觉，也失去了它的活力，失去了与生活的纯粹联结。诗意已被说教所取代，我不再觉得自己是一个拥有意义深远故事的人，而是感觉自己成了他人心中“我应该代表什么”这一观念的例证。而且，那片绿色的湖泊本身也不复存在，再也无法映照出任何东西。我身上已无任何能被身体容纳之物，在理论中漂泊。故事通过意象承载象征的独特能力——即叙事的象征策略——已被牺牲。因为我的解读受到摩尔的启发，我关于“自恋”的故事并不显得病态。但一种诊断的氛围笼罩其上，挤走了故事中的神秘感，挤走了叙述中的诗意，也挤走了我生命中的惊奇。

Let's go one step further, and let's take ourselves out of the Teller's experience and put ourselves into the mind of the Conductor of this same story. Having been on both sides myself, I know that when, as Conductor, I listen with an ideological frame the Teller feels I am not listening to him. The close, intimate attention to the images in the story not only have the wonder squeezed out of them, but morality enters in wonder's stead. In this case, "narcissism" could belong to Moore, a classic Jungian, or a Freudian, each with a different result. Yet this air of diagnosis, this strange, covert moralism as pathology is hinted at with the transposition of a myth onto my story, would be common to all.

让我们更进一步，暂时跳出讲述者的体验，试着站在同一个故事中领航员的立场上思考。我本人曾身处双方角色，深知当我作为领航员带着某种意识形态框架去倾听时，讲述者会觉得我并没有真正听他在讲什么。对故事中意象那种密切而亲密的关注，不仅会榨干其中的奇妙感，还会让道德观念取而代之。在这种情况下，“自恋”可能归因于摩尔、一位典型的荣格学派学者，或一位弗洛伊德学派学者，各自得出的结论各不相同。然而，这种诊断的意味——这种将神话套用到我的故事中时所暗示的、作为病理表现的奇怪而隐晦的道德主义——对所有人来说都是共通的。

Why? In part, because in our culture, when people think of Narcissus, or the ubiquitous "Oedipus," they think of a complex, not a story. When we recast everyday stories into the mythical dimension, we are beset by our own cultural conserve, to borrow Moreno's term. "Myth" in our culture has been conserved as "pathology." No matter how salutary the intentions of a Conductor who finds inspiration in archetype, in the context of the Playback enactment, the cultural conserve works against wonder and wisdom, toward didacticism and pathologizing. (As we shall see later, the restoration of story to the realm of wonder is the challenge before Playback as we reclaim Soul from its incarceration by Western culture in pathology.)

为什么？部分原因在于，在我们的文化中，当人们想到纳西索斯，或是无处不在的“俄狄浦斯”时，他们想到的是一种心理复合体，而非一个故事。当我们把日常故事重新塑造成神话维度时，我们会受到自身文化保守性的困扰——借用莫雷诺的术语来说。“神话”在我们的文化中已被固化为“病理”。无论从原型中汲取灵感的领航员初衷多么良善，在一人一故事演绎的语境中，这种文化“凝固”都会阻碍惊奇与智慧的涌现，反而助长说教主义和病理化倾向。（正如我们稍后将看到的，将故事还原到惊奇的领域，正是一人一故事面临的挑战——我们正试图将“灵魂”从西方文化将其囚禁于病理化的牢笼中解救出来。）

Playback is not and never has been a theater of the mind. It has always been a theater of the earth, the soil. Holding the image of the foreign seed salesman before us, we see Playback as occupying two positions in the tradition of storytelling: "the resident tiller of the soil" and the "trading seaman." This tradition recognizes that a story is not only the property of specially endowed shamans; but is often the offering a traveler from some far away place gives in exchange for a meal and a bed. Jonathan's role as the Peace Corps agricultural specialist combined both these types -- he was the foreigner selling seeds. The trading seaman knows that his chances of a bed and a meal for another night are dependent on the value of his story, whether he has something of use to the community. The importance of having something useful to offer is most keenly felt in the "resident" teller, who cannot afford to waste the community's time with a story, and, I suspect, cannot himself conceive of a story that is not integral to the craft of living. The usefulness may be a moral, or practical advice, or a proverb which reaffirms the values of communal living. This usefulness lends to the storyteller the power of being a man "of counsel." Counsel not as an answer or an ideology; rather, as "woven into the fabric of real life [which] is wisdom."25 Playback offers this kind of real life wisdom in direct contrast to the esoteric shamanistic wisdom of the seer. We offer seeds-that is, stories which the community may use to increase its yield (of joy, wisdom, life).


一人一故事从来都不是，也从未是心灵的剧场。它一直都是“大地的剧场”，是“土壤的剧场”。将那位外国种子推销员的形象浮现在眼前，我们看到一人一故事在讲故事的传统中占据着两个位置：“常驻的耕作者”和“经商的航海者”。这一传统认识到，故事不仅是天赋异禀的萨满的专属财产；它往往也是来自遥远之地的旅人，为换取一顿饭和一张床铺而献上的礼物。乔纳森作为和平队农业专家的角色融合了这两种身份——他就是那位兜售种子的外国人。那名“经商的航海者”深知，自己能否再获得一晚的食宿，取决于故事的价值——即他能否提供对社区有用的东西。而“本地”讲述者对提供有价值内容的重要性感受最为深刻，因为他无法浪费社区的时间去讲无用之谈，而且我猜想，他自己也无法构思出一个与生活技艺无关的故事。这种实用性可能体现在道德启示、实用建议，或是重申共同生活价值观的谚语上。这种实用性赋予讲故事者作为“智者”的力量。这里的“谋士”并非指提供答案或宣扬某种意识形态，而是指“编织进现实生活织锦中的智慧”。25一人一故事所呈现的正是这种现实生活中的智慧，这与先知所代表的深奥萨满式智慧形成了鲜明对比。我们播撒的种子——即那些故事——能被社区用来增加其收获（喜悦、智慧、生命）。

Playback is not a theater of idea or of ideology. We are a 'humble' theater of seeds, which are very small and require the dark in order to grow. What any ideological approach to story destroys is that darkness in which the seed of meaning grows. We must not presume to know or to judge the meaning a story has for those who tell, witness, and enact the story. We must not declare that only certain kinds of plants are beautiful. Rather, simply the growing of the seed into a plant connects us to the wonder of life. Each and every and any story can connect us to the wonder of life if we bring our humble attention, our soil intelligence, to bear upon it.

一人一故事不是理念或意识形态的剧场。我们是一个“谦卑”的种子剧场，种子虽小，却需要黑暗才能生长。任何以意识形态为导向的故事解读方式，都会摧毁那片让意义之种生长的黑暗。我们绝不能妄自揣测或评判故事对讲述者、见证者和表演者而言所具有的意义。我们绝不能宣称只有某些植物才是美丽的。相反，仅仅是种子生长为植物这一过程，就让我们与生命的奇迹相连。只要我们将谦卑的关注和土壤智慧倾注于其中，每一个、任何一个故事都能让我们与生命的奇迹相连。

### THE HUMANISTIC PROGRAM AND THE PROBLEM OF “SHADOWLESSNESS” 人文主义项目与“无阴影”问题

With its original goal to enhance human creative potential, the psychological ideology Playback is philosophically most susceptible to is a humanistic one. Humanistic psychology encompasses an array of methods which have in common setting a premium on consciousness and the harmony implied with the attainment of wholeness. But the term "wholeness" is deceptive, for it does not mean inclusive of everything within the psyche. Rather, "wholeness" is descriptive of a desired state of harmonious integration. It is Pacific in geographical origin and pacific in emotional tone. "Wholeness" is a surrogate for a new kind of "holiness" which posits that full consciousness of one's issues, and the act of it getting over them" and "working through them," leads one to a kind of psychological sainthood. Everything negative, which is everything that one denies, and all that lives in the darkness of the unconscious, is seen as blocking the progress of one's Self toward becoming "whole." It pushes these energies downward and under as the Self transcends upward into the light. In its valuation of enlightenment, humanistic psychology tends to be "shadowless."26

一人一故事这一心理理念最初旨在提升人类的创造潜能，从哲学角度看，它最易与人本主义产生共鸣。人本主义心理学涵盖了一系列方法，这些方法的共同点在于高度重视意识，以及实现“整体性”所蕴含的和谐。但“整体性”这一术语具有误导性，因为它并不意味着包含心理中的所有内容。相反，“完整性”描述的是一种理想的和谐整合状态。它地理上源自太平洋，情感基调也如太平洋般平和。“完整性”是某种新型“神圣性”的代名词，这种观念认为，对自身问题的充分觉察，以及“克服”和“化解”这些问题的过程，将使人达到一种心理上的圣洁境界。一切消极的事物——即人所否认的一切，以及潜藏在无意识黑暗中的所有内容——都被视为阻碍“自我”迈向“完整”的进程。当“自我”向上超越、迈向光明时，这些能量便被向下、向深处推挤。在对开悟的评价上，人本主义心理学往往呈现出“无阴影”的倾向。26

Shadowlessness is not dramatic, but the opposite: an end to drama. Not only does the seed require the dark to grow, but the evisceration of the unconscious with full light kills off the secret, dark, mysterious part of ourselves wherein our creative energy lies.27 The "negative" is critical to the creative. Rather than convert everything to the angelic in a quest for transcendence, we should keep in mind Keats' definition of genius: "I mean Negative Capability, that is, when man is capable of being in uncertainties, Mysteries, doubts, without any irritable reaching out after fact & reason.,,28 Keats' own genius intuited the importance of creating evil as well as good if one were to grasp the essence of life with art. The capacity to render evil beautiful puts one in profound moral uncertainty. Keats understood that this moral uncertainty was necessary for truth and beauty to flourish. But many humanistically inspired psychological programs tend to erase ncertainty from our lives. The humanistic program would not only take this uncertainty and replace it with certainty, it would also take our darkness where our moral uncertainties and creative sympathies lie and urge us to erase it entirely.

“无阴影”并非戏剧性的，恰恰相反：它是戏剧的终结。种子不仅需要黑暗才能生长，而且用完全的光明将无意识彻底掏空，会扼杀我们自身中那个蕴藏着创造能量的隐秘、黑暗而神秘的部分。27 “消极”对创造力至关重要。与其在追求超越的过程中将一切转化为天使般的存在，我们更应铭记济慈对天才的定义：“我指的是‘消极能力’，即当人能够置身于不确定性、神秘与怀疑之中，而不急于去追寻事实与理性……”。28济慈的天才直觉告诉他，若要通过艺术把握生命的本质，创造邪恶与创造善同样重要。将邪恶描绘得美丽动人的能力，会使人陷入深刻的道德不确定性之中。济慈深知，这种道德不确定性是真理与美得以蓬勃发展的必要条件。但许多受人本主义启发的心理项目往往倾向于从我们的生活中抹去这种不确定性。人本主义项目不仅会将这种不确定性替换为确定性，还会将我们内心深处——那里蕴藏着道德不确定性和创造性共情——的阴暗面彻底抹去。

Light kills stories. Literally. When electricity was introduced to an African village by well meaning liberals who sought to better the lives of the villagers, the villagers were thrilled. A light bulb was installed in each house. At night, the villagers would gather around the light bulb as they had once gathered around the fire. But no one spoke. None of the stories were told.29 The same loss of story in the name of progress occurred in Europe at the turn of the century, as Padraic Column, the folklorist, beautifully describes:

光会杀死故事。字面意义上的杀死。当一群怀着善意的自由派人士为了改善村民的生活，将电力引入一个非洲村庄时，村民们欣喜若狂。每户人家都安装了一盏电灯泡。到了晚上，村民们会围着电灯泡聚在一起，就像他们曾经围着篝火一样。但没有人说话。没有一个故事被讲述出来。29世纪之交，欧洲也以“进步”之名发生了同样的故事流失，民俗学家帕德里克·科伦对此有精妙的描述：

> The storyteller seated on a roughly made chair on a clay floor did not look unusually intelligent or sensitive. He certainly did not look histrionic. What was in his face showed that he was ready to respond to and make articulate the rhythm of the night. He was a storyteller because he was attuned to this rhythm and had in his memory the often repeated incidents that would fit it. ...
> 
> 那个坐在泥地上、靠着一把粗糙木椅的讲故事的人，看起来既不特别聪明，也不特别敏感。他显然不像是那种夸张做作的人。从他的神情中可以看出，他已准备好回应并清晰地表达出夜的韵律。他之所以能成为讲故事的人，正是因为他与这种韵律相契合，并且记忆中储存着许多与之相契合、被反复讲述过的故事。……
> 
>  A rhythm that was compulsive, fitted to daily tasks, waned, and a rhythm that was acquiescent, fitted to wishes, took its place. But when the distinction between day and night could be passed over as it could be in towns and in modem houses, the change of rhythm that came with the passing of day into night ceased to be marked. This happened when light was prolonged until it was time to turn to sleep. The prolongation of light meant the cessation of traditional stories in European cottages . 30
> 
>  一种强迫性的、与日常事务相适应的节奏逐渐消退，取而代之的是一种顺从的、与愿望相适应的节奏。但当昼夜之分可以被忽略——正如在城镇和现代住宅中那样——随着白天转入夜晚而带来的节奏变化便不再明显。这种情况发生在光线被延长至该入睡之时。光线的延长意味着欧洲农舍里传统故事的终结。30

The Greeks found a way to incorporate and to honor the darkness and its purpose in creating harmony in the universe through the metaphor of the vessel. Before every performance of a Greek drama, on the tiers of the amphitheater, at carefully determined intervals, were placed seven urns. The actors were supposed to use their voices during the performance to set each of these urns vibrating. When this was achieved, it meant that the universe was in harmony. 31 The beauty of the idea was that the actors had to enact a full panorama of emotions in order to attain the harmony. Without the dark, deep voices of melancholy and evil, and the high-pitched tones of terror and hysteria and the pitches of reason and wisdom in-between, without the full range of human emotion, the harmony could not be generated.

古希腊人通过“器皿”这一隐喻，找到了一种将黑暗及其在创造宇宙和谐中的作用融入并予以尊崇的方式。在每场希腊戏剧开演前，圆形剧场的看台层上，都会按精心测定的间隔摆放七只陶罐。演员们在演出中需通过声音使每只陶罐产生共振。当这一效果实现时，便意味着宇宙已达和谐。31这一理念的精妙之处在于，演员必须演绎出完整的情感全景，才能达成这种和谐。若没有忧郁与邪恶所代表的低沉深邃之声，没有恐惧与歇斯底里所代表的高亢音调，以及介于两者之间的理性与智慧之声——若缺少人类情感的完整谱系，和谐便无从产生。

This image of the hollow container, its surface vibrating when the actors emote," move outward, one after another until a cosmic song is generated, serves as a powerful metaphor for the stance of the actors toward the universe. We are the containers ourselves, empty of judgment, who will use all the tones of the human voice to fill the empty theater with the human story", calling forth the cosmic song with our voices.

这个空心容器的意象——当演员们抒发情感时，其表面便会“向外振动，一波接一波，直至奏响一曲宇宙之歌”——有力地隐喻了演员们面对宇宙的姿态。“我们自身就是容器，不带任何评判，将运用人类声音的所有音调，用人类的故事填满空荡的剧场”，用我们的声音唤起那首宇宙之歌。

The Greek urns, and other metaphors we may generate within our community, allow us to retain Goethe's and Keats' ideals of creative genius as we pursue our healing vision of membership without killing ourselves with enlightenment 11." Remember the court jester: Wisdom can come through the fool, not just the sage.32

希腊陶罐，以及我们社群内可能产生的其他隐喻，使我们得以在追求“成员归属”这一疗愈愿景的同时，既不被“启蒙”所扼杀，又能延续歌德和济慈关于创造性天才的理想11。请记住宫廷弄臣：智慧不仅来自智者，也能通过傻瓜显现。32

### THE HEAT SHIELD 隔热罩

Jo Salas has written honestly and passionately of the healing power of Playback not as being derived from art; but, rather, that the healing power is the art itself, the "ontological" gift of art as the means by which chaos acquires form and meaning.33 It is this ontological healing value that could be appropriated by therapeutic agendas seeking to control, for one reason or another, this unique art form. But, despite Playback's long evolution toward therapy, Playback has one marvelous protection against such a theft: the heat, the heat of our feelings when a performance is bad. Into this heat we may now finally go. Inside it we shall paradoxically discover the waters which can heal the divisions in our community.

乔·萨拉斯曾坦率而充满激情地写道，一人一故事的疗愈力量并非源于艺术；相反，这种疗愈力量本身就是艺术，是艺术作为“本体论”天赋的体现——正是通过艺术，混沌才获得形式与意义。33正是这种本体论层面的疗愈价值，可能会被那些出于各种原因试图控制这一独特艺术形式的治疗议程所挪用。然而，尽管一人一故事长期以来一直朝着治疗方向演变，它却拥有一道抵御此类挪用的奇妙屏障：那股热度——当演出不理想时我们情感的热度。此刻，我们终于可以走进这股热度之中。在其中，我们将出人意料地发现那能疗愈我们社区内裂痕的治愈之水。

Whether we like it or not, or understand it or not, this "heat" is evidence that the healing power of Playback is increased the better Playback is performed. There is some part of us which rebels against using Story as a prescription for our behavior, craving the art to nurture and heal us without moralizing. For, when we are moralized we are pathologized. We feel that there is something "wrong" with our story as we lived it or experienced it. When Playback is not performed well or performed with covert therapeutic or moral agendas, this ontological healing power of art is not realized, and we feel violated or cheated. Even those within the community who recognize and honor the psychodramatic support and training for our art form find ourselves feeling this "heat" when the art fails. Personally, whether the failure is at the level of performance skills or, in a more subtle way, the authenticity of the enactment has been contaminated by the hidden didacticism of a therapeutic agenda, this failure to meet me at the level of my own imagination and experience evokes feelings of sadness, despair, and rage within me.

无论我们是否喜欢，是否理解，这种“热度”都证明：一人一故事表演得越好，其疗愈力量就越强。我们内心深处有部分会抵制将“故事”作为行为准则的做法，渴望艺术能在不进行道德说教的情况下滋养和疗愈我们。因为，一旦被道德说教，我们就被病理化了。我们感到，自己亲身经历或体验过的故事中似乎存在某种“问题”。当一人一故事表演得不够好，或者带着隐蔽的治疗或道德目的进行时，艺术这种本体论层面的疗愈力量就无法得以实现，我们会感到被侵犯或受骗。即使是社区中那些认可并尊重我们这一艺术形式所依托的心理剧支持与培训的人，在艺术表现失败时，也会感受到这种“热度”。就我个人而言，无论这种失败体现在表演技巧层面，还是以更微妙的方式——即表演的真实性被治疗议程中隐含的说教性所污染——这种未能在我的想象与体验层面与我产生共鸣的失败，都会在我内心激起悲伤、绝望和愤怒的情绪。

I have thought for a long time that my rage and sadness at times in the Teller's chair were the result of my own personal story. Whenever I feel therapy contaminate art, I feel the hidden pathologizing of the imagination my mother's Freudian world view which was visited upon me when I was a teenager. Sometimes, my unconscious tricks me, and I perpetrate on others the same pathologizing inflicted on me. It is a lonely place, and devoid of human beauty. All is ugliness there.

我长期以来一直认为，坐在讲述者的椅子上时，我时而感到的愤怒和悲伤，源于我个人的经历。每当我感到治疗侵蚀了艺术，我便察觉到想象力中潜藏的病理化倾向——那是我母亲的弗洛伊德式世界观，在我十几岁时便强加给了我。有时，我的潜意识会欺骗我，让我将曾经加诸于我的那种病理化倾向，如法炮制地施加给他人。那是一个孤独的地方，毫无人性之美可言。那里尽是丑陋。

But I am not alone in feeling the pain and anger when my imagination is denied full humanity through membership. I have watched my imaginative son try to make his own way through the world, looking for kindred spirits. Many of his friends are "Trojans" rather than "Greeks," and he is keenly aware that he has something which they do not. His favorite cousin fights against a presumably enlightened school system as he tries to make sense out of his riveting gift for mime, for which he has no name. My son and my nephew are hardly the first children to wonder why their friends don't know how to 11play," or who struggle against adults unable to recognize or nurture powers and energies which seem greater and more marvelous than things with names. My identity as an artist, consciously valuing my imagination, only heightens the drama and the pain for me; but the denial of the salutary role of the imagination in human life effects us all, for we all possess imaginations.

但当我的想象力因身份归属而无法获得完全的人性时，感受到这种痛苦和愤怒的并非只有我一人。我目睹了那个富有想象力的儿子试图在世间开辟自己的道路，寻找志同道合者。他的许多朋友都是“特洛伊人”而非“希腊人”，而他深知自己拥有他们所没有的东西。他最喜欢的表弟正与一个自诩开明的教育体系抗争，试图理解自己那令人着迷的默剧天赋——尽管他无法为这种天赋命名。我的儿子和侄子绝不是第一批疑惑“为什么朋友们不懂得如何‘玩’”，或是与那些无法识别、无法培育那些看似比有名之物更伟大、更奇妙的能力与能量的成年人抗争的孩子。作为一名艺术家，我自觉珍视自己的想象力，这只会加剧我内心的戏剧性与痛苦；但否认想象力在人类生活中所发挥的积极作用，却影响着我们所有人，因为我们每个人都拥有想象力。

This rejection of creative vitality can happen in the most subtle of ways even in Playback- as, for instance, when an image given in a story is "dissected," to uncover the "truer" "hidden value" behind it. 34 1 also feel violated when my own story is reduced to a narrative of "issues" in Playback group process. I feel a terrible, damp echo of my own loneliness as a dreaming child in a school with uniforms when actors fail to give me back the story at the level of image and feeling at which I experienced it. When it is truly bad, I feel that my experience of being in the world has been trashed. When it is mediocre, then all the uniqueness of my experience seems reduced to a dull, gray average. All these examples of the failure of the art of Playback as art reconfirm for me how challenged we are by the original transformational vision of Playback. It is not easy to heal the imagination from the insults and denials and alienations of modern, therapeutic life.

这种对创造性活力的排斥甚至可能以最微妙的方式出现在一人一故事中——例如，当故事中呈现的某个意象被“解构”，以揭示其背后“更真实”的“隐含价值”时。34当我的故事在一人一故事小组过程中被简化为“问题”的叙述时，我也感到自己的权益受到了侵犯。当演员们无法以我当初体验到的意象和情感层次将故事还给我时，我便感受到一种可怕而沉闷的回响——那是我在穿校服的学校里，作为那个做着梦的孩子所经历的孤独。当表现极其糟糕时，我感觉自己身处世间的体验被彻底践踏了；当表现平庸时，我体验中的所有独特性似乎都被降格为一种沉闷、灰暗的平庸。所有这些一人一故事艺术作为艺术形式的失败案例，都再次印证了一人一故事最初的转化愿景对我们构成了何等巨大的挑战。要疗愈想象力所遭受的、来自现代治疗式生活的侮辱、否定与异化，绝非易事。

But it is not only my imagination which has been violated. Something that contains my imagination, something that animates it, is feeling the violation keenly when I am cheated out of full aesthetic expression of my experience. I have heard others who do not value their imaginations as highly as I do complaining of feeling violated,, disappointed, frustrated, hurt when the performances don't awaken fully the sleeping desire within the imagination. People who have no identity as artists feel something in them, for which they have no name, damaged, hurt, denied. What is this thing without a name that feels hurt? What is this energy that fires up when the performances are bad? What has been cheated? And what has "it" been cheated of? Whence the heat?

但受到侵犯的不仅仅是我的想象力。当我的体验无法通过美学表达得到充分展现时，那承载着我的想象力、赋予它生命力的某种东西，也在深刻地感受到这种侵犯。我曾听过一些不像我这样重视想象力的人抱怨说，当演出未能完全唤醒想象力中沉睡的渴望时，他们会感到受到侵犯、失望、沮丧和受伤。那些没有艺术家身份的人，也会感到自己内心某种无法言名之物受到了损害、伤害或否定。这种感到受伤的、无名之物究竟是什么？当演出糟糕时，这种被点燃的能量又是什么？究竟是什么被欺骗了？而“它”又被剥夺了什么？这股热量从何而来？

It is the Soul trying to be heard over the din of therapy, Psyche herself protesting against being pathologized, crying out for us to speak to her in her own language: beauty.

那是灵魂正试图在治疗的喧嚣中被听见，是普西刻（Psyche）本人在抗议自己被病理化，在呼喊着让我们与她对话，用她自己的语言：美。

### AN ENCOUNTER 一次相遇

The same question of how to be effective which inspired the 70S theatrical experiments, including Playback, has inspired James Hillman, the iconoclastic Jungian, to spin his decades-long critique of ego psychology. In the twenty years that Playback's quest for effectiveness has been influenced by therapeutic ideas, Hillman has turned to aesthetics as the answer to how to make therapy effective again, appropriating the imagination and beauty to psychology. I believe it is no accident that, in virtually the same historical time span, a psychologist and a story-oriented theater have evolved to the point where they each require something from the other to believe themselves effective. Now, as Playback and Hillman meet face to face in this essay, we would be advised to follow the course of the classic Moreno encounter, and try to see ourselves both with and in each other's eyes.

那个曾激发70年代戏剧实验（包括一人一故事）的“如何产生效用”之问，同样也激发了这位特立独行的荣格学派学者詹姆斯·希尔曼，使他展开了长达数十年的对“自我心理学”的批判。在一人一故事对效用的探索受到治疗思想影响的这二十年间，希尔曼转而将美学视为重振治疗效用的答案，将想象力与美引入心理学领域。我认为，几乎在同一历史时期，一位心理学家与一种以故事为导向的戏剧形式，都发展到了彼此都需要从对方那里汲取某些要素才能确信自身有效性的地步，这绝非偶然。如今，当一人一故事与希尔曼在这篇论文中面对面相遇时，我们不妨效仿莫雷诺经典相遇的模式，试着通过彼此的眼光来审视自己，并在彼此的视角中重新认识自己。

In linguistically dense, self-consciously ardent, and maddeningly esoteric rhetoric Hillman "revisions" psychology as "a discipline of the imagination. "35 He aspires to "free psychology from personalistic confines and to revert its vision to poetic principles."36 The first step is to abandon the medical model altogether. Psychology's consummate error, Hillman argues, is in the medical model's pathologizing of Soul. If psychology is to reclaim its eponymous roots as "soul knowledge," then it must cease to pathologize Soul:

希尔曼以语言凝练、自觉热忱且令人抓狂的玄奥修辞，将心理学“重新定义”为“一门想象力的学科”。35他渴望“将心理学从个人主义的局限中解放出来，使其视野回归诗意的原则”。36第一步是彻底摒弃医学模式。希尔曼认为，心理学最根本的错误在于医学模式将“灵魂”病理化。如果心理学要重拾其作为“灵魂知识”的本源，就必须停止将“灵魂”病理化：

> We cannot recover soul from its alienation in professional therapy until we have a vision of pathologizing which does not require professional treatment in the first place.37
> 
> 除非我们对“病理化”形成一种全新的认知——即这种病理化从一开始就不需要专业治疗——否则我们就无法将“灵魂”从专业治疗中的异化状态中解救出来。37

Instead of pathologizing Soul, psychology should be about the business of "soulmaking." "Soulmaking" is not "aimed at the betterment of persons"; it has no covert moral agenda.38 Nor is it another method among too many. "Soulmaking" is no program for self improvement, for the proper activity of psyche-therapy is neither to improve the personality, nor even tend to the personality. Rather, the activity of psyche-therapy should be consistent with its etymology: "to care for and serve for the soul."39

心理学不应将灵魂病理化，而应致力于“塑造灵魂”之事。“塑造灵魂”并非“旨在改善个人”；它没有隐含的道德议程。38它也不是众多方法中的一种。“塑造灵魂”并非自我提升的计划，因为心理治疗的恰当活动既不是为了改善人格，甚至不是为了关注人格。相反，心理治疗的活动应当与其词源相一致：“关怀并服务于灵魂”。39

The way to rescue the modern alienated soul is for psychology to return to its roots in the Italian Renaissance, where Hillman claims psychology was born. As psychology reclaims its Renaissance roots, it will inevitably turn to the imagination as its arena of both inquiry and practice; for, it is in Renaissance writings that the imagination is recognized as the agent of the making of Soul. As Hillman develops his concept of Soul via the Renaissance, Soul sounds less like a religious concept than an idea about the nature of the imagination: "To be in soul is to experience the fantasy in all realities and the basic reality of fantasy," Hillman asserts.40

拯救现代异化之魂的途径，在于心理学回归其发源于意大利文艺复兴时期的根源——希尔曼认为心理学正诞生于此。随着心理学重拾其文艺复兴的根源，它必然会将想象力作为其探究与实践的领域；因为，正是文艺复兴时期的著作将想象力视为“塑造灵魂”的推动者。随着希尔曼通过文艺复兴时期的视角来阐发其“灵魂”概念，“灵魂”听起来与其说是一个宗教概念，不如说是一种关于想象力本质的理念：“处于灵魂之中，就是体验所有现实中的幻想，以及幻想的基本现实，”希尔曼断言。40

This imaginal Renaissance psyche, Soul, is not to be confused with the later Christianized spirit. Unlike spirit, which lives in the air and avoids the vicissitudes of the body, "Soul sticks to the realm of experience and to reflections within experience."41 Soul is "a self sustaining and imagining substrate .... [It is] that unknown component that makes meaning possible, turns events into experiences”

这种源自文艺复兴时期的想象性心理——“灵魂”——不应与后来被基督教化的“精神”相混淆。与栖居于空气之中、避开肉体沧桑的“精神”不同，“灵魂扎根于经验领域，并扎根于经验中的反思。”41灵魂是“一种自我维持且富有想象力的基质……[它是]那个使意义成为可能、将事件转化为经验的未知成分”

> Soul: refers to the deepening of events into experiences ... the imaginative possibilities in our natures, the experiencing through reflective speculation, dream, image, and fantasy -that mode which recognizes all realities as primarily symbolic or metaphorical. 42
>
> 灵魂：指事件向体验的深化……我们本性中的想象可能性，以及通过反思性思辨、梦境、意象和幻想所进行的体验——这种模式将所有现实主要视为象征或隐喻。42

How the Soul is "made," how we "deepen" the events of our life into experiences endowed with meaning, is through The Word. "Only continued attempts at accurate soul speech can cure our speech of its chatter and restore it to its first function, the communication of SOUI."43 Not only is the Soul's language oral narrative; but, according to Hillman, "depth psychology [itself] is the great carrier of the oral tradition, the telling of tall tales." 44

灵魂是如何“被造就”的，我们又是如何将生命中的事件“深化”为富有意义的体验的，这一切都是通过“话语”实现的。“唯有持续尝试精准的灵魂言语，才能治愈我们言语中的喋喋不休，使其回归其最初的功能——即传达‘SOUI’。”43灵魂的语言不仅是口述故事；而且，据希尔曼所言，“深度心理学[本身]正是口述传统的伟大载体，是讲述传奇故事的载体。”44

Having introduced this much of Hillman's deliberately dense rhetoric, let's see how these initial ideas apply to Playback. To begin with, we have in common the inadequacy of the therapeutic or the aesthetic alone to contain our vision. Hillman reaches for the aesthetic to grab hold of the imagination as the path of soul knowledge; we reach for the therapeutic to help us articulate the salutary and celebrational quality of our art. For Hillman, the enemy of the imagination has become psychotherapy itself, with its pathologizing of the Soul and its covert moralistic agenda of personal betterment. He would rather awaken individuals to seeing "life less as the resultant of pressures and forces than the enactment of mythical scenarios."45 His vision of life as nonpathological, 'imaginal' enactment is consistent with Playback's original vision of each person as capable of creative human action. Playback, too, shied away from 'pathologizing' in its refusal to engage in abusive spectacle making. It refused to pathologize the creative process, seeking instead a non-egocentric means to enhance creativity. Further, Jonathan made no claims that his method of theater would improve one's life. He only sought "a context for communication" in which theater and life would further the participants in their respective creative capacities. So we see both Hillman and Playback concerned with the salutary creative potential of human imagination. '

在介绍了希尔曼这些刻意晦涩的论述之后，让我们来看看这些初步观点如何应用于一人一故事艺术。首先，我们有一个共同点，那就是仅靠治疗或美学都不足以容纳我们的视野。希尔曼借助美学来把握想象力，将其视为通往灵魂认知的途径；而我们则借助治疗，以帮助我们阐明艺术所具有的益处和庆祝性特质。对希尔曼而言，想象力的敌人已然变成了心理治疗本身——它将灵魂病理化，并暗含着个人自我完善的道德主义议程。他更希望唤醒个体，使他们将“生活视为神话场景的演绎，而非压力与力量作用的结果”。45他将生命视为非病理性的“意象化”演绎这一观点，与一人一故事最初的愿景——即每个人都具备创造性的人类行动能力——是一致的。一人一故事同样通过拒绝参与制造具有伤害性的表演，从而避开了“病理化”的倾向。它拒绝将创造过程病理化，而是寻求一种非以自我为中心的途径来提升创造力。此外，乔纳森从未声称他的戏剧方法能改善人们的生活。他仅寻求“一种沟通的语境”，在此语境中，戏剧与生活能共同促进参与者发挥各自的创造力。由此可见，希尔曼与一人一故事都关注人类想象力所蕴含的益于身心健康的创造潜能。

Secondly, both Playback and Hillman are in retreat from ideology. Playback, in its break from the ideology of a "theater of cruelty," sought a different route to effective acting, a route which recognized the threat to authenticity of the ideological ego. Playback did not want to inculcate new scripts in people, but the opposite. Some of us are now wary -- and I believe rightly so -- of therapeutic scripting, whether in the Story-centered approach of the jungians, or in the transcendent and shadowless goal of the humanists. Hillman condemns all existing forms of psychological practice for producing scripted behavior, "programs" for the Self which, he believes, insult the soul. "A discipline of the imagination does not have to become a program of the imagination.,,46

其次，一人一故事和希尔曼都在远离意识形态。一人一故事在与“残酷剧场”意识形态决裂的过程中，寻求了一条通向有效表演的另一条道路——这条道路认识到意识形态自我对真实性的威胁。一人一故事并不想向人们灌输新的剧本，而是恰恰相反。我们中的一些人现在对治疗性剧本化持警惕态度——我认为这是有道理的——无论是在荣格学派的故事中心疗法中，还是在人本主义者所追求的超然且无阴影的目标中。希尔曼谴责所有现有的心理实践形式，认为它们制造了剧本化的行为，即针对“自我”的“程序”，而他认为这些程序侮辱了灵魂。“想象力的修习不必沦为想象力的程序。”46

Thirdly, this discipline of imagination which addresses the soul is oral narrative. Rather than a script, Playback uses story as a means to awaken story in others. Hillman implies the same requirement that both the concern and the method of psychology be story, the "oral tradition." While we might be well advised to question Hillman's rhetorically drenched claim that depth psychology is the carrier of the oral tradition, we can agree that the language of the narrative imagination is the language of soul. (See Epilogue below.) We should have little difficulty in agreeing with him when he says, "We are different at the end of the story because the soul has gone through a process during the telling." 47

第三，这种面向灵魂的想象力修习就是口述故事。一人一故事并非使用剧本，而是将故事作为唤醒他人内心故事的手段。希尔曼也暗示了同样的要求：心理学的关注点和方法都应是故事，即“口述传统”。尽管我们或许有理由质疑希尔曼那句充满修辞色彩的论断——即深度心理学是口述传统的载体——但我们可以认同，叙事想象的语言就是灵魂的语言。（参见下文《后记》。）当他说“故事结束时，我们已然不同，因为在讲述的过程中，灵魂经历了一个过程”时，我们应该不难认同他的观点。47

Finally, as Hillman defines Soul, we cannot help but find articulation for something we sense keenly in our process. Hillman's conception of Soul as the vehicle whereby 'events are *deepened*' into experiences speaks powerfully to the Playback experience. In fact, I might go so far as to say that Hillman has given us a superb definition of what happens during the Playback process -that the participants are collectively engaged in an imaginal process of *deepening* events into experiences, of using the imagination to deepen facts into meaning as we participate in metaphorical enactments, in "fantasy work." We make no claims for what the meaning of the events should be; only provide a means for that imaginative, metaphorical deepening to take place.

最后，根据希尔曼对“灵魂”的定义，我们不禁发现，这恰恰道出了我们在过程中敏锐感受到的某种东西。希尔曼将“灵魂”视为一种载体，通过它，“事件被*深化*”为体验，这一观念有力地呼应了一人一故事的体验。事实上，我甚至可以说，希尔曼为一人一故事过程中发生的事情给出了绝佳的定义——即参与者集体投身于一种想象性过程，将事件*深化*为体验，在我们参与隐喻性演绎、进行“幻想工作”时，运用想象力将事实深化为意义。我们并不对事件的意义该是什么样做出任何断言；只是提供一种途径，让这种富有想象力的、隐喻性的深化得以发生。

### DRAMA AND SOULMAKING 戏剧与灵魂塑造

Like the 70s theater artists, Hillman turns to the rituals "of religion, theater, loving, and play" in his search for a methodology of soulmaking. Ritual itself is a source for "deliteralizing events," that is, for deepening events into experiences endowed with meaning.

与70年代的戏剧艺术家们一样，希尔曼在寻求塑造灵魂的方法论时，将目光投向了“宗教、戏剧、爱与游戏”中的仪式。仪式本身就是“使事件脱离字面意义”的源泉，即把事件深化为富有意义的体验。

> As we go into ritual, the soul of our actions "comes out"; or to ritualize a literal action, we "put soul into it." Here not only can the priest and the alchemist point the way; so too can the actor, the entertainer, the ballplayer. They are able to divest the concrete of its literalism by the psychological style they bring to an action. 48
> 
> 当我们进入仪式时，我们行为的灵魂便“显现出来”；或者说，为了将一个字面意义上的行动仪式化，我们会“赋予其灵魂”。在此，不仅神职人员和炼金术士能指明方向，演员、杂耍艺人和运动员同样可以。他们能够通过赋予行动以独特的心理风格，使具体事物摆脱其字面意义的束缚。48

This psychological style is a special combination of play and love, a delighting in and wondrous erotic engagement with the world. It is more than speech, because the soul needs a language that speaks to itself in its own language -- "emotionally, dramatically, sensuously, and fantastically." The 'method' is "to build new imaginal arenas for the bull, new imaginal circuses for the crowd of persons and theaters for the images, new imaginal processions Hillman writes, leaving it to us to imagine what these new arenas, circuses, theaters and processions might be.49

这种心理风格是游戏与爱的特殊结合，是对世界的欣喜与充满奇妙情欲的投入。它超越了言语，因为灵魂需要一种能用其自身语言与之对话的语言——“情感的、戏剧的、感官的、奇幻的”。这种“方法”是“为公牛构建新的想象竞技场，为人群构建新的想象马戏团，为意象构建新的想象剧场，以及新的想象游行队伍”，希尔曼写道，并留给我们去想象这些新的竞技场、马戏团、剧场和游行队伍究竟会是什么样子。49

He also turns to a Renaissance idea of theater as a source for this language which would inform a putative method. It is a theater which was no theater at all, but simply an idea of one. It was called "the memory theater," and was the creation of Giulio Camillo, a man of singular brilliance in rhetorical performance. No one ever actually saw his memory theater, and it is unclear not only where it was, but what it was. Evidence suggests that it was conceived as a fairly large box. This box was to be opened by an individual. Therein he would find images placed in highly symbolic locations which were designed to activate the viewer's entire memory of everything he had experienced or learned.50 This "box" was never built. Yet, despite its lack of physical existence, the power of the idea of this theater was enormous in Renaissance thinking about the human mind; so enormous that, in references to it it is not a box at all. It becomes a huge -- but hidden -- open air amphitheater, built in a secret location known only to certain elite.

他还援引文艺复兴时期关于剧场的理念作为这种语言的源泉，这种语言将构成本质上的一种方法。那是一种根本算不上剧场的剧场，而仅仅是一种关于剧场的构想。它被称为“记忆剧场”，是修辞表演领域才华卓绝的朱利奥·卡米洛的创作。实际上，没有人真正见过他的“记忆剧场”，不仅其位置不明，其本质也扑朔迷离。有证据表明，它被构想为一个相当大的盒子。这个盒子将由一个人打开。在其中，他会发现置于高度象征性位置的图像，这些图像旨在唤醒观者对其所经历或学到的所有事物的完整记忆。50这个“盒子”最终从未被建造出来。然而，尽管它并不存在于现实世界中，但这一剧场理念在文艺复兴时期关于人类心灵的思考中却具有巨大的影响力；其影响力之大，以至于在提及它时，它根本不再是一个盒子。它变成了一座巨大的——但隐蔽的——露天圆形剧场，建在只有某些精英才知道的秘密地点。

This theater has little to do with our idea of the history of theater art, or even of action. Rather, Camillo's memory theater provides a metaphor of the psyche as a theater of images. It is this metaphor of the psyche that Hillman wants to replace the 19th century hydraulics metaphor which Freud used. Any psychological method that would match this metaphor must be, in Hillman's eyes, as densely iconic and imagistic as Camillo's lost box. He is anxious not to confuse this idea with literal theater, i.e., with Moreno. He despises anything literal and believes that literalization kills the soul. So we must not, according to Hillman, make a simplistic correlation between the inner theater of the soul and an outer stage. Literal action is not the language of soul. Mythical action is.

这种剧场与我们对戏剧艺术史乃至行动的理解几乎毫无关联。相反，卡米洛的记忆剧场提供了一个将心灵比作意象剧场的隐喻。希尔曼希望用这种关于心灵的隐喻来取代弗洛伊德所使用的19世纪“水力学”隐喻。在希尔曼看来，任何与这一隐喻相契合的心理方法，都必须像卡米洛那只失落的盒子一样，充满象征意义和意象。他极力避免将这一理念与字面意义上的戏剧——即莫雷诺的理论——混为一谈。他鄙视一切字面化的东西，并认为字面化会扼杀灵魂。因此，根据希尔曼的观点，我们绝不能将灵魂的内在剧场与外在舞台简单地画上等号。字面意义上的行动并非灵魂的语言，而神话般的行动才是。

However, Hillman virtually drops his typical Jungian mythical bias when he gingerly approaches what a method to access this "theater" should look like. Gone is the Jungian trip fantastique into myth and archetype. Instead, "psychologizing" or soulmaking proceeds with simple, here-and-now questions which echo our own when we approach a Teller. "Our questions will be addressed towhat things are, and where, and who and in which precise way they are,"51 Hillman declares, abandoning the "why" questions of ego psychology. As he elaborates on how soulmaking can be engendered in someone, he sounds uncannily like he is describing the art of Playback conducting:

然而，当希尔曼谨慎探讨通往这一“剧场”的方法应呈现何种形态时，他几乎摒弃了其典型的荣格式神话偏好。那种荣格式的、通向神话与原型的奇幻之旅已不复存在。取而代之的是，“心理化”或“灵魂塑造”通过简单而切合当下情境的问题展开，这些问题恰如我们面对讲述者时内心所思。“我们的问题将指向事物是什么、在哪里、是谁，以及它们以何种确切的方式存在，”51希尔曼宣称，摒弃了自我心理学中的“为什么”式提问。当他详细阐述如何在某人身上引发“灵魂塑造”时，他的表述竟与描述一人一故事领航的艺术惊人地相似：

> Psychologizing [i.e., soulmaking] asks "What?" What happened? What do you feel? What do you want? This search for "whatness" or quiddity, the interior identity of an event, its essence, takes one into depth. It is a question from the soul of the questioner that quests for the soul of the happening. 52
> 
> 心理化[即“灵魂塑造”]问的是“什么”：发生了什么？你有什么感受？你想要什么？这种对“何物性”或“本质性”——即事件的内在身份、其本质——的追寻，将人带入深度。这是提问者灵魂中发出的问题，旨在探寻事件的灵魂。52

### THE AESTHETIC HEART 审美之心

Yet, for all of the sheer mightiness of his intellectual effort to recover the Soul from its alienation through some putative dramatic, story-centered method, the question of psychology's effectiveness was still not answered for Hillman. The problem lay in Hillman's continued focus on the interior and the subjective, the "depth" direction. "Whatness" finally emerges as more important than "deepening" as Hillman shifts his own focus away from the human being to the actual world in which we live, the "whatness" that surrounds us. Though we have been pursuing "soul sophistication" for a century, now, Hillman says our actual physical environment has become hideous during those same one hundred years. As we advanced in (presumed) inner awareness, the world disintegrated into ruin. He wants out of the interior focus of even his own "revisioned" psychology's microscope onto the Soul and to move the Soul outward, into the world. This shift toward the world is the critical step toward psychology "imagining itself more like an aesthetic activity" than a science. 53 He wants, finally, to get out of the "ology" altogether, and enter, at last and fully, into aesthetics. The agent for this shift is the heart.

然而，尽管希尔曼竭尽全力，试图通过某种假定的、以戏剧和故事为中心的方法，将灵魂从异化中拯救出来，但心理学是否有效这一问题对他而言仍未得到解答。问题在于希尔曼始终将焦点放在内在与主观层面，即“深度”方向上。随着希尔曼将关注点从人类自身转向我们所生活的现实世界——即环绕我们的“何物性”——“何物性”最终被证明比“深化”更为重要。尽管我们已追求“灵魂的洗练”一个世纪之久，但希尔曼指出，在这同一百年间，我们实际的物理环境却变得丑陋不堪。当我们（自以为）在内在觉知上不断进步时，世界却已分崩离析，沦为废墟。他希望摆脱甚至自己那套“重新构想”的心理学中对灵魂的内省式聚焦，将灵魂向外延伸，融入世界。这种向世界的转向，是心理学迈向“将自身更多地视为一种审美活动”而非科学的关键一步。53最终，他希望彻底摆脱“学”的范畴，最终且完全地进入审美领域。促成这一转变的媒介是心灵。

Now, as we turn to the heart, my discussion above about the shadowlessness of humanistic psychology is critical to keep in mind. By "heart," Hillman does not mean the sentimental or interior locus of emotion. He means nothing so abstract or gooey. He certainly does not mean a moralizing, transcendent vehicle for universal love, as might be construed by humanistically inspired Playback practitioners. "I am not talking of body feelings in simplistic psychology--whatever I feel is good; deep down inside my heart, I'm okay; what comes from the heart is good per se," Hillman cautions. He is after a specific heart, located in a specific historical, intellectual tradition (the Italian Renaissance), to help him with his quest for psychology being effective in the world. 54

现在，当我们转向“心”这一概念时，必须牢记我上文关于人本主义心理学“无阴影性”的论述。希尔曼所说的“心”，并非指感伤的情感或内心的情感中心。他所指的绝非如此抽象或黏腻的东西。他当然也不是指某种道德说教式的、超越性的普世之爱载体——尽管受人本主义启发的一人一故事实践者可能会如此解读。“我所谈论的并非简单心理学中的身体感受——无论我感到什么都是好的；在我内心深处，我很好；发自内心的东西本身就是好的，”希尔曼提醒道。他所追求的是一种特定的“心”，它植根于特定的历史与思想传统（意大利文艺复兴），以助他实现心理学在现实世界中发挥效力的追求。54

Rather than drown in Hillman's dense and fascinating scholarship, let us accept that we are speaking of an *agent for soulmaking*, which *thinks*, *imagines*, and *desires*. This new heart will apprehend the *world* , not just the Self, in a new way. It will be a heart which possesses a type of "intelligence" which allows the heart to apprehend the world, the "whatness," aesthetically. With this new, aesthetic heart attuned to beauty itself, soulmaking is now reframed by Hillman as "a self-steering process through aesthetic reflexes."55 That is, this aesthetic heart is our guide through soulmaking.

与其沉溺于希尔曼深奥而迷人的学术研究中，不如我们接受这样一个观点：我们所谈论的是一个*塑造灵魂的媒介*，它能够*思考*、*想象*和*渴望*。这颗崭新的“心”将以一种崭新的方式去领悟*世界*，而不仅仅是“自我”。这将是一颗拥有某种“智慧”的心，这种智慧使它能够以审美的方式领悟世界，即“何物性”。凭借这颗与美本身相契合的新审美之心，希尔曼将“灵魂塑造”重新定义为“通过审美反射进行的自我引导过程”。55也就是说，这颗审美之心是我们进行灵魂塑造的向导。

Hillman here presents a profound challenge to the Playback practitioner. For he is saying that it is not enough to heal through art. It is not enough to say that we all need beauty to feel healed. This type of aestheticization of healing does not help us to heal the ugliness of the world, but only keeps us inwardly directed into the Self's propagation of "good feelings." We have fed the soul with beauty, and in our soul we feel this nurturance, we recognize this ontological gift as our lives are rendered formally coherent and beautiful. This could be where Playback stops: nurturing the alienated soul with beauty through imaginative acts. But Hillman challenges us to go one step further. We must not put beauty inside us and hoard it within while the world uglifies and expires around us. We must not settle for taking the story and making it beautiful so long as the story of the earth is one of unspeakable horror. Our lives may be felt as more and more significant and beautiful while the world continues to grow uglier and uglier. If we are to heal ourselves, then we must cease to use confession, the confessions of the interior, feeling heart as our method of healing. We must turn outward, recognizing that emotions are not *within* us, but are all *around* us:

希尔曼在此向一人一故事实践者提出了深刻的挑战。因为他指出，仅通过艺术来疗愈是不够的；仅说我们都需要美来获得疗愈感也是不够的。这种将疗愈“审美化”的做法无助于我们疗愈世界的丑陋，而只会让我们内心持续沉溺于“自我”所传播的“美好感受”之中。我们用美滋养了灵魂，在灵魂深处感受到这份滋养；当我们的生命被赋予形式上的连贯与美好时，我们便认识到这份本体论的馈赠。这或许就是一人一故事的终点：通过想象性的行为，用美来滋养那被异化的灵魂。但希尔曼挑战我们更进一步。我们绝不能将美置于内心并将其囤积于内，而任由周围的世界日益丑陋、走向消亡。只要地球的故事仍是难以言喻的恐怖，我们就绝不能满足于仅仅将故事做得很好看。当世界变得越来越丑陋时，我们的生活却可能被感知为越来越有意义、越来越美丽。如果我们要疗愈自己，就必须停止将坦白——内心的坦白、内心的感受——作为疗愈的方法。我们必须转向外部，认识到情绪并非*在我们内部*，而是*环绕*在我们周围：

> Like afflictions, emotions put me in the center of things, giving importance and existential assurance to human beings. They seem so centrally mine. Yet they are external to the individual person. We share in emotions and hold them in common; they transcend history and locality; we read them in another's face beyond language and culture, feel them in the gestalt of landscapes and natural things, receive them from images buried thousands of years ago and from the sounds and shapes and words of inorganic art objects. Grief, jealousy, comedy have their images that require no interpretive apparatus; they bear archetypal significance beyond your or my personal experiences of them. 56
> 
> 就像苦难一样，情绪将我置于万物中心，赋予人类以重要性和存在上的确信。它们似乎如此核心地属于我。然而，它们其实是独立于个体之外的。我们共享情绪并将其视为共同财富；它们超越了历史与地域； 我们在语言和文化之外从他人的面容中读出它们，在风景和自然事物的整体意象中感受到它们，从埋藏了数千年的图像中，以及无机艺术品的声响、形态和文字中接收它们。悲伤、嫉妒、喜剧都有其无需解释装置的意象；它们承载着超越你或我个人体验的原型意义。56

Hillman's hint of the world as a theater of feeling, his evocation of the iconic power of emotions, directly challenges Playback's vision of enhancing creativity in individual life. He exhorts us to reframe our embrace of membership as a "re-membering" of the fragmented world alienated from beauty. This "re-membering" is another gloss on the injunction to the bard to sing over the bones to revitalize the dead body, rejoining its members. If we are to fulfill our original mandate both to enhance and to empower the imaginatively and socially disenfranchised, then Playback must reify membership as re-membering not just of individuals, but of the body of the world itself. We must also re-examine what "healing" can be in our sick and diseased world. We must listen to Hillman's call to the soul of the world:

希尔曼将世界比作情感剧场的暗示，以及他对情感图腾力量的唤起，直接挑战了一人一故事关于提升个体生活创造力的愿景。他敦促我们将对归属感的拥抱重新定义为对那个与美相疏离、支离破碎的世界的一种“重聚”（re-membering）。这种“重聚”是对吟游诗人那句箴言的另一种诠释——即在骸骨旁吟唱以重振亡者之躯，使其肢体重新结合。如果我们要履行最初的使命——既要提升那些在想象力和社会层面被剥夺权利者的能力，又要赋予他们力量——那么一人一故事就必须将归属感具体化为一种“重聚”，不仅是对个体的重聚，更是对世界这个整体的重聚。我们还必须重新审视，在这个病入膏肓的世界里，“疗愈”究竟意味着什么。我们必须倾听希尔曼对世界之魂的呼唤：

> Therapy must shift its focus from saving the soul in the personal patient to saving the soul of the world, the resurrection of the world rather than man, the celebration of creation before the redemption of creativity in the individual. 57
> 
> 治疗的焦点必须从拯救个体患者的灵魂，转向拯救世界的灵魂；从人的复活转向世界的复活；从个体创造力的救赎，转向对造物的颂扬。57

What does Playback begin to look like if we meet Hillman's call to awaken the aesthetic heart, and turn it toward the soul of the world? It begins to look less and less like a "stepchild," and more and more like itself.

如果我们响应希尔曼唤醒审美之心并将其转向世界之魂的呼吁，一人一故事将呈现怎样的面貌？它将越来越不像一个“继女”，而越来越像它本来的样子。

### PLAYBACK AS ENSOULMENT 作为赋予灵魂的一人一故事

We in the Playback community have comfortably, assumed that a significant part of our healing power depends upon the liberating effects of confession. We have framed "telling" as a type of confession, analogous with the "telling" that goes on in traditional therapy, including psychodrama. This assumption has blocked from our perception the more radical and startling process we are engaged in. For Playback does not work upon the confessional heart; it invites the aesthetic heart to come into the world. It has done so long before Hillmanever gave us a name for the heart we call forth in others and in ourselves. Always we have been a method of "re-membering" via the aesthetic heart.

我们一人一故事社群中的人们一直理所当然地认为，我们疗愈力量的很大一部分依赖于坦白的解放效应。我们将“讲述”界定为一种坦白，类似于传统治疗（包括心理剧）中进行的“讲述”。这种假设阻碍了我们对自身所参与的、更为激进且令人震惊的进程的认知。因为一人一故事并非作用于坦白之心；它邀请的是审美之心步入世间。早在希尔曼为我们所唤醒的——无论是他人还是自身——那颗心命名之前，它便已如此运作。我们始终是一种通过审美之心实现“重聚”的方法。

We invite a Teller to "tell a story," thus beckoning her (or him) into an aesthetic mode. We welcome what they will tell us with our own desiring aesthetic hearts, eager for the story (not the hidden truth). The Teller begins to tell a story. Sometimes, the Teller begins to drop downward into confession -but the moment we say, "Choose someone to play..." We not only lift the Teller's heart out of the confessional mode, we literally lift her eyes out of her downward, inward gaze and turn her attention to the world.58 As we do so, more than the creative within the individual is redeemed. The Playback process resurrects the world when the actors rise, beckoned by the Teller's aesthetic heart. "Watch!" For the actors are about to *ensoul* us all.

我们邀请讲述者“讲一个故事”，从而将她（或他）引入一种审美模式。我们怀着自己渴望的审美之心，热切期待着故事（而非隐藏的真相），欢迎他们将要讲述的内容。讲述者开始讲述一个故事。有时，讲述者会逐渐滑入坦白的状态——但就在我们说出“选一个人来扮演……”的那一刻 我们不仅将讲述者的心从坦白模式中解救出来，更将她的目光从向下、向内的凝视中真正地提升起来，使她的注意力转向世界。58当我们这样做时，被拯救的不仅仅是个人内心的创造力。当演员们在讲述者审美之心的召唤下起身时，一人一故事过程便让世界重获新生。“看！”因为演员们即将为我们所有人*赋予灵魂*。

We "ensoul" the "whatness" of the story, its quiddity, its essence. Playback enactments animate the surface, the *anima mundi*, or the soul of the world of the story. We are *animators*. We set the story into movement with enlivened images, and when we do so, we "move" the Teller and the witnesses out of confession into *reflection*., "the soul's habitual activity."59 Ensoulment animates the world of her story so that we, the audience and all those present, can participate in the animated world of story through the reflected animation -- the 'playback.' All those who witness the Playback event contact this ensouled world as the actors respond in action to the aesthetic heart of our Teller calling us forth.

我们为故事的“何物性”、其本性及其核心“赋予灵魂”。一人一故事使表层、即故事世界的“世界之魂”（*anima mundi*）焕发生机。我们是“赋予者”。我们通过充满生机的意象让故事动起来，而当我们这样做时，便将讲述者和见证者从“坦白”中“引向”*反思*， “灵魂的惯常活动”。59 赋予灵魂使她的故事世界焕发生机，从而让我们——观众及所有在场者——能够通过这种经反思而活化的呈现——一人一故事，参与到这个充满生机的故事世界中。所有见证一人一故事活动的人，都会接触到这个被赋予灵魂的世界，因为演员们正通过行动回应我们讲述者那呼唤我们的审美核心。

There has been a transfer from the inner world of the Teller to actors and audience through the "vessel" of the Conductor. But the type of transference which has occurred is not the projection of inward contents of the psyche outward onto things or people, as in psychodrama and other forms of therapy. Rather, as we animate the world of the story, we "move the heart from its confessional mode of experiencing to a prayerful response to its images -witnessing."60 To witness is to be present as a reflector of meaning, to be present with the attention of soul. Playback is a theater of reflection not of the Self, but of the soul as we collectively engage our aesthetic hearts to steer the self through and into beauty. Within this mystery of reflection lies perhaps the greatest challenge to the Playback practitioner. When the actors stand, empty of judgment, ready to reflect, their own souls are made available to the soul of the Teller. We are chosen for our roles in the story by our own "quiddity," the "whatness" of each of us. If we stand back and reflect upon the roles we are chosen to play, and the stories we engender in performance, we will see the *anima mundi* of our particular Playback group.61

通过领航员这一“媒介”，讲述者的内心世界已向演员和观众进行了转移。但这种转移并非像心理剧及其他治疗形式那样，将心理的内在内容投射到事物或他人身上。相反，当我们赋予故事世界以生命时，我们“将心灵从坦白式的体验模式，转向对故事意象的祈祷式回应——即见证”。60见证意味着作为意义的反思者而临在，以灵魂的关注而临在。一人一故事是一种反思的剧场，它反思的不是“自我”，而是灵魂——当我们集体调动审美之心，引导自我穿越并融入美之中时。在这反思之谜中，或许蕴藏着对一人一故事实践者最大的挑战。当演员们站立时，不带任何评判，准备好进行反思，他们自己的灵魂便向讲述者的灵魂敞开了大门。我们之所以被选中在故事中扮演特定角色，正是源于我们自身的“本质”，即每个人独特的“本然性”。如果我们退后一步，反思自己被选中扮演的角色，以及我们在表演中催生的故事，我们将看到我们这个特定一人一故事小组的“世界之魂”*anima mundi*。61

We do not interpret; as Hillman says, we are all far too good at making meanings when what we need is to generate responses. We respond to the call of the aesthetic heart through a special kind of attention, or attending, to the thinking, imagining, speaking heart of the Teller. It is an intimate act, the kind of attention we would give to the beloved. This is less spiritual than it is animal knowing and loving. We seek to "know" this story in an animal sense, for the aesthetic heart is filled with desire. It seeks to know intimately, and "intimacy releases [us] from confession into immediacy, the courage of immediate intimacy" with the Teller. 62 This type of attention Hillman calls *noticia*. *Noticia*, the act of intimate attention to the small, surface cues coaxes the essence out of the surface so that we can apprehend its soul. 63 We go deep not beyond the surface, but deep "on" the surface. We hold ourselves close to the face of the story.

我们并不进行诠释；正如希尔曼所言，我们都太擅长赋予意义，而我们真正需要的是激发回应。我们通过一种特殊的关注——即全神贯注于讲述者那思考、想象、言说的内心——来回应审美之心的召唤。这是一种亲密的举动，如同我们给予挚爱之人那样的关注。这与其说是精神层面的，不如说是动物性的认知与爱。我们寻求以一种动物性的方式“认识”这个故事，因为审美之心充满了渴望。它寻求亲密的认知，而“亲密感将我们从坦白中解放出来，进入与讲述者‘即刻的亲密’——即即刻亲密所需的勇气”。62希尔曼将这种关注称为*noticia*。*Noticia*，即对细微表层线索给予亲密关注的举动，能从表层中引出本质，使我们得以领悟其灵魂。63我们深入的并非超越表层，而是深入“表层之上”。我们将自己紧贴在故事的表面。

By allowing this intimate knowing of the face of the story to fill us and lead us into action, the gift the Teller has given us is mysteriously transferred to the group. We are entrusted to carry the face of the story with our own faces. Prayer and witnessing behold the face of the story as we ensoul it with our own aesthetic hearts guiding our soulmaking. Then, the enactment completed, the face of the story beheld, the actors face the Teller. The face of the story meets the face of the Teller through our faces. And, the audience faces us, and they, too, reflect the story.

当这种对故事面貌的亲密体悟充盈我们内心并引领我们付诸行动时，讲述者赐予我们的礼物便以神秘的方式传递给了整个群体。我们受托以自己的面貌承载故事的面貌。在祈祷与见证中，我们凝视着故事的面貌，同时以自身的审美之心为其注入灵魂，以此引导我们的灵魂塑造。随后，当演绎完成，故事的面貌被凝视之后，演员们便面对讲述者。故事的面貌通过我们的面貌与讲述者的面貌相遇。而观众也面对着我们，他们同样映照出故事的痕迹。

All of these reflections are contained by the ensoulment. When we turn and face the Teller, we freeze; and irk that stillness and silence, which is the grammar of prayer, the entire enactment is stilled. In our minds' eyes we hold up the images we have given her, which she has given us, reflecting back to the Teller not her own, literal face; not her "ego," not her Self. But we reflect on our faces the inward face of the story she has been seeing inside. We show her her own aesthetic heart, brought out into the world for a moment and then returned to her, ensouled by us. The face of the story is the face of her aesthetic heart as it faces her own soul, reflected in our faces, reflected in the faces around her.

所有这些映照都蕴含于赋予灵魂的过程中。当我们转身面对讲述者时，我们定格了；而当这种静止与沉默——即祈祷的语法——被打破时，整个演绎便归于静止。在我们心灵之眼中，我们高举着我们给予她的、而她又给予我们的意象，向讲述者映照回的并非她自身字面意义上的面貌；既非她的“自我”，也非她的真我。而是将她内心所见的故事之面貌，映照在我们的脸上。我们向她展示她自己的审美之心——它被带入世间片刻，随后又被我们赋予灵魂，归还于她。故事的面貌，正是她那审美之心面对自身灵魂时的面貌，它映照在我们的脸上，也映照在她周围众人的脸上。

The ensoulment has taken place.

赋予灵魂的过程已然完成。

### THE DEBT TO APHRODITE 对阿芙罗狄蒂的欠债

When soul appears, beauty appears. Not beauty which prettifies. Not beauty which removes the monsters or changes the devils to angels. Not beauty which glosses over or covers. Not beauty which makes nice. Rather, a kind of beauty which finds beauty "in the form of what is presented, that which is breathed in, aisthesis., and by which the value of each particular thing strikes the heart, where judgments are heartfelt responses." This beauty, which appears when we ensoul the story,

当灵魂显现时，美便随之显现。不是那种粉饰太平的美。不是那种消除怪物或将恶魔变成天使的美。不是那种粉饰太平或掩盖真相的美。不是那种装模作样的美。而是一种“在呈现的形式中、在被吸入的气息中、在感知（aisthesis）中发现美，并由此使每一件具体事物的价值直击心灵，而判断正是发自内心的回应”的美。这种美，正是在我们为故事注入灵魂之时显现的，

> is the manifest anima mundi [soul of the world]...
> 
> 便是显现的“世界之魂”（anima mundi）……
> 
> [and] refers to appearances as such, created as they are, in the forms in which they are given, sense data, bare facts, Venus Nudata. Aphrodite's beauty refers to the luster of each particular event--its clarity, its particular brightness. [We apprehend] the very sensibility of the cosmos, that it has textures, tones, tastes ... [for] beauty appears wherever soul appea*rs. 64
> 
> [并且]指向现象本身——它们本然的创造形态，以被呈现的形式存在，即感官数据、赤裸的事实、‘裸体维纳斯’（Venus Nudata）。阿芙罗狄蒂的美，指向每个具体事件的光辉——其澄澈，其独特的明媚。[我们领悟到]宇宙本身的感性，即它具有质感、音调、滋味……[因为]美出现在灵魂出现之处。64

In the history of theater and art, Dionysus and Apollo have a long tradition which we have seen influencing the direction of Playback. Not only did Playback refuse to be a theater of the rarefied soul, the Apollonian Grotowski, but an even bolder move was Jonathan's rejection of the Dyonisian "dismemberment" of the Artaud inspired experiments. While I hesitate to characterize Playback as a theater of Aphrodite, since Playback rightly. distrusts all mythical aggrandizement’s of its humble work, I do believe that we can honor the presence of Aphrodite in our theater as the style which properly opposes the Apollonian and Dyonisian.

在戏剧与艺术史上，狄俄尼索斯与阿波罗有着悠久的传统，我们已看到这一传统对一人一故事的发展方向产生了影响。一人一故事不仅拒绝成为一种精妙灵魂的戏剧——即阿波罗式的格罗托夫斯基——更进一步，乔纳森还大胆地摒弃了受阿尔托启发的、带有狄俄尼索斯式“肢解”特征的实验。虽然我犹豫于将一人一故事定性为阿芙罗狄蒂式的戏剧——因为一人一故事理所当然地不信任任何对其谦逊创作的神话式美化——但我确信，我们可以将阿芙罗狄蒂的存在视为我们戏剧中的一种风格，这种风格恰恰与阿波罗式和狄俄尼索斯式相对立。

`Because Aphrodite has been degraded in the patriarchy as a "sex" goddess, her essential meaning has been lost. Aphrodite symbolized for the ancient worldthat essence of soul in the most ordinary, tangible, surface things. She is the goddess who makes the mundane, the superficial, the "skin" of our lives lustrous and radiant. Her body seamlessly embraced the entire world, her ubiquity achieved through the idea of her skin covering the surface of the earth with amorous radiance. Her power was rhapsodic, in that she called forth beauty. Recovered without the degrading lens of the patriarchy, Aphrodite's power to carry beauty on the surface of everything, even the most trivial, metaphorically describes the movement of Playback away from the ideology of the specially elected masculine-creator toward our democracy of ordinary creativity. Aphrodite reminds us that this democracy is realized when we bring our attention to the surface of the story, to its "face," and call it forth. Unlike Dionysus, who spreads his energies through dismemberment and dismantling, Aphrodite spreads her luster over the entire surface of the world coherently, sparking soul into the surface of all for she is all the surface. She combines those elements of the oceanic and the mundane which are at the core of our embrace of the quotidian language of everyday story, entrusting it to carry all we need it to carry without any Apollonian ideology or Dionysian dissection. Aphrodite is the metaphor for the beauty we find in our art form through our attention, our loving noticing, of the "face value" of the story.

`由于阿芙罗狄蒂在父权制中被贬低为“性”女神，其本质的意义已然丧失。对古代世界而言，阿芙罗狄蒂象征着最平凡、最具体、最表层事物中蕴含的灵魂本质。她是那位让平凡、肤浅之事——即我们生活的“表皮”——焕发光彩与光辉的女神。她的身体无缝地拥抱了整个世界，其无处不在的特质源于这样一种意象：她的肌肤以充满爱意的光辉覆盖着地球表面。她的力量充满狂想色彩，因为她能唤起美。若能摆脱父权制那贬损的视角，阿芙罗狄蒂将美赋予万物表层——即便是最微不足道之物——的力量，便隐喻性地描述了一人一故事运动如何摆脱“被特别选中的男性创作者”这一意识形态，转向我们普通人的创造性民主。阿芙罗狄蒂提醒我们，当我们把注意力投向故事的表面——它的“面貌”——并将其唤醒时，这种民主便得以实现。与通过肢解和拆解来散播能量的狄俄尼索斯不同，阿芙罗狄蒂将她的光辉连贯地铺展在世界的整个表面，为万物的表面点燃灵魂，因为她本身就是整个表面。她融合了海洋性与世俗性这两大要素——它们正是我们拥抱日常故事语言的核心所在——并将这种语言托付给它，让它承载我们所需的一切，而无需任何阿波罗式的意识形态或狄俄尼索斯式的解构。阿芙罗狄蒂正是我们通过关注、通过对故事“表面价值”的充满爱意的察觉，在艺术形式中发现之美的隐喻。

Chekhov beautifully describes these aspects of Aphrodite in his story, The Student. One cold evening while walking home, a student stops to warm himself by a fire in a priest's garden. Inspired by the fire, begins spontaneously to recount the story of Peter's betrayal of Christ to a peasant woman and her idiot daughter who are warming themselves, too. As he builds the drama of the story, he slowly becomes aware of some power moving within him. When he observes the face of the peasant woman listening to his account, the student says he has moved his listeners not because of his own storytelling talents; it is not his power which has moved her. Rather, as the teller-actor of the story, Chekhov's student senses the presence of the nearness of the peasant woman's "soul" to the soul of Peter in the story. As the student looks at the peasant woman's face, he understands his role as the agent of soul moving through him.

契诃夫在他的短篇小说《学生》中，对阿芙罗狄蒂的这些特质进行了精妙的描绘。一个寒冷的傍晚，一名学生在回家途中，停在一处神父的花园里，围着篝火取暖。受篝火的启发，他开始向同样在取暖的一位农妇和她那愚笨的女儿，即兴讲述彼得背叛基督的故事。随着故事张力的逐渐增强，他渐渐察觉到某种力量正在自己体内涌动。当他凝视着聆听他讲述的农妇的面容时，这位学生说，他之所以打动听众，并非源于他自身的讲故事才能；打动她的也不是他自身的力量。相反，作为故事的讲述者兼表演者，契诃夫笔下的这位学生感知到，农妇的“灵魂”与故事中彼得的灵魂之间存在着一种亲近感。当这位学生凝视着农妇的脸庞时，他明白了自己作为“灵魂”的媒介，正让这种力量通过自己流动。

This experience of being the creative agent, the conductor, who helps the woman to experience this special closeness between her soul and Peter's, lifts the student from despair. By the end of the storytelling, as he looks out over the world around him, the student

这种作为创造性媒介、作为领航员的体验——他帮助这位农妇体验到她与彼得灵魂之间这种特殊的亲近感——使学生从绝望中解脱出来。在讲故事结束时，当他眺望周围的世界时，这位学生

> ... was thinking that the truth and beauty which had guided human life there, in the garden and the chief priest's palace, had continued without a break up to the present day and evidently constituted the most important thing in the lives of men and on the earth in general... and life seemed to him delightful, miraculous, and full of high significance. 65
> 
> ……不禁想到，那些曾指引人类生活的真理与美——无论是在花园里，还是在大祭司的宫殿中——一直延续至今，从未间断，显然构成了人类生活乃至整个地球上最重要的事物……生活在他眼中显得美妙、神奇，且充满崇高的意义。65

The student has told a story, and his audience has been moved. This experience has moved him from cynicism and despair to feelings of hope and the "high significance" of life all over the earth. It has happened through the ordinary act of telling a story (which may be why this story was Chekhov's personal favorite). The student's feeling of the continuity of "truth and beauty" echoes Keats and the Romantics, who, along with the American Transcendentalists, share with Playback an aesthetic vision of an ensouled world, a world in which the ordinary surface of things carries something of wonder and meaning -- the world of Aphrodite regained.

学生讲完了一个故事，听众们深受感动。这一经历使他从愤世嫉俗和绝望中走出来，转而怀抱希望，并感受到整个地球上生命所蕴含的“崇高意义”。这一切都源于讲故事这一平凡的举动（这或许正是契诃夫之所以将这个故事视为个人最爱的原因）。这位学生对“真理与美”的连续性的感受，与济慈和浪漫主义者们遥相呼应。他们与美国超验主义者一样，都与一人一故事共享着一种美学愿景：一个被赋予灵魂的世界，一个事物平凡的外表之下蕴含着奇妙与意义的世界——一个重获阿芙罗狄蒂恩典的世界。

### THE STEPCHILD GROWS UP 继女的成长

Now we are finally ready for this "stepchild" to show how fundamentally different she is from her stepfather, Moreno. Let us begin by saying that, because psychodrama bases its dramatic enactment on the confessional heart of scientized ego psychology, the form and style of action is virtually opposite to the Playback form, which responds to the aesthetic heart. Psychodrama dismembers through penetrating the surface (Dionysus). Playback ensouls through animating the 're-membered' surface (Aphrodite). Psychodrama actors work to bring insight through revealment; Playback actors work to bring revelation through our meeting with the face of the story

现在，我们终于准备好让这位“继女”展现她与继父莫雷诺之间根本性的差异了。首先需要指出的是，由于心理剧将其戏剧化演绎建立在科学化自我心理学的“坦白”之心之上，其行动的形式与风格几乎与一人一故事形式截然相反——后者则呼应着审美之心。心理剧通过穿透表象（狄俄尼索斯）来解构；一人一故事则通过赋予“重聚”的表象以生命（阿芙罗狄蒂）来赋予其灵魂。心理剧演员致力于通过揭示来获得洞见；一人一故事演员则致力于通过我们与故事面貌的相遇来获得启示。

Psychodrama builds its drama from the idea of confession, of partial revealment, and seeks to reveal more as it progresses. Consequently, it is interruptive and driven by the Self further inward. The "form" we become aware of in psychodrama, as the interview progresses, is likely to be a complex or some ideational awareness as we move *inward*. Playback, in contrast, moves the action forward by invoking the formal powers of contouring as the aesthetic heart of the Teller is directed by the Conductor's questions to shape the drama. We work *toward containment*. Playback seeks to gather only so much information as necessary to enact the story, trusting that the images will carry resonance. We are discriminatory, recognizing like all artists that emotions alone are insufficient; we must shape them. As the interview progresses, we become increasingly aware of the nascent enactment being contoured by the Conductor's questions as we move *outward*, casting actors in their roles. This contouring and shaping we do, this containment with language as our tool, is art growing within us as the interview continues. We progress by the laws of Art, of form, as we simultaneously shape and find the story.

心理剧基于坦白与部分揭示的理念构建戏剧，并随着进程的推进寻求进一步的揭示。因此，它具有中断性，并由“自我”驱动向更深处推进。随着访谈的进行，我们在心理剧中所意识到的“形式”，往往是在我们*向内*移动时所产生的某种复杂结构或观念性觉知。相比之下，一人一故事通过调动“轮廓塑造”这一形式力量来推动剧情发展——当讲述者的审美之心在领航员的提问引导下塑造戏剧时，我们便朝着*包容*的方向努力。一人一故事只收集演绎故事所必需的信息，并相信这些意象会产生共鸣。我们具有鉴别力，如同所有艺术家一样，认识到仅靠情感是不够的；我们必须对情感进行塑造。随着访谈的深入，当我们*向外*拓展、为角色选定演员时，我们越来越清楚地意识到，那个初生的演绎正被领航员的提问所塑形。这种我们所进行的塑形与雕琢，这种以语言为工具的“收束”，正是随着访谈的持续而在我们内心生长的艺术。我们遵循艺术与形式的法则前行，在塑造故事的同时，也发现着故事。

Psychodrama filters "story" through an ideology steeped in scientification, and builds the drama of enactment via this ideology. Enactments are conceived as complex manifestations of the system of the interior psyche (and culture) as conceived by Moreno. Some part of this psyche is being probed and taken apart. Hence, the drama builds through prodding further, penetrating through in one area only (the area of conflict, trauma, denial, etc.). Psychodrama would take the top off the container of the confession, so its form opens and opens, prying sometimes, banging at the door other times as actors repeat and expand in as many ways as possible to open up the confessing heart. Using the auxiliaries to pull back layer after layer of resistance, the quality of a psychodrama is *agon*, conflict.

心理剧将“故事”过滤于一种浸染着科学化色彩的意识形态之中，并借此意识形态构建出演绎的戏剧性。演绎被视为莫雷诺所构想的内在心理（及文化）体系的复杂表现形式。这种心理的某个部分正被探查并拆解。因此，戏剧性通过进一步的探询而构建，仅在某个特定领域（如冲突、创伤、否认等）进行深入穿透。心理剧会揭开坦白之“容器”的盖子，使其形式不断敞开——时而撬动，时而猛敲大门，演员们通过尽可能多的方式反复演绎并拓展，以敞开坦白之心。借助辅助人员层层剥离抵抗，心理剧的本质在于*agon*，即冲突。

But in Playback, the actors *layer* image upon image to amplify the mystery--which may be quite joyful--of the story. Because Playback has no ideology for story, and makes no case for these enactments as being reflections of an interior psyche, it does not penetrate. It stays on the surface, looking for the face of the story. Through *noticia*, the Playback conductor and actors can find in the surface all that they need.

但在一人一故事中，演员们将*层层叠加*一个又一个意象，以放大故事中的神秘感——这可能充满喜悦。因为一人一故事对故事没有既定意识形态，也不主张这些演绎是内在心理的反映，所以它不会深入探究。它停留在表面，寻找故事的面貌。通过*noticia*，一人一故事的领航员和演员们可以在表面上找到他们所需的一切。

Because psychodrama filters the story through ideology, the auxiliaries work from the script written by this ideology. When we double, we often double with a purpose or direction, a narrative line which comes from the script of psychodrama's ideas about the Self and culture. Though we use our own insight and our own words, we are informed always by this ideational script. Further, the term "auxiliary ego" suggests support or assistance in the service of this ego, so our whole warm-up to the action is to serve this ego. "Ego," here, means far more than the person; the psychodrama actor is conceived as an assistant to the entire role-complex of the Self. Playback actors also have a script which grounds us -- the telling of the story by the Teller. But we do not serve "ego," this interior complex system. We serve the Teller's own creation of experience, her own imagining substrate -- her soul -- which has shaped the story for us to enact. We are not the auxiliaries, but the animators of her story. How utterly and simply we are just that: animators, those who set this story into motion, seeking to reflect the face of the story with our own animated faces.

由于心理剧通过意识形态过滤故事，辅助角色们便依据这一意识形态所撰写的剧本进行表演。当我们进行“双重扮演”时，往往带着某种目的或方向，遵循一条源自心理剧关于“自我”与文化观念的剧本所衍生的叙事线索。尽管我们运用自己的洞察力和语言，但始终受制于这一观念剧本。此外，“辅助自我”一词暗示着为这个“自我”提供支持或协助，因此我们整个行动前的热身环节都是为了服务于这个“自我”。这里的“自我”远不止指个人；心理剧演员被视为整个“自我”角色复合体的助手。一人一故事的演员也有一个作为根基的剧本——即讲述者讲述的故事。但我们并非服务于“自我”——这个内在的复杂系统。我们服务的是讲述者自身创造的体验，是她想象的基底——她的灵魂——正是它塑造了供我们演绎的故事。我们不是辅助者，而是赋予她故事的生命力的人。我们就是如此彻底而单纯地扮演着这一角色：赋予生命力的人（animators），那些让故事动起来的人，试图用我们生动的面孔来映照故事的面貌。

These differences between psychodrama and Playback coalesce in the conceptions of the stage in both disciplines. Psychodrama, for all of its attention to actual space and time, uses the stage as the site for interiorizing. As auxiliaries work to pull back the layers hidden within the confession, the protagonist is led further and, further into and within her Self. The stage space becomes increasingly symbolic of the interior of the psyche as the drama proceeds with this penetrating spiraling inward. Usually at some point in a psychodrama, one loses one's sense of real space and time when the layers have been pulled back and the core of the psyche is exposed. We are located within the protagonist's psyche. Playback, because our dramatic unfolding is kept close to the face of the story moving outward to the world, does not convert the stage space into an abstract interior. We keep to the images, the icons, the "faces" of the story. No matter what kind of a face the Teller gives us when she tells her story, we meet it with our own.

心理剧与一人一故事之间的这些差异，在两种学科对舞台的理解中交汇。心理剧尽管非常关注实际的空间和时间，却将舞台作为内化的场所。当辅助者努力剥开坦白中隐藏的层层面纱时，主角被引导着不断深入、更深入地进入并沉浸于她的“自我”之中。随着戏剧以这种深入内心的螺旋式推进，舞台空间越来越象征着心理的内在世界。通常在心理剧的某个时刻，当层层面纱被剥开、心理的核心暴露出来时，人们会丧失对真实时空的感知。我们置身于主角的心理世界之中。在一人一故事中，由于我们的戏剧性展开始终紧贴着向外延伸至世界的叙事表层，因此并未将舞台空间转化为抽象的内在空间。我们始终紧扣故事中的意象、图腾以及“面貌”。无论讲述者在讲述故事时向我们展现何种面貌，我们都会以自己的面貌与之相遇。

I must now ask myself, out loud, if I have not accomplished in this essay the opposite of my hope -- namely, to have reframed Playback as yet another therapeutic modality among a surfeit. Do I mean to persuade you that Playback is Hillman's putative method realized?

此刻，我必须大声自问：在这篇论文中，我是否恰恰做到了与我的期望相反——即将一人一故事重新定义为众多治疗模式中又一种，而这些模式早已泛滥成灾。我是否想说服你们相信，一人一故事正是希尔曼所设想方法的实现？

No, I do not.

不，我并非如此。

I have deliberately excised from my discussion most of Hillman's Jungian ideology. Because Hillman shares with all Jungians a bias toward the mythical over the quotidian, he calls his psychology "polytheistic." Aphrodite, crucial as she is to reinstate without the degrading lens of patriarchy, is not enough for Hillman. He wants to call all of the gods to be present during the lifelong process of soulmaking.66 However, Playback does not require that all the gods be present. We may find archetypal resonance in a story, but we do not require the presence of archetypes to ensoul. Ours is a more human and more humble enterprise. If the gods choose to appear, they may do so without an invitation from us.

我刻意从讨论中剔除了希尔曼大部分的荣格主义理念。因为希尔曼与所有荣格学派学者一样，倾向于将神话置于日常之上，因此他将自己的心理学称为“多神论”的。阿芙罗狄蒂虽然对于在摆脱父权制贬低视角的前提下恢复其地位至关重要，但对希尔曼而言还远远不够。他希望在“灵魂塑造”这一终生的过程中，召唤所有神灵共同参与。66然而，一人一故事并不要求所有神灵都必须在场。我们或许能在某个故事中发现原型的共鸣，但并不需要原型的现身来赋予灵魂。我们的事业更为人性化，也更为谦逊。如果神灵选择现身，即使没有我们的邀请，它们也会自发而来。

However, I do believe that Hillman would see the usefulness of Playback as a training vehicle for the new type of psychologist he envisions working to awaken the aesthetic heart. He does want a "training... based in the imagining, sensing heart: call it forth and educate it Training programs must be themselves "embodiments of the anima mundi, whether in language, in arts, in rituals, attempting to train the eye and ear, nose and hand to sense truly, to make moves, right reflexive acts, to craft well.67 Certainly, Playback would meet these requirements.

不过，我确信希尔曼会认可一人一故事作为一种培训工具的价值，它能培养他所设想的那种致力于唤醒“审美之心”的新型心理学家。他确实希望有一种“基于想象与感知之心的培训……唤醒并培养它。培训项目本身必须是‘世界之魂（anima mundi）的化身’，无论是在语言、艺术还是仪式中，都应致力于训练眼睛、耳朵、鼻子和双手去真实地感知，去做出恰当的反应，去精湛地创作。”67毫无疑问，一人一故事完全符合这些要求。

As we embark on the project of how to develop excellent Playback practitioners, we can use Hillman's idea of ensoulment as I have applied it here as a foundation for our training at the Jonathan Fox School. We can then lay down four cornerstones upon this foundation of ensoulment: story, acting/theater, politics or ethics, and psychology. As we train our future practitioners in the capacity for story, we must renew our debt to the oral tradition; increase our awareness of Playback as a context, or a container, for story; and explore the many aspects of story as knowledge, story as art, and story as medicine. We can also place ourselves in an aesthetic tradition which includes the European Romantic Poets, especially Keats who first coined the term "soulmaking,"68 and the American Transcendentalists and others from the many cultures represented in our community. As we build our scholarship of story, we can join Hillman in rescuing myth from its allopathic jail. The training in acting and theatrical skills shall make us great lovers, great it noticers," as we learn the arts of Noticia and of Reflection, attending to the face of the story with our faces and bodies and hearts and minds. As we renew Playback's commitment to empower the disenfranchised, our training program should expand the idea of who is disenfranchised, and how, with the recognition that what is now oppressed is the earth herself. We can develop a moral vision which is not based on pathologizing but, rather, awakening the anima mundi. And, the fourth cornerstone, psychology, should include but not be limited to psychodrama. Expanding the discipline of psychology to include the cognitive work of Bruner, Sacks, and Gardner, the explorations of the aesthetics of soul in the work of Moore and other disciples of Hillman, and pursuing the idea of essence or "quiddity" as first developed by the American psychologist William James, we can develop our own psychological tradition informed by the aesthetics of being.69 In this larger context, psychodrama will become but one of many ways to develop our emotional repertoire without continuing to confuse its language with our unique grammar of ensoulment in performance. Psychodrama and Moreno's group action methods are still a fluid translation to many aspects of our group life, and our new independence from our "stepfather" should in no way mean that we abandon all that he has given us.

当我们着手开展如何培养优秀一人一故事实践者的项目时，我们可以将希尔曼关于“赋予灵魂”的理念——正如我在本文中所应用的那样——作为乔纳森·福克斯学院培训的基础。在此“赋予灵魂”的基础之上，我们可以奠定四大基石：故事、表演/戏剧、政治或伦理，以及心理学。在培养未来实践者讲述故事的能力时，我们必须重新珍视口述传统；增强对一人一故事作为故事语境或容器的认识；并探索故事作为知识、艺术和疗愈的诸多层面。我们还可以将自己置于一种美学传统之中，该传统包括欧洲浪漫主义诗人——尤其是最先提出“灵魂塑造”这一术语的济慈68——以及美国超验主义者，还有我们社群中代表众多文化的其他人士。在构建我们的故事学术体系时，我们可以与希尔曼一道，将神话从其“对症下药”的牢笼中解救出来。“表演和戏剧技巧的训练将使我们成为伟大的爱者、伟大的‘察觉者’”，当我们学习“察觉”与“反思”的艺术时，便会用我们的面貌、身体、心灵和思想去关注故事的面貌。当我们重申一人一故事对赋权被剥夺权利者的承诺时，我们的培训项目应当拓展“谁被剥夺了权利”以及“如何被剥夺”的定义，并认识到当下遭受压迫的正是地球本身。我们可以发展一种道德愿景，它不基于病理化，而是唤醒“世界之魂”（anima mundi）。此外，作为第四块基石的心理学，应当包含但不限于心理剧。通过将心理学学科拓展至布鲁纳、萨克斯和加德纳的认知研究，纳入摩尔及希尔曼其他门徒作品中对灵魂美学的探索，并深入探讨美国心理学家威廉·詹姆斯最初提出的“本质”或“quiddity”理念，我们可以建立一种植根于“存在美学”的独特心理学传统。69在这一更广阔的语境中，心理剧将仅仅成为我们拓展情感表现力多种途径中的一种，而不再将其语言与我们在表演中独特的“赋予灵魂”语法混为一谈。心理剧和莫雷诺的群体行动方法，依然是对我们群体生活诸多方面的灵活诠释，而我们从“继父”那里获得的新独立性，绝不意味着我们要抛弃他给予我们的所有馈赠。

What shall we then be practitioners of --art? Therapy?

那么，我们将成为什么的实践者——艺术？还是治疗？

We shall become practitioners of Playback.

我们将成为一人一故事剧场的实践者。

## EPILOGUE 'WHERE DID THE WARM THING GO?" 后记:"那温暖的东西去哪儿了？"

I would like to leave you with a story. It is a story about how the soul first came into human consciousness through a story. It is also an idea, and I am not ashamed to say that I like stories which have ideas in them. This particular story gives us a clue as to why it is that our form, ensoulment, may be the healing modality we most need today as we turn away from the century of the confessional heart toward the aesthetic heart of the world. The story comes from Joseph Campbell, and it is his explanation for why human beings need imaginations to give birth to stories. So, since it's a story about birth, it should come as no surprise that the story has death in it.

我想与大家分享一个故事。这是一个关于灵魂如何通过故事首次进入人类意识的故事。这也是一种思想，我毫不羞愧地承认，我喜欢那些蕴含思想的故事。这个故事为我们提供了一个线索，解释了为何在今天——当我们正从“坦白之心”的世纪转向世界的“审美之心”之际——我们的形态，即“赋予灵魂”，或许正是我们最需要的疗愈方式。这个故事出自约瑟夫·坎贝尔之手，是他对人类为何需要想象力来孕育故事的阐释。既然这是一个关于诞生的故事，那么故事中出现死亡也就不足为奇了。

> One evening, a long, long, long time ago, when humankind still carried its fire about with it for the long, dark nights of dancing and eating, someone died. The body lay there, and it did not move. Loved ones gathered around it as the darkness fell, wondering why it did not move.
> 
> 很久很久很久以前的一个傍晚，当时人类还带着火种四处游走，为漫长而黑暗的夜晚里的歌舞宴饮取暖，有人去世了。尸体静静地躺在那里，一动不动。天色渐暗，亲人们围拢在尸体周围，不解地揣测着它为何一动不动。
>
> Someone from the tribe reached to touch it, and pulled back, in horror and amazement, for the body was cold. Other hands came close to touch, and continued to touch the entire surface of the body, especially the face, looking for the warmth which had once been there. Noses came close to smell, and others rubbed up against the cold body as if they could bring the warmth back through their own warm bodies. Yet no matter how intimate their attending, no matter how close their noticing, they could not find the warmth which had once been there.
>
> 部落中有人伸手去触碰，却因惊骇与惊讶而猛地缩回手——因为尸体已经冰冷。其他人的手也凑近去触摸，并继续抚摸着遗体的每一寸肌肤，尤其是脸庞，寻找着曾经存在过的温暖。有人凑近嗅闻，还有人用身体蹭着冰冷的遗体，仿佛能通过自己温暖的身体将那份温暖带回来。然而，无论他们多么亲密地守候，无论他们观察得多么仔细，都无法找到曾经存在过的温暖。
> 
> Where did the warm thing go?
>
> 那份温暖去了哪里？
> 
> They puzzled and puzzled. The warm thing that had been there, where was it? This thing was cold. Where was the warmth?
>
> 他们百思不得其解。曾经存在于此的温暖之物，究竟去了哪里？这具躯体已然冰冷。那份温暖又去了哪里？
> 
> After a long time, as the fires of the evening began to die, one of them began to imagine where the warm thing was. She imagined that the warm thing might have gone upward, toward the place where the sun was, for it liked warmth. She began to speak of the journey of the warm thing toward the sun. And as she told the story, another member of the tribe began to see in his mind's eye this warm thing that had been inside his brother. It seemed to him that it had flown up to this place in the sky easily, for as he touched his brother's dead body, he noticed how heavy it was. So the warm thing must have been the thing that made his brother's body light enough to run swiftly in the hunt, and to dance. It was the thing which animated his brother's body and made it beautiful to watch. He began to dance her words as they told the story of where the warm thing went. As they performed, all the members of the tribe remembered his face when it was alive.
> 
> 过了许久，当傍晚的篝火开始熄灭时，其中一人开始想象那温暖之物身在何处。她想象着，那温暖之物或许已向上升去，朝向太阳所在之处，因为它喜爱温暖。她开始讲述那温暖之物朝太阳飞去的旅程。随着她讲述的故事，部落中的另一位成员在脑海中开始看见这个曾存在于他兄弟体内的温暖之物。在他看来，它似乎轻而易举地飞到了天空中的那个地方，因为当他触摸兄弟的遗体时，发现那遗体是多么沉重。因此，那温暖之物必定就是让他的兄弟在狩猎时能轻盈奔跑、在舞蹈中翩然起舞的源泉。正是它赋予了兄弟的身体生命力，使其舞姿如此动人。随着她讲述那温暖之物去向的故事，他开始随着她的话语起舞。在他们共舞之际，部落的所有成员都忆起了他生前那张生动的面容。
> 
> And thus it was that as the story grew in the telling, the body of their member was ensouled.
>
> 就这样，随着故事在讲述中逐渐丰富，他们这位族人的躯体重新获得了灵魂。

All stories are children of this first story about death, and each story seeks to answer this question in its own way: "*Where did the warm thing go?*" Where did the warm thing go? Is the question of this cold, dying time in which we are living now. It is a question which Playback has one way of answering -- if you have the courage to ask with us.

所有故事都是关于死亡的这个最初故事的子嗣，而每个故事都试图以自己的方式回答这个问题：“*那温暖的东西去了哪里？*”那温暖的东西去了哪里？这就是我们如今所处这个冰冷、垂死时代的疑问。这是一个一人一故事能给出某种答案的问题——只要你有勇气与我们一同发问。
