# What is "Good" Playback Theatre? 什么是"好"的一人一故事剧场？


Jo Salas


As guest conductor, I took part in a performance with London Playback Theatre at a weekend celebration held in support of the Beijing Women's Conference. About 80 people crowded into the performance space, an oddly shaped room upstairs at the Royal Festival Hall. The audience was very diverse: women attending the weekend festival, individuals and families who had come especially to see playback, some elderly couples strolling by the Thames who wandered in out of curiosity. I had been warned that British audiences were not easy to engage, so I was relieved when they responded as fully as any other audience. Within minutes strong feelings had been expressed and enacted, people were laughing and crying, and the actors, musician and I were working smoothly together in spite of our unfamiliarity with each other. As the show went on there were some inspired moments of acting, as well as occasional awkwardness or confusion. At the end of the show the applause was warm. People lingered talking to us and to each other before dispersing into the afternoon.


作为客邀请领航员，我参加了伦敦一人一故事剧场为支持北京妇女大会而举办的周末庆祝活动的演出。大约 80 人挤进了表演场地———皇家节日音乐厅楼上的一个奇形怪状的房间。观众非常多样化：有参加周末庆典的妇女，有专程来看一人一故事演出的个人和家庭，还有一些在泰晤士河畔散步的老年夫妇，他们出于好奇也走进来观看演出。我曾被警告过，英国观众不容易吸引，因此当他们像其他观众一样全情投入时，我松了一口气。短短几分钟内，强烈的情感得到了表达和演绎，人们又哭又笑，演员、乐师和我尽管互不相识，却合作得十分顺畅。随着演出的进行，出现了一些令人鼓舞的表演时刻，偶尔也会出现尴尬或困惑。演出结束时，掌声热烈。人们流连忘返，相互交谈着，然后散入了下午的时光。


The other performers and I came together before we too went our separate ways. Most of them shared my feeling that it had gone well. But one young woman was in tears of frustration. "I hated it!" she said. She felt that the actors had missed opportunities to be as sophisticated as they could be, and, particularly, that she had failed to show the scope of her own skill.


我和其他表演者在分道扬镳之前走到了一起。他们中的大多数人都和我一样，觉得一切都很顺利。但有一位年轻女士流下了沮丧的泪水。她说："我讨厌这样！"。她觉得演员们错失了展现他们精湛演技的机会，尤其是她没有展现出自己的精湛技艺。


How to account for the radical difference in our perception? My judgment of success was based on my feeling that the performers, myself included, had achieved an acceptable level of performance. Our scenes and so on had been generally well-shaped, and accurate in embodying the essence of the teller's experience. The audience had participated fully from the outset, in spite of the diversity of background, age, ideology, and contact with playback. The familiar phenomenon of audience members lingering afterwards, connected to each other as they had not been before, was another sign to me that what we had done had succeeded.


如何解释我们认知上的巨大差异？我对成功的判断是基于我的感觉，即包括我在内的演员们都达到了可以接受的表演水平。我们的场景等总体上塑造得很好，准确地体现了讲述者经历的本质。尽管观众的背景、年龄、意识形态以及与一人一故事的接触各不相同，但他们从一开始就全情投入。事后，观众们流连忘返，彼此间产生了前所未有的连接，这种熟悉的现象在我看来是我们的工作取得成功的另一个标志。


But for Chloë, the unhappy actor, the audience's satisfaction meant nothing. She hadn't even noticed it. She had been focused on artistic issues to the complete exclusion of the show as a social event. To her, success was something that happened only on stage, not in the room as a whole.


但对于克洛伊这个不开心的演员来说，观众的满意度毫无意义。她甚至没有注意到这一点。她一直专注于艺术问题，完全忽略了演出这一社会事件。对她来说，成功只发生在舞台上，而不是这个容纳我们每个人的房间中。


Playback theatre is theatre, and theatre is art.


一人一故事剧场是剧场，剧场是艺术。


Playback is theatre not simply because that is how it has been named, but because what we do in playback fulfills the very essence of theatre's intention: to convey human experience by enacting it in distilled form; to embody narrative and meaning in the realm of space and time.


一人一故事剧场之所以是剧场，并不仅仅因为它的名字是这样命名的，而是因为我们在一故事中的所作所为实现了剧场的本质意图：通过演绎的方式凝练、传达人类经验；在空间和时间的领域中体现叙事和意义。


It is also an interactive social process with the purpose of service to its audience; a purpose that most other kinds of theatre do not share.


它也是一个以服务观众为目的的互动式社会过程；这是大多数其他类型的戏剧所不具备的目的。


Since playback's earliest days, questions relating to aesthetics and to artistic standards have raised themselves constantly. Can this really be theatre if it is also service? Are we doing it well enough? Can we expect to be acknowledged by others as theatre artists? How can we recognize and measure success in playback? How can we refine it? What do we mean by "good" playback?


自一人一故事诞生之初，有关美学和艺术标准的问题就不断出现。如果它同时是一种服务，那它还能是戏剧吗？我们做得足够好吗？我们能否期望作为戏剧艺术家得到他人的认可？如何认识和衡量一人一故事的成功？我们如何才能完善它？何谓“好的”一人一故事？


What we do is art, but not only art, and maybe not always art. Playback's effectiveness involves artistic excellence—but artistic excellence alone does not ensure success, and further, playback theatre can, in certain circumstances, be fulfilled by people lacking artistic sensibility, skill, or experience.


我们所做的是艺术，但不仅仅是艺术，也许并不总是艺术。一人一故事剧场的有效性涉及到卓越的艺术性——但仅有卓越的艺术性并不能确保成功，此外，在某些情况下，缺乏艺术感觉、技巧或经验的人也能完成任务。


## Evaluating Art 评价艺术


All art is the manifestation of the artist's impulse to create, which itself is an urge to synthesize and to reveal. Art is the revelation of meaning—not the simple depiction of beauty or obedience to laws of form, but the artist's inspired attempt to convey her or his perception of a meaning that emerges from or underlies the randomness of common experience. For the perceiver of art, there is a sense of recognition, delight, a confirmation of truth. The philosopher Newton speaks of the artist's task as being not to create beauty where none existed, but in fact "to lift a corner of the veil and reveal beauty:" and by beauty, he means rightness of form and design, not necessarily harmony. Gregory Bateson discusses art in terms of pattern, meaning the presence of elements that repeat in a connected design, so that if you see one part of a pattern you also learn about the other, unseen part. The internal metaphors of a work of art contain information about wider realms—the unconscious, or the nature of reality itself—that are not directly perceivable. The artist's deliberate placement of one element in relation to another matches or illuminates the perceiver's intuition of truth: it is the fact of this relatedness, says Bateson, that is the true subject of art.


所有艺术都是艺术家创作冲动的体现，而创作冲动本身就是一种综合和揭示的冲动。艺术是对意义的揭示——不是对美的简单描绘，也不是对形式法则的遵从，而是艺术家在灵感的启发下，试图传达其对意义的感知。这种意义来自于共通经验的随机性，或者说是共通经验的基础。对于艺术的感知者来说，有一种认同感、愉悦感和对真理的确认感。哲学家牛顿认为，艺术家的任务不是在不存在美的地方创造美，而是"揭开面纱的一角，展现美"。格雷戈里-贝特森（Gregory Bateson）从模式的角度来讨论艺术，意思是在一个有联系的设计中重复出现的元素，这样，如果你看到模式的一部分，你也会了解到另一个不可见的部分。艺术作品的内部隐喻包含了更广阔领域的信息——无意识或现实本身的性质——这些都是无法直接感知的。贝特森说，艺术家有意将一个元素与另一个元素联系在一起，从而匹配或照亮了感知者对真理的直觉：正是这种关联性的事实，才是艺术的真正主题。


Art has the capacity to imply the presence of design, of pattern, as a feature of reality. It can affirm an ontological meaning, which is the knowledge that being itself is purposeful. When we raise the question of assessment of a work of art—"how good is it?"—it is this function of affirming meaning that we are asking about. As human beings, we need the affirmation that art gives us, and we want it in strong measure.[1] We need art in order to integrate and comprehend our experience. We are disappointed, even dismayed, by inferior art. We may travel long distances and pay large amounts of money to experience "good" art. If we are artists ourselves we invest enormous effort, usually unpaid, in making our art as fine as we can possibly make it. And as artists, we are not content with anything less than an evaluative aesthetic judgment. We are pleased to know someone has enjoyed our creation; but there is a world of difference between hearing "I enjoyed it" and "This is very good." The pronouncement of value indicates the perceiver's "yes!"—that inward recognition of meaning and truth. It indicates that the artist has succeeded, at least to some degree, in his profoundest task of lifting a corner of the veil and revealing a part of the connecting pattern that lies underneath. The creator of the world itself, according to Genesis, pronounced his work to be good when it was completed, an expression of satisfaction recognizable by any artist.


艺术有能力暗示设计和模式作为现实特征的存在。它可以肯定一种本体论意义，即存在本身是有目的的。当我们对一件艺术作品提出"它有多好"的评价问题时，我们所追问的正是这种肯定意义的功能。作为人类，我们需要艺术给予我们的肯定，而且我们强烈地希望得到这种肯定。[1]我们需要艺术来整合和理解我们的经验。我们对劣质艺术感到失望，甚至沮丧。为了体验"好的"艺术，我们可能会长途跋涉，花费巨资。如果我们自己就是艺术家，我们就会投入巨大的精力，通常是无偿的，尽可能地使我们的艺术品精美绝伦。作为艺术家，我们不会满足于任何少于美学评价的东西。我们很高兴知道有人喜欢我们的创作；但听到"我喜欢"和"这非常好"之间有着天壤之别。对价值的宣示表明了感知者的"是的！"——对意义和真理的内在认可。它表明艺术家至少在某种程度上成功地完成了他最深刻的任务，揭开了面纱的一角，揭示了隐藏在面纱之下的连接模式的一部分。根据《创世纪》，世界本身的创造者在其作品完成时宣布它是好的，这是任何艺术家都能识别的一种满意的表达。


How do ordinary people, not professional critics, evaluate the art that we encounter? What is it that leads us to perceive something as good art? Do the music, literature, art and performance that most strongly move and inspire us have anything in common?


普通人而非专业评论家如何评价我们所接触的艺术？是什么让我们认为某些东西是好的艺术？最能打动和激励我们的音乐、文学、艺术和表演有什么共同之处吗？


There are elements, I believe, shared by all art that is widely recognized as being great. Whether the work is visual, written or performed, these elements include order (in the sense of purposeful design), an integrity of form with some kind of internal cohesiveness, the presence of originality, a high degree of skill in execution, evidence of conviction and inspiration on the part of the artist, and the ineffable sense that the work of art speaks of a reality beyond its own scale.


我认为，所有被公认为伟大的艺术都有一些共同的元素。无论作品是视觉的、书面的还是表演的，这些要素都包括秩序（在有目的的设计意义上）、具有某种内在凝聚力的完整形式、独创性的存在、高超的执行技巧、艺术家信念和灵感的证据，以及艺术作品所表达的超越其自身规模的现实的不可言喻的感觉。


If an artistic creation possesses all these qualities in abundance, it is likely to be considered by most people as great art. It will create in its audience the strongest experience of revelation of meaning. In the absence of these qualities, or of some of them, a work of art is less likely to generate a consensus of value: its impact will vary from one perceiver to another. But art that is less than great may still fulfill art's deepest purpose for some people. It is perfectly possible for an audience member to be riveted and transported by an amateur concert even if the performers lack the skill of professionals. It depends on the presence of other elements, not exclusively aesthetic: more about this later.


如果一项艺术创作拥有所有这些丰富的品质，那么它就有可能被大多数人视为伟大的艺术。它将给观众带来最强烈的意义启示体验。如果缺乏这些特质或其中某些特质，艺术作品就不太可能产生一致的价值共识：它的影响会因人而异。但是，对于某些人来说，不那么伟大的艺术作品仍然可以实现艺术最深刻的目的。一场业余音乐会，即使表演者缺乏专业技巧，观众也完全有可能被其吸引和感染。这取决于其他因素的存在，而不仅仅是美学因素：稍后再详述。


## Evaluating Interactive Social Events 评价互动式社交活动


As I said earlier, playback theatre is an art, and therefore subject to assessment according to the presence or absence of the elements mentioned above. However, it is more than art: a playback event is also integrally an interactive social event, and subject to success or failure as such. (It is significant that there is no obvious term to refer to the realm of our work that lies beyond pure art. The absence of a familiar or fully adequate term reflects the common difficulty in comprehending and discussing what I am referring to as the "interactive social" domain.)


如前所述，一人一故事剧场是一门艺术，因此可以根据上述要素的有无进行评估。然而，它不仅仅是一门艺术：一人一故事在本质上也是一个互动式的社交活动，因此也有成败之分。(重要的是，没有一个明显的术语来指代我们工作中超越纯艺术的领域。没有一个耳熟能详或完全恰当的术语，反映出在理解和讨论我所说的"互动式社交"领域时普遍存在的困难）。


We live our lives in proximity to others. The groups in which we find ourselves as children—family, neighborhood, and school—may be nurturing, or inadequate, or destructive. With maturity comes the desire to choose, to the degree possible, the nature and quality of the groups that form our social world, the people we live and work with, the groups we learn and play with.


我们的生活与他人息息相关。我们在孩提时代所处的群体——家庭、邻里和学校——可能是滋养性的，也可能是不适当的，或者是破坏性的。随着年龄的增长，我们希望尽可能地选择构成我们社会世界的群体的性质和质量，选择与我们一起生活和工作的人，选择与我们一起学习和玩耍的群体。


Although most people seek out groups that are rewarding and try to avoid those that are disappointing or harmful, they are not necessarily aware of the components of successful interactive events, and therefore may not be able to consistently choose, build or maintain groups that are truly satisfying and effective. You may notice that your evening language classes run smoothly and feel enjoyable while meetings with colleagues at work leave you dissatisfied or diminished, without realizing that the difference between them is no accident. As in the domain of art, there are distinct elements that are likely to lead to fulfillment, whether in an ongoing group or in a one-time event.


虽然大多数人都在寻找那些能给自己带来回报的团体，并尽量避免那些令人失望或有害的团体，但他们并不一定了解成功的互动活动的组成部分，因此可能无法始终如一地选择、建立或维持真正令人满意和有效的团体。你可能会注意到，你的晚间语言课进行得很顺利，感觉很愉快，而与同事的工作会议却让你不满意或减分，却没有意识到它们之间的差异并非偶然。就像在艺术领域一样，无论是在一个持续性的团体中还是在一次性的活动中，都有可能产生成就感的独特因素。


Consider a family gathering, a class, or a town meeting. Although each has its own particular demands, they also share common criteria of success. These include planning and organization according to the purpose of the gathering; a congenial and appropriate physical environment; an opportunity early in the proceedings for each person to be seen and heard; an atmosphere of respect; some form of participation or engagement from all present; the acknowledgment and inclusion of diverse concerns, points of view, and feelings; time management; a sense of achievement in relation to the meeting's intent; and an adequate closure at the end. The more these elements are present, the more the event will be experienced as successful. In their absence, an event is felt to be a failure. Participants are likely to feel disappointed, excluded, frustrated, bored, or left wanting, depending on which aspects were inadequate. A family gathering in which some members sit silent while others laugh and chat will not be successful in bringing the family together; a town meeting that is unplanned will not make progress toward its purpose; a classroom that is too cold or crowded will not be conducive to good learning.


考虑一下家庭聚会、课堂或市政会议。虽然每种活动都有自己的特殊要求，但它们也有共同的成功标准。这些标准包括：根据聚会的目的进行规划和组织；一个融洽和适当的物质环境；在会议开始时让每个人都有机会被看到和听到；一种尊重的氛围；所有与会者某种形式的参与或投入；承认和包容不同的关注点、观点和感受；时间管理；与会议目的相关的成就感；以及在会议结束时的适当收尾。这些要素出现得越多，活动就越成功。如果没有这些要素，活动就会被认为是失败的。参与者很可能会感到失望、排斥、沮丧、无聊或意犹未尽，这取决于哪些方面做得不够。一次家庭聚会，如果一些成员沉默不语，而另一些成员却谈笑风生，那么这次家庭聚会就不会成功；一次没有计划的市政会议就不会在实现其目的方面取得进展；一个太冷或太拥挤的教室就不利于良好的学习。


Moreno's method of sociometry addresses particularly the questions relating to inclusiveness and participation. He considered that in any group there is an intricately evolving pattern of relationships between members, resulting in a greater or lesser degree of inclusion and contributing therefore to the group's vitality and effectiveness. Sociometry explores this system of interconnections, with the goal of maximizing and enlivening the participation of all those present.


莫雷诺的社会计量学方法特别涉及与包容性和参与性有关的问题。他认为，在任何群体中，成员之间都存在着一种错综复杂、不断发展的关系模式，或多或少会产生包容性，从而增强群体的活力和有效性。社会计量学探索这种相互联系的系统，目的是最大限度地提高和活跃所有参与者的参与度。


Groups of many different kinds—vocational, recreational, geographical— are often referred to as "communities," a word rather wistfully overused in our post-industrial era where it is common for people not to know the names of their neighbors. What does "community" mean? It is, I think, the idea of a group of people who acknowledge, value, and maintain their connectedness and their commonality. Jean Vanier, who founded a series of group homes for disabled people in France, described community as "a group of people who are yearning to bring each other forth."


许多不同类型的团体——职业团体、娱乐团体、地域团体——经常被称为"社群"，在我们这个后工业时代，人们不知道邻居的名字是很常见的事情。"社群"是什么意思？我认为，它是指一群人认可、重视并维系他们之间的联系和共同性。让-瓦尼埃（Jean Vanier）曾在法国为残疾人创办了一系列集体之家，他将社群描述为"一群渴望相互促进的人"。


The qualities that might actually lead to the creation of this kind of community are similar to those I have suggested as necessary to a successful interactive social event: the attention to inclusiveness, the care taken with environment and structure, the matching of design to purpose. Whether in an ongoing situation or within one particular event, fulfillment of these criteria can create community in Vanier's sense—a gathering of people who seek to know each other, to listen to each other, to remember and care for each other.


实际上，可能导致创建这种社群的特质与我提出的成功的互动式社交活动所必需的特质相似：注重包容性、关注环境和结构、设计与目的相匹配。无论是在一个持续的环境中，还是在一个特定的活动中，满足这些标准都可以创造出瓦尼尔意义上的社群——一个寻求相互了解、相互倾听、相互缅怀和相互关怀的人们的集合。


## A Meeting Place 聚会场所


A playback theatre event is integrally both an artistic event and an interactive social event involving both complex group dynamics and sensitive one-to-one communication.


一人一故事剧场既是一个艺术活动，也是一个互动的社交活动，既涉及复杂的群体动态，也涉及敏感的一对一交流。


If the task of art is the creation of form to express the artist’s perception of meaning, then the playback artist’s task, specifically, is to create cohesive and shapely pieces of theatre based on an acute sense of the meaning of the teller’s story. It is their artist's sensibility that enables playback performers to transform moments from real lives, however simply or roughly told, into well-crafted dramas that have resonance for the audience as well as for the teller. Drawing on their aesthetic sense of story, as well as language, metaphor, and stagecraft, they create art, embodying a pattern of elements in relation to each other in such a way as to suggest the wisdom contained in a larger reality. For the audience, watching and listening is an aesthetic experience—in other words, an experience of affirmation, expansion, revelation, and delight.


如果说艺术的任务是创造形式来表达艺术家对意义的感知，那么一人一故事剧场表演艺术家的具体任务就是根据对讲述者故事意义的敏锐感知，创造出具有凝聚力和形体感的剧场作品。正是艺术家的敏锐触觉，使一人一故事表演者能够将真实生活中的片段，无论讲述得多么简单或粗糙，转化为精心制作的戏剧，让观众和讲述者产生共鸣。他们凭借对故事的审美意识，以及语言、隐喻和舞台技巧，创造出艺术，体现出各种元素之间的关系模式，从而暗示出更大的现实中所蕴含的智慧。对于观众来说，观看和聆听是一种审美体验，换言之，是一种肯定、拓展、启示和愉悦的体验。


This art is different from an art that only seeks to convey the artist's vision: it is an art that is committed to affirming ordinary people's experience and to fostering connections between them so that the communities we live in can grow in compassion and humanity. We strive to hone our art in order to offer it as service. Playback is a fusion, perhaps a unique one, of artistic and social phenomena. The more it can fulfill the demands of both, the more it can succeed.


这种艺术不同于只寻求传达艺术家观点的艺术：它致力于肯定普通人的经验，并促进他们之间的联系，从而使我们生活的社群能够在同情和人性中成长。我们努力磨练自己的艺术，以提供服务。一人一故事是艺术与社会现象的融合，也许是独一无二的融合。它越能满足两者的要求，就越能取得成功。


Every playback practitioner is familiar with the experience of frustration and chagrin when the process seems to fail, and with the elation that can come when it works. The assessment depends somewhat on whether you are considering a whole show or a single scene, whether it is a performance or an applied context such as a therapy or training group, whether you are a performer, a teller, or an audience member, and so on. However, I believe that the criteria I've proposed for success in art and interactive social events will consistently apply, along with some further qualities specific to playback.


一人一故事的实践者都熟悉在这一过程似乎失败时的沮丧和懊恼，以及成功时的欣喜。这一评价在某种程度上取决于你考虑的是整场演出还是一个场景，是表演还是应用领域，如治疗或培训小组，你是表演者、讲述者还是观众，等等。不过，我相信，我所提出的艺术和互动式社交活动的成功标准将始终适用，同时还有一人一故事所特有的一些进一步的特质。


Here are two more moments from my own experience when the question of success or failure has been notable for one reason or another:


下面是我亲身经历的另外两个时刻，由于这样或那样的原因，成功或失败的问题一直引人注目：


Four of us from my company, Hudson River Playback Theatre, went to perform as part of a college symposium on reproductive rights. It was several hours' drive from our home. We got there to find that the preparation for our show had been minimal. The location was the corner of a large open space with people wandering through on their way somewhere else. Very few people came for the show. We began by making personal statements relating to the theme, feeling more exposed doing this than if there had been a large audience. One of the organizers told a moving and very disturbing story about an abortion she had had many years ago. The actors enacted with passion and ingenuity. After it was over, the audience was silent. No-one else wanted to tell anything. We continued as best we could, hoping to somewhat redeem the situation with the strong ending that we had planned. But shortly before the scheduled close our host jumped up and said she was sorry, she'd made a mistake about the time frame and we had to end immediately. It felt very abrupt. In spite of expressions of gratitude from some of the people who were there, we felt that our show had been a failure.


我所在的哈德逊河一人一故事剧场有四个人去参加一个关于生育权的大学研讨会。那里离家有几个小时的车程。到了那里，我们发现演出的准备工作少得可怜。演出地点是一个大空地的一角，人们在去往其他地方的路上徘徊。来看演出的人寥寥无几。我们首先发表了与主题相关的个人感言，感觉这样做比有大量观众在场时更有感染力。其中一位组织者讲述了她多年前堕胎的一个感人至深的故事。演员们的表演充满激情和智慧。故事结束后，观众鸦雀无声。没有人再想讲什么。我们尽最大努力继续表演，希望用我们计划好的强有力的结尾来挽回局面。但就在预定结束前不久，我们的主持人跳起来说对不起，她搞错了时间，我们必须马上结束。感觉非常突然。尽管现场的一些人表达了感激之情，但我们还是觉得我们的演出失败了。


Looking back at our choices that day, I think that we failed, and I especially as the conductor, to create a safe enough space for this tiny audience who had come to explore an extremely delicate topic. Daunted by the inhospitable room and the small turn-out after our long drive, we didn't sufficiently adapt our plan to the situation we found. Our own discomfort as we made our personal statements at the beginning should have warned us that the audience would probably feel similarly exposed. If we had built a better connection with them at the outset, perhaps they would have been willing to journey with us from one story to the next. But the actors' stagework, although skillful and sophisticated, could not by itself draw the audience into more participation. It was in the social domain, not the artistic, that this performance was lacking.


回顾我们当天的选择，我认为我们，尤其是作为领航员的我，没有为前来探讨一个极其微妙话题的小观众们创造一个足够安全的空间。在长途跋涉之后，我们被这间不友好的房间和寥寥无几的观众吓到了，我们没有充分调整计划以适应我们所发现的情况。我们在一开始做个人陈述时，自身的不适感本应提醒我们，观众可能也会有类似的暴露感。如果我们一开始就与观众建立更好的联系，也许他们会愿意与我们一起从一个故事走到下一个故事。但是，演员们的舞台表演虽然娴熟而精巧，但其本身并不能吸引观众更多的参与。这场演出缺乏的是社会性，而不是艺术性。


Another moment:


另一个时刻:


At the Playback Theatre conference in Finland in 1993 there were performances by a number of different groups. A few were companies who had come with all or most of their members, but most were ad hoc groups composed of representatives usually from companies with a common language, if not a common country. All the performances showed a knowledge of playback forms, and some were more accomplished than others, but none fully embodied the playback spirit, in my view, until the last day, when a pick-up group of New Zealanders, Australians, and Hungarians took the stage. They had recognized in each other a kind of anarchic energy and wanted to play together, although they shared neither language nor playback style. Their performance was a great reassurance to me: I had been wondering unhappily what was missing from all the others (all the more troubling to me since four of the groups had asked me to perform with them as a musician). Looking back on it from the perspective of a few years, I recall a joyful interactive creativity among the actors, which made up for the complete absence of actorly polish; and a no-holds-barred attitude to the audience from the conductor. She faced us as though we were a many-headed organism, which we were, and teased, provoked, and cajoled us to tell our stories, not necessarily the ones we thought we wanted to tell. The large audience came alive and felt like a group for the first time in the entire conference.


1993 年在芬兰举行的一人一故事剧场会议上，有许多不同的团体进行了表演。有几个团体是带着全部或大部分成员来的，但大多数是临时组成的团体，其代表通常来自有共同语言的团体，即使没有共同的国家。所有表演都显示出对一人一故事的了解，有些表演比其他表演更有造诣，但在我看来，没有一个表演完全体现了一人一故事的精神，直到最后一天，一个由新西兰人、澳大利亚人和匈牙利人组成的表演团体登台表演。尽管他们既没有共同的语言，也没有共同的一人一故事，但他们在彼此身上发现了一种无政府主义的能量，并希望一起表演。他们的表演给了我极大的安慰： 我一直在不甘心地想，他们到底缺少了什么（其中有四个小组邀请我作为乐师与他们同台演出，这更让我感到不安）。从几年前的角度回想起来，我记得演员们之间充满了欢乐的互动创造力，这弥补了演员完全缺乏演技打磨的缺憾；而领航员对观众的态度也毫无保留。她面对我们，就好像我们是一个多头的有机体，而我们确实是，并且她逗弄、挑衅、劝说我们讲述自己的故事，而不一定是我们认为自己本想讲的故事。在整个会议期间，广大听众都活跃起来，第一次有了集体的感觉。


## Acting and Other Playback Criteria 表演和其他一人一故事的标准


The degree of success of the three performances I've mentioned (London, New York, and Finland) can be accounted for in a general way by the presence or absence of the qualities that I listed earlier as criteria for value in art—artistic form, skill and inspiration, etc; and in social events—skillful attention to inclusiveness, environment and structure, etc. There are also elements that are specific to playback theatre, some that are clearly in the realm of art, others that are simultaneously questions of art and non-art.


我前面提到的三场演出（伦敦、纽约和芬兰）的成功程度，可以通过我前面列出的艺术价值标准（艺术形式、技巧和灵感等）和社交活动价值标准（对包容性、环境和结构等的巧妙关注）的存在与否来大致说明。此外，还有一人一故事剧场特有的元素，有些明显属于艺术范畴，有些则同时是艺术与非艺术的问题。


In a public performance, the audience wants, expects, and deserves an aesthetic experience. How do we, the playback artists, ensure that they get it?


在公开演出中，观众希望、期待并理应获得审美体验。作为一人一故事的表演者，我们该如何确保他们获得这种体验呢？


Like any other artists, playback performers strive to find form in which to embody meaning. The primary artistic task in playback is the transformation of personal stories into theatre pieces, works of art with integrity of form and design. In our work, the meaning we seek to convey is the meaning embedded in the teller’s experience.


与其他艺术家一样，一人一故事的表演者努力寻找体现意义的形式。一人一故事剧场的主要艺术任务是将一人一故事转化为戏剧作品，转化为具有完整形式和设计的艺术作品。在我们的作品中，我们所要传达的意义就是讲述者经历中蕴含的意义。


The tools with which playback performers (including musician and conductor) make such transformations are those of the stage, beginning with aesthetic concerns like the visual set-up. Most importantly, there is the quality of the actors' performance. Good actors use movement freely and creatively. They know how to move and position themselves on stage in order to embody the story and to create an aesthetic, evocative stage picture at every moment. They use language with accuracy, economy and gracefulness. During scenes, they make imaginative and judicious use of the boxes and cloth props. There is a co-creative give-and-take between actors and musician. They have mastered the different demands of specific forms—fluid sculptures, pairs, etc. The ritual framing of the show and each segment within it is fulfilled with presence and dignity.


一人一故事的表演者（包括乐师和领航员）进行这种转换的工具是舞台上的工具，首先是视觉设置等美学问题。最重要的是演员的表演质量。优秀的演员能够自由地、创造性地使用动作。他们知道如何在舞台上移动和定位，以体现故事情节，并在每一时刻创造出美观、令人回味的舞台画面。他们的语言准确、经济、优美。在场景中，他们富有想象力地合理使用箱子和布道具。演员和乐师之间有一种共同创造的付出和回报。他们掌握了特定形式的不同要求——流动塑像、一对对等。演出的仪式框架和其中的每一个环节都是以临在和尊严来完成的。


The quality of acting itself goes beyond the specific use of movement, voice, etc. Playback theatre requires a particular kind of acting which we can call authentic acting, as opposed to the more stylized acting familiar from television, film and most other kinds of theatre, however naturalistic. In playback acting, the actor does not use a code to depict emotion but draws her portrayal directly from her sense of the teller and his story.


表演本身的质量超越了动作、声音等的具体运用。一人一故事剧场需要一种特殊的表演，我们可以称之为真实的表演，而不是电视、电影和其他大多数剧场（无论多么自然）所熟悉的风格化表演。在一人一故事的表演中，演员不使用固定套路来描绘情感，而是直接从对讲述者及其故事的感觉中进行描绘。


At its best, this kind of acting can be as powerful and inspired as that of great traditional actors, whose genius is precisely their quality of authenticity built on prodigious skill and training. However, formal theatre training may not be a good preparation for playback acting. Traditional actors are used to basing their performance on a script, not an empathic sense of an actual, present human being, and their technique may distance them from teller, audience, and fellow performers. They may also find it challenging to summon the generosity that playback actors need: part of Chloë's difficulty in the London performance was her concern to make an impression as an “actress” rather than a sense of service to the audience.


在最佳状态下，这种表演与伟大的传统演员的表演一样具有震撼力和启发性，而传统演员的天才正是建立在高超的技巧和训练之上的真实品质。然而，正规的戏剧训练可能无法为一人一故事剧场的表演做好准备。传统演员习惯于根据剧本进行表演，而不是对真实存在的人产生共鸣，他们的表演技巧可能会拉开他们与讲述者、观众和其他表演者之间的距离。他们也可能会发现，要唤起一人一故事演员所需要的包容心也是一种挑战：克洛伊在伦敦演出时遇到的部分困难是，她执着于给人留下"演员"的印象，而缺乏为观众服务的意识。


Experienced playback actors use a high degree of artistic awareness and skill in their acting: authentic acting does not mean that it is unmediated by an aesthetic consciousness. But because authenticity is the key, it is sometimes possible for completely inexperienced actors to produce very good acting drawing on nothing but empathy and spontaneity. On the other hand, people with a little experience—enough to have lost their beginner's innocence, but not enough to have acquired artistic skill— sometimes fall into serious mistakes in their acting. The problems are often in their attempts to depict story or emotion nonliterally—brandishing a piece of red cloth to show anger, for example, or following the teller's actor around the stage in a clumsy attempt to "be" his inner self. Brand-new actors in their innocence do not think of being anything but literal, while sophisticated actors know how to move fluently and clearly between literal and symbolic action on stage.


经验丰富的一人一故事演员在表演中运用了高度的艺术意识和技巧：真实的表演并不意味着不以审美意识为中介。但正因为真实是关键，所以有时完全没有经验的演员也可以凭借共鸣和自发性做出非常出色的表演。另一方面，稍有经验的人——足以褪去初学者的天真，但还不足以掌握艺术技巧——有时会在表演中犯下严重错误。问题往往出在他们试图用非文字的方式来描绘故事或情感——例如，用一块红布来表示愤怒，或者跟着讲述者的演员在舞台上走来走去，笨拙地试图"成为"他的内心。初出茅庐的演员天真无邪，不会想到要"做"什么，而老练的演员则知道如何在舞台上流畅、清晰地穿梭于写实动作和象征动作之间。


The music in playback is another area where artistry is key. Again, spontaneity and empathy on the musician's part can go a long way toward making up for a lack of musical talent or training. But the aesthetic quality of a performance will be greatly enhanced by music that is expertly and sensitively played.


一人一故事中的音乐是艺术性的另一个关键领域。同样，乐师的自发性和共鸣可以在很大程度上弥补音乐天赋或训练的不足。但是，如果音乐演奏得专业而敏锐，则会大大提高演奏的美学质量。


In addition to these artistic considerations, there are elements indivisibly in the realms both of art and of social interaction, and equally essential to playback's success. There is the faithfulness of the enactment to the teller's story, which depends on attentive and empathetic listening on the part of conductor, actors and musician. There is the co-creativity between actors, between actor and musician, between the conductor and the rest of the team. This includes the elusive quality of improvisation itself, which operates on a constant reciprocity of offers, in turn deriving from the performers' sense of the story and where it needs to go in order to be fulfilled. There is the shape of the event itself; the structure of the performance, how to begin, how to end, with decisions based both on an artistic sense of design and on the particular needs of the context and the audience. And encompassing all, the ritual and ceremony that underlie the entire performance, expressed in the quality of the performers' presence, the music, and the palpable sense of occasion—the “heightened dramatic event,” as Jonathan Fox describes it.


除了这些艺术方面的考虑之外，还有一些艺术和社会互动领域不可分割的要素，它们对一人一故事的成功同样至关重要。忠实于讲述者的故事，这取决于领航员、演员和乐师的细心聆听和感同身受。演员之间、演员与乐师之间、领航员与团队其他成员之间需要共同创作。这包括即兴创作本身难以捉摸的特质，它是在不断的互助条件下进行的，而互助条件反过来又来自于表演者对故事的感知、故事的方向才能实现。活动本身的形式、表演的结构、如何开始、如何结束，这些决定都是基于艺术设计感以及环境和观众的特殊需求。此外，还包括作为整场演出基础的仪式和典礼，表现为表演者的风采、音乐的质量以及明显的场合感——正如乔纳森-福克斯所描述的"高度戏剧性事件"。


## The Role of the Conductor 领航员的角色


The conductor embodies the fusion of art and social action: every aspect of the role carries both an aesthetic and an interactive consideration. It is the conductor who must greet and warm up the audience at the outset of the show; nothing is more crucial to the event's success. If the conductor in this initial moment is able to acknowledge and include all the disparate elements of the audience, to show a sensitivity to subgroups of age, culture, ethnicity or background, to create a sense of ease in those who are new to playback and eagerness in people who have come before, to establish an easy pattern of question and response, to direct the focus to the show's theme if there is one, then the performance is likely to unfold in a way that is satisfying to all. As the show proceeds, the conductor must remain attuned to the developing dynamics of the audience, the themes that emerge, the stories that may be untold but present. In his or her interactions with each individual teller, the conductor must create an immediate and genuine rapport, then elicit the story with grace and economy, maintaining contact with the rest of the audience while listening for the essence of the teller's experience, crystallizing it into the form of a story, and ensuring that the actors have enough but not an excess of information. As the performance draws to an end, the conductor will probably be the one to guide a closure, to draw attention to the collective story that has emerged, perhaps to thank the hosts, or to announce upcoming further events.


领航员体现了艺术与社会行动的融合：角色的每一个方面都包含着美学和互动的考量。在演出开始时，领航员必须向观众致意并热身，这对活动的成功至关重要。如果领航员能够在这一开场时刻认识到并包容观众的差异性，对不同年龄、文化、种族或背景的小群体表现出敏感性，让那些初次接触一人一故事的观众感到轻松，让那些曾经来过这里的观众感到热切，建立一种简单的提问和回答模式，将焦点引向演出的主题（如果有的话），那么演出就有可能以一种让所有人都满意的方式展开。随着演出的进行，领航员必须时刻关注观众的动态发展、浮现的主题、可能未曾讲述但却存在的故事。在与每一位讲述者的互动中，领航员必须立即与他们建立起真正的融洽关系，然后优雅而节制地引出故事，与其他观众保持联系，同时倾听讲述者经历的精髓，将其凝结成故事的形式，并确保演员获得足够但不过量的信息。当演出接近尾声时，领航员很可能会引导大家结束演出，提请大家注意已经浮现的集体故事，也许是为了感谢主办方，或者是为了宣布即将举行的其他活动。


Are these tasks social or artistic? They are tasks that we are more familiar with in social events; but in playback they must also be fulfilled with an aesthetic attention to form, design, and style. A playback actor's adequacy is measured by his basic performance skills in combination with his attunement to the teller, the story, and his fellow-performers; a playback conductor by her assurance and skill in handling group and interpersonal connections, and by the elegance and integrity of her own demeanor.


这些任务是社会性的还是艺术性的？它们是我们在社交活动中更熟悉的任务；但在一人一故事中，它们也必须以对形式、设计和风格的审美关注来完成。衡量一人一故事的演员是否称职，要看他的基本表演技巧，以及他对讲述者、故事和同伴的适应程度；衡量一人一故事的领航员是否称职，要看她在处理群体和人际关系时的自信和技巧，以及她自身举止的优雅和正直。


Some conductors have, in addition to these qualities, a charm or force of personality that might be called charisma. When combined with the essential skills of the conductor, charisma can be a positive factor that draws audiences into warmth and connection. However, charisma without skill and integrity can exert power in a way that is dangerous and incompatible with "good" playback, drawing trust to a conductor who does not deserve it.


有些领航员除了具备这些素质外，还具有一种魅力或人格力量，可以称之为人格魅力（charisma）。当人格魅力与领航员的基本技能相结合时，它可以成为吸引观众的积极因素，让观众感受到温暖和联系。然而，没有技巧和正直的魅力会以一种危险的、与"好的"一人一故事不相容的方式发挥力量，将信任引向一个不值得信任的领航员。


In my examples of success and failure above, fulfillment of both artistic and social considerations accounted, in my opinion, for the success of the London and Finland performances. In London, Chloë's criticism only emphasized the fallacy of assessing a show by looking solely at what is happening on stage—in effect, by ignoring all but purely artistic criteria. In the reproductive rights performance, our artistic proficiency was not enough to make up for our shortcomings in the interactive realm.


在我上述成功和失败的例子中，我认为艺术和社会两方面的考虑都是伦敦和芬兰演出成功的原因。在伦敦的演出中，克洛伊的批评只是强调了只看舞台上发生了什么来评估演出的谬误，实际上是忽略了除纯艺术标准之外的所有标准。在生育权利的演出中，我们的艺术水平不足以弥补我们在互动领域的不足。


## Context and Standards 背景与标准


In a playback performance there is an implicit contract regarding standards between the playback team and the audience. The audience has come to see professional theatre, they have paid at the door, and they legitimately expect to see competent work. They will be disappointed and critical if the performance falls below a basic level of artistic skill. Although no audience assessment will be unanimous, there will be a general consensus about the line between adequacy and inadequacy in the use of stage, voice, movement, and the artistic shaping of scenes.


在一人一故事的演出中，演出团队与观众之间有一个关于标准的隐性契约。观众是来观看专业戏剧的，他们在门口付了钱，理所当然地希望看到合格的作品。如果演出低于基本的艺术水平，他们会感到失望并提出批评。虽然观众的评价不会是一致的，但在舞台、声音、动作的运用以及场景的艺术塑造方面，观众对适当与不适当之间的界限会有一个普遍的共识。


The contract is different in non-performance contexts, where stories are enacted not by a company but by members of a training or therapeutic group where there is no separately-defined audience. In a therapy group, the emphasis is entirely on the process, and there may be little if any attention paid to artistic skills. The same is true using playback with groups of managers, teachers, or social workers to help them focus on current issues in their workplace. In such settings, the participants are not expected to be skillful performers, and artistic skill on their part is not necessary to the success of the work.


在非演出环境中，契约有所不同，在这种环境中，故事不是由剧团而是由培训或治疗小组的成员表演的，没有单独界定的观众。在治疗小组中，重点完全放在过程上，对艺术技巧的关注可能很少。与管理人员、教师或社会工作者团体一起使用一人一故事，帮助他们关注工作场所当前的问题，也是同样的道理。在这种情况下，我们并不期望参与者成为娴熟的表演者，他们的艺术技能也不是工作成功的必要条件。


However, this inherent flexibility of standards in playback does not extend to the conductor. In any context, with any group, with any purpose, the social and interactive aspects of playback must be expertly handled if the work is to fulfill itself. Whether in performances, workshops, or other applications, a sociometric awareness of the group is essential—acknowledging individuals and subgroups, inviting tellers, being alert to structures of influence and allegiance. Playback's purpose is always to bring people together, to affirm the individual and the group. Without mastery of the conductor's role, this fundamental goal cannot be achieved.


然而，一人一故事中这种固有的灵活标准并没有延伸到领航员身上。在任何情况下，与任何团体，出于任何目的，如果要实现作品本身的目的，就必须熟练处理一人一故事的社交和互动方面。无论是在表演、工作坊还是其他应用中，对群体的社会计量意识都是至关重要的——认识个人和小群体，邀请讲述者，对影响力和服从权威的结构保持警惕。一人一故事的目的始终是将人们聚集在一起，承认个人和群体。如果不掌握领航员的角色，这一基本目标就无法实现。


## Problematic Playback Theatre 有问题的一人一故事剧场


Inevitably, as playback has been adopted by enthusiasts around the world, a few have developed practices based on a fundamental misunderstanding of playback theatre's purpose. Such work can never meet the criteria for "good" playback, because it is built on distortion.


随着一人一故事剧场被世界各地的爱好者所采用，不可避免地有少数人在对一人一故事剧场的目的存在根本误解的基础上发展出了一些做法。这样的作品永远无法达到"好的"一人一故事的标准，因为它是建立在失真的基础上的。


One such misapplication is the performance of playback in a comedic, entertainment manner, with little skill in sociometry or subtle communication on the part of the conductor, and insufficient respect for the story on the part of the actors. These companies may enjoy success in the sense that their audiences are enthusiastic and they may be in demand for contracted performances. However, their work is not good playback: the deeper meaning of the stories is ignored or lost, the dynamics and themes of the event remain undeveloped, and the audience is entertained without being affirmed in their humanity.


其中一种误用是以喜剧、娱乐的方式表演一人一故事，领航员缺乏社会计量或精微沟通的技巧，演员对故事也不够尊重。这些剧团可能会获得成功，因为他们的观众很热情，他们可能会被要求签约演出。然而，他们的作品并不是好的一人一故事：故事的深层含义被忽视或丢失，活动的动力和主题仍未得到发展，观众得到了娱乐，却没有得到人性的肯定。


Another pitfall for playback performers, particularly conductors, is the mishandling of playback's therapeutic dimension. All playback theatre is broadly therapeutic in the sense that the process is potentially and ideally healing for all present, including the performers. Telling one's story publicly, seeing it reflected back, realizing that it is accepted and valued by others is a healing experience for the teller. For the audience, as well, there is an integration and affirmation that strengthens the connections between them. If the basic criteria for successful playback are met, this healing potential will be fulfilled. But some performers do not trust the story to carry its meaning on its own terms. Instead, with the goal of being more therapeutic, they force a "psychological" interpretation, usually with the effect of limiting instead of deepening the meaning. Actors may do this through an interpretive enactment of the scene, conductors through interpretive questions and comments during the interview. (Interpretation in the psychological sense means imposing one's therapeutic insight on the story; it is distinct from artistic interpretation, the filtering of the story through one's artistic sensibility, a process which is integral in playback.)


对于一人一故事的表演者，尤其是领航员来说，另一个陷阱是对一人一故事的治疗性处理不当。所有一人一故事剧场都具有广泛的治疗意义，即这一过程对包括表演者在内的所有在场者都具有潜在的理想疗效。公开讲述自己的故事，看到故事被反映出来，意识到故事被他人接受和重视，这对讲述者来说是一种治疗体验。对于观众来说，也是一种融合和肯定，加强了他们之间的联系。如果符合成功一人一故事的基本标准，这种治疗潜力就会得到发挥。但是，有些表演者并不相信故事本身能够承载其意义。相反，为了达到更好的治疗效果，他们会强行进行"心理"诠释，其结果通常是限制而非深化故事的意义。演员可以通过对场景的解释性表演，领航员可以通过访谈中的解释性问题和反馈来达到这一目的。(心理学意义上的诠释是指将自己的治疗意见强加于故事；它有别于艺术诠释，即通过一人的艺术感受力对故事进行过滤，这一过程在一人一故事中不可或缺）。


I once watched a conductor with a teller who told a brief but resonant story about how she survived her childhood by singing to herself whenever her family seemed too crazy. The conductor probed and delved until she elicited further information about the teller's birth and her present relationship to her family. In the enactment the teller's original story, rich with image and metaphor like a small, enigmatic painting, was buried in a rambling framework of psychological detail.


有一次，我观看了一位领航员与一位讲述者的谈话，她讲述了一个简短但却能引起共鸣的故事：每当她的家人显得过于疯狂时，她就自言自语地唱歌，以此来度过童年。这位领航员不断探究，直到引出更多关于讲述者的出生以及她现在与家人关系的信息。在表演过程中，讲述者原本的故事就像一幅神秘的小油画，具有丰富的形象和隐喻，但却被埋没在一个漫无边际的心理细节框架中。


A third danger area is the failure to understand playback's vigor as theatre and its roots in oral tradition, offering it instead as a quasi-religious New Age experience replete with candles and prayers, weak in cultural reference and artistic integrity.[2]


第三个危险领域是不理解一人一故事剧场作为戏剧的活力及其源于口述传统的根基，而是将其作为一种充斥着蜡烛和祈祷的准宗教式的新时代（New Age）体验，这缺乏文化关联和艺术性的真诚[2]。


## "Good Enough" Playback Theatre "足够好"的一人一故事剧场


In an essay called "The Face of the Story," Mimi Katzenbach describes playback's remarkable capacity to embody meaning simply in the enactment itself. If the story is heard and enacted with empathy and an intuitive grasp of its essences, then all its multiplicity of allusion to myth, history, and psychological truth will be present and it is unnecessary, even a detraction, to make it more explicit. Playback scenes are not superficial versions of stories told to therapists. The story's meaning is in the face that it shows us; what we see there is enough.


米米-卡岑巴赫（Mimi Katzenbach）在一篇名为《故事的面貌》（The Face of the Story）的文章中描述了一人一故事的非凡能力，即仅仅在表演本身就能体现意义。如果故事是在感同身受、直观把握其本质的情况下被聆听和演绎的，那么故事中所有与神话、历史和心理真相有关的多层次内容都会呈现出来，而将其更刻意更明显地呈现是没有必要，甚至是有损害的。一人一故事的场景并不是向治疗师讲述故事的肤浅版本。故事的意义在于它向我们展示它的面貌；我们看到的就是足够的。


Fortunately, the majority of playback performers are committed to its fundamental purposes. They understand the dual requirement for artistic and interpersonal sophistication; they are artists enough to respect the aesthetic qualities of story itself without distortion in the direction of psychological interpretation. And they achieve what we might call "good enough" playback, on the analogy of Bettelheim's idea of "good enough" parenting: playback that generally fulfills both the artistic and interactive criteria to an adequate degree despite inevitable shortcomings.


幸运的是，大多数一人一故事的表演者都致力于其基本宗旨。他们理解艺术性和人际关系复杂性的双重要求；他们是艺术家，足以尊重故事本身的美学特质，而不会在心理阐释方面出现扭曲。按照贝特尔海姆（Bettelheim）关于"足够好的"养育子女的观点，他们可以实现我们所说的"足够好的"一人一故事：尽管存在不可避免的缺陷，但一般来说，一人一故事能够充分满足艺术和互动的标准。


In striving to fulfill the work as best it can, every company faces choices about how collectively-oriented they want to be. What is more important to the group, the highest possible standards of performance or a democratic access to conducting, acting, and music? Do you leave the conducting in the hands of the most skilled, or do you commit to training everyone to conduct? Do you rotate roles during a performance as an expression of equality, in spite of a possible drop in standards? The guideline of "good enough" playback is the final determinant of how a group balances the sometimes conflicting demands of democracy and excellence.


在尽最大努力完成工作的过程中，每个剧团都面临着如何以集体为导向的选择。对团体而言，什么更重要，是尽可能高标准的表演，还是民主地参与领航、表演和音乐？是让最熟练的人负责领航，还是致力于培训每个人的领航能力？在演出过程中，你是为了体现平等而轮换角色，尽管可能会降低水准？一人一故事"足够好"的准则是一个团体如何平衡有时相互冲突的民主与卓越要求的最终决定因素。


The idea that "good enough" playback theatre depends on fulfilling the criteria for success both in art and in interactive social events has implications for training. Whether in companies or in workshops, new performers can be encouraged to develop an artist's sensibility—an aesthetic awareness of form and design, and a commitment to art's purpose of revealing meaning through inspired metaphor and image. They can also, from the beginning, learn about the need for sensitivity and skill in communication and in guiding the functioning of a group.


一人一故事剧场的"足够好"取决于在艺术和社会互动活动中都达到成功的标准，这一观点对培训工作具有启示意义。无论是在剧团还是在工作坊中，都可以鼓励新演员培养艺术家的感性——对形式和设计的审美意识，以及通过有创意的隐喻和图景来实现揭示意义的艺术目的。他们还可以从一开始就了解到，在交流和指导团体运作方面，需要有敏感性和技巧。


Many people come to playback with a background in one but not both of these areas— artists who are unsophisticated in human relationships, or helpers and healers who are undeveloped as artists. Training that emphasizes the importance of both sides can help people acquire the balance that is essential.


许多人在接受一人一故事培训时，只具备其中一个领域的背景，却没有同时具备这两个领域的背景——们是在人际关系方面不够成熟的艺术家，或者是助人者和治疗者但作为艺术家却尚未发展成熟。强调这两方面重要性的培训可以帮助人们获得必要的平衡。


## Trance, Subjectivity, and Judgment 催眠、主观和判断


The response to art and the perception of value is necessarily subjective. In playback, subjectivity of response has a special meaning, because of the marked difference in perspective between audience member, teller and performers.


对艺术的反应和对价值的感知必然是主观的。在一人一故事中，反应的主观性具有特殊的含义，因为观众、讲述者和表演者之间的视角明显不同。


For playback or any theatre to work, there must be an engagement on the part of the audience so that they allow themselves to believe in the illusion of another place and time, whether it is the Forest of Arden or the teller's childhood home. This is a kind of trance, willingly entered into by audience members. Part of the conductor's job is to entrance, to induct audience and teller into an altered state for the duration of the enactment (while remaining firmly out of trance herself). Even the actors are mildly in trance. While they remain fully aware of themselves and each other, thinking about the story and what needs to happen, they are also caught up in the reality conjured by their acting. The music is a powerful trance inducer, altering the state of the teller, audience, and actors from the first notes of the setting-up music.


一人一故事剧场或任何剧场要发挥作用，观众必须参与其中，进入一种幻觉，让自己相信自己进入了另一个时空，无论是亚顿森林还是讲述者童年的家。这是一种催眠状态，是观众自愿进入的。领航员的部分工作就是进入，在表演过程中让观众和讲述者进入一种不一样的状态（而她自己则坚定地保持在催眠状态之外）。就连演员也处于轻微的催眠状态。虽然他们仍然完全意识到自己和每个人，思考着故事和需要发生的事情，但他们也陷入了自己的表演所幻化的现实中。音乐是一种强大的催眠诱导剂，从铺垫音乐的第一个音符开始，就会改变讲述者、观众和演员的状态。


The stronger the trance, the stronger the perception of success and of artistic value. The trance is almost always strongest for the teller, and weakest for the performer. All playback performers have had the experience of a teller praising them warmly after a scene that they felt they had done poorly. Audience members may also let themselves believe in a story in spite of occasional mistakes or awkwardness in the enactment. (But if the performance is not "good enough" playback, the trance will not work and audience and teller will remain unmoved.)


催眠感越强烈，对成功和艺术价值的感知就越强烈。讲述者的催眠感几乎总是最强的，而表演者的催眠感最弱。一人一故事的表演者都有过这样的经历：在一场戏结束后，讲述者热情地称赞他们，而他们却觉得自己做得不好。尽管在表演过程中偶尔会出现错误或尴尬，观众也会让自己相信故事。(但如果演出不是"足够好"的一人一故事，催眠就不会起作用，观众和讲述者都会无动于衷）。


The peculiar strength of the teller's trance means that in non-performance situations, such as workshops, training groups, or therapy contexts, stories may be enacted with great success by people who lack skill or artistic sensibility—success in the sense that


讲述者催眠感的特殊力量意味着，在非演出情境下，如工作坊、培训小组或治疗情境中，缺乏技巧或艺术感觉的人也可以非常成功地表演故事。


the teller and other group members feel that the story has been fulfilled satisfactorily in spite of the inexpert acting. Leading children's therapy groups, I have seen tellers completely transfixed by enactments that were clumsy and superficial, just barely adhering to the story, even though the young actors were doing as well as they could.


成功的意义在于，讲述者和其他小组成员都觉得，尽管表演不专业，故事还是圆满完成了。在领导儿童治疗小组时，我曾见过讲述者完全被那些笨拙而肤浅的表演所惊呆，尽管小演员们已经尽其所能地表演得很好，但也只是勉强符合故事情节而已。


The teller would readily project his story onto the action and feel satisfied that it had been reflected fully.


讲述者很容易将自己的故事投射到表演中，并对故事得到充分反映感到满意。


Subjectivity of response also means that the perception of artistic merit will vary according to context and expectation, as we discussed earlier. I remember a two-hour workshop for recreation workers in which a man told a story about being taken as a child to see Hooverville, the shantytown of homeless people who lived in New York's Central Park during the Depression. The man's father hoped to teach his son to appreciate the privilege and security that he had. But what Victor, the teller, remembered was the moment when a boy his own age stared back at him, a burning challenge in his eyes. In a flash Victor understood this boy's human dignity, and that it was wrong to stare as though he were an animal in a zoo. In the enactment the man who played the homeless boy fulfilled the climactic moment with unforgettable intensity and truth. He was fully invested in the role and its meaning. An experienced actor could not have done better.


反应的主观性还意味着对艺术价值的认识会因背景和期望而异，正如我们之前所讨论的那样。我记得在一次为娱乐工作者举办的两个小时的工作坊上，一位男士讲述了他小时候被带去看胡佛村的故事，胡佛村是大萧条时期纽约中央公园里无家可归者的棚户区。这名男子的父亲希望教导儿子珍惜他所拥有的特权和安全感。但出纳员维克多记得，当时一个与他同龄的男孩回过头来盯着他，眼神中充满了挑衅。一瞬间，维克多明白了这个男孩的人格尊严，明白了把他当成动物园里的动物一样盯着是不对的。在表演中，扮演流浪男孩的男子以令人难忘的力度和真情演绎了这一高潮时刻。他全身心地投入到角色及其意义中。一个经验丰富的演员也不可能做得更好。


He was probably to some degree in trance, believing so deeply in the reality of the boy in Hooverville that his belief informed his portrayal. Another element in the impact of his acting was the context itself, a workshop in which no-one had previous experience with playback or any other kind of performing. I and my co-leader therefore had no expectation of skillful acting and were possibly more impressed than we might have been by someone in our own company doing the same thing.


他可能在某种程度上处于催眠状态，对胡佛镇男孩的真实情况深信不疑，这种信念贯穿了他的表演。他的表演产生影响的另一个因素是背景本身，在这个讲习班上，没有人有过一人一故事或任何其他表演的经验。因此，我和我的合作带领者对娴熟的演技不抱任何期望，而且可能比我们自己剧团的人做同样的事情给我们留下的印象更深刻。


This subjectivity and flexibility of standards is not unique to playback, but in fact a component in any realm of art. Context and expectation play a part in any audience's experience of any performance. There is always an interplay between what is actually offered by the artists, on the one hand, and what the audience brings to it, on the other. The same is true of non-performance art forms such as literature or painting. Value is ultimately a co-creation of artist and perceiver, which accounts for the genuine artistic meaning found at every point along the spectrum of actual accomplishment.


这种主观性和标准的灵活性并不是一人一故事所独有的，事实上任何艺术领域都存在。观众对任何表演的体验都离不开背景和期望。艺术家实际提供的东西与观众带来的东西之间总是存在相互作用。文学或绘画等非表演艺术形式也是如此。价值归根结底是艺术家和观众共同创造的，这也是在实际成就的每一点上都能发现真正艺术意义的原因。


## Playback's Fulfillment 一人一故事的实现


In my small town there lives a world-famous pianist who performs here once or twice a year. His most recent concert fell on the same night as one of our playback shows. I was very sorry to miss it: this man's music is a treasure, a consummation of the art of the keyboard. As my imagination went from his concert, an all-Bach solo recital, to our playback theatre show, the difference between these two kinds of performances was very clear. Much as I love the magic of playback's mercurial creativity, I also sometimes want the grandeur and perfection of art that is painstakingly developed and rehearsed.


在我所在的小镇上，住着一位世界著名的钢琴家，他每年都会在这里演出一两次。他最近的一场音乐会与我们的一人一故事演出在同一天晚上。我非常遗憾地错过了这场音乐会：他的音乐是珍宝，是键盘艺术的结晶。当我的想象力从他的音乐会——全巴赫独奏音乐会——转向我们的一人一故事剧场演出时，这两种演出之间的区别非常明显。虽然我很喜欢一人一故事的神奇水银泻地般的创造力，但有时我也想得到经过艰苦发展和排练的艺术的宏伟和完美。


Playback is not great art. It cannot meet art's most rigorous criteria, because it is of the moment. Although performers may develop their skills for years, becoming elegant and compelling both in their acting and in the aesthetic transformation of life into theatre, their on-the-spot enactments cannot, by their nature, be built of the highly crafted language and choreography of formal theatre. However, like other art which may not attain greatness, it nevertheless has the power, depending on context and audience perception, to fulfill art's deepest purpose: the revelation of pattern and ontological meaning.


一人一故事不是伟大的艺术。它无法满足艺术最严格的标准，因为它是当下的。尽管表演者可能会经过多年的技艺磨练，在演技和将生活转化为戏剧的审美方面都变得优雅而引人注目，但他们的现场表演就其本质而言，不可能建立在正式戏剧的精雕细琢的语言和编排之上。然而，就像其他可能无法达到伟大境界的艺术一样，它也有能力根据语境和观众的感知，实现艺术最深层的目的：揭示模式和本体的意义。


Those moments when playback reaches a peak of brilliance, those moments that we all recognize and remember, are invariably times when there has been a perfect fusion between the artistic and the interactive aspects of playback. They may happen in any setting, with inexperienced actors as well as with trained performers. In a non-performance setting, it can seem like a kind of miracle when the group members reach into themselves and discover an artist's sensibility for the first time. In my experience, this quantum leap is always called forth by a strength of emotion toward the teller and his story. It has nothing to do with a conscious striving; it is a response to humanity combined with the potency of the artist's reaching for form. The remarkable thing is that the newly-achieved level of artistry invariably creates a similar leap in the group's understanding of each other. It is a process of synergy in which the art and the group interaction mutually enhance each other.


一人一故事达到辉煌顶峰的时刻，那些我们都认识并记住的时刻，无一例外都是一人一故事的艺术性和互动性完美融合的时刻。这些时刻可能发生在任何场合，可能是经验不足的演员，也可能是训练有素的表演者。在非演出环境中，当团队成员深入自我，首次发现艺术家的感性时，这似乎是一种奇迹。根据我的经验，这种质的飞跃总是由对讲述者及其故事的强烈情感所激发。它与有意识的努力无关；它是对人性的一种回应，与艺术家追求形式的力量相结合。难能可贵的是，新达到的艺术水平必然会让小组成员对彼此的理解上产生类似的飞跃。这是一个艺术与群体互动相互促进的协同过程。


In performance, the conductor's attunement to the audience and to the teller combines with the actors' empathy, accuracy, inspiration, and teamwork to create theatre that can be matchless in its beauty and truth. An entire performance is unlikely to remain on such a soaring level; playback is also "Rough Theatre," in Peter Brook's phrase, with the inherent unevenness of any art whose creative process is unmasked and visible to all.


在表演中，领航员与观众和讲述者的默契配合，与演员的同理心、准确性、灵感和团队合作相结合，可以创造出无与伦比的美感和真实感。一人一故事剧场也是彼得-布鲁克所说的"粗糙的剧场"，它具有任何艺术与生俱来的粗粝感，其创作过程是不加掩饰的，所有人都能看到。


Playback theatre belongs rightfully in the realm of art; it seeks to fulfill art's fundamental purpose of revealing meaning through the aesthetic distillation of experience and perception. It is also integrally an interactive and altruistic social event. To be judged as "good," playback must fulfill the criteria for success in both these realms, as well as in those unique to playback itself.


一人一故事剧场理所当然地属于艺术的范畴；它试图通过对经验和感知的审美提炼来实现艺术揭示意义的根本目的。同时，它也是一种互动和利他主义的社交活动。要想被评为"好的"，一人一故事必须同时满足这两个领域的成功标准，以及一人一故事本身独有的标准。


The degree of skill required from the actors varies according to context. In performance situations, the performers must have sufficient artistry and experience to achieve "good enough" playback if they are to satisfy audience and teller. In non-performance contexts, playback can fulfill its purpose without a high degree of skill from the actors: stories may be told and enacted with satisfaction for the teller and the group as a whole in spite of a lack of experience and acting ability.


对演员技能的要求因环境而异。在表演情境中，表演者必须具备足够的艺术性和经验，才能实现"足够好"的一人一故事，才能让观众和讲述者满意。在非演出情境中，一人一故事的演绎不需要演员有很高的技巧也能达到目的：尽管缺乏经验和表演能力，故事的讲述和演绎也能让讲述者和整个团体感到满意。


In any context, however, the conductor's artistry and interpersonal skill is necessary to playback's fulfillment. The conductor's role is an embodiment of playback's fusion of art and social action, both within and outside of performance.


然而，在任何情况下，一人一故事的实现都离不开领航员的艺术修养和人际互动能力。无论在演出内还是演出外，领航员的角色都是一人一故事融合艺术与社会行动的体现。


And in any context, performance or otherwise, there is the possibility of those moments that show us the ideal fulfillment of playback's promise; the ephemeral, magical fusion of artistry and humanity.


而在任何情况下，无论是演出还是其他，都有可能出现那些让我们看到一人一故事理想实现的时刻；艺术性与人性短暂而神奇的融合。
