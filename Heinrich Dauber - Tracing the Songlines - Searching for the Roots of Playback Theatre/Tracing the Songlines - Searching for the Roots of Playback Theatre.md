# Tracing the Songlines: Searching for the Roots of Playback Theatre 追寻“歌之路”：一人一故事剧场溯源

Heinrich Dauber [1]

海因里希·道伯 [1]

“To exist is to be perceived.” [2] Bruce Chatwin

“存在即被感知。” [2] 布鲁斯·查特温

“For ages storytelling has been the best form of remembering, since it is simultaneously preserving and remembering.” [3] Harald Weinrich

“自古以来，讲故事一直是最理想的记忆形式，因为它既在保存，也在记忆。” [3] 哈拉尔德·韦因里希

Bruce Chatwin-brilliant storyteller, talented photographer, and nomad to the core-called the mythical maps that the Australian Aborigines create to orient their walkabouts through a vast continent of time and space “songlines.” In 1991, Chatwin died at 51 in Nice from fungus poisoning, which he had contracted earlier in China by consuming a 1000-year-old dove egg. For his entire life, Chatwin was a passionate “traveler,” both through his own memories and through distant continents and distant times. His stories are packed with internal and external drama and at the same time always reflect the archetypal themes of humanity’s search for beauty and meaning.

布鲁斯·查特文——一位才华横溢的讲故事者、杰出的摄影师，以及骨子里流淌着游牧精神的游子——将澳大利亚原住民为在广阔的时空大陆上进行漫游而绘制的传说中的地图称为“歌之路”。1991年，查特文因真菌中毒在尼斯去世，享年51岁。此前他在中国食用了一枚有1000年历史的鸽蛋，从而感染了真菌。查特文一生都是位充满激情的“旅行者”，既穿梭于自身的记忆之中，也游历于遥远的大陆与遥远的时光之间。他的故事既充满内在与外在的戏剧性，同时也始终映射出人类对美与意义的追寻这一原型主题。

Songlines exist, not only in Australia, but wherever supraindividual cultural patterns of time and space intersect with personal life-patterns. The search for the source of playback theatre can be understood as an attempt to describe the hidden personal and historical roots of playback theatre as songlines.

歌之路不仅存在于澳大利亚，更存在于任何超个体的时空文化模式与个人生活轨迹交汇之处。追溯一人一故事剧场的源头，可以理解为一种尝试，即把一人一故事剧场中隐藏的个人与历史根源描述为歌之路。

In the Western tradition one could also say songlines appear where Lares and Penates meet. Penates were the family gods in Etruscan and Roman mythology (therefore representing the forebears). Lares were the gods of the household (therefore representing the place or the environment).

在西方传统中，也可以说歌之路出现在拉尔斯（Lares）与佩纳特斯（Penates）交汇之处。佩纳特斯是伊特鲁里亚和罗马神话中的家族之神（因此代表祖先）。拉尔斯是家庭之神（因此代表场所或环境）。

From this perspective, playback theatre forms a cultural antithesis to the anonymous electronically manipulated information society. Playback theatre, as an intimate theatre of personal stories, creates new songlines, requires the intimate participation, face-to-face, between people. [4] As teller as well as audience member, as actor as well as conductor, we contribute to the creation of new songlines, in every performance and in the whole playback theatre movement. When and where they are created is not coincidental, however, just as there are no coincidences in our personal lives or in historical contexts.

从这一视角来看，一人一故事剧场构成了对匿名且受电子技术操控的信息社会的文化对立面。作为讲述个人故事的亲密戏剧，一人一故事剧场创造了新的“歌之路”，需要人们之间面对面、亲密的参与。[4]无论是作为讲述者还是观众，作为演员还是领航员，我们都在每一场演出以及整个一人一故事剧场运动中，为创造新的“歌之路”贡献力量。然而，它们的诞生时间与地点绝非偶然，正如在我们的个人生活或历史语境中，也不存在任何偶然。

The fact that this first academic symposium about playback theatre takes place in the (reunified) Germany, seems to me-without sounding nationally arrogant or presumptuous-no more coincidental. Viewed in this light, this symposium takes place in a double historical and cultural context.

这次关于一人一故事剧场的首届学术研讨会恰好在（统一后的）德国举行，在我看来——虽不愿显得民族自大或妄自尊大——这绝非偶然。从这一角度看，本次研讨会处于双重历史与文化语境之中。

According to the unanimous assessment of various Anglo-Saxon participants in the Symposium, such an academic Symposium could not take place in the USA nor in New Zealand/Australia, because there it would not be met with comparable scientific-theoretical interest within an open academic environment. Be that as it may, three departments at the University of Kassel, Education, Social Work and Economics, were prepared to finance such a Symposium from the start; in addition, the president showed his sustained support. (Perhaps this applies exclusively to the cultural environment at Kassel, since at other German Universities theatre pedagogy courses are as a rule being dismantled.) [5] Possibly, this also reflects a distinctly Teutonic approach to art and theatre- wanting to reassure oneself epistemologically of the foundation on which one’s own creative activity is actually based, and that creative expression can also be substantiated through theory, preferably with a dialectical argument in the form of a pyramid topped with a “fundamental contradiction.” [6]

根据多位参加研讨会的盎格鲁-撒克逊与会者的一致评价，这样的学术研讨会既无法在美国举行，也无法在新西兰或澳大利亚举行，因为在那里，开放的学术环境中不会对其产生同等程度的科学理论兴趣。无论如何，卡塞尔大学的教育学、社会工作学和经济学三个系从一开始就愿意为此次研讨会提供资金支持；此外，校长也表现出了持续的支持。（也许这仅适用于卡塞尔的文化环境，因为在德国其他大学，戏剧教育课程通常正在被裁撤。） [5]这或许也反映出一种鲜明的日耳曼式艺术与戏剧观——即希望在认识论层面上确信自身创作活动所立足的基础，并认为创造性表达也能通过理论加以论证，最好是以“根本矛盾”为顶点的金字塔式辩证论证。[6]

It is more important to recognize in this context that in the mid-nineties the German debate about the German (Nazi) past obviously changed. Various events contributed to this change in political culture: the diaries of author Victor Klemperer, composed in secret from 1933-45; Steven Spielberg’s film Schindler’s List; the debate, which was led not only in historical circles, but also in the wider public, on Daniel Goldhagen’s theories presented in his book Hitler’s Willing Executioners; but especially the exhibit presented by the Hamburg Institute for Social Research, “Crimes of the Wehrmacht;” as well as-last but not least-a remarkable debate in the Bundestag (German Parliament) about all these questions and topics. [7]

在此背景下，更重要的是要认识到，九十年代中期，德国关于德国（纳粹）过去的讨论显然发生了变化。各种事件促成了这种政治文化的转变：作家维克多·克莱普勒尔于1933至1945年间秘密撰写的日记；史蒂文·斯皮尔伯格的电影《辛德勒的名单》； 不仅在历史学界，而且在更广泛的公众中引发的关于丹尼尔·戈德哈根在其著作《希特勒的乐意刽子手》中所提出理论的讨论；尤其是汉堡社会研究所举办的“国防军的罪行”展览；以及——最后但同样重要的是——德国联邦议院（Bundestag）就所有这些问题和议题展开的一场引人注目的辩论。[7]

A representative of many Germans is the delegate of the SPD Party and Human Rights Speaker, Freimut Duve, quoted here out of the Bundestag minutes from the 163rd meeting on March 13, 1997:

德国社会民主党（SPD）代表兼人权事务发言人弗赖穆特·杜韦（Freimut Duve）是许多德国人的代表，以下引自1997年3月13日联邦议院第163次会议的会议记录：

> Last week, I was in the peculiar situation that I found the house in Osejek, in which my Jewish grandmother was picked up. I never thought-in the sixty years that I have lived-that I would ever speak to a woman who had seen this. We never thought that anyone still lived. I spoke with a woman. She described precisely how it had happened: under the protection of German soldiers. But it was Croatian Ustashas, who threw the old woman, who was disabled in her legs, into the truck. We do not know whether she died in Auschwitz or in another camp.
>
> 上周，我经历了一件不同寻常的事：我找到了位于奥塞耶克的那栋房子——我的犹太祖母当年就是在那儿被带走的。在我活过的这六十年中，我从未想过有朝一日会与一位亲历过此事的女性交谈。我们从未想过还有人活着。我与一位女士交谈了。她详细描述了当时的情况：是在德国士兵的“保护”下发生的。但将那位双腿残疾的老妇人扔进卡车的，却是克罗地亚的乌斯塔沙分子。我们不知道她是死在奥斯维辛集中营，还是死在其他集中营。

All of this has contributed to the fact that for some time now in Germany, after many years of making these topics taboo, the reception of stories about personal experience has grown-along with the willingness to tell them publicly. Similar cultural tendencies are shown in other countries, for example in South Africa with its Truth Commission. [8] It is no accident that in playback theatre, alongside the individual past, the collective past plays an important role. The site in Kassel in which this first academic symposium on playback took place is the renovated iron-foundry of the Henschel factory, which produced locomotive engines and tanks for the Nazis in the war; because of this factory, Kassel was firebombed by the allies. As a result, more than 40,000 people died in one night. This location is very symbolic, and many participants made sympathetic references towards it.

所有这些因素共同促成了这样一个事实：在德国，经过多年将这些话题视为禁忌之后，近年来，人们对个人经历故事的接受度日益提高，同时人们也越来越愿意公开讲述这些故事。其他国家也出现了类似的文化趋势，例如南非的“真相与和解委员会”。[8]在一人一故事剧场中，集体记忆与个人记忆并重，这绝非偶然。卡塞尔举办首届一人一故事剧场学术研讨会的场地，是经过翻新的亨舍尔工厂铸铁厂——该厂曾在战争期间为纳粹生产机车发动机和坦克；正因这座工厂的存在，卡塞尔曾遭到盟军的火攻。结果，一夜之间就有超过4万人丧生。这个地点极具象征意义，许多与会者都对此表示了同情。

In addition, there is another societal reason for the growing interest in personal stories. While individuals live socially more and more isolated lives, and community-building rituals have almost disappeared in modern industrialized society, a historically new interest in personally experienced stories has formed. This is the case for districts establishing history workshops, and for institutions such as nursing-homes, with their conversation-circles, as well as for larger institutions and wider political changes connected to the political and economic bonds which are bringing peoples together around the world. [9]

此外，人们对个人故事日益浓厚的兴趣还有另一个社会原因。当个体在社会中过着越来越孤立的生活，而社群建设的仪式在现代工业化社会中几乎消失殆尽时，一种对亲身经历的故事的历史性新兴趣便应运而生。无论是各区设立的历史工作坊，还是养老院等机构组织的“交谈圈”，乃至与全球政治经济纽带相关的更大规模机构及更广泛的政治变革，都体现了这一趋势。[9]

Should this assessment prove correct, around the end of this century a cultural wave of personal stories will approach us-who might be readier than the playback theatre movement? Is it prepared to take on this challenge and the moral, educational, political and artistic responsibility connected with it?

如果这一评估被证明是正确的，那么在本世纪末，一股个人故事的文化浪潮将向我们袭来——还有谁比一人一故事剧场运动更准备充分呢？它是否准备好迎接这一挑战，并承担与之相关的道德、教育、政治和艺术责任？

## Playback’s Roots 一人一故事剧场的根源

Songlines form where collective and individual life patterns intersect, in the “chatter every evening between Lares and Penates.” [10] In the search for playback theatre’s songlines, it seems interesting and worthwhile to get closer to Jonathan Fox and Jo Salas’s common history-on a long walk or over a glass of wine. I have discovered this much about songlines:

“歌之路”形成于集体与个体生活模式的交汇处，即“拉雷斯与佩纳特斯之间每晚的闲谈”之中。[10] 在追寻一人一故事剧场的歌之路时，若能借一次长途漫步或一杯葡萄酒之机，更深入地了解乔纳森·福克斯与乔·萨拉斯共同的历史，似乎既有趣又值得。关于歌之路，我已发现以下几点：

In the biographies of Jonathan Fox and Jo Salas, two contemporary movements of the seventies come together: on the one hand, the culture of political resistance, as expressed through “pedagogy of liberation” and the connected fight against the “culture of silence” (Freire); on the other, the search for alternatives to the consumer society of mass-produced goods and services, for a simpler, more “convivial” community-oriented lifestyle (IIlich). [11] Both of these movements greatly influenced the original development of playback theatre and its political and moral character. [12]

在乔纳森·福克斯和乔·萨拉斯的生平中，七十年代的两大当代思潮交汇在一起：一方面是政治抵抗文化，它通过“解放教育学”以及与之相关的反对“沉默文化”（弗莱雷）的斗争得以体现；另一方面则是对大规模生产商品与服务的消费社会的替代方案的探索，即寻求一种更简单、更“共生”且以社群为导向的生活方式 （伊利奇）。[11] 这两大运动都对“一人一故事剧场”的早期发展及其政治与道德特质产生了深远影响。[12]

Freire views the “teacher” and the “student” as partners in a dialogue about reality, the structure of which is reflected in the individual and collective consciousness. Fox extends this framework around the conductor, storyteller, actor, and audience member. The goal remains the same-a dialogue about individual experience, which leads to decoding (and changing) commonly experienced reality. In Freire’s concept this process is attempted through pedagogical means, while in playback theatre this is carried out more implicitly than explicitly.

弗莱雷将“教师”和“学生”视为关于现实的对话伙伴，而现实的结构则反映在个体和集体意识之中。福克斯则将这一框架扩展至领航员、故事讲述者、演员和观众。目标始终如一——围绕个人体验展开对话，从而解码（并改变）共同经历的现实。在弗莱雷的概念中，这一过程试图通过教育学手段实现；而在一人一故事剧场中，这一过程更多是以隐性而非显性的方式进行的。

Illich’s criticism refers to the modern context of alienation, in which experts and institutions decide which “needs” people have and how to satisfy them. Fox extends this criticism to include modern theatre. Playback theatre does not have a traditional “audience.” The members of the audience are invited to become tellers of their own experiences in their own words in a community-building ritual. [13]

伊利奇的批评指向了现代异化语境，在该语境中，专家和机构决定人们拥有哪些“需求”以及如何满足这些需求。福克斯将这一批评延伸至现代戏剧领域。一人一故事剧场没有传统的“观众”。观众被邀请在一种构建社群的仪式中，用自己的语言讲述自己的经历，成为讲述者。[13]

In the eyes of its founder playback does not wear the hat of the Californian egotrippers, with their culture of fun. Nor is it allied to the creative performance coaching method practiced in modern organizations, much less a “theatre sport,” in which groups spontaneously compete against each other.

在其创始人看来，一人一故事剧并不戴着加州“自我陶醉者”那顶以娱乐文化为特征的帽子。它也不与现代组织中实行的创意表演辅导方法相关联，更不是那种群体之间自发竞争的“戏剧运动”。

Playback theatre is neither therapeutic theatre nor social work. It is neither shamanic therapy nor theatre of the oppressed. Perhaps it is a bit of all of these. The original songlines of playback are made up of the conjunction of the Penates of Jonathan and Jo’s families with Lares from all over the world. They combined these to compose the first playback theatre songline-rhythm. Obviously, New Zealand’s Lares play a special part, not only because Jo Salas was born in New Zealand and Jonathan Fox met her there, but also because apparently in New Zealand (differently from in Australia) a special tradition has grown and been maintained, in which their own past as a colonial frontier state as well as that of the native Maori culture is treated with respect. Therefore, they continue to keep their collective memory alive, despite the fundamental differences in their cultural roots. [14]

一人一故事剧场既不是治疗性戏剧，也不是社会工作；既不是萨满疗法，也不是受压迫者剧场。也许它兼具了这些特质的某些方面。一人一故事剧场最初的“歌之路”由乔纳森和乔两家中的“家神”（Penates）与来自世界各地的“拉尔斯”（Lares）交汇而成。他们将这些元素融合，谱写出了第一段一人一故事剧场“歌之路”节奏。显然，新西兰的“拉雷斯”扮演着特殊的角色，这不仅因为乔·萨拉斯出生于新西兰，乔纳森·福克斯也是在那里与她相遇，还因为显然在新西兰（与澳大利亚不同），一种特殊的传统已经形成并得以延续，即对作为殖民边疆国家的自身历史以及毛利原住民文化都怀有敬意。因此，尽管他们的文化根源存在根本差异，他们仍持续维系着集体记忆。[14]

That playback theatre-especially in the USA and Germany-is connected with psychodrama, is evidenced by Jonathan Fox’s professional background (he edited Moreno’s collected writings), and playback’s publications and reviews, several of which have appeared in psychodrama publications. [15] Jonathan Fox and Jo Salas certainly bore the stamp of their personal histories (New York, Nepal, New Zealand) and education [16] before they became involved in therapeutic practice (Jonathan for a brief period, Jo over many years as a music therapist).

一人一故事剧场——尤其是在美国和德国——与心理剧存在关联，这一点可从乔纳森·福克斯（Jonathan Fox）的专业背景（他曾编辑过莫雷诺的文集）以及一人一故事剧场的相关出版物和评论中得到印证，其中部分内容曾发表于心理剧刊物上。[15]乔纳森·福克斯和乔·萨拉斯（Jo Salas）无疑都打上了他们个人经历（纽约、尼泊尔、新西兰）以及受教育经历[16]，这些都深刻地烙印在他们身上，随后他们才投身于治疗实践（乔纳森仅短暂从事过，而乔则作为音乐治疗师工作了多年）。

Intellectually, from the perspective of collective songlines within a European/North American context, playback theatre stems (like psychodrama, gestalt therapy and theme-centered interaction) from the magic triangle of Vienna-Berlin-Frankfurt, which produced countless social, cultural, political and philosophical innovations during the period lasting from the turn of the century to the beginning of the Nazi terror. The representatives of this ferment-Jacob Levy Moreno, Fritz Perls, Martin Buber, Kurt Lewin, Erich Fromm, and Ruth Cohn-only to name a few, valued each other highly and learned from each other. As Jews, socialists, and radical humanists, they were forced to emigrate. Subsequently, in Germany their ideas and political-artistic-pedagogical concepts were buried in oblivion for almost fifty years, until the late seventies, when some were reimported from America, most commonly in the form of therapeutic methods. [17]

从思想层面来看，以欧洲/北美语境中的集体“歌之线”为视角，一人一故事剧场（与心理剧、格式塔疗法及主题中心互动疗法一样）源于维也纳-柏林-法兰克福的“魔法三角”——这一地区在世纪之交至纳粹恐怖统治开始的这段时期内，孕育了无数社会、文化、政治和哲学上的创新。这一思想激荡时期的代表人物——雅各布·莱维·莫雷诺、弗里茨·珀尔斯、马丁·布伯、库尔特·勒温、埃里希·弗洛姆和露丝·科恩（仅举几例）——彼此高度推崇，并相互学习。作为犹太人、社会主义者和激进的人道主义者，他们被迫流亡。此后，在德国，他们的思想以及政治、艺术和教育理念被埋没在遗忘之中近五十年，直到七十年代末，其中一些才从美国重新引入，最常见的形式是治疗性方法。[17]

As part of the generation that was born during the war, it took me many years to fathom the wealth of people and ideas which were irretrievably lost from Germany and Europe as a result of the Nazis’ criminal policies. I have often mourned this deeply. [18] I wonder if the Jewish heritage of Jonathan Fox and Jo Salas (as well as other speakers and participants at the Symposium in Kassel) is meaningful in the search for playback theatre’s songlines. Besides the central question in the Jewish religion and philosophy, “How can we counteract forgetting?” it seems to me that an internationally cosmopolitan education which was nourished by European Jewish heritage, and which according to Hannah Arendt, grew out of a “position outside-any-societal-restraints” led to a fully nonprejudiced behavior. [19]

作为战争期间出生的一代人，我花了许多年才真正领悟到，由于纳粹的罪恶政策，德国和欧洲不可挽回地失去了多少人才和思想财富。我对此常常深感痛惜。[18]我想知道，乔纳森·福克斯和乔·萨拉斯（以及卡塞尔研讨会的其他演讲者和参与者）所传承的犹太文化遗产，在探索一人一故事剧场的“歌之线”时是否具有重要意义。除了犹太宗教和哲学中的核心问题——“我们如何对抗遗忘？”——之外，在我看来，那种由欧洲犹太传统滋养的、具有国际大都会色彩的教育——据汉娜·阿伦特所言，这种教育源于“超脱任何社会束缚的立场”——最终催生了完全无偏见的行为。[19]

Jonathan Fox was obviously always fascinated by this Berlin-Vienna-Frankfurt magical triangle of the twenties and thirties. (Perhaps that is where his first interest in Moreno arose.) In 1920 in Schriften des Vaters (later, Words of the Father), Moreno anonymously published “Die Reihe der reinen Örter I” [The Series of Pure Places], a text on Stegreiftheater, with the subtitle, “Invitation to an Encounter.” [20]

乔纳森·福克斯显然一直对二十世纪二三十年代的柏林-维也纳-法兰克福这一“魔幻三角”着迷。（也许正是由此，他才对莫雷诺产生了最初的兴趣。）1920年，莫雷诺在《父亲的著作》（Schriften des Vaters，后更名为《父亲的话》）中匿名发表了《纯粹之地的系列 I》（Die Reihe der reinen Örter I）[The Series of Pure Places]，这是一篇关于即兴戏剧（Stegreiftheater）的文本，副标题为“相遇的邀请”。[20]

Nevertheless, Fox-similar to Paul Goodman amongst others (myself included)-is not from the direct lineage of this generation, but perhaps in Goethe’s sense of Wahlverwandtschaften [literally ‘chosen relations’], he is a “grand-nephew by choice.” It remains for future research [21] to document the development of this partly underground, partly purposely buried tradition. [22] Fox himself showed in a historical essay about Moreno’s Stegreiftheater in New York, how this tradition, at least indirectly, was influenced by the post-revolutionary Russian theatre movement called the “Blue Blouses.” Such seeds, via the intermedial station of the Living Newspaper, became psychodrama.

尽管如此，福克斯——与保罗·古德曼等人（包括我本人）相似——并非这一代人的直接传人，但或许按照歌德所说的“Wahlverwandtschaften”（字面意思为“选择的亲缘关系”），他是一位“选择的侄孙”。关于这一部分处于地下、部分被有意埋没的传统的发展历程，仍有待未来的研究[21]来考证。[22]福克斯本人在一篇关于莫雷诺在纽约的“即兴剧场”（Stegreiftheater）的历史论文中指出，这一传统至少间接地受到了被称为“蓝衬衫”的俄罗斯革命后戏剧运动的影响。这些种子通过“活报剧团”这一媒介中转站，最终发展成了心理剧。

In Europe, especially in Germany, this tradition continued after the Second World War with Hilarion Petzold, and among others, the Russian in Paris Vladamir Iljine, a psychotherapist and psychoanalyst from the school of the Hungarian psychoanalyst Ferenczi. Petzold, like Fox, was also trained by the Morenos and later founded the so-called “Integrated Therapy.” [23]

在欧洲，尤其是德国，这一传统在第二次世界大战后由希拉里昂·佩佐尔德（Hilarion Petzold）等人延续下来，其中包括旅居巴黎的俄罗斯人弗拉基米尔·伊利因（Vladimir Iljine）——他是一位心理治疗师和精神分析师，师从匈牙利精神分析师费伦茨（Ferenczi）。佩佐尔德与福克斯一样，也曾接受过莫雷诺夫妇的培训，后来创立了所谓的“整合疗法”[23]。

These few examples only outline the larger context in which playback theatre was “invented” by Jonathan Fox. At a glance it is clear how inappropriate it would be to define playback-or other recent forms of this tradition, such as gestalt pedagogy-as directly related to forms of therapy (in this case, to psychodrama). Whoever is active in this scene today, is culturally, and often also through personal experience, anchored in a broad and varied tradition.

这些例子仅概述了乔纳森·福克斯“发明”一人一故事剧场的更广泛背景。不难看出，若将一人一故事剧场——或该传统中的其他新近形式（如格式塔教育学）——直接定义为与某种治疗形式（在此指心理剧）相关，是多么不恰当。当今活跃于这一领域的任何人，无论从文化层面，还是往往通过个人经历，都植根于这一广泛而多元的传统之中。

## The Historical Challenge 历史的挑战

Right at the beginning of the first meeting of German-speaking playback theatre groups in Bad Bevensen in 1996, a woman from Berlin told the following story. While visiting an exhibit in the newly opened Centrum Judaicum in Berlin, she stood in front of a photo of a little girl in the window of a house, which, before the war, was located across from the museum. The house was now gone. Only the photo of this (Jewish) child remained. While she reflected on what had happened to the girl, an older American visitor entered the room, looked around and went straight towards the photo. Without explaining himself further, he gestured to the photo and said quietly, “I knew her.”

1996年，在巴德·贝文森举行的首届德语区一人一故事剧场团体会议伊始，一位来自柏林的女士讲述了以下故事。她参观柏林新落成的犹太文化中心（Centrum Judaicum）时，站在一张照片前——照片中，一个小女孩正站在一栋房子的窗前，这栋房子在战前就位于博物馆对面。如今，那栋房子已不复存在。唯余这张（犹太）女童的照片留存至今。正当她沉思着这个女孩的命运时，一位年长的美国游客走进展厅，环顾四周后径直走向那张照片。他未作任何解释，只是指向照片轻声说道：“我认识她。”

Everyone present was deeply moved by this story. One of the first comments about it was, “How can we recover the cultural heritage which we have lost? Could it be that the spread of playback theatre in Germany is a part of our own tradition returning back?”

在场所有人都被这个故事深深打动。关于此事的最初评论之一是：“我们该如何找回已经失去的文化遗产？难道一人一故事剧场在德国的传播，正是我们自身传统回归的一部分？”

This brings up two corresponding questions, which are as speculative as they are fascinating: What would have become of today’s Europe, if these traditions had not been lost almost two generations ago? And what would have become of America without the influence of the scientists, artists, and authors who fled Europe?

这引发了两个相互关联的问题，它们既充满推测性，又引人入胜：如果这些传统在近两代人之前没有失传，今天的欧洲会变成什么样？如果没有那些逃离欧洲的科学家、艺术家和作家的影响，美国又会变成什么样？

The history of Germany, Europe, and America cannot be undone. Nevertheless, precisely out of this reciprocal, multifaceted, interconnected heritage grows the historical challenge: for the German-speaking area, to become conscious of our own cultural roots and to reclaim the lost humanitarian traditions; for the USA, to critically account for the foundation of its own cultural modernization.

德国、欧洲和美国的历史无法重来。然而，正是在这种相互影响、多面且相互关联的遗产基础上，历史性的挑战应运而生：对德语区而言，是要觉察自身的文化根源并重拾失落的人道主义传统；对美国而言，则是要批判性地审视其文化现代化的根基。

In addition, there is a third challenge, not just valid for Europe and the USA, but a truly global challenge: to preserve humanity’s common cultural heritage, which does not only consist of worldwide information networks. This applies especially to the linkage with pre-electronic ‘songlines’ and pre-literary forms of collective cultural self-reflection. Only when we are able to know and express where we come from will we be able to understand where we need to go. Museums, history exhibits, and theatre and concert performances could contribute much to the formation of such a historical consciousness. The stories told in playback theatre are the most alive form of such individual and collective self-reflection that I know: Playback mobilizes the whole person, and allows one to form thoughts, feelings, and impressions in a bodily expression that connects individual and collective experiences in a unique way.

此外，还有第三个挑战，它不仅适用于欧洲和美国，更是一个真正的全球性挑战：保护人类共同的文化遗产，而这种遗产不仅仅由全球信息网络构成。这尤其涉及与电子时代之前的“歌之路”以及文字出现前集体文化自我反思形式的联系。只有当我们能够了解并表达自己的来处，才能明白自己该往何处去。博物馆、历史展览以及戏剧和音乐会演出，都能为这种历史意识的形成做出巨大贡献。在我所知的此类个体与集体自我反思形式中，一人一故事剧场所讲述的故事最为鲜活：一人一故事剧场调动人的全身心，让人通过身体表达来形成思想、情感和印象，并以独特的方式将个体与集体体验联系起来。

What Bruce Chatwin reported about the songs of the Australian Aborigines can also apply to the stories told in playback theatre: “A man’s verses were his title deeds to territory. He could lend them to others. He could borrow other verses in return. The one thing he couldn’t do was sell or get rid of them.” [24]

布鲁斯·查特温（Bruce Chatwin）关于澳大利亚原住民歌曲的描述，同样适用于一人一故事剧场中讲述的故事：“一个人的诗句就是他对领地的产权凭证。他可以把它们借给别人，也可以借来别人的诗句作为回报。唯独有一件事他不能做，那就是出售或抛弃它们。”[24]

[21] 未来学家罗伯特·容克在其自传中，记述了母亲在二十年代于柏林的活动： 她与卡巴莱艺术家兼社会评论家查理·罗林霍夫（Charly Röllinghof）在库尔弗斯特大道的“柏林第一家即兴剧场”共同创立了该剧团。这项工作与她当时略显丰腴的身材可谓天作之合。因为在那里，她无需背诵和朗诵他人撰写的冗长台词，而是可以随心所欲地表达自己的想法。这种对古老戏剧形式“即兴喜剧”（Commedia Dell’Arte）的复兴，立刻吸引了热爱创新的柏林人。那句口号“每晚都是一场首演”，与“主题由你决定”的挑战同样吸引了观众。事实上，这五六位聚在一起承担这一风险的喜剧演员，在演出前从未知道这次的主题会是什么。帷幕一拉开，就会有观众喊出某个时事热点或人名，表演便就此展开。我只清楚记得其中一场即兴表演。当晚，我母亲饰演了一位果敢的五十岁女性，她是“铁腕古斯塔夫”的妻子——古斯塔夫当时是柏林最受欢迎的人物之一。当时，他正乘坐那辆老式马车从施普雷河畔前往塞纳河畔，以此表达和平与理解的意愿。由于交通工具过于陈旧，这段旅程耗时异常之久；更何况，这位英雄途经的每个城镇，人们都会用大量白兰地、啤酒和开胃酒来款待他，以至于他常常难以找到回马车的路。当古斯塔夫醉酒沉睡时，他的妻子却被冷落在柏林，时而咒骂，时而因思念而晕厥，时而绝望，时而歌唱，时而大发雷霆。妈妈在这方面非常在行，我看到了她完全不同的一面。但几周后，这种即兴表演被证明过于耗费精力。此外，柏林人正在追寻新的刺激，‘即兴剧场’的观众也越来越少。” 罗伯特·容克，《尽管如此——我为未来而活》（慕尼黑：克瑙尔出版社，1994年），第56页及后续。
