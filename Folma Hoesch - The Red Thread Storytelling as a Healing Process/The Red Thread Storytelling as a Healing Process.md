# The Red Thread: Storytelling as a Healing Process 红线：作为一种疗愈过程的讲述

Folma Hoesch [1]

福尔玛·霍施

Playback theatre has a deep effect on people who tell their stories and on groups in which stories are told and played back. Although it is not psychotherapy, I would like to say that it is healing. [2]

一人一故事剧场对讲述故事的人以及在其中讲述和重现故事的群体都产生了深远的影响。尽管它并非心理治疗，但我仍想说它具有治愈作用。[2]

Playback theatre is a new approach to theatre, built on the tradition of oral culture. There is no written text, nobody knows beforehand what is going to be told and enacted.

一人一故事剧场是一种建立在口述文化传统基础上的新型戏剧形式。它没有书面剧本，没有人事先知道将要讲述和演绎的内容。

Playback theatre has a certain form. It is a vessel in which something can come into existence. Although the content is unknown beforehand, a sense of order rather than chaos prevails. Each time something unique evolves, there is a process. One can see the meaning of it clearly, at least by looking back on it. In a short time, a group is created whether people knew each other before or not-a group with its own theme, with a red thread. [3] There is a clear path rather than a labyrinth; there is a definite way in, a center and a way out.

一人一故事剧场具有特定的形式。它是一个容器，其中可以孕育出某种事物。尽管内容事先未知，但其中弥漫的是一种秩序感，而非混乱。每次都会演变出独一无二的事物，这是一个过程。人们可以清晰地看到其中的意义，至少在回顾时是如此。在短时间内，无论参与者此前是否相识，都会形成一个团体——一个拥有自身主题、贯穿始终的线索的团体。[3]这里有一条清晰的道路，而非迷宫；有一个明确的入口、一个中心，以及一条出口。

At the beginning of a playback event we face the unknown. We feel both openness and anxiety. We trust that once again something will come into being that has a gestalt and a meaning, through which community will be created.

在一人一故事活动的开端，我们面对着未知。我们既感到开放，又感到焦虑。我们相信，又将诞生某种具有整体形态和意义的事物，借此建立起共同体。

Usually the people who discuss and write about playback theatre actually do it, as actors, conductors, musicians. In this essay my perspective is that of the spectators and the tellers of stories.

通常，那些讨论和撰写关于一人一故事剧场的人，实际上也参与其中，担任演员、领航员或乐师。在这篇文章中，我的视角是作为观众和故事讲述者的视角。

From this point of view a series of questions arise: why does a person tell a story? How does someone come to be a teller? To whom am I telling my story? Am I telling it to the conductor, who invited me and others to tell? Am I talking to the other spectators or seminar participants? Do I want to hear myself telling? Do I want to see what I tell? Which story am I going to present? And how much is this related to my personal history, how much does it have to do with the group in which the story is told? When in the group’s process do which stories get told? These are many questions. I cannot answer them all, but I feel challenged to ask them and think about them and express my thoughts in writing. I am interested especially in one aspect: how is a story connected with the stories that were told beforehand?

从这一视角出发，一系列问题随之浮现：一个人为何要讲述故事？一个人是如何成为讲述者的？我是在向谁讲述我的故事？我是在向那位邀请我和其他人讲述的领航员讲述吗？我是在与其他观众或工作坊参与者对话吗？我是否想听自己讲述？我是否想看到自己讲述的内容？我将呈现哪个故事？这与我的个人经历有多大关系？又与讲述故事的群体有多大关联？在群体的互动过程中，哪些故事会在何时被讲述出来？问题很多。我无法一一作答，但提出这些问题、思考它们并以文字表达我的想法，对我而言是一种挑战。我特别关注一个方面：一个故事是如何与之前讲述过的故事相联系的？

During playback performances or among groups who do playback we find again and again that the stories respond to each other and furthermore, that they offer patterns for solution and transformation. To me this means that something is happening that is doing good and can be healing both for the individual member and for the group as a whole.

在一人一故事表演中，或者在进行一人一故事的群体中，我们一次又一次地发现，这些故事彼此呼应，而且它们还提供了解决和转化的模式。对我来说，这意味着正在发生一些有益的事情，既能治愈个体成员，也能治愈整个群体。

To get to know more about this phenomenon I chose a very pragmatic approach: I will retell stories that were told in a two-week seminar which I attended, and think about the connections between them. My main interest will be in the motifs of the stories. Which elements, motifs, figures from a story come up in a later one? How do they connect? From these observations, I will build a hypothesis about how stories come into being, how they are generated from each other, and further, how we can look at the life of a group and understand the relation between the individual and the whole.

为了更深入地了解这一现象，我选择了一种非常务实的做法：我将重述我在为期两周的工作坊上听到的故事，并思考它们之间的联系。 我主要关注故事中的主题。一个故事中的哪些元素、主题或人物会在后续的故事中出现？它们之间是如何关联的？基于这些观察，我将提出一个假设，探讨故事是如何产生的，它们如何相互衍生，以及我们如何通过观察群体的生命历程来理解个体与整体之间的关系。

## Two Women Tell a Story 两位女士讲的一个故事

On the morning of her 55th birthday, Anna was sitting in a seminar with Jonathan Fox near Bern in Switzerland. She had decided not to tell anybody about her birthday because she still felt a little unconnected to this group. Erna, a young person sitting next to her, seemed to be in a depressed mood. When Anna asked her how she was doing she said that her father had died the day before. She had found out only early that morning, since her contact with her family was not very good. She did not want to go see her mother now, but rather to take part in the seminar as circumspectly as possible, without saying anything about it. This coincidence of birth(day) and death touched both women deeply. In the following go-round, Erna decided to speak about the death of her father, and Anna mentioned her birthday. Both got a lot of sympathy, congratulations, and little signs of thoughtfulness during the day.

在55岁生日的当天上午，安娜正坐在瑞士伯尔尼附近的一个工作坊上，与乔纳森·福克斯一起。她决定不告诉任何人自己的生日，因为她仍然觉得自己与这个群体有些格格不入。坐在她旁边的年轻女孩埃尔娜，看起来情绪有些低落。当安娜问她近况如何时，她回答说父亲前一天去世了。由于与家人的联系并不密切，她直到当天清晨才得知这一消息。她现在不想去见母亲，而是希望尽可能低调地参加工作坊，对此事只字不提。生（日）与死的这一巧合深深触动了两位女性。在随后的发言环节中，埃尔娜决定谈谈父亲的离世，而安娜则提到了自己的生日。当天，两人都收获了许多同情、祝贺，以及一些细微的体贴之举。

In the evening of this day Jonathan offered Erna the chance to have the group play something back for her. She told the following story: *Yesterday I brought my sandwich for lunch as usual because I did not want to go with the others to the restaurant, which was loud and expensive. So I walked by myself on a sunny path, which led me unexpectedly to a cemetery. After having lunch there I walked between the graves and read the inscriptions on the tombstones. I was amazed how many times they said something about thankful children and loving parents. Late that night I came home. My answering machine was blinking, one of my sisters wanted to talk to me urgently. Although it was very late, I called, but when she didn’t pick up the phone right away I gave up. The next morning I called my sister again, but her phone was busy. So I called my mother. She was crying, unable to talk. Now I knew what had happened: my father had died. But I didn’t want to go home and I decided to come to the seminar even if this was very egoistic.*

当天晚上，乔纳森提议让大家为埃尔娜做一段演绎。她讲述了以下故事：*昨天我像往常一样带了三明治当午餐，因为不想和大家一起去那家喧闹又昂贵的餐厅。于是我独自沿着一条阳光明媚的小路走着，没想到竟走到了一个墓地。在那里吃完午餐后，我穿行在墓碑之间，读着墓碑上的铭文。我惊讶地发现，有多少墓志铭提到了感恩的子女和慈爱的父母。深夜我才回到家。答录机指示灯在闪烁，是我其中一位姐姐急着要找我。虽然已经很晚了，我还是打了过去，但她没立刻接电话，我就放弃了。第二天早上我又给姐姐打电话，但她的电话一直占线。于是我给母亲打了电话。她正在哭，说不出话来。这时我才明白发生了什么：父亲去世了。但我不想回家，决定还是来参加工作坊，哪怕这很自私。*

The three scenes were played back to Erna. After this Jonathan asked her whether she wanted to see something else. And now she wondered about how her father died because she did not know. And she generated a fantasy about her father’s last moments: She assumed that amidst the turmoil of her mother’s loud emotional outbursts, her father had died alone and unobserved, almost happy to leave all the trouble behind.

我们为埃尔娜演了三个场景。之后，乔纳森问她是否还想看点别的。这时，她开始好奇父亲是怎样去世的，因为她对此一无所知。于是，她脑海中浮现出关于父亲生命最后时刻的想象：她推测，在母亲情绪失控、大声咆哮的混乱中，父亲是独自一人、无人察觉地离世的，甚至几乎为能摆脱这一切麻烦而感到欣慰。

This scene was also enacted. Erna was thankful and sat down again. The group and especially Anna were deeply touched by Erna’s story and her father’s lonely death. Still immersed in these images and feelings, Anna was very surprised when Jonathan invited her to tell a story, too, as a birthday present and as closure for the day. Without any idea what to tell she sat in the teller’s chair. In order to help her, Jonathan asked about her at progressively younger ages, to help her find a story. In Anna’s head was the notion of birthdays, and she thought about the different birthday parties that her mother had created for her during her childhood. She recalled photographs of these parties, which she kept in an album. But all this somehow didn’t feel important enough, it seemed empty and didn’t touch her. She even felt a slight aversion to bringing her mother into the picture. All of a sudden it became clear what she wanted to tell. A feeling of warmth went through her whole body, the stream of images stopped, and she felt an opening in her heart.

这一场景也被演绎了出来。埃尔娜心怀感激，又坐了下来。小组成员，尤其是安娜，都被埃尔娜的故事以及她父亲那孤独的离世深深打动了。安娜仍沉浸在这些画面和情感中，当乔纳森邀请她也讲一个故事——作为生日礼物，也为这一天画上句号——时，她大感惊讶。她完全不知道该讲什么，便坐到了讲述者的椅子上。为了帮她，乔纳森问起她越来越年幼时的往事，希望能帮她找到一个故事。安娜脑海中浮现出“生日”这个概念，她想起了童年时期母亲为她筹办的各种生日派对。她回忆起那些派对的照片，这些照片都保存在相册里。但这一切不知为何总觉得不够重要，显得空洞，也未能触动她的心弦。她甚至感到一丝抗拒将母亲纳入故事的念头。突然间，她清楚地知道自己想讲述什么了。一股暖流贯穿全身，脑海中的画面流淌戛然而止，她感到心扉豁然敞开。

Anna recounted: *For the past few years l’ve kept a photo by my bedside of me at six months old with my father. It is the only existing photo of my father and me, because a few weeks later he was called in as a soldier, was later taken as prisoner of war, and went missing in Russia. I would like to see the scene when the photo was taken: my father is sitting on the grass in our garden with his baby on his lap. Both are smiling. A few meters in front of them my mother is kneeling down with the camera. We are a happy family, at least in this moment. Far away, in the background, the war is already present.*

安娜回忆道：*“过去几年里，我一直在床头放着一张照片，是我六个月大时和父亲的合影。这是现存唯一一张我和父亲的照片，因为几周后父亲就被征召入伍，后来成为战俘，并在俄罗斯失踪了。 我想看看这张照片拍摄时的场景：父亲坐在我们花园的草地上，怀里抱着婴儿。两人都在微笑。他们前方几米处，母亲正跪着举着相机。我们是一个幸福的家庭，至少在这一刻是如此。而在远处的背景中，战争的阴影已经悄然降临。”*

Until this evening Anna had never realized that the photo of her and her father must have been taken by her mother, that her mother is present though invisible. For Anna, the enactment of her story was the most beautiful birthday present she could think of.

直到今晚，安娜才意识到，那张她和父亲的照片一定是由母亲拍摄的，母亲虽然看不见，却真实地存在于画面中。对安娜来说，这个故事的呈现是她能想到的最美好的生日礼物。

Looking at the two stories I realized only later that there is a close connection between Anna’s story and Erna’s story. Erna’s story is about being alone and being together, belonging and not belonging, pulling back because there is no peaceful security in her family. She talks about it in several variations: her lunch by herself, apart from the group, the difficulties in getting in touch with her sister, the impossibility of talking to her mother, the fantasy about her father’s lonely death in the midst of the family. At the edge of the story, the motif of love between parents and children appears in the tombstone inscriptions-which are surprising and even strange to Erna.

审视这两个故事后，我后来才意识到安娜的故事与埃尔娜的故事之间有着密切的联系。埃尔娜的故事关乎孤独与相伴、归属与疏离，以及因家庭中缺乏安宁与安全感而选择退缩。 她从多个角度谈及这一点：独自一人、远离群体的午餐；与姐姐联系的困难；无法与母亲交谈；以及关于父亲在家人环绕中孤独离世的幻想。在故事的边缘，父母与子女之间的爱这一主题出现在墓碑铭文中——这对埃尔娜来说既令人惊讶，甚至有些奇怪。

In Anna’s life, love between parents and child and an intact family could not exist because the war destroyed everything. In her experience of family there was no father, so she wouldn’t consciously have sought out a father-daughter scene. Nevertheless, the photo came to her mind, and she chose precisely the only scene in which father, mother and daughter were present and deeply connected.

在安娜的生命中，亲子之爱和完整的家庭本就不可能存在，因为战争摧毁了一切。在她对家庭的体验中没有父亲，所以她也不会刻意去寻找父女相处的场景。尽管如此，那张照片还是浮现在她脑海中，而她选中的恰恰是唯一一张父亲、母亲和女儿齐聚一堂、情感深厚相连的场景。

I have told this story, of which I know the interior perspective and the preceding history, in order to use it as an example. This background knowledge is rare in playback theatre, because the stories are not commented on, analyzed, or worked out therapeutically.

我讲述这个故事——我了解其中的内在视角和前因后果——是为了将其作为范例。这种背景知识在一人一故事剧场中很少见，因为这些故事不会被评论、分析或进行治疗性探讨。

In summary of this sequence I want to underline the following aspects. The second story responds to the first. Nobody is consciously looking for such a connection; the tellers themselves are totally unaware of it. Some motifs appear on several levels. The feeling of not belonging comes up already at the beginning of the day, when both women plan not to say anything about the special experiences they are going through. It leads like a red thread through Erna’s story and finds a counterpoint in the story of Anna. This element, the feeling of belonging together, already arose in the tombstone inscriptions, holding surprise and a slight shock for the teller.

总结这一环节时，我想强调以下几个方面。第二个故事呼应了第一个。没有人有意识地在寻找这种联系；讲述者自己对此也完全不知情。某些主题在多个层面反复出现。那种“不属于这里”的感觉早在当天一开始就显现出来——当时两位女性都计划对各自正在经历的特殊体验保持沉默。这种感觉如同红线般贯穿于埃尔娜的故事中，并在安娜的故事中找到了呼应。这一元素——即“彼此归属”的感觉——早在墓碑铭文环节就已浮现，给讲述者带来了惊喜和些许震撼。

There is a level of feeling, thinking and telling with which the tellers identify. They think, “I had better not say anything because I don’t really belong to these people around me.” And there is another level with which they both do not identify-safety, love and togetherness.

讲述者会认同某种层面的感受、思考和表达方式。他们会想：“我最好什么都别说，因为我其实并不真正属于身边的这些人。”而还有另一种层面，他们都不认同——那就是安全感、爱与归属感。

Here I am drawing on Arnold Mindell’s model of process-oriented psychology to describe these two sides of the story telling process. [4] Mindell describes stream of consciousness as a process of signals being sent and perceived. Instead of dividing the soul into the conscious and the unconscious as other approaches do, he distinguishes between the part with which a person is identifying, i.e. what he accepts as all right for himself and others, and the other side with which he does not identify-i.e., what he does not accept, what he sees as outside of himself, what he thinks he cannot do or must not do, what is disturbing for him or what he is even fighting against in himself or in others. “People identify with their intentions or ‘primary’ process. ‘Secondary’ processes are experienced as being foreign and distant.” [5] The secondary happens unwillingly to them. Mindell uses the terms primary and secondary very differently from what we are used to in other sciences.

在此，我借鉴了阿诺德·明德尔（Arnold Mindell）的“过程导向心理学”模型来描述叙事过程的这两个方面。[4]明德尔将意识流描述为一种信号的发送与感知过程。他没有像其他理论那样将灵魂划分为意识和无意识，而是区分了人认同的那一部分——即他认为对自己和他人都无妨的部分——以及他不认同的另一面，即他无法接受的部分、他视为自身之外的部分、他认为自己无法做到或绝不能做的事，以及那些令他感到困扰，甚至是他正在与自身或他人抗争的内容。“人们认同自己的意图或‘首要’过程。而‘次级’过程则被体验为陌生的、遥远的。” [5]次级过程对他们而言是无意中发生的。明德尔对“首要”和“次级”这两个术语的用法，与其他科学领域中我们惯常的用法大不相同。

Between both sides of the process there is an edge. [6] A person says for example: “I cannot do this.” Probably she does not want to get into a discussion or a fight with somebody, or she feels unable to do it. However, this matter is troublesome for her; again and again it shows up in her life; it is somehow knocking at her door. The edge holds a lot of energy. There are strong prohibitions around it, bad experiences, negative images. To step over the edge consciously is not easy. The signals from the edge are strong hints about a person’s frontier of growth.

在这个过程的两面之间存在着一条界限。[6]例如，某人说：“我做不到。”她可能是不想与人争论或争吵，也可能是觉得自己无能为力。然而，这件事对她来说很棘手；它一次又一次地出现在她的生活中；它仿佛在敲她的门。这条界限蕴含着巨大的能量。围绕着它的是强烈的禁忌、糟糕的经历和负面的意象。有意识地跨越这条界限并不容易。来自界限的信号，强烈地暗示着一个人成长的边界。

I use this psychological model for the process of telling stories as well. The different motifs are looked at like the different parts or figures within the individual or the different members or roles in groups. The edge appears in the feelings and values of the tellers. In our stories we find Erna having trouble believing that the inscriptions on the tombstones are to be taken seriously, and Anna not even thinking of the possibility that her family had been complete at the beginning. Erna expresses the edge clearly, by showing her estranged feelings about the inscriptions. She cannot totally trust these expressions of love because her experiences in this field were negative. The secondary motif is connected with other people. Looking at Anna we see the edge in the fact that it never came into her awareness that the photo was taken by her mother. The experience of a whole family is secondary for her. Being invited to tell a story without having a plan, an aspect could come up which was new and surprising for her.

我同样将这一心理模型应用于讲故事的过程。不同的主题被视为个体内部的不同部分或形象，或是群体中的不同成员或角色。那道界限体现在讲述者的情感和价值观中。在我们的故事中，我们发现埃尔娜难以相信墓碑上的铭文是值得认真对待的，而安娜甚至从未设想过自己的家庭在最初曾是完整的。埃尔娜通过对墓碑铭文的疏离感，清晰地表达了“边缘”的存在。她无法完全信任这些爱的表达，因为她在这一领域的经历是负面的。次级意象则与他人相关。观察安娜，我们可以看到这种“边缘”体现在她从未意识到那张照片是她母亲拍摄的事实上。对她而言，整个家庭的经历是次级的。当被邀请在没有预先计划的情况下讲述故事时，可能会浮现出对她来说既新鲜又令人惊讶的方面。

The edge can also arise in a more blatant way, such as somebody saying, “I cannot do this or that,” or “I don’t like this, I hate this…” We will see this in the stories I will discuss later. Primary for both tellers is the feeling of not belonging to the group, either the family or the seminar. Secondary-i.e., outside their definition of themselves in this moment, is community, togetherness, and love. The secondary motifs seem to wait until they can show up in a different way. In them we find the power of change.

这种“边缘感”也可能以更直白的方式呈现，例如有人说：“我做不了这个或那个”，或者“我不喜欢这个，我讨厌这个……”我们将在后面讨论的故事中看到这一点。对这两位讲述者而言，首要的感受是自己不属于这个群体——无论是家庭还是工作坊。次级的——即超出他们当下自我定义范畴的——是社群、团结与爱。这些次级主题似乎在等待时机，以便以另一种方式显现。在其中，我们发现了变革的力量。

The terms conscious and unconscious don’t seem to fit here because the whole process is happening more or less consciously. The tellers actively decide how to shape their stories. But it seems to me that there are forces of the soul working which are not planned and cannot be planned. In playback theatre we use the word spontaneity for this. [7] And in the end this is what I am discussing. But how it happens in detail, how the forces interact, how they complete each other and make a whole with the potential of healing: that is my question, and I want to collect more examples to explore it.

“有意识”和“无意识”这两个术语似乎并不适用，因为整个过程或多或少都在有意识的状态下进行。讲述者会主动决定如何塑造自己的故事。但在我看来，灵魂中似乎存在着一些无法预料、也无法规划的力量。在一人一故事剧场中，我们用“自发性”来形容这种现象。[7]归根结底，这正是我所探讨的。但具体如何发生、这些力量如何相互作用、如何相互补充并形成具有疗愈潜力的整体——这正是我的疑问，我也希望收集更多案例来深入探索。

## Connecting Stories 连接故事

In the beginning of a playback event there is quite often a feeling of discomfort. Nobody knows what is about to happen, neither actors nor visitors. The stomach is a little uneasy, feels empty, the brain seems to be somewhat dizzy. We sit on a spectator’s seat and ask ourselves: “Do I want to get involved here? Do I feel like telling a story? And if I wanted to, will a story come to my mind? Do I have anything to talk about? Or is my life totally uninteresting for others? I could easily feel embarrassed if I say anything.” Nobody seems to know the rules here.

在一人一故事活动开始时，人们往往会感到一种不适。无论是演员还是观众，都不知道接下来会发生什么。胃里有些不舒服，感觉空空的，大脑似乎有些发晕。我们坐在观众席上，自问：“我想参与其中吗？我有没有讲述故事的冲动？即使想讲，脑海中会有故事浮现吗？我有值得分享的内容吗？还是说我的生活对别人来说完全索然无味？如果开口说话，我很容易感到尴尬。”似乎没有人知道这里的规则。

A group, like an individual, has a primary process, issues and ideologies with which they identify, and a secondary process, consisting of the things that happen to them with which they do not want to identify. Most primary processes are connected with issues. The secondary ones are usually emotional and are concerned with hurt, happiness, power struggles and spiritual needs, all of which the group has an edge against. “The primary process is the sense of ‘we’ which the members refer to, the thing which makes the family or group different from others… A city’s local environment or a nation’s myth may be primary.” [8]

一个群体，就像个体一样，既有其认同的“首要过程”、议题和意识形态，也有由那些他们不愿认同的经历构成的“次级过程”。大多数首要过程都与某些议题相关。次级过程通常是情感层面的，涉及伤害、快乐、权力斗争和精神需求，而群体在应对这些方面往往具有优势。“首要过程是指成员所指涉的‘我们’这一感觉，正是这种感觉使家庭或群体区别于其他群体……一个城市的当地环境或一个民族的神话可能就是首要过程。”[8]

The primary process of a playback group is very open. There is a connecting idea, especially if it is not our first playback evening. It could be something like, Stories will be told and acted out here, and it is new and fascinating. But this idea works more like a “blank access,” an open approach with low structure, without content, and with only a few rules of behavior for the visitors. [9]

一人一故事小组的首要过程非常开放。其中存在一个连接性理念，尤其是当这不是我们的首次一人一故事之夜时。这个理念可能类似于：“这里将讲述并演绎故事，这既新颖又引人入胜。”但这种理念更像是一种“空白式进入”，即一种结构松散、没有既定内容、仅对访客设定少量行为规则的开放式方法。[9]

On the other hand, the tellers do choose what to tell and there are connections between stories that are conscious, or at least very close to how a person is identifying at the given moment. That is what I want to talk about now, before I get to the secondary elements.

另一方面，讲述者确实会选择讲述的内容，且故事之间存在着有意识的关联，或者至少与讲述者在特定时刻的自我认同非常贴近。在探讨次级元素之前，我想先谈谈这一点。

Like many other seminars and evenings, our week in Bern began with stories about leaving home, travelling, arrival, etc. The actual situation of the participants is the connecting element. Somebody plucks up courage and tells something. But to whom? To the conductor? Of course, he invited them and that is important. They feel welcomed. Or is it to the other group members? Maybe I am curious and a little restless; I like to show myself, to be seen, to take space. Or do I tell the story to myself? Do I need to make myself feel that I am here, not just present, watching, participating, but actively here in this place in this moment? The teller takes a role in an open field. The role can be: I am active, I am not a quiet one without courage. It can also be: I am helpful, I’m helping this event get started. Or it can come from other motivations. The group begins to generate a structure. A community creates itself by taking roles.

与许多其他工作坊和晚会一样，我们在伯尔尼的那一周也是从关于离家、旅行、抵达等故事开始的。参与者的实际处境是这些故事的纽带。有人鼓起勇气讲了些什么。但讲述的对象是谁？是领航员吗？当然，是他邀请了大家，这一点很重要。参与者因此感到被欢迎。还是其他小组成员？也许我出于好奇，内心有些躁动；我喜欢展现自我，被看到，占据空间。又或者，我是在对自己讲述这个故事？我是否需要让自己真切地感受到：我就在这里——不仅只是在场、旁观、参与，而是在这个时刻、在这个地方积极地存在？讲述者在开放的场域中扮演着某种角色。这个角色可以是：我是积极的，我不是那个缺乏勇气、沉默寡言的人。也可以是：我是有帮助的，我正在助力这场活动的展开。或者，它可能源于其他动机。群体开始形成一种结构。一个共同体正是通过承担角色而自我建构的。

Like the different roles, the stories being told in this beginning phase don’t look alike. A few examples:

就像不同的角色一样，这个初始阶段讲述的故事也各不相同。以下是几个例子：

Fritz comes up as the first teller in our seminar. *I was early at the station this morning. Usually I am late and in a hurry. But today I had time to go to a bookshop and buy a postcard. It shows a man flying over the city, while standing on a book. With pleasure I watched people running around. A little later I got on the bus where a friendly Spanish woman explained to me where to get off.*

弗里茨是我们工作坊上首位讲述者。*今天早上我早早到了车站。通常我都会迟到，而且总是匆匆忙忙。但今天我有时间去书店买了一张明信片。明信片上画着一个站在书上、飞越城市上空的男人。我兴致盎然地看着人们来回奔走。过了一会儿，我上了公交车，一位友善的西班牙女士向我解释了该在哪儿下车。*

After this we hear and see the story about Erica’s chaos: *I was in the middle of moving. I was staying at my friend’s place. Her husband was opposed to my being there because nobody had told him beforehand. There was a lot to organize with boxes and things, and I didn’t know how to cope anymore. My husband had already left totally exhausted for the new city and was not available to help. But then my friend’s husband became very caring and helpful. Unfortunately, I missed my chance to say goodbye to him, because everything was so rushed and chaotic.*

随后，我们听到了关于埃里卡那段混乱经历的故事：*当时我正忙着搬家。我暂住在朋友家。她丈夫反对我住在那儿，因为事先没人告诉过他。箱子和各种东西需要整理的事项堆积如山，我已经不知所措了。我丈夫早已精疲力尽地前往新城市，无法提供帮助。但随后我朋友的丈夫却变得非常体贴且乐于助人。遗憾的是，由于一切都太仓促、太混乱，我错过了向他道别的机会。*

The two stories stand in deliberate contrast to each other. Surprisingly they show the tellers from a very unusual side, a side even somewhat unknown to the tellers themselves. Normally things are more the other way around. Fritz is always in a hurry. Erica is well organized and does not live in chaos. And the opposite motif comes up in both stories: in Fritz’s scene there are other people at the station running around, and in Erica’s story the caring friends maintain overview and organization. The mentally conscious identity and the secondary parts seem to be mixed. Their roles seem to be exchanged. Is there a special reason for this? Do we pay attention to what is unusual about ourselves because it is different from everyday life? I would like to leave this question for later.

这两个故事刻意形成了鲜明对比。令人惊讶的是，它们展现了讲述者非常不寻常的一面，甚至连讲述者自己都有些不了解的这一面。通常情况恰恰相反。弗里茨总是行色匆匆，而埃里卡则井井有条，从不生活在混乱之中。而两个故事中都出现了相反的主题：在弗里茨的情节里，车站里其他人都在四处奔走；而在埃里卡的故事里，那些体贴的朋友们却能保持全局视野并井然有序。他们内心深处的自我认同与次级角色似乎混杂在了一起，角色似乎发生了互换。这背后是否有特殊的原因？我们之所以关注自身的不寻常之处，是否正因为它与日常生活不同？我想将这个问题留待稍后探讨。

I especially want to highlight the friendly Spanish woman helping Fritz in the bus. The question of who is Swiss and who is a foreigner, or who feels at home and who is far away from his origins, is another variation of the theme of belonging and feeling estranged and will be a very important theme in this seminar.

我特别想强调那位在公交车上帮助弗里茨的友善西班牙女士。谁是瑞士人、谁是外国人，或者谁感到宾至如归、谁又远离故土——这些问题是归属感与疏离感主题的另一种变体，也将成为本次工作坊的一个非常重要的主题。

In the process of beginning, roles are created for the different members of the group, as well as a beginning structure and a first feeling of “we are a group.” A special state of consciousness is evolving. As people from a Western culture we are used to thinking, analyzing, explaining, and understanding what happens. These explanations are not necessarily expressed. They are clear for everybody. But we love to give speeches, write essays, make up paradigms to explain things. Playback theatre does not explain. The stories will not be commented on. After a scene is acted out the conductor will ask the teller whether what the actors played is like what she told. Only if she feels that something really was different, does not fit or was even hurtful, will she say: “No, it was different.” In this case, the conductor can decide to let the actors play it again. Or, and this can be just as appropriate, the initial enactment is accepted as a variation of what happened. Quite often the actors have picked up on a secondary element, which the teller had not seen so far or did not have access to. But most of the time the tellers say: “Yes, this is how it was.” We all know that it cannot have been like this. The story in itself has fictional aspects and the play even more. The players do not use costumes or make-up. A few boxes and colored pieces of fabric define the stage, words are improvised and music underlines the scene. This is not what the teller experienced in the first place. Something like a miracle is happening here.

在开场过程中，小组成员各自的角色逐渐形成，初步的结构也随之确立，同时大家开始产生“我们是一个整体”的最初感受。一种特殊的意识状态正在形成。作为西方文化背景中的人，我们习惯于思考、分析、解释和理解所发生的一切。这些解释未必会被明说，但对每个人来说都是显而易见的。但我们热衷于发表演讲、撰写文章、构建范式来解释事物。一人一故事剧场不作解释。故事不会被评论。场景演绎结束后，领航员会询问讲述者：演员的表演是否与她所讲述的内容一致。只有当她觉得某些地方确实不同、不符，甚至令人受伤时，她才会说：“不，这不一样。” 这种情况下，领航员可以决定让演员重演一遍。或者——这同样合适——将最初的演绎视为对实际发生事件的一种变体。很多时候，演员们捕捉到了某个次级元素，而讲述者此前并未察觉或无法触及。但大多数时候，讲述者会说：“是的，就是这样的。” 我们都知道，事情不可能完全是这样。故事本身就带有虚构的成分，而戏剧更是如此。演员们不穿戏服，也不化妆。几个箱子和彩布构成了舞台，台词是即兴发挥的，音乐则为场景增添了色彩。这并非讲述者最初的亲身经历。这里正在发生某种近乎奇迹的事情。

It seems to me that two very simple facts are important: It is very unusual for us to tell our own stories to an audience and it is even more unusual that we can watch them. We hardly ever see our own life from outside, we do not look at it on a stage, we do not think of it as fiction. Playback theatre does not comment or judge, there is no explanation or understanding with words and terms. We just watch. That puts us in a different state of consciousness very quickly. A slight trance is created. We begin to “think” in stories and pictures, we watch and add stories to other stories, fit pictures to pictures without our rational primary process getting started. In his book Acts of Service, Jonathan Fox shows us that trance induction is part of preliterary acting. [10] It is surprising to me how quickly a slight trance is created only by the means of telling and watching.

在我看来，有两个非常简单的事实至关重要：我们向观众讲述自己的故事本就极为罕见，而能够亲眼目睹这些故事则更为罕见。我们几乎从未从外部视角审视过自己的生活，不会将其置于舞台之上，也不会将其视为虚构。一人一故事剧场不作评论也不作评判，没有用语言和术语进行的解释或理解。我们只是观看。这会让我们迅速进入一种不同的意识状态。一种轻微的恍惚感由此产生。我们开始用故事和画面来“思考”，一边观看一边将故事叠加到其他故事上，将画面与画面拼接在一起，而我们理性的首要思维过程却并未启动。乔纳森·福克斯（Jonathan Fox）在其著作《服务的行动》（Acts of Service）中向我们揭示，催眠诱导是前文字时代表演的一部分。[10]仅凭讲述与观看就能如此迅速地营造出一种轻微的恍惚状态，这令我感到惊讶。

A moment of irritation, disorientation, slight chaos is a necessary element of this process. Only when I give up wanting to understand what I experience in rational terms will this trance work for me and evolve as a source of creativity.

一瞬间的烦躁、迷失方向、轻微的混乱，是这一过程不可或缺的要素。只有当我放弃用理性术语去理解自身体验的念头时，这种恍惚状态才会对我产生作用，并演变为创造力的源泉。

## The Connections Between Several Stories 几个故事之间的联系

At the beginning of the process, tellers often reveal stories that show themselves in a positive light. Here I want to talk about a very different aspect, that part of the evening or seminar when stories are told which are especially difficult for the protagonists, where somebody broke down or failed or didn’t act at all like a hero. One can imagine that a certain trust is necessary. People need to feel safe and it is important that they have had the opportunity before to show that they were not always in a bad position or in the role of the victim. Sometimes the way in can come from a conscious articulation of one of the red threads already part of the group theme.

在整个过程中，讲述者往往会分享那些让自己显得积极正面的故事。在这里，我想谈谈一个截然不同的方面，即在晚会或工作坊上，当讲述那些对当事人来说尤为艰难的故事时——那些讲述者曾崩溃、失败，或完全没有表现得像个英雄的故事。可以想象，这需要一定的信任。人们需要感到安全，而且重要的是，他们此前曾有机会证明自己并非总是处于劣势或扮演受害者的角色。有时，切入点可以来自对小组主题中已有的某条“红线”的有意识阐述。

During the second week of our seminar, there was an afternoon when the tellers, adult men and women, many of them teachers, talked about childhood experiences where they felt abandoned.

在我们工作坊的第二周，有一个下午，讲述者们——成年男女，其中许多是教师——谈到了他们童年时期感到被遗弃的经历。

Fritz started: *I am 12 years old, home alone; my mother is at work and pays little attention to me. I go to visit her in the shoe shop where she sells shoes, to be closer to her. But I feel that I am tiresome, she doesn’t have time for me. In the evening she comes home. She talks about what happened during the day, but she tells only my father, not me. I feel excluded and alone.*

弗里茨率先开口：*我12岁，独自在家；母亲在上班，几乎不理会我。为了离她更近些，我去了她经营的鞋店看她。但我觉得自己很烦人，她没时间理我。晚上她回家后，会聊起白天发生的事，但只跟父亲说，不跟我说。我感到被排斥和孤独。*

The next one to talk was Richard: *In my story I am 12 years, too. I am on my way home from school. The other kids tease me, they bully and bother me. I am afraid and sick of it at the same time, I want to be left alone. I take my book bag and swing it around me on one of its straps. They have to give up and leave me in peace. In school I was a lot better than they were, especially in French. My book bag was my weapon.*

接下来发言的是理查德：*“在我的故事里，我也12岁。我正放学回家。其他孩子嘲弄我，欺负我，烦扰我。我既害怕又厌倦这一切，只想被放过。我拎起书包，用其中一条肩带把它在身前甩来甩去。他们只好放弃，不再打扰我。在学校里，我的表现比他们强得多，尤其是法语。我的书包就是我的武器。*

Both tellers are the same age in their stories; both are alone. But their experience is totally different. Fritz does not get enough attention, he feels put off, and he misses love. Yet he cannot do anything about it. Richard gets too much attention, but he is able to fight.

两位讲述者在故事中同龄；两人都孤身一人。但他们的经历却截然不同。弗里茨得不到足够的关注，他感到被冷落，渴望关爱。但他对此无能为力。理查德则受到过多的关注，但他能够奋起反抗。

This motif becomes the trigger for a third story. Ingrid says: *I was 13. It was just before we were given our report cards in school. The teacher told me to come up to him after class. As soon as I could I ran up to him in the hallway. He turned to me and told me in front of all the other students that my grades were very bad. I felt very embarrassed because everybody was watching.*

这一主题成为了第三个故事的契机。英格丽德说：*我当时13岁。那是在学校发成绩单的前夕。老师让我下课后去找他。一有机会，我就冲到走廊上找他。他转过身来，当着所有其他学生的面告诉我，我的成绩非常差。因为大家都盯着我看，我感到非常难为情。*

After the scene had been enacted, the conductor, this time a member of the group, offered Ingrid a transformation of her story. This time she creates a scene in which the teacher talks to her in a place where she feels protected, and he is more understanding. The group members who played teasing pupils before are now in the role of the protecting wall around the encounter.

场景演绎结束后，领航员——这次由小组成员担任——向英格丽德提出了对她故事的转化方案。这次她构想了一个场景：老师在一个让她感到受保护的地方与她交谈，而且态度更加体谅。之前扮演调侃同学的小组成员，现在则扮演了围绕这次对话的“保护墙”的角色。

The main motifs in this story are the same: age, feeling left alone, feeling put down. The missing part of the first story, the protective loving atmosphere, is replaced in the second story by the fight, the self-protection, and pushing away the schoolmates. In the transformation of the third story it is possible to find a situation and a figure incorporating protection and love: the understanding teacher.

这个故事的主要主题与前两个相同：年龄、被孤立的感觉、被贬低的感觉。第一个故事中缺失的部分——那种充满爱与保护的氛围——在第二个故事中被抗争、自我保护以及推开同学的行为所取代。在第三个故事的转化中，我们可以发现一种既包含保护又蕴含爱的情境和人物：那位善解人意的老师。

These connections are easy to recognize. This phase of the group process is characterized by a good feeling of trust. On the other hand, we have to consider that group members are now conducting. They are taking risks; the task is new and exciting for them. That may well be a reason why there are stories about schooldays and feelings of fear and loneliness. The tellers know what they are going to tell before they come up to the stage. They have primary reasons and we can expect that primary motifs are the connecting elements between the stories.

这些关联很容易被识别出来。小组过程的这一阶段以良好的信任感为特征。另一方面，我们必须考虑到，小组成员此刻正在进行表演。他们正在承担风险；这项任务对他们来说既新鲜又令人兴奋。这很可能就是为什么会有关于学生时代以及恐惧和孤独感的故事。讲述者在走上舞台之前就知道自己要讲什么。他们有首要原因，我们可以预期，这些首要主题正是连接各个故事的纽带。

The second story seems to give the teller of the first story a clue that he could have helped himself and fought for his needs. But I am quite sure that Richard did not do this on purpose. It seems to me that the second teller offers an alternative, a solution in which the active and passive roles are exchanged. Fritz is a victim of desolation, Richard wants to be left alone. The third story ends with a transformation. It brings a kind of peace. The stories do not finish in an open-ended or unsettled fashion, and the process of telling comes into balance. The atmosphere in the group is relaxed now. The next story can address another theme.

第二个故事似乎给第一个故事的讲述者提供了一个启示：他本可以自助并为自己的需求而抗争。但我相当确定，理查德并非有意为之。在我看来，第二位讲述者提供了一种替代方案，一种主动与被动角色互换的解决方案。弗里茨是孤立感的受害者，而理查德则希望被独自一人。第三个故事以一种转化收尾，带来了一种平和感。这些故事的结局并非开放式或悬而未决的，讲述的过程也达到了平衡。此刻小组的氛围已变得轻松。下一个故事可以探讨另一个主题。

In this story, the central motif is carried on. A person is alone and needs care because of this. Urs talks about his old mother: *She is living in her own apartment, and I worry about her. I need to check on her, but she doesn’t pick up the telephone. My fourteen year-old son has the clue: this is the time when grandmother takes a bath, and so she cannot answer the phone. But the whole family is fed up because there is so much trouble.* The motif of being alone and needing help is seen from a very different perspective here. For the teller it is not in the realm of his normal routine, and it eats at him.

在这个故事中，核心意象得以延续。一个人因孤独而需要关怀。乌尔斯谈到了他的老母亲：*她住在自己的公寓里，我很担心她。“我得去看看她，但她不接电话。”我十四岁的儿子给出了线索：“这是奶奶洗澡的时间，所以她没法接电话。”但全家人都受够了，因为这太麻烦了。*在这里，“孤独与需要帮助”这一母题被从一个截然不同的视角呈现出来。对讲述者而言，这超出了他日常生活的范畴，令他倍感煎熬。

The motifs make a chain, but they are not put together like pearls. They connect in a very special way. In the first story the main motif is brought up, but there is no solution. In a secondary way-i.e., in connection with other people, the solution is present: Fritz’s parents are connected-they talk to each other and listen. But this doesn’t help the protagonist. He feels excluded.

这些主题构成了一条链条，但它们并非像珍珠那样被串联起来。它们以一种非常特殊的方式相互关联。在第一个故事中，主要主题被提出，但没有解决方案。在次要层面——即与他人相关联的层面，解决方案是存在的：弗里茨的父母之间有联系——他们互相交谈并倾听。但这对主人公毫无帮助。他感到被排斥。

The next story shows a very different solution, and it shifts the perspective. The teller is not the victim anymore, but he is an active hero, who fights for his goals. But he stays alone at the end. In this nothing has changed. In the third story the motif is different yet again. The teller feels alone and unprotected as in the first story. She, too, does not come up with a solution right away. But in the transformation she can point the way to finding protection and support.

接下来的故事展现了一种截然不同的解决方式，并转变了视角。讲述者不再是受害者，而是一个为目标而奋斗的积极英雄。但他最终依然孤身一人。在这点上，一切都没有改变。在第三个故事中，主题又发生了变化。讲述者像第一个故事中的主人公一样，感到孤独且缺乏保护。她同样无法立即想出解决办法。但在转化的过程中，她能够指明寻找保护与支持的方向。

Missing elements of one story are told in the next one. But the whole solution is not found in one step, but rather is built up in several steps. The fourth story brings in a new perspective and places a different role at the center. It is the son who can express that he feels oppressed by his mother’s needs and his ex-wife’s pressure. I think it is remarkable that the motif of age, which connected the first three stories, appears again at the edge. The teller’s son who knows that grandmother is taking her bath at this time of day is the same age as Fritz, Richard, and Ingrid in their stories.

一个故事中缺失的元素会在下一个故事中得到补充。但完整的解决方案并非一步到位，而是通过多个步骤逐步构建而成。第四个故事引入了新的视角，并将不同的角色置于中心位置。正是这位儿子表达了自己因母亲的需求和前妻的压力而感到压抑的心情。我认为，将前三则故事串联起来的“年龄”这一主题，再次隐约浮现，这一点尤为耐人寻味。那位知道祖母每天此时都在洗澡的讲述者的儿子，与弗里茨、理查德和英格丽德在各自故事中的年龄完全相同。

## Finding the Theme 寻找主题

There was a unique composition of people in our seminar. The vast majority were teachers, mainly from primary schools but also high school, college and university level. A minority were psychologists. Some people were trained as both teachers and psychologists. Men and women were there in more or less equal numbers. Ages were balanced pretty much between thirties and mid-fifties. The most striking characteristic was the distribution of nationalities. The majority of members were Swiss. Only two members were foreigners, a Greek woman and a German one. The only full-time psychologist was a man formerly from the Alsace, a part of France belonging to Germany and France at different historical times and occupied by the Nazis in World War II. Everybody had been living in Switzerland for a long time. In this area one could find a clear majority and minority as well. In addition, the leader of the seminar, Jonathan Fox, was American. This very fact gave a special importance to the minority of “foreigners.” The relatively strong borders of the Swiss group were opened up in an important aspect.

我们工作坊的参与者构成颇为独特。绝大多数是教师，主要来自小学，但也包括中学、大专院校和大学的教师。少数是心理学家。还有一些人既受过教师培训，也受过心理学培训。男女比例大致相当。年龄分布主要集中在三十多岁到五十多岁之间。最引人注目的特点是国籍分布。大多数成员是瑞士人。只有两名成员是外国人，分别是来自希腊和德国的女性。唯一的全职心理学家是一位男性，他原籍阿尔萨斯——这一地区在不同的历史时期曾先后隶属于德国和法国，并在第二次世界大战期间被纳粹占领。每个人都在瑞士生活了很长时间。在这个群体中，既能看到明显的多数群体，也能看到少数群体。此外，工作坊的主持人乔纳森·福克斯是美国人。这一事实本身就赋予了“外国人”这一少数群体以特殊的意义。瑞士群体原本相对坚固的界限，在某个重要方面被打破了。

I mention this constellation in detail because it was very significant in the process of finding the group’s theme during this two-week seminar. My being German made me a member of a minority that faces quite a few prejudices in Switzerland because of history. This certainly influenced my awareness. I think it was sharpened.

我之所以详细提及这种构成，是因为它在为期两周的工作坊中确定小组主题的过程中具有重要意义。作为德国人，我属于一个在瑞士因历史原因而面临不少偏见的少数群体。这无疑影响了我的意识。我认为这种意识因此变得更加敏锐。

I had already had a long talk with André, the man from Alsace, during our first lunch. He is my age, which means we both experienced World War II as the overwhelming element of our childhood, but from very different perspectives. Our encounter was very open-minded and brought us in good contact instead of separating us, as it could have. At the time, in 1995, the theme of World War II was relatively far away for Swiss people. (This has changed a lot in the meantime.) None of the other people present in the group had experienced it. It had not played an important role in the history of their families. World War II used to be a part of the national myth-the myth of the heroic soldier defending the Swiss border against the evil surrounding the country.

在第一次共进午餐时，我已与来自阿尔萨斯的安德烈进行过长时间的交谈。他与我同龄，这意味着我们俩都曾将第二次世界大战视为童年时期最具决定性的经历，但我们的视角却截然不同。我们的相遇非常开放，这使我们建立了良好的联系，而非像原本可能发生的那样将我们分隔开来。当时，即1995年，第二次世界大战这个主题对瑞士人来说还相对遥远。（此后情况已大不相同。）小组中其他在场的人都没有亲身经历过那场战争。它在他们的家族历史中并未扮演重要角色。第二次世界大战曾是国家神话的一部分——那个关于英雄士兵保卫瑞士边境、抵御包围国家的邪恶势力的神话。

On the second day of the seminar André came forward as a teller after Jonathan had asked me to be the conductor. André told: *When I was three years old my father had to hide from the Nazis. My mother used to bring him food and provide him with information every night. So my 13 year-old brother and I were alone at home very often. One night my brother was so terribly afraid that he trembled with his whole body, and I walked him around our apartment in order to help him relax. This memory came back to my mind this morning when I had to leave my 15 year-old son alone at home because he needs to stay in bed with a fever. I could hardly do it. But I promised to talk to our neighbor and to his older sister and to call him to find out how he is doing.*

工作坊的第二天，在乔纳森请我担任领航员后，安德烈站出来担任讲述者。安德烈讲述道：*我三岁那年，父亲不得不躲避纳粹的追捕。母亲每晚都会给他送去食物并传递情报。因此，我13岁的哥哥和我经常独自在家。有天晚上，我哥哥害怕得浑身发抖，我便带他在公寓里走来走去，想帮他放松下来。今天早上，当我不得不把发烧卧床的15岁儿子独自留在家里时，这段记忆又浮现在我脑海中。我几乎做不到这一点。但我承诺会跟我们的邻居和他的姐姐沟通，并给他打电话询问他的情况。*

The two scenes were played, the second as a transformation of the first. I must say I was touched very deeply by the fact that André told this story in this setting with me, a German woman, as the conductor, and I mentioned this to him. In response André gave me a chestnut he had picked up in front of the building, saying that chestnuts surely must have been one of the main childhood toys for me, too. The experience of war gave us a strong connection in front of the Swiss group members, who, as we assumed in that moment, had played with “real” toys, not chestnuts. A minority is uniting here, bringing up a theme they usually have to hold back, because it does not have a great resonance in everyday Swiss life. What is not part of the identity of the majority comes up as a theme of the minority. The secondary is made “primary.”

我们演绎了两个场景，第二个场景可视为第一个场景的转化。我必须说，安德烈在这样的场合——由我这位德国女性担任领航员——向我讲述这个故事，让我深受感动，我也向他提到了这一点。作为回应，安德烈递给我一颗他在楼前捡到的栗子，说栗子肯定也是我童年时期的主要玩具之一。这段战争经历让我们在瑞士小组成员面前建立起强烈的联结——正如我们当时所推测的，他们小时候玩的是“真正的”玩具，而不是栗子。一个少数群体正在此处凝聚，提出一个他们通常不得不压抑的话题，因为这个话题在瑞士的日常生活中并没有太大的共鸣。那些不属于多数群体身份认同的部分，便成为了少数群体的主题。次要的被提升为“主要”。

I make the hypothesis that the later stories about loneliness, fear, feeling excluded and excluding oneself, i.e. wanting to be independent, are variations of this motif which was raised in the very beginning. In connection with majority and minority, the historical and political elements come into play-in Jung’s terms, the collective aspect. [11]

我提出一个假设：后来关于孤独、恐惧、被排斥感以及自我排斥（即渴望独立）的故事，都是这一最初提出的主题的变体。在多数与少数的关联中，历史和政治因素开始发挥作用——用荣格的话来说，就是集体层面。[11]

And now, to underline my hypothesis, a second story: The seminar included a whole lot of experiments of how to use playback in school. In this phase Eugen, who works in the field of ongoing teacher training, projected a photo on a screen both as a trigger of stories and as a background for the stage. It showed an old Mediterranean house with flowerpots in the windows. In the middle, a door, on either side two old people on chairs, and in front a big dog. Eugen asked the group to “find” stories to go with this picture.

现在，为了强调我的假设，再讲一个故事：工作坊包含大量关于如何在学校中运用一人一故事方法的实验。在此阶段，从事教师在职培训工作的欧根将一张照片投影到屏幕上，既作为故事的触发点，也作为舞台的背景。照片上是一栋地中海风格的老房子，窗台上摆着花盆。正中央是一扇门，两侧各有一位坐在椅子上的老人，门前还站着一只大狗。欧根请小组成员“寻找”与这张照片相配的故事。

Jakob recounts the following: *Several years after the war the old parents sit in front of their house in the sun. They have waited for many years for their son to come back from being prisoner of war. But so much time has passed, and they are about to give up hope. From far away a tired young man approaches. He is walking very slowly, because he is sick and weak. The two old people don’t notice him. But the dog jumps up and runs toward the man, wagging his tail. Everybody is very happy about the return.*

雅各布讲述了以下故事：*战争结束几年后，一对年迈的父母坐在自家门前晒太阳。他们已经等了许多年，盼着被俘的儿子能从战俘营回来。但时间流逝得太久，他们几乎要放弃希望了。远处走来一位疲惫的年轻人。他走得很慢，因为病弱不堪。两位老人并没有注意到他。但那只狗突然跳起来，摇着尾巴朝那人跑去。大家都为他的归来感到非常高兴。*

By telling this story a fundamental rule of playback theatre was broken. The conductor did not ask for a self-experienced story. When Jakob is questioned, he remembers that his grandmother told him this story, her own experience, when he was an adolescent. And it touched him a lot. The group, too, was deeply touched by the story and the enactment as well. We needed a long feedback round to contain all the feelings. We can see here that a collective theme was brought up-the return of the prodigal son, and with it the return to feeling at home, belonging to the community, the broken wholeness, and the healing of it.

通过讲述这个故事，一条一人一故事剧场的基本规则被打破了。领航员并没有要求讲述亲身经历的故事。当雅各布被问及时，他回忆起自己青少年时期，祖母曾向他讲述过这个故事——那是她自己的亲身经历。这个故事曾深深打动了他。小组成员同样被这个故事及其演绎深深打动。我们需要进行一轮漫长的反馈交流，才能容纳所有的情感。我们可以看到，一个集体主题被提了出来——“浪子回头”，以及随之而来的归家之感、归属感、破碎的整体性及其疗愈。

By the way, the photo was an advertisement for an absolutely banal product. Some of the group members knew it from television. Jakob did not. We had a discussion at this point about why it is so important to tell stories from one’s own experience in playback settings. The spreading out of the feelings into the group is unavoidable if stories have a collective aspect and are not connected with a personal history. This can break the frame in which playback is working.

顺便提一句，那张照片其实是一则极其平庸的商品广告。小组中有些人曾在电视上见过，雅各布却不知道。此时我们展开了一场讨论，探讨为何在一人一故事剧场的情境中，讲述源于自身经历的故事如此重要。如果故事具有集体性却与个人历史脱节，情感在小组中扩散是不可避免的。这可能会打破一人一故事剧场运作的框架。

On the other hand, we can see the power playback theatre contains. Our seminar took place in 1995. The Swiss identity of being a neutral country which had not participated in World War II was intact. Half a year later the complaints about Swiss banks holding back information about Jewish bank accounts started a new process of reflecting Switzerland’s role during the Nazi era. Today, in 1998, the public discussion is still going on and step by step the country is finding a new primary identity. Looking back on our seminar I think that the theme was already in the field, and a mixed group like ours was picking it up and working on it.

另一方面，我们可以看到一人一故事剧场所蕴含的力量。我们的工作坊举办于1995年。当时，瑞士作为未参与第二次世界大战的中立国的身份认同依然完好无损。半年后，关于瑞士银行隐瞒犹太人银行账户信息的投诉，引发了一场重新审视瑞士在纳粹时代所扮演角色的新进程。如今，在1998年，公众讨论仍在继续，这个国家正一步步寻找新的核心身份认同。回望我们的工作坊，我认为这一主题当时就已经存在于社会现实中，而像我们这样由不同背景的人组成的混合团体，正是将其提出来并加以探讨的。

## A Contribution to Understanding Groups 对理解团体的贡献

I want to use this story to address the question of how groups work and become creative, or to be more exact, to explore which paradigm about groups can help us understand the relationship between the individual and the group.

我想通过这个故事来探讨团体是如何运作并产生创造力的，或者更确切地说，探索哪种关于团体的范式能帮助我们理解个体与团体之间的关系。

As a first step I go back to that single spectator, the teller of a story. Playback theatre works with scenes, series of scenes, living pictures. Nothing is commented on or analyzed. The images remain. For most of us living in western countries, this works as a trance induction, as mentioned above.

作为第一步，我将回到那位唯一的观众——故事的讲述者身上。一人一故事剧场以场景、一系列场景以及活生生的画面为创作载体。其中没有任何评论或分析，画面本身便足以说明一切。对于我们这些生活在西方国家的人来说，正如前文所述，这种形式起到了诱导恍惚状态的作用。

We can best compare this state of mind to a dream. In dreams, too, nothing is explained, commented on or analyzed, the pictures speak for themselves. And they have their own logic. Jung called the logic of dreams compensatory. [12] The dream shows me something about myself which I don’t see. It is closely connected with the present situation in my life, but the central point is outside my own awareness. It shows me my own experience from a totally different viewpoint and makes more visible what I am not observing clearly. Often the dream leads to a solution in difficult situations, which would not otherwise come to mind. It seems to me that the altered state of consciousness of the spectators in playback theatre opens this kind of dreamlike logic, and that the connections between stories can be understood according to the way we look at dreams.

这种心境最贴切的比喻莫过于梦境。在梦中，同样没有任何解释、评论或分析，画面自会诉说一切。它们有着自身的逻辑。荣格将这种梦的逻辑称为“补偿性逻辑”。[12] 梦向我揭示了那些我未曾察觉的自我。它与我当下的生活状况紧密相连，但核心所在却超出了我的自我意识范围。它从一个截然不同的视角向我展示自身的经历，并使那些我未能清晰观察到的内容变得更加显而易见。往往在困境中，梦境会带来一种解决方案，而这种解决方案在其他情况下是无法想到的。在我看来，一人一故事剧场中观众的意识的状态改变开启了这种梦境般的逻辑，而故事之间的联系也可以按照我们解读梦境的方式来理解。

In this dreamlike state the secondary elements of personal processes become more accessible. The edge, holding us back from the secondary stuff in our everyday controlled state of being awake, has much less energy and can be overcome as easily as in dreams. This happens particularly if somebody is invited to tell unexpectedly and has no time to think about it, plan or become fearful. This is why the stories of Erna and Anna demonstrate especially clearly how the surprising parts, the strange elements come into play, and how the healing power of life starts working.

在这种梦境般的状态下，个人心理过程中的次要元素变得更容易被感知。在日常受控的清醒状态中，那道阻碍我们接触次要内容的屏障，其能量已大为减弱，可以像在梦中一样轻易地被克服。当有人被意外邀请讲述故事，且没有时间去思考、计划或感到恐惧时，这种情况尤为明显。正因如此，埃尔娜和安娜的故事特别清晰地展示了那些出人意料的片段、那些奇异元素是如何发挥作用的，以及生命的治愈力量是如何开始起作用的。

In psychodrama we describe the soul as some kind of stage where inner figures play a huge drama. [13] They fight against each other, they help each other, they block and complement each other perfectly. In the best case they build a beautiful whole in which many different creative powers work together and create a rich life. But the intrapsychic figures have a grave difficulty: they all have more or less the same history. They had the same parents, fought against the same outer conditions, and they usually have the same blocks and edges. The roles are pretty fixed through the life they all have in common. Changes are always changes of the whole system bringing everything out of balance, and that is why they release a lot of fear for other inner and of course outer figures.

在心理剧中，我们将灵魂描述为一个舞台，内心的角色们在此上演一场宏大的戏剧。[13]他们相互斗争，相互帮助，既相互阻碍又完美互补。在最佳情况下，他们构建起一个美丽的整体，其中多种不同的创造力量协同作用，共同创造出丰富多彩的生活。但这些内在心理角色面临一个严重的困境：他们都拥有或多或少相同的经历。它们拥有相同的父母，对抗着相同的外部环境，通常也面临着相同的阻碍与局限。这些角色因它们共同经历的人生而变得相当固化。任何变化都意味着整个系统的改变，从而打破原有平衡，这也正是它们为何会对其他内在角色——当然也包括外在角色——产生强烈恐惧的原因。

When we think about a group in this way, it is basically the same. The individual group members correspond to the inner figures. They act together. The loud ones overwhelm the silent, some have a central role, others stay on the margin. There are majorities dominating the process and minorities being overpowered or silenced and sometimes disruptive.

当我们以这种方式思考一个群体时，其本质也是如此。群体的个体成员对应于这些内在角色。他们共同行动。嗓门大的人压倒沉默的人，有些人扮演核心角色，另一些人则处于边缘。有主导进程的多数派，也有被压制或被沉默的少数派，有时少数派还会制造混乱。

In recent years, groups are often looked at under the model of the field, and different elements from field theory are applied in the description of groups. [14] Groups have certain roles. One member or a small subgroup takes the leadership role, sometimes explicitly, more often from the background. Others are the opponents, the critics. Some do not say much, but they feel a lot and are often very sensitive. Each group has its own majority. It is defined according to the goals of the group and the ideas the group is following. The more clearly the goal is defined, the more clearly the majority can be defined. From this, a minority always emerges. But it does not always become evident in the same way. The goal of the majority corresponds to the primary process of the group, the minority represents the secondary process.

近年来，人们常从“场域”的模型来审视群体，并将场域中的不同元素应用于团体的描述中。[14]团体具有特定的角色。一名成员或一个小型子群体承担领导角色，有时是公开的，但更多时候是在幕后。其他人则是反对者、批评者。有些人虽不言多，但感受深刻，往往非常敏感。每个群体都有自己的多数派。其定义取决于群体的目标以及群体所遵循的理念。目标界定得越清晰，多数派就越容易界定。由此，少数派总是随之出现。但其显现方式并不总是相同。多数派的目标对应着群体的主要过程，而少数派则代表次要过程。

It is not clear at the beginning who is going to be the minority. If, for example, everybody is tired, the eager ones who want to go on are the minority. If most of the people in the group are Swiss and two non-Swiss members feel connected by the theme of the war it can easily happen that their experiences during World War II
become a secondary theme. This can be on two different levels at the same time: for the minority it is a real experience and for the majority it is unknown and it becomes an archetype, a basic pattern of fear. The minority is channeling an important theme of the majority, which is repressed in everyday life.

起初并不清楚谁会成为少数派。例如，如果大家都感到疲惫，那些渴望继续前进的人就是少数派。如果小组中大多数人是瑞士人，而两名非瑞士成员因战争这一主题产生共鸣，那么他们二战期间的经历很容易成为一个次要主题。这可能同时存在于两个不同的层面：对少数派而言，这是真实的经历；对多数派而言，这是未知的，因而成为一种原型，一种恐惧的基本模式。少数派正在传递多数派的一个重要主题，而这个主题在日常生活中被压抑了。

Between the two parts there is an edge. As long as the majority insists on its primary goal and wants to keep its balance (homeostasis), the secondary part is looked at as irritation, disturbance, even as a hostile element. The more the goal is emotion-laden, the more a group will become polarized.

这两部分之间存在着一条界限。只要多数派坚持其首要目标并希望保持平衡（稳态），次要部分就会被视为干扰、骚动，甚至被视为敌对因素。目标的情感色彩越浓，群体就越容易两极分化。

Mindell works with the concept that each group is able to find new solutions if it is possible to give each of the parts enough space to express themselves and maintain fairness for all sides. “Filling the role of the leader, the follower, the silent one, the wise one or the disturber is essential for the life of the community. Only when all the roles are filled and interact, can the entire field discover its own human and self-governing capacity.” [15] Groups naturally own the power to find a way to balance and harmony and experience themselves anew as whole. “If there is a tendency to create and express itself, it dreams itself to conclusion … creating a new and unpredictable collective center, a numinous community experience.” [16]

明德尔（Mindell）提出这样一种理念：如果能够给每个成员足够的表达空间，并确保各方公平，那么每个群体都能找到新的解决方案。“扮演领导者、追随者、沉默者、智者或搅局者的角色，对社区的生存至关重要。只有当所有角色都得到充实并相互作用时，整个群体才能发现其自身的人性与自我治理能力。” [15] 群体天生具备寻找平衡与和谐之道的能力，并能以整体之姿重新体验自身。“如果存在一种创造和表达自我的倾向，它就会梦境般地达成结局……从而创造出一个崭新且不可预测的集体中心，一种神圣的社群体验。”[16]

In playback theatre groups don’t have a theme at the beginning. People want to be together, experience something new; they hope to find nice entertainment and to feel good. This is a very open attitude. Nevertheless a structure is created and a sequence of stories with a red thread appears in a short period.

在一人一故事剧场中，团体一开始并没有既定主题。人们只是想聚在一起，体验些新事物；他们希望找到愉快的娱乐方式，并感到心情舒畅。这是一种非常开放的态度。尽管如此，在短时间内仍会形成一种结构，并出现一系列以红线贯穿的故事。

One aspect seems especially important to me. The people getting together for a playback performance usually have only loose connections. Some are friends beforehand, most of them just came to watch. The visitors do not look at themselves as a group. They are somewhat distant. Their personal stories, the histories of their families, the inner and outer living conditions are not the same. They do not yet have a process as a group.

其中一个方面在我看来尤为重要。聚集在一起进行一人一故事剧场表演的人们通常只有松散的联系。有些人原本就是朋友，但大多数人只是来观摩的。观众并不将自己视为一个群体。他们之间保持着某种距离。他们的个人经历、家庭背景、内外部生活条件各不相同。他们尚未形成作为群体的互动过程。

If a group stays together for longer, as in a seminar, a stronger dynamic develops. The participants create a shared experience, become part of a common story, adopt roles, experience tension. Here playback offers a possibility to work quickly with the developing conflicts, helping the group gain distance from them and incorporating them into overall group life. The stories will not be judged or evaluated. Each carries equal weight. Everyone has a right to the teller’s chair. A good conductor will invite the silent people. The stories create a process, they find their theme, and they offer solutions. This creates a community in a short time.

如果一个团体在一起的时间较长，比如在工作坊中，就会形成更强烈的互动动力。参与者共同创造一段经历，成为共同故事的一部分，扮演不同角色，经历紧张时刻。此时，一人一故事为快速处理不断涌现的冲突提供了可能，帮助团体与这些冲突保持距离，并将它们融入整体的团体生活中。这些故事不会被评判或评价，每个故事都具有同等分量。每个人都有权坐上讲述者的位置。一位优秀的领航员会主动邀请那些沉默的人。故事本身会形成一个过程，它们会找到自己的主题，并提供解决方案。这能在短时间内建立起一个共同体。

The group dynamics are not ignored but rather are brought to a different level. They work now in the process of the stories, the pictures, and the motifs. The stories talk to each other. Primary and secondary elements communicate as if they were the figures on the stage of the group’s soul. The visitors, spectators, even the tellers just watch without interfering too much. If two people have different viewpoints or feelings, both stories can be told and played and coexist.

团体动力并未被忽视，而是被提升到另一个层面。它们如今在故事、画面和主题的进程中发挥作用。故事之间相互对话。首要元素与次要元素相互交流，仿佛是群体灵魂舞台上的角色。访客、观众，甚至讲述者都只是静静观察，不做过多干预。如果两个人有不同的观点或感受，两个故事都可以被讲述、演绎并共存。

Very important for this process to happen is the trance I was talking about earlier, the images and story trance. This slight change in the state of consciousness allows a dreamlike connection and logic. Stories answer each other and communicate like parts of a dream or like the dream and the actual life of the dreamer. There is an opportunity for the inherent tendency for wholeness.

对于这一过程的发生，我之前提到的“恍惚状态”——即意向与故事的恍惚状态——至关重要。这种意识状态的微小变化，催生出一种如梦似幻的联结与逻辑。故事彼此呼应、相互交流，如同梦境的各个片段，又如同梦境与做梦者现实生活的交织。这为内在的“整体性”倾向提供了实现的机会。

This makes playback very helpful in the process of creating community even when there is conflict. Institutions and companies have learned this and use playback for their purposes.

这使得一人一故事在构建社区的过程中极具价值，即使存在冲突亦是如此。各类机构和企业已经认识到这一点，并将其用于自身目的。

One point is important to mention. We see this in “normal” playback evenings as well as in institutional settings. When people who have been quiet so far are invited to tell their story, when for example after the quick and outspoken men, the more reserved women are invited to come to the stage (or vice versa); or when in an institution the ones in not very powerful positions, like the nurses in a hospital or secretaries in an office, tell their stories, playback’s power to acknowledge all sides and to fill in the missing parts will become very strong, and the spontaneity has a good chance of working. If somebody is invited to talk who is in a special situation, like Erna and Anna in our seminar, the unexpected and creative can come up.

有一点值得特别提及。无论是在“常规”的一人一故事演出中，还是在机构环境中，我们都能观察到这一现象。当此前一直沉默的人被邀请讲述自己的故事时——例如，在那些言辞犀利、直言不讳的男性之后，那些较为内敛的女性被邀请上台（反之亦然）； 或者在机构中，当那些地位并不显赫的人——比如医院的护士或办公室的秘书——讲述自己的故事时，一人一故事在承认各方立场并填补缺失部分方面的力量将变得非常强大，而自发性也很有可能奏效。如果邀请处于特殊处境的人发言，比如我们工作坊中的埃尔娜和安娜，意想不到的创意便可能涌现。

In his book about playback theatre, Jonathan Fox tells the story of Gary who was loud and disruptive in nursery school. [17] In drama he was allowed to do exactly what was normally forbidden: he could act like an animal. This gave him and the others access to his secondary parts, his aggression and his loud voice, which were usually a disruption. So the adults could develop a new attitude, as could Gary himself.

乔纳森·福克斯在其关于一人一故事剧场的著作中，讲述了加里（Gary）的故事——他在幼儿园时总是大声喧哗、扰乱秩序。[17]在戏剧课上，他被允许做那些平时被禁止的事情：他可以像动物一样表演。这使他和同伴们得以接触到他那些通常被视为扰乱秩序的次要部分——他的攻击性和响亮的嗓音。因此，成年人能够培养出新的态度，加里本人也是如此。

The training allows the playback actors to be very flexible in changing roles. The spectators experience this high flexibility by just watching. The open setting also demonstrates flexibility. The visitor can see how the same person is acting as a mother, a wall, a dog, a child. This creates a playful distance and openness, facilitating the changing and taking up of new roles and attitudes, which is so difficult to do in every day life.

这种训练使一人一故事表演者能够在角色转换上非常灵活。观众仅通过观看就能体验到这种高度的灵活性。开放式的表演环境也体现了这种灵活性。观众可以看到同一个人如何扮演母亲、墙壁、狗和孩子等角色。这营造出一种充满趣味的距离感和开放性，有助于人们转换并接纳新的角色和态度——而这在日常生活中是极其困难的。

The very place where change happens is often the individual. The creativity of the soul lives there. The group lives through these impulses and the group can block or support them. For me this shines light on the rule that in playback the stories have to be personal stories. It sets a frame inside of which spontaneity and creativity of the soul, the dreamlike qualities in us human beings, can unfold and be held together at the same time. The teller’s attitude is always his or her own involvement, it is the personal attachment: this is my story. This way the stories stay apart from each other, they don’t spread into the whole group. The next teller is not caught in the story told before, but is free to find his or her own version and the possibility of a new solution.

变革发生的真正场所往往是个体本身。灵魂的创造力就栖息于此。群体通过这些冲动而活，群体既可能阻碍这些冲动，也可能支持它们。对我而言，这阐明了一人一故事规则的意义，分享的故事必须是个人经历的故事。它设定了一个框架，在其中，灵魂的自发性和创造力——我们人类内心的梦境般的特质——既能自由展开，又能被稳稳承载。讲述者的态度始终体现为自身的投入，即那种个人情感的联结：这是我的故事。这样，各个故事便保持独立，不会蔓延至整个群体。下一位讲述者不会被前一个故事所束缚，而是能够自由地寻找自己的版本以及新的解决方案的可能性。

On the other hand, “there is no such thing as independent change. The world changes, and calls us or dreams us up to fill its roles, and changes us. Or we change and touch everything in the environment. … When a group has tried its best to work on a problem, the momentous change of one individual will change everything.” But without earlier group work the individual cannot do anything. “Individual, couple and group changes happen interdependently.” [18]

另一方面，“世上并不存在独立的改变。世界在变化，它召唤我们或将我们‘梦境化’以填补其角色，并由此改变我们。或者，是我们自身发生改变，进而触动环境中的万物。……当一个团体已竭尽全力处理某个问题时，某一个个体的重大转变将改变一切。”但若没有前期团体工作的铺垫，个体便无能为力。“个体、伴侣和团体的改变是相互依存的。” [18]

The many-headed group has a special chance in this process: it is not necessary that the solution is found at once. Many stories, many motifs work together and build on each other. Fritz, Richard and Ingrid are each working alone and together on answers of how to deal with loneliness and how to overcome the feeling of being a victim. And there is not only one solution, many ways can be shown. Wholeness arises only in the end, at the very end, maybe only after the end.

在这个过程中，多头小组拥有一个特殊的机会：解决方案并不一定能立即找到。许多故事、许多主题相互交织、彼此呼应。弗里茨、理查德和英格丽德既各自独立，又共同探索着如何应对孤独、如何克服受害者心态的答案。而且解决方案并非唯一，可以展现出多种途径。整体性只会在最后、在最末端显现，也许甚至要等到结束之后。

The same is true for an essay like this one. How playback works cannot and does not need to be explained in one article, but rather the answer will come together through the chorus of many voices, by discussing the many aspects, and by playing together.

对于像这样的一篇随笔，情况也是如此。一人一故事是如何运作的，无法也不需要在一篇文章中解释清楚，而是要通过众声合唱、探讨各个方面以及共同参与，答案才会逐渐浮现。
