# Shaman like Playback Theatre 萨满式的一人一故事剧场

By Heinrich Dauber

作者：海因里希·道伯

Somewhere in the world: a playback theatre performance - performed in the classical style of the New Yorker School. The conductor is warming up the audience familiarizing it with the subject or interviewing the narrator. The players are sitting on stage, uprightly, concentrated, calm and severe at the same time, with a shaman like attitude and with a Zen like concentration, just the way they have eventually read it in Jonathan Fox or Jo Salas - whatever this means. They are listening to a sequence of events, feelings, atmospheres, going to get into contact with what they are listening to. Own stories are coming to their minds, evoking connected feelings. Following these feelings inside of them, trying to follow the thread running through the story, trying to find the quintessence of what is told outside of them. Images are arising. First ideas of how to perform are sliding in between these images. As experienced playback performers they are trying to be in-between or at least to appear as if they were in-between. Likewise the musician and the conductor are floating somewhere among several levels of external and internal impressions and incentives, being present and unclasped all in one.

在世界某个地方：一场一人一故事剧场演出正在进行——采用纽约学校的经典风格呈现。领航员正在引导观众进入状态，让他们熟悉主题，或对讲述者者进行访谈。演员们端坐于舞台之上，姿态端正，既专注又沉静，神情严肃，既带着萨满般的姿态，又拥有禅宗般的专注——这正是他们最终从乔纳森·福克斯或乔·萨拉斯的作品中领悟到的，无论这意味着什么。他们聆听着一连串的事件、情感与氛围，试图与所听内容建立联系。他们脑海中浮现出自己的故事，唤起相关的情感。顺着内心的这些感受，试图追寻贯穿故事的主线，试图从外界讲述的内容中提炼出精髓。画面在脑海中浮现。关于如何演绎的初步构想，正穿梭于这些画面之间。作为经验丰富的一人一故事表演者，他们试图处于“中间状态”，或者至少表现得仿佛处于这种状态。同样，乐师和领航员也漂浮在内外印象与动机的多个层次之间，既全然在场，又保持着一种松弛自如的状态。

## The Loss of Great Tales 伟大故事的失落

As a part of the animated nature, as a not on instinct acting upper mammal, human beings with all their evolutionary imprint are above all characterized by the ability of understanding themselves as storied creatures being able to reflect their own history and future in respect of being individual and part of a community. Individuation (Who am I?) and enculturation’? (To whom do I belong to?) are interweaving in the process of socialisation. The appropriate balance between these two poles is again and again a concern in the life long search of autonomy and relatedness; it is the huge topic of interactionistic social psychology in the tradition of Ervin Goffman. Are not these questions and the related interpersonal conflicts concerning identity exactly the questions and background topics of every great theatre that intends more than just offering a journey in tremendous imageries? “Who am I? To whom do I belong to? What remains of my life after I will have gone? What am I going to bequest? Will I leave a trace in the collective memory of my community? At all times the *great stories* have been helping to clarify all these questions: myths of creation of the beginning of the world, stories about the fights of gods, stories about God’s union with His people and its fulfilment in His son, stories about great discoveries, scientific inventions and visionary designs of society. All these tales have been giving orientation and kept us grounded; their disappearance we may want to bewail together with Neil Postman. Nowadays in public, in schools, in universities, political parties and churches we are rather used to refer to the printed word of canonical texts in the tradition of religious communities, of the bill of the human rights in either literary or political form, of personal documents of important humanists or philosophers of several decades, all of them to a large extent written documents. We try to give the growing up generation an understanding of its message just by reading. In their everyday life they are fired with messages of a different kind, in a different language; messages that are neither related to any tradition, nor to stories from a different time and world, but only to themselves and to what they are expected to consider themselves.”It’s you who is responsible for your fate. You should start a business on your own. Every man is the architect of his own fortune.” The focus of these messages is almost all the time the individual ego, thought of as a share that should be boosted as profitable as possible and rarely or never the community or even existential questions exceeding beyond such as questions concerning a reasonable shape of one’s life, life or death.

作为有生命力的自然的一部分，作为一种不凭本能行事的高等哺乳动物，人类带着其所有的进化印记，最显著的特征在于能够将自身理解为“有故事的生物”——既能作为个体，又能作为社群的一员，反思自身的历史与未来。个体化（“我是谁？”）与文化化（“我属于谁？”）在社会化过程中相互交织。在这两极之间寻求恰当的平衡，始终是人类在追求自主与联结的漫长征途中反复关注的问题；这也是埃尔文·戈夫曼（Ervin Goffman）传统中交互主义社会心理学的一个重大议题。这些关于身份认同的问题以及相关的人际冲突，不正是每一部旨在超越单纯提供壮丽意象之旅的伟大戏剧所探讨的核心问题和背景主题吗？“我是谁？我属于谁？在我离去后，生命中将留下什么？我将留下什么遗产？我会在社群的集体记忆中留下痕迹吗？自古以来，*伟大的故事*一直帮助我们厘清所有这些问题：关于世界开端的创世神话，关于诸神之战的故事，关于神与祂子民结合并在祂的儿子身上得以实现的故事，以及关于伟大发现、科学发明和富有远见的社会蓝图的故事。所有这些故事一直为我们提供方向，让我们脚踏实地；若它们消逝，我们或许会像尼尔·波斯特曼那样共同哀叹。如今，在公共场合、学校、大学、政党和教堂中，我们习惯于援引宗教社群传统中的经典文本、以文学或政治形式呈现的人权宣言，以及过去几十年间重要人文主义者或哲学家的个人文集——这些在很大程度上都是书面文献。我们试图仅通过阅读，让成长中的新一代理解这些信息所传达的内涵。而在他们的日常生活中，却充斥着另一种语言、另一种性质的信息；这些信息既不与任何传统相关，也不涉及不同时代和世界的故事，而仅关乎他们自身，以及社会期望他们如何看待自己。“你的命运由你自己负责。你应该自己创业。每个人都是自己命运的缔造者。”这些信息的焦点几乎总是个人的自我——被视为应尽可能提升其“收益”的资产——而极少甚至从未涉及社群，更遑论超越此范畴的存在主义问题，例如关于人生合理形态、生死等议题。

## Shamans in Traditional Pre-literary Cultures 传统前文字文化中的萨满

In pre-literary societies shamans had the personal task to establish ties between the individual and its community, between the dead and the living people, between the past and the future, between all that has gone and all that is going to be. They carried out their task by celebrating rituals that were characterized by playing music, dancing, touching each other, wearing masks and painting images. The traditional shamans were spiritual guides, conductors, acted as intermediaries of several interior worlds, and sometimes not always they had healing abilities, were medicine men, captivators or chiefs of their clan or community. They did not work by means of texts but - to use the expressions of playback theatre - by means of sound, movement and fluid sculptures, by staging resonant, moving, colourful views. They were qualified for their task because of their unique personal experience of being part of more than one reality. In the majority of the cases they reached this particularly due to suffering through deep personal crisis.

在前文字社会中，萨满肩负着建立联系的个人使命：连接个体与其社群、逝者与生者、过去与未来，以及一切已逝之事与即将发生之事。他们通过举行仪式来履行这一使命，这些仪式以演奏音乐、跳舞、相互触碰、佩戴面具和绘制图画为特征。传统萨满是精神向导和领航员，充当多个内在世界的媒介；他们有时（并非总是）具备治愈能力，担任巫医、驱邪者，或是氏族或社群的首领。他们并非依靠文字来工作，而是——借用一人一故事剧场的术语来说——通过声音、动作和流动塑像，通过呈现共鸣、动人且绚丽的景象来施展法力。他们之所以胜任这一职责，是因为拥有身处多个现实之中的独特个人体验。在大多数情况下，他们正是通过经历深刻的个人危机才达到这种境界。

Traditional pre-literary shamans did not live in an environment coined by the ego- consciousness of modem men. The idea of a restrained ego, restrained from others and the world was not known. In a certain way we could say nowadays they worked with pre-personal, archaic, magic or somewhat mythical states of consciousness. They still could access what remains closed for us today, their contact to a higher power, to an overall experience of the reality of life, not being limited by a cut-out fooling us with a purely rational and egocentric view of the world taken as reality. Even if their spectrum of consciousness was limited in a pre-rational way, it still was less ex-clusively and less re-strictited than that of modem men.

传统的前文字时代的萨满并不生活在现代人“自我意识”所塑造的环境中。他们并不了解“受约束的自我”——即被他人和世界所束缚的自我——这一概念。从某种意义上说，我们今天可以认为，他们所运用的是一种前个人化的、原始的、魔幻的，或者说带有某种神话色彩的意识状态。他们依然能够触及今天对我们而言仍属封闭的领域——他们与更高力量的联结，以及对生命现实的整体体验，而不受那种将纯粹理性且以自我为中心的世界观视为现实的、欺骗我们的“剪影”所局限。即使他们的意识范畴在非理性层面上有所局限，但与现代人相比，其意识仍更为开放，也更少受束缚。
