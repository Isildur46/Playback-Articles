# A Ritual for Our Time 属于我们这个时代的仪式

Jonathan Fox

乔纳森·福克斯

Every playback theatre actor is familiar with the disconcerting experience that often comes just when a performance is over. An audience member will come up, shake your hand warmly as if in congratulation, and say: “But tell me. What is the purpose of it anyway?”

每位一人一故事剧场的演员都熟悉那种往往在演出刚结束时便会出现的令人不安的体验。会有观众走上前来，热情地与你握手，仿佛是在表示祝贺，然后问道：“不过，请告诉我，这到底有什么意义呢？”

We groan inwardly, because to answer such a philosophical question when we are still vibrating from the last teller's story is pretty tough. Unfortunately, even in calm moments, far away from the stage lights and applause, it's not much easier.

我们内心不禁叹息，因为当仍沉浸在上一位讲述者的故事余韵中时，要回答这样一个哲学问题实在太难了。遗憾的是，即使在远离舞台灯光和掌声的平静时刻，回答起来也并不容易。

For in fact the playback process is not simple to describe or understand. One reason is its flexibility: it can be adapted to many different specific needs in education and mental health as well as function as artistic theatre. This means that as a method it spans the conventional categories of theatre, psychology, and education.

事实上，一人一故事剧场的过程既难以描述，也难以理解。原因之一在于它的灵活性：它既能适应教育和心理健康领域的多种具体需求，又能作为艺术剧场发挥作用。这意味着，作为一种方法，它跨越了戏剧、心理学和教育这三个传统范畴。

Nevertheless, the question is valid: What *is* the purpose of playback theatre?

尽管如此，这个问题依然成立：一人一故事剧场的目的究竟*是*什么？

In this essay, I will attempt to frame an answer. I will discuss the theme first from the point of view of the community—that is, the groups which experience playback theatre, either in ticketed performances or a special setting (such as school, workplace, or community center). Then I will turn to the playback performers themselves and some of the challenges they face in fulfilling the promise of their form.

在这篇文章中，我将尝试构建一个答案。我将首先从社群的角度探讨这一主题——即那些通过购票观看演出或在特殊场合（如学校、工作场所或社区中心）体验一人一故事剧场的群体。然后，我将转向一人一故事剧场演员本身，以及他们在兑现这种艺术形式承诺时所面临的一些挑战。

## Red Threads 红线

I'd like to begin with some stories.

我想先讲几个故事。

*The teller, a woman in her twenties, tells about being on a trek in Asia and getting lost. She and her friend were far from a village. Darkness was falling, and no one came to help. The setting was strange and scary. There was nothing to do but camp out in a kind of shepherd’s hut. The teller felt miserable at their bad luck. But nothing bad happened. In fact, they woke up to find themselves in an incredibly beautiful spot, surrounded by rhododendrons, with snow-capped mountains in the distance. Terror had turned into joy; threat into blessing.*

*这位讲述者是一位二十多岁的女性，她讲述了自己在亚洲徒步旅行时迷路的故事。她和朋友离村庄很远。天色渐暗，却无人前来相助。周围的环境既陌生又令人恐惧。除了在一种牧羊人的小屋里露营外，别无他法。讲述者因这倒霉的遭遇感到沮丧。但并没有发生什么坏事。事实上，她们醒来时发现自己身处一个美得令人难以置信的地方，四周环绕着杜鹃花，远处是白雪皑皑的山峰。恐惧已化作喜悦；威胁变成了祝福。*

The story that followed right after took place in New York City.

紧接着讲述的故事发生在纽约市。

*A middle-aged woman tells about meeting the daughter of an acquaintance of her mother’s, a woman from overseas—a country in Asia, to be exact. The teller (who used to live in New York, but had moved away) had expected to have to show the visitor around. She feared it would be a troublesome and perhaps boring afternoon. As it turned out, the young woman was not only kind and interesting, but also very knowledgeable about the city. In fact, as the day progressed the teller realized that the young woman was showing her around. Instead of being the leader, she was being led; instead of the giver, she was being given to. It was a pleasant surprise.*

*一位中年妇女讲述了她与母亲一位熟人的女儿相遇的经历，这位年轻女子来自海外——确切地说，是亚洲的一个国家。讲述者（曾住在纽约，但已搬走）本以为自己得带这位访客四处逛逛。她担心这会是一个麻烦且或许无聊的下午。结果证明，这位年轻女子不仅善良有趣，而且对这座城市非常了解。事实上，随着一天的推移，讲述者意识到，其实是那位年轻女子在带她游览。她不再是领路人，而是被引领者；不再是给予者，而是被给予者。这真是一份愉快的惊喜。*

Let me point out some features of this storytelling process. These two stories form a kind of point and counterpoint for each other. While they both share a theme of unexpected happiness, in the first there is no guide, or bringer of joy, while in the second there is such a figure in the presence of the young woman. The counterpoint creates a kind of dialogue. It is as if in the first story the teller is saying, “Sometimes we are lost and alone, and it turns out all right.” And the second teller answers, “That may be true, yes. And it is also true that sometimes, even when we are *not* lost and alone, we can have a surprise and find ourselves guided by a stranger, finding new pleasure in old territory.”

请允许我指出这个叙事过程的几个特点。这两个故事彼此形成了一种对位关系。虽然它们都以“意想不到的幸福”为主题，但在第一个故事中没有向导，也没有带来快乐的人，而在第二个故事中，这位年轻女子身边却有这样一位人物。这种对位关系形成了一种对话。仿佛在第一个故事中，讲述者说：“有时我们会迷路且孤身一人，但结果却一切安好。”而第二个讲述者回答：“这或许是真的，没错。但同样真实的是，有时，即使我们*并非*迷失且孤单，也会遇到惊喜，发现自己被一个陌生人引导，在熟悉的领地里找到新的乐趣。”

It is important to remind ourselves here that in this example the process of the tellers coming forward was spontaneous; the conductor did not invite one kind of story or another. Yet even though each teller told her own personal tale, the stories connected on a social level. This “red thread” can be understood as containing a kind of folk wisdom.

在此需要提醒的是，在这个例子中，讲述者们站出来分享的过程是自发的；领航员并未刻意邀请某种特定类型的故事。然而，尽管每位讲述者讲述的都是自己的个人故事，这些故事在社会层面上却产生了联系。这条“红线”可以被理解为蕴含着一种人民的智慧。

Another feature of this process is that inevitably, there is not one thread, but many. For instance, the first teller has an epiphany in a remote spot in Asia; while in the second story, Asia, in the form of the young immigrant, comes to the West (“You don't have to go to the other side of the world”). In the first story, joy follows fear, disappointment, and despair, while in the second, there is nothing so dramatic (“Even a boring obligation can turn into something unexpectedly wonderful.”) In the first story, the teller is a young adult, while in the second story, the teller is old enough to be the mother of the first, (“Adventure is for youth, to be sure, but even when you are my age, such joy can still be found”). In playback these “messages” are communicated through the medium of enacted story, through movement, color, music, and metaphoric action.

这一过程的另一个特点是，不可避免地，并非只有一条线索，而是有许多条。例如，第一位讲述者在亚洲的偏远之地顿悟；而在第二个故事中，亚洲以年轻移民的形式来到了西方（“你不必去世界的另一端”） 。在第一个故事中，喜悦紧随恐惧、失望和绝望之后，而在第二个故事中，则没有那么戏剧性的转折（“即便是无聊的职责，也可能变成意想不到的美好。”）。在第一个故事中，讲述者是一位年轻成人，而在第二个故事中，讲述者的年纪足以成为前者的母亲，（“冒险固然属于青春，但即便到了我这个年纪，依然能找到这样的喜悦”）。在一人一故事表演中，这些“信息”是通过故事演绎这一媒介传递的，借助肢体动作、色彩、音乐和隐喻动作来表达。

A third feature derives from the context. We have learned that the story elements, in addition to speaking to the community in a general way, usually relate very specifically to the circumstances of the group. In this case, the audience comprised students at the start of a long training workshop. It is natural at such a time for students to be fearful of getting overwhelmed and “lost.” Others often worry that the course might be dull and unchallenging. In this case, the course was a PT training, and both tellers, each in her way, seemed to be reminding their peers to trust in the spontaneity process.

第三个特征源于具体情境。我们了解到，故事元素除了以普遍的方式在社群中讲述出来之外，通常还与该群体的具体处境密切相关。在此案例中，观众是一群正处于长程培训工作坊开头阶段的学生。在这种时候，学生们自然会担心自己被压得喘不过气来，甚至“迷失方向”。还有人常担心课程可能会枯燥乏味、缺乏挑战。本案例中的课程是 PT 培训，两位讲述者虽各有所法，但似乎都在提醒同伴们要信任自发性创作的过程。

The way the red threads carry through a playback event is so rich that as conductor I like to let the process be as undirected as possible, worried that if I make too many suggestions, I will in fact restrict this often unconscious form of dialogue (there are special cases where I act otherwise—see section below on Shamanic Conducting).

“红线”贯穿整个一人一故事活动的方式如此丰富，因此作为领航员，我倾向于让这个过程尽可能不受干预——我担心如果提出太多建议，反而会限制这种往往无意识的对话形式（当然也有特殊情况，我会采取不同的做法——详见下文关于“萨满式引导”的部分）。

Now let me go to a second event, which took place during a playback theatre workshop in the USA on Social Change. There were twelve participants, 10 whites and 2 African-Americans. During the first two days the stories were about white children experiencing the reality of living in a racially divided land.

现在让我转向第二个事件，它发生在美国一场关于社会变革的一人一故事剧场工作坊中。当时有十二名参与者，其中十名白人，两名非裔美国人。在前两天，讲述的故事都围绕着白人儿童在种族分裂的土地上生活的现实展开。

In one, the teller at age five witnesses prejudice for the first time as she sees her aunt speak insultingly to a beloved servant. In another, the teller tells about being a teenager as the family moves to the suburbs to get away from the blacks.

在其中一个故事中，五岁的讲述者第一次目睹了偏见——她看到自己的姑姑用侮辱性的语气对一位深受喜爱的女仆说话。在另一个故事中，讲述者回忆自己十几岁时，全家为了远离黑人而搬到了郊区。

Following these stories, with the encouragement of the leader, one of the African-Americans tells.

在这些故事之后，在引导者的鼓励下，一位非裔美国人开始讲述。

*It is a story about his grandfather, in whose house he had grown up—in particular, his grandfather’s personal dignity and high standards for his family. There is a strong incident of racism in the story, in the form of grave injustice suffered by the grandfather at work. But there is a stronger theme of overcoming racism. In fact, the teller never knows what his grandfather suffers until much later, when he learns about it as an adult, because the grandfather had kept it from the children.*

*这是一个关于他祖父的故事，他就是在祖父家中长大的——他祖父对个人尊严以及家庭有很高的标准。故事中存在一个强烈的种族主义事件，表现为祖父在工作中遭受的严重不公。但故事中更突出的主题是战胜种族主义。事实上，讲述者直到很久以后成年后才得知祖父的遭遇，因为祖父一直对孩子们隐瞒了这一切。*

In the unfolding of the stories in this workshop, the same contrapuntal elements are present. What stands out here is that with the story of the black teller, we hear from the “other voice.” One of the strongest features of playback theatre is that it allows diverse voices to be heard in a context of empathy.

在这个工作坊的故事展开过程中，同样存在着这种对位式的元素。这里引人注目的是，通过这位黑人讲述者的故事，我们听到了“另一种声音”。一人一故事剧场最突出的特点之一，就是在同理心的背景下让多元的声音被倾听。

A final point: the stories told by the whites were not heroic tales, nor were they victim stories. Instead they were accounts involving shameful deeds of their own families, painful to share and to watch. Such honesty enables a confrontation with truth that is all too rare in a human society where countries and their institutions often demand heroic official histories. Playback theatre honors the people's voice, be it joyful or ashamed, triumphant or oppressed. One of its purposes is to let this voice be heard, before witnesses, in all its richness and variety.

最后一点：白人讲述的故事既不是英雄传奇，也不是受害者叙事。相反，这些故事涉及他们自己家族的受辱事件，无论是讲述还是观看都令人痛苦。这种坦诚使人们能够直面真相——而在当今社会，各国及其机构往往要求官方历史必须充满英雄主义色彩，这种坦诚实属罕见。一人一故事剧场尊重人民的声音，无论其是欢欣还是羞愧，是胜利还是受压迫。其目的之一，就是让这些声音在见证者面前被倾听，展现其全部的丰富性和多样性。

## Restoring Oral Traditions 恢复口述传统

What we have seen so far in these examples is the presence of a special kind of discussion taking place through the tellers‟ stories. It is not an ordinary discussion, because it occurs through scenes enacted on a stage. We find more action and fewer words than in ordinary discussion. Moreover, the emphasis is not cognitive. Ideas may be inherent in the stories; we may be able to read a message or moral in them. Often insight will follow the enactment. But ultimately, the playback stories are *stories*, with setting, character, plot, and image. And as in stories, the value, or meaning, often reveals itself only indirectly. For example, in the stories described above, there are points being made not only about racism and prejudice in America, but about adults‟ behavior to children and the place of human dignity.

从上述例子中，我们可以看到一种通过讲述者故事展开的特殊讨论形式。这并非普通的讨论，因为它是通过舞台上的场景演绎而成的。与普通讨论相比，这种讨论更多的是行动，而非言语。此外，其重点并不在于认知层面。故事中可能蕴含着某些思想；我们或许能从中解读出某种信息或寓意。通常，在表演结束后，洞见才会随之浮现。但归根结底，一人一故事剧场中的故事仍是*故事*，拥有场景、人物、情节和画面。正如所有故事一样，其价值或意义往往只能间接显现。例如，在上述故事中，所探讨的不仅涉及美国的种族主义和偏见，还涉及成年人对儿童的行为以及人的尊严所处的位置。

These aspects bring us back in touch with the oral tradition, which is rooted in sensory perception and welcomes emotion. The modern professional world has tended to champion other values. Playback has often been looked upon suspiciously by institutions, because it appears “soft,” and does not focus directly on problems or return concrete solutions. Actually, today, more than two decades after playback first started, there is a more receptive climate to playback's holistic kind of communication because scholars have been reframing the argument. Sensory interaction with the environment, iconic thinking, need for narrative, and the marriage of feeling and thought are fundamental, it is now believed, to the human mind.[1] Thus playback now can be appreciated for the fullness of its approach to communication, which will often provide the *conditions* necessary to find a truly enduring answer.

这些方面让我们重新与口述传统建立联系，这种传统植根于感官知觉，并欢迎情感的表达。现代职业世界往往更推崇其他价值观。机构通常对一人一故事持怀疑态度，因为它看起来“软性”，既不直接聚焦于问题，也不提供具体的解决方案。实际上，如今，在一人一故事诞生二十多年后，人们对一人一故事这种整体性的沟通方式已持更开放的态度，因为学者们正在重新构建论述框架。如今人们普遍认为，与环境的感官互动、图式思维、叙事需求以及感受与思考的融合，是人类思维的基础。[1]因此，一人一故事如今因其对沟通的全面方法而受到赏识，这种方法往往能提供寻找真正持久答案所必需的*条件*。

Since playback theatre engages many aspects of our intelligence, it penetrates our consciousness in a particularly profound manner. Amazingly, we remember the stories we see in playback theatre. I remember the first story I ever conducted, almost thirty years ago. The teller is a person I see only once in ten years, but the truth contained in her story, which was about getting lost and finding her way, has also remained with me and acts as a kind of ever-present guide as I go about my life.

由于一人一故事剧场调动了我们智识的多重维度，它以一种特别深刻的方式渗透进我们的意识。令人惊奇的是，我们能记住一人一故事剧场中看到的故事。我至今仍记得近三十年前我首次领航讲述的故事。讲述者是我十年才见一次的人，但她故事中所蕴含的真理——关于迷失与寻路——也一直伴随我至今，在我的人生旅途中充当着一种永恒的指引。

The concept of discourse implies exchange between equals, and there is an implied belief in playback theatre that if we can speak and listen to each other in this deep way, good will come of it. In a playback performance, we are *all* experts, in that we all have stories with potential answers embodied within them—this holds for the youngest child, the humblest adult, the most wizened elder. The task of the playback leader or team is to create the atmosphere in which the folk feel free to come forth and tell.

“谈话（discourse）”这一概念意味着平等者之间的交流，而一人一故事剧场中隐含着这样一种信念：如果我们能以这种深刻的方式相互倾听与表达，必将收获美好的结果。在一人一故事中演出，我们*所有人*都是专家，因为我们每个人都有蕴含潜在答案的故事——无论是最年幼的孩子、最谦逊的成年人，还是最睿智的长者，皆是如此。一人一故事带领者或团队的任务，就是营造一种氛围，让参与者能够自由地站出来讲述。

Those of us who have been telling, acting out, and witnessing playback stories over a long time know the value of this communal telling. It is one of the reasons, I believe, why there tends to be so little turnover in playback theatre performing companies: the process is life-enhancing on such a deep level that we want to stay involved.

我们这些长期参与讲述、演绎和见证一人一故事剧场故事的人，深知这种群体性的讲述的价值。我相信，这正是一人一故事剧场表演团体人员流动率通常很低的原因之一：这个过程在如此深层次上丰富了我们的生活，以至于我们渴望一直参与其中。

To sum up what we have concluded so far, playback theatre offers a kind of community conversation through stories, and this conversation, even though it contains not one, but many themes, and is often indirect in making its points, gives scope for the expression of a popular truth.

总结我们迄今得出的结论：一人一故事剧场通过故事提供了一种社群对话，这种对话虽然包含的主题不止一个，而是多个，且往往以间接的方式表达观点，但它为表达一种广受认可的真理提供了空间。

## Awake to the World 觉醒于世界

Not always, but often enough that we recognize it as normal, the audience feels *good* after playback theatre. Of course, this is true of most theatre as entertainment. But in this case, the feeling is deeper, since the subject of the play is the audience themselves, their life-world. The process of identifying one's own story and witnessing another's often leads to a feeling of communal renewal.

虽然并非每次都是如此，但频率之高足以让我们将其视为常态：观众在观看一人一故事剧场后会感到*愉悦*。当然，对于大多数作为娱乐形式的戏剧而言，这也是普遍现象。但在这种情况下，这种感受更为深刻，因为戏剧的主题正是观众自身及其生活世界。识别自身故事并见证他人故事的过程，往往会带来一种群体性焕新的感受。

This renewal takes place on a number of levels. The first is individual. I can best illustrate it by noting the changes in a teller's body tension during the enactment of his story. At the start, he will be more or less tense. He may still be searching for the precise thing to tell. He may not quite trust the conductor sitting next to him, asking questions. He may feel shy of the others watching. Once the enactment begins, the body tenses in another way. There is excitement and intense engagement now, as he watches the actors take on the challenge of portraying his life. Then, when the actors hit a chord of truth, capturing the essence of what he was trying, so imperfectly and incompletely, to convey, there is a sudden relaxation, often accompanied by a sigh. Then there is a final body shift, once the enactment is completed, and the teller can take in the empathic response of the audience. The teller returns to his chair in a very different state than when he left it. He is likely to be smiling (sometimes through lingering tears). It is not easy to describe this new state-of-being: He is “in touch.” He feels “lighter.”

这种焕然一新发生在多个层面。首先是个体层面。我可以用讲述者在演绎自己故事时身体紧张度的变化来最好地说明这一点。起初，他或多或少会感到紧张。他可能仍在寻找确切的讲述内容，可能不太信任坐在身旁、不断提问的领航员，也可能对周围注视的其他人感到羞怯。一旦演绎开始，身体的紧张感便会以另一种方式呈现。此刻，他目睹演员们接受挑战、演绎他的人生，心中充满兴奋与强烈的投入感。随后，当演员们触及到真相的共鸣点，精准捕捉到他原本试图传达却因不完美和不完整而未能充分表达的核心时，他的身体会突然放松，往往伴随着一声叹息。当演绎结束时，身体会发生最后一次变化，讲述者得以感受观众的共情回应。讲述者回到座位时的状态，与离开时已截然不同。他很可能面带微笑（有时眼角还挂着未干的泪水）。这种新的存在状态难以言表：他“与内心相通了”，感觉“轻松了”。

The second level is social. The playback experience reduces the sense of alienation often felt by the inhabitants of the modern community. Of course, playback now exists in a variety of places, from large cities to small towns, and in many different cultures. But despite this variety, it can be generally said that more often than not in a public setting the audience comes as strangers to one another. Thus there is always a tension in the audience as well as among prospective tellers, since there is no way of knowing what the play will be about or how it will be handled. Members of the audience are naturally wary. This wariness will be present even among a group that is already established, such as a school class or a group of fellow workers. The social interaction that is such an important part of the playback experience works to dispel the fear of being among strangers, planting in its stead a sense of connectedness. Audience members often are amazed that they are able to participate in a personal and meaningful experience with people who only hours ago felt so distant. Their aloofness is transmuted into a correspondingly strong openness to discourse, and even afterwards, as they exchange comments, often a joyful feeling.[2]

第二层是社会层面的。一人一故事的体验减轻了现代社群居民常感到的疏离感。当然，如今一人一故事活动已遍布各大城市和小城镇，并融入了多种不同的文化。但尽管形式多样，总体而言，在公共场合，观众往往彼此素不相识。因此，无论是观众之间还是潜在的讲述者之间，总会存在一种紧张感，因为无人知晓演出将讲述什么内容，也无法预知其呈现方式。观众自然会心存戒备。即使在已经建立起关系的小组中，例如一个班级或一群同事，这种戒备感依然存在。作为一人一故事体验中至关重要组成部分的社会互动，能够消除身处陌生人群中的恐惧，取而代之的是建立一种联结感。观众们常常惊讶地发现，自己竟能与几个小时前还觉得如此疏远的人共同参与一场个人化且富有意义的体验。他们的疏离感转化为同样强烈的对谈话的开放性，甚至在活动结束后，当他们交流感想时，往往还会涌现出一种喜悦之情。[2]

Finally, the very sensuousness of live theatre, with its sights and sounds, images and rhythms, helps bring us out of our shell and open us up to the physical world around us.

最后，现场戏剧那极具感官魅力的视听体验、画面与节奏，有助于让我们走出自我封闭的状态，向周围的物理世界敞开心扉。

For those of us living in cities and caught up in cerebral matters, the playback experience constitutes a wake-up for our senses.

对于我们这些生活在城市中、深陷于头脑活动的人来说，一人一故事剧场的体验就像是一种对我们感官的唤醒。

Taking into consideration these mood effects on the individual, social, and environmental level, we can say that overall, playback theatre focuses the individual and group energy in such a way that the audience undergoes a trance experience, often feeling both energized and relaxed afterwards, along with a sense of physical and psychological renewal. We come away from the performance awake and open to the world. This effect is not unlike what people have experienced since time immemorial after special communal ceremonies. After playback theatre we can hear once more the leaves making music in the trees; see again the sunlight dancing on the water; enjoy pleasurable and supportive contact with others; find new hope.[3]

考虑到这些在个人、社会和环境层面上产生的情绪效应，我们可以认为，总体而言，一人一故事剧场通过某种方式凝聚了个人和群体的能量，使观众经历一种恍惚般的体验，演出结束后往往既感到精力充沛又感到放松，同时伴随着身心焕然一新的感觉。我们从演出中走出来时，精神焕发，对世界敞开心扉。这种效果与自古以来人们在特殊集体仪式后所经历的体验颇为相似。在一人一故事剧场之后，我们又能听到树叶在树上奏响的乐章；再次看到阳光在水面上起舞；享受与他人之间愉悦而相互支持的接触；并找到新的希望。[3]

## On the Threshold of the New 站在新纪元的门槛上

One can easily imagine other kinds of discourse-enhancing activities, such as a public debate, classroom discussion, or even a therapy session. And one can easily bring to mind other kinds of relaxing, enlivening experience, such as a yoga class, ballroom dancing, or a walk in the forest. What is not so common, perhaps, is an experience that accomplishes both. What makes this possible in playback is its nature as a heightened dramatic event.

人们不难想象其他能促进对话的活动，例如公开辩论、课堂讨论，甚至心理治疗。同样，人们也能轻易联想到其他令人放松、焕发活力的体验，例如瑜伽课、交谊舞，或是林间漫步。但或许并不常见的是，一种能同时实现这两者的体验。正是一人一故事作为一种高度戏剧化事件的本质，使其成为可能。

By comparison, we might think of a wedding or a funeral in our times. Or communal dances in other times that had the purpose of preparing hunters for the hunt, or healers to lay hands on the sick. These kinds of rituals are utterly serious and intended to produce transformation.[4]

相比之下，我们可以想想当今时代的婚礼或葬礼。或者其他时代那些旨在帮助猎人准备狩猎、或让治疗师为病人施以治疗的集体舞蹈。这类仪式极其庄重，旨在带来转化。[4]

It is commonplace to say that our modern age has seen a decline in ritual, such that many people today hardly know its power. Thus playback theatre is often asked to perform a more limited task, such as to teach shy adolescents expressiveness, or company managers team-building techniques. And it can accomplish these objectives, but in my view only in the framework of the more profound goal associated with a ritual.

人们常说，现代社会中的仪式已日渐式微，以至于如今许多人几乎不再了解仪式的力量。因此，一人一故事剧场往往被要求承担更为有限的任务，例如教导害羞的青少年如何表达自我，或是向公司经理传授团队建设技巧。虽然它能够实现这些目标，但在我看来，这只能在与仪式相关的更深层目标框架内才能实现。

The playback theatre event frequently begins with flatness. The audience, unfamiliar with the form and its ritual process, waits suspiciously. But slowly, as the ritual unfolds and people get caught up in it, feelings rise, with the inevitable consequence that a fully undertaken playback event will be strongly emotional. The music will play a haunting refrain. The actors will render a scene with particular power. The conductor will use just the right word at the right time. And suddenly a teller will spring out of her chair, to tell a story that comes from a buried corner of her soul. And this story will spring another teller into a deep nexus of feeling and memory. This letting loose of emotional energy is a natural part of the ritual.

一人一故事剧场活动往往平缓地开场。观众因不熟悉这种形式及其仪式流程，怀着怀疑的态度静候。但随着仪式的展开，人们逐渐沉浸其中，情感随之涌动，其必然结果是：一场真正投入的一人一故事剧场活动将充满强烈的情感。音乐会奏响令人难忘的副歌，演员们会以非凡的力量演绎场景，领航员会在恰当的时机说出恰如其分的词语。突然间，一位讲述者会从椅子上跃起，讲述一个源自她灵魂深处隐秘角落的故事。而这个故事又会将另一位讲述者带入情感与记忆的深层交汇点。这种情感能量的释放是仪式中自然而然的一部分。

Sometimes the informality of the playback theatre stage, in which actors are present as themselves, often relating to the audience between stories in a human way, is deceptive. Moreover, playback can take place anywhere, in rooms belonging to everyday life, with virtually no equipment. But there is nothing informal about the ritual.

有时，一人一故事剧场舞台的非正式感——演员以真实自我示人，在故事间隙常以人性化的方式与观众互动——会给人一种错觉。此外，一人一故事几乎可以在任何地方进行，在日常生活中的房间里，几乎不需要任何设备。但这个仪式本身绝不是随意的。

Not long ago in Israel, I conducted a demonstration of playback in a university classroom for community workers. The conditions were not stable—people came and went; there was little sense of decorum in the crowded classroom space. Nevertheless, I proceeded. Space was cleared for a stage; at its rear was put a row of chairs for audience actors. To the right of the stage I placed two chairs, for myself as conductor and the teller, who would come from the group. In short, the ritual space was set. In my manner, I tried to give a sense of the temporal aspect of ritual as well: at a certain point, my words became more formal, their pace slow and rhythmic.

不久前在以色列，我曾在一所大学的教室里为社区工作者进行了一场一人一故事的演示。当时环境并不稳定——人们进进出出；拥挤的教室里几乎没有秩序感。尽管如此，我还是继续进行了演示。腾出空间搭建了一个舞台；舞台后方摆放了一排供“观众演员”就座的椅子。我在舞台右侧放置了两把椅子，分别供我作为领航员以及从小组中选出的讲述者使用。简而言之，仪式空间就此布置完毕。我试图通过自己的举止，也传达出仪式的时间维度：在某个时刻，我的言语变得更为正式，语速缓慢而富有节奏感。

The first teller told an everyday tale of waking up in the morning. Volunteers from the audience acted it out. Then a second teller came forward and told a life-threatening secret of twenty years. The secret was that he had been a political radical in another country and had committed violent, illegal acts against an unlawful, repressive regime.

第一位讲述者讲述了一个关于早晨醒来的日常故事。观众中的志愿者们将其演绎出来。随后，第二位讲述者走上前来，讲述了一个埋藏了二十年的、危及生命的秘密。这个秘密是：他曾在另一个国家担任政治激进分子，并针对一个非法的、压迫性的政权实施过暴力、非法的行为。

The public revelation of this secret at this particular time in Israel's troubled history, when violent terrorism had just been the cause of innocent death, was in itself potentially explosive. The teller, in choosing to tell the story, traversed a boundary of normal acceptance and even safety, in the evident hope that the ritual frame would provide a context for understanding. As conductor I did not hesitate to follow through with the enactment of the scene, even though it involved killing. Held by the ritual, actors from the audience enacted this difficult tale as fully as they had the first. This willingness alone was a sign of deep interpersonal acceptance of the teller. Inevitably, there was a flood of response after the enactment; the ritual holding was even more important after the story than before. We enacted many fluid sculptures to express the different reactions of the witnesses. The result was a charged atmosphere. The outcome might well have been chaotic, but the order held—individuals with very diverse perspectives were able, in the playback ritual, to listen to one another. Social discourse took place on a profound level, and the teller for the first time since he had fled to Israel, could stand openly before his neighbors.

在以色列动荡的历史中，恰逢暴力恐怖主义刚刚导致无辜者丧生之际，此时公开揭露这一秘密本身就具有潜在的爆炸性。讲述者选择讲述这个故事，跨越了社会普遍接受甚至安全的界限，显然是希望仪式框架能提供一个理解的语境。作为领航员，尽管该场景涉及杀戮，我仍毫不犹豫地推进了这一场景的演绎。在仪式的支撑下，来自观众席的演员们将这个艰难的故事演绎得与第一个故事一样淋漓尽致。仅凭这种意愿，就足以表明大家对讲述者有着深刻的人际接纳。演绎结束后，回应如潮水般涌来；在故事讲述之后，仪式的支撑作用比之前更为重要。我们演绎了许多流动塑像，以表达目击者们不同的反应。现场气氛因此变得异常凝重。结果本可能陷入混乱，但秩序得以维系——在一人一故事仪式中，那些观点迥异的个体能够倾听彼此。社群对话在深层次上展开，而这位讲述者自逃往以色列以来，首次能够开诚布公地站在邻里们面前。

Anthropologist Victor Turner uses the word “liminal” to describe how participants in a ritual go to the threshold of normality—and then beyond. His studies in Africa led him to his concept of “social drama,” in which community problems are dealt with through ecstatic ceremony. Thus for Turner, rituals, with their departure from everyday order, can create a field for creativity, in which the collective can leap beyond, as it were, what had been possible before. This is what happened in the Israel example. The teller crossed a threshold of disclosure, and carried his listeners with him in the process, such that the audience could regard the pressing social issue of terrorist violence from a fresh perspective, that of the terrorist himself.

人类学家维克多·特纳（Victor Turner）使用“阈限状态”（liminal）一词，来描述仪式参与者如何抵达常态的门槛——并进而跨越其界限。他在非洲的研究促使他提出了“社会戏剧”的概念，即通过狂喜的仪式来处理社群问题。因此，在特纳看来，仪式因其偏离日常秩序，能够创造一个创造力场域，使集体得以超越以往的局限。以色列的案例正是如此。讲述者跨越了门槛，坦诚相告，并在这一过程中带动了观众，使观众能够从恐怖分子本人的视角——这一崭新的视角——审视恐怖主义暴力这一紧迫的社会问题。

This inherent power of the PT ritual is why, despite its flexibility, playback theatre does not always fit into the narrow confines of commissioning organizations, who want to be sure the show is “light,” or focuses on team-building, or who want to avoid certain topics. It is also why playback theatre groups can fail—from their own insufficient grasp of heightened dramatic events. It takes a long time to grow into the role of leading playback rituals.

正是这种一人一故事剧场仪式所固有的力量，使得尽管它具有灵活性，但一人一故事剧场并不总是能适应委托机构的狭隘限制——这些机构往往希望确保演出“轻松愉快”，或侧重于团队建设，抑或试图回避某些话题。这也正是一人一故事剧场团体可能失败的原因——源于他们自身对高度戏剧化事件的把握不足。要成长为能够引领一人一故事剧场仪式的带领者，需要很长的时间。

Rituals of transformation, with their climate of bursting possibility, are not risk-free. If the representative of a potential client group grasps the idea of dramatic ritual inherent in playback, she will naturally want an assurance that those leading the ritual have the necessary knowledge to conduct it properly. This is only appropriate.

充满无限可能的转化仪式并非毫无风险。如果潜在客户群体的代表理解了一人一故事中固有的戏剧性仪式概念，她自然会希望确保仪式带领者具备正确开展仪式的必要知识。这完全合情合理。

Often, however, an institutional representative's hesitation comes from a fear of feelings, from the very idea of a heightened event taking place. Then the problem lies with the representative, her institution, and even the society at large, but not, I believe, with the playback actors. Indeed playback theatre will succeed in teaching specific outcomes, such as young people to express themselves more freely or managers to work better together, when the dramatic ritual is allowed to flower fully. In such a case, there may be other far-reaching benefits for the group as well.

然而，机构代表的犹豫往往源于对感受的恐惧，源于对高度戏剧化事件发生的担忧。那么，问题就出在代表本人、其所属机构，甚至整个社会身上，但我认为，问题并不在于一人一故事剧场演员。事实上，当戏剧性仪式得以充分绽放时，一人一故事剧场将成功实现特定的教育目标，例如让年轻人更自由地表达自我，或让管理者更好地协作。在这种情况下，该群体还可能获得其他深远的益处。

Widespread lack of understanding about rituals and their usefulness means that the most crucial part of a playback event often takes place long beforehand, in the planning discussions. Host organizations need to be clear that playback creates a ritual that will stir feelings and respond to problems only indirectly; that everyone present will have an equal voice; and that there is a great potential for creative breakthroughs in these rituals.

人们对仪式及其效用的普遍误解意味着，一人一故事活动最关键的部分往往早在活动开始前，就在策划讨论中就已经展开。需要向主办方明确：一人一故事所创造的仪式会激发情感，且仅以间接方式回应问题；在场每个人都将拥有平等的发言权；而且这些仪式中蕴藏着巨大的创造性突破潜力。

More and more institutions are calling on playback theatre to conduct rituals, especially at moments of transition. Examples of such occasions are orientation sessions (beginnings) and retirements (terminations). Playback theatre groups are also called upon at times of crisis, when the need for a strong method as well as a strong container is clear. And playback teams are often called on to perform for a particular group at regular intervals. Thus students in their first week of nursing school could share through PT their feelings about the start of their training, including stories about what inspired them towards nursing. A university faculty department that was being discontinued invited playback to their final party. A married couple in crisis is invited to share their stories in playback, offering each a new way to see the other's perspective. The monthly playback performances, open to all, that are performed by so many companies throughout the world provide a regular place for individuals in their communities to share moments of their lives in an ongoing way. These and many other examples attest to playback's effectiveness in providing groups with a positive way to learn from the past and look to the future.

越来越多的机构开始邀请一人一故事剧场来主持仪式，尤其是在过渡时期。此类场合的例子包括新生入学指导会（开端）和退休仪式（结束）。在危机时刻，当人们明显需要一种强有力的方法以及一个稳固的“容器”时，也会邀请一人一故事剧场团体参与。此外，一人一故事剧团还经常受邀定期为特定群体进行演出。例如，护理学院入学第一周的学生可以通过一人一故事剧分享他们对培训开始的感受，包括当初是什么激励他们选择护理专业的故事。一个即将被撤销的大学院系曾邀请一人一故事剧团参加他们的告别聚会。处于危机中的夫妻受邀在一人一故事中分享他们的故事，这为双方提供了一种从对方视角看问题的新方式。世界各地众多剧团每月举办的、向所有人开放的一人一故事演出，为社群中的个人提供了一个持续分享生活片段的固定场所。这些以及许多其他例子都证明了一人一故事的有效性，它为群体提供了一种积极的方式，既能从过去中学习，又能展望未来。

## The Playback Performer's Triad 一人一故事剧场表演者的“三要素”

Let us now turn to the playback practitioners themselves. Jo Salas, in “What Is „Good‟ Playback Theatre?” (see chapter one), emphasizes that playback theatre is art and shares the task of all art, which is to convey meaning through coherent design, integrity of form, originality, and skill in execution. In this spirit, many playback companies work hard on staging, dynamics, use of metaphor, improvisation, and mastery of the basic PT dramatic forms in order to fulfill their artistic task of creating form for the meaning in a teller's story.

现在让我们将目光转向一人一故事剧场的实践者本身。乔·萨拉斯在《什么是“好的”一人一故事剧场？”（见第一章）一文中强调，一人一故事剧场是一种艺术，它与所有艺术一样，肩负着通过连贯的设计、形式的完整性、原创性以及精湛的表演技巧来传达意义的使命。本着这一精神，许多一人一故事剧团在舞台呈现、动态处理、隐喻运用、即兴创作以及掌握一人一故事剧的基本戏剧形式等方面倾注心血，以履行其艺术使命——塑造讲述者故事的意义。

But art alone is not enough. Playback theatre is also an interactive social event, Jo argues, in which much time is spent focusing away from the stage (greeting the audience, introducing the performance, eliciting feelings, invoking stories, interviewing the teller, and so forth). Managing an interactive social event requires a wholly different set of skills. These include good planning and organization, providing the right physical environment, giving those present a chance to be heard, and creating an atmosphere of respect. “Good” playback must fulfill the criteria of success in both these realms. Jo concludes that “good” playback resides in a zone where these elements become indistinguishable from one another.[5] To me this is an important formulation.

但仅靠艺术是不够的。乔认为，一人一故事剧场也是一种互动性的社交活动，其中大量时间都花在舞台之外（问候观众、介绍演出、激发情感、征集故事、访谈讲述者等）。管理这样一场互动性社交活动需要一套截然不同的技能。这些技能包括周密的规划与组织、提供适宜的物理环境、给予在场者发声的机会，以及营造相互尊重的氛围。“优秀的”一人一故事必须同时满足这两个层面的成功标准。乔总结道，“优秀的”一人一故事存在于这些要素彼此难以区分的境界之中。[5]对我而言，这是一个重要的构想。

Typically playback theatre groups lean towards one side or another. Either they come primarily from an artistic background and are interested in theatrical performing, or they come primarily from a mental health or educational background and are interested more in what we might call the workshop. The former camp has more expressive skills, the latter more group process skills. Sometimes, they even look critically at each other. The “artists” are impatient with process concerns; the “therapists” are disdainful of theatrical elements. Such taking of sides misses the point. All playback practitioners need to develop capacities in *both* domains, no matter what their special interest or emphasis.

通常，一人一故事剧场剧团会倾向于其中一方。要么他们主要来自艺术背景，对戏剧表演感兴趣；要么主要来自心理健康或教育背景，更关注我们所说的工作坊环节。前者拥有更强的表现力，后者则更擅长群体互动技巧。有时，他们甚至会相互持批判态度。“艺术家”们对过程问题不耐烦；“治疗师”们则对戏剧元素不屑一顾。这种站队行为偏离了重点。所有一人一故事实践者都需要在*这两个*领域培养能力，无论他们的特殊兴趣或侧重点是什么。

If what I have been describing above is true, however, there is a third aspect that is no less important than art and social interaction in creating good playback—ritual. Creating ritual demands different skills yet again: the invocation of a transpersonal dimension, an adherence to rules of conduct; building ecstatic emotional energy; the sparse, rhythmical, highly specialized use of language; and a goal of transformation. To lead the ritual properly, actors focus on their presence on stage. They carefully practice the manner of their listening to the story, setting up a scene, and giving the acknowledgment afterwards. The conductor learns to keep the ritual moving forward, no matter what kind of teller or story; be a guardian of truth (finding the “deep story”), and knowing just when the rules of playback conduct should be adhered to and when they should be relaxed (see table 1).

然而，如果我上述的描述属实，那么在创造好的一人一故事的过程中，还有一个方面其重要性丝毫不亚于艺术和社交互动——那就是仪式。创造仪式又需要不同的技能：唤起超越个人的维度，恪守行为准则；积聚狂喜的情感能量；语言运用需简洁、富有节奏且高度专业化；以及追求转化的目标。为了正确引导仪式，演员们专注于自己在舞台上的临在感。他们会仔细练习倾听故事的方式、场景的布置以及演绎后给予的致意。领航员学会无论面对何种讲述者或故事，都能让仪式持续推进；成为真实性（truth）的守护者（发掘“深层故事”），并精准把握何时应恪守一人一故事领航的行为准则、何时又应适当放宽（参见表1）。

Thus a good playback actor and leader must be skilled in a triad of roles—as an artist, a host, and a shaman.

因此，一位优秀的一人一故事演员和带领者必须精通三重角色——即艺术家、主持人与萨满。

This is no light task. It is why serious playback practitioners spend years in apprenticeship and learning.

这绝非易事。正因如此，认真的一人一故事实践者才会花费数年时间进行学徒训练和学习。

There are numerous areas where the requirements of these domains seem to place opposite demands on the actors. The art of PT allows aesthetic distance, while the ritual demands involvement. The socially interactive aspect allows for a relaxed, informal contact, while the ritual demands transpersonal intensity. It is an objective of the artist to entertain and delight the audience; the host to establish trust and put people at their ease; and the shaman to engender an atmosphere of enchantment and even confusion as a stepping stone to entering what has been called the “other thought.”

在许多方面，这些领域的不同要求似乎对演员提出了相互矛盾的诉求。PT 作为一门艺术允许保持审美距离（aesthetic distance），而作为仪式则要求全身心投入。社交互动方面允许轻松、非正式的接触，而仪式则要求超越个人的投入。艺术家的目标是娱乐和取悦观众；主持人的目标是建立信任并让人们感到放松；而萨满的目标则是营造一种迷人的氛围，甚至制造些许困惑，以此作为进入所谓“另一种思维”的跳板。

So how do you plan your opening? Something funny and/or dramatic (artistic)? A clear explanation of what will happen, followed by introductions all around (socially interactive)? Or a slow rhythmical talk, with musical accompaniment, that may not even seem to make much sense (shamanistic)? Clearly, the playback practitioner needs not only to have skills in each domain, but also to be able to blend paradoxical elements effectively. Often, in fact, an opening, while it may lean to one side or another, will accomplish objectives in each of the domains.

那么，你打算如何设计开场？是选择一些幽默和/或戏剧性（艺术性）的环节？还是先清晰说明后续流程，随后进行全场介绍（社交互动）？抑或是配以音乐伴奏、节奏舒缓的独白，内容甚至可能看似毫无逻辑（萨满式）？显然，一人一故事实践者不仅需要掌握每个领域的技能，还必须能够有效地融合这些看似矛盾的元素。事实上，开场环节虽然可能偏向其中某一方，但往往也能在各个领域中实现相应目标。

If we think of ritual as being a part of this triad upon which the playback experience is based, then there follow certain considerations for the practitioner. I would like to mention some of them here.

如果我们将仪式视为一人一故事体验所基于的这一三元结构的一部分，那么实践者就需要考虑一些相关事项。我在此想提其中几点。

First, even though the playback process is spontaneous, the ritual has its rules: The teller must come to the chair; the teller must stay in the chair during the enactment; the teller must tell a personal story. The actors stand when picked for a role; the actors do not talk during the interview. The conductor does not interrupt the enactment; the conductor checks in with the teller after the enactment; the conductor dismisses the teller from the chair. These are *some* of them.

首先，尽管一人一故事剧场的演绎过程是自发的，但仪式自有其规则：讲述者必须坐到椅子上；在演绎过程中，讲述者必须坐在椅子上；讲述者必须讲述一个个人故事。演员被选中扮演角色时需起立；演员在访谈过程中不得说话。领航员不得打断演绎；领航员在演绎结束后需与讲述者确认感受；领航员负责请讲述者离开椅子。这些只是*其中一部分*。

Without the clear framework provided by the rules, spontaneity can quickly turn into chaos, creativity to confusion. With it, the members of the audience feel safe enough to let themselves go into trance, allowing unforeseen breakthroughs.

若没有规则提供的清晰框架，自发性很快会演变成混乱，创造力也会沦为迷茫。有了这些规则，观众才能感到足够安全，从而让自己进入一种恍惚状态，从而促成意想不到的突破。

The need for ritual in playback goes beyond differences between kinds of PT groups, be they professional performing or intimate living room groups. It goes beyond differences in audience size: as much attention must be paid to these formalities with a group of four (teller, conductor, actor, and witness) as with a group of four hundred.

一人一故事中对仪式的需求超越了不同类型 PT 团队之间的差异，无论它们是专业的表演团体，还是亲密的客厅团队。它也不仅限于观众规模的差异：无论是四人小组（讲述者、领航员、演员和见证者），还是四百人的群体，都必须同样重视这些礼仪规范。

Because of their intensity, rituals place a heavy personal demand on their shamans, and it is essential to take time beforehand to assume the role and afterwards to release it. “Artistic” PT groups sometimes err in modeling themselves on the professional theatre, arriving in time to do a minimum amount of theatrical warming-up and departing as soon as the show is over; while the process-oriented groups sometimes err in trying to give feedback right away and “discuss” the event. Rituals, however, require a specific kind of warm-up and cool down. After being a conductor in playback, for example, it sometimes takes me more than twenty-four hours to return to what I would consider a normal state. And this is after I have been in a state of disturbance for up to two days beforehand.

由于其强度极高，仪式对萨满提出了严苛的个人要求，因此必须预先花时间进入角色，并在结束后及时抽离。“艺术型”即兴表演团体有时会犯这样的错误：效仿专业剧团的做法，仅在演出前匆匆进行最低限度的戏剧热身，演出一结束便立即离场；而注重过程的团体有时则会犯这样的错误：试图立即给予反馈并“讨论”此次活动。然而，仪式需要一种特殊的热身和放松过程。例如，在担任一人一故事环节的领航员之后，我有时需要超过二十四小时才能恢复到我所认为的正常状态。而在此之前，我甚至可能已经处于一种持续长达两天的精神动荡状态之中。

The idea of playback as service has its locus in this ritual function. Although the artist and the shaman share certain qualities—both are often “called” to their work, for example—there is a fundamental difference. The artist's paramount concern is the creation of his or her art, demanding an ultimate loyalty to personal vision, while the shaman's focus resides in others. In a playback performance, when these roles are in apparent conflict—for instance when the artistic integrity of a show is threatened by a disabled teller whose manner may be slow and halting—there is no doubt in my mind that the shaman role takes precedence. I am not there to look brilliant, or ultimately to create art for art's sake. Nor are my actors. We are there to conduct a process. It has inspired this person to come forward and tell. It is our responsibility to accept the teller and the story fully and to rise to the challenge of creating an atmosphere of deep attention in the audience. Such situations are common in playback, especially when the ritual is strong, for it is then that the isolated feel safe enough to come forward.

将一人一故事视为一种服务的理念，其根源正在于这种仪式功能。尽管艺术家和萨满拥有某些共同特质——例如，两者往往都是被“感召”从事其工作的——但二者之间存在根本性的差异。艺术家最关心的莫过于艺术创作，这要求对个人愿景保持绝对的忠诚；而萨满的关注点则在于他人。在一人一故事演出中，当这些角色出现明显冲突时——例如当一位行动不便的讲述者因语速缓慢、断断续续而威胁到演出的艺术完整性时——在我看来，萨满的角色无疑应优先于艺术家。我并非为了显得才华横溢而在此，也并非最终为了“为艺术而艺术”而创作。我的演员们亦是如此。我们到场是为了领航一个过程。正是这个过程激励了这位讲述者走上台前讲述。我们的责任是完全接纳讲述者及其故事，并迎接挑战，在观众中营造出一种全神贯注的氛围。这种情况在一人一故事中很常见，尤其是当仪式感很强的时候，因为只有那时，那些感到孤立的人才会觉得足够安全，从而走上台前。

Playback's grounding in ritual is perhaps a reason why it can flourish in many different cultures, with different artistic and social traditions. The warm-ups may differ throughout the playback world, and the actors may have very different styles, but the ritual is constant. It provides the safety, and paradoxically, the power.

一人一故事植根于仪式，这或许正是它能在拥有不同艺术和社会传统的多种文化中蓬勃发展的原因。世界各地的一人一故事热身环节可能各不相同，演员的风格也可能大相径庭，但仪式始终如一。它提供了安全感，也带来了——看似矛盾却又真实——的力量。

## Life and Death Stories 生与死的故事

Playback actors of moderate experience sometimes complain about a particular playback event being dull. “The stories weren't deep,” they say. “They were only anecdotes.” In time, they will hopefully learn to “hear” the archetypal image inherent in any—even the most mundane-seeming—story. More often than not, however, the sequence of playback stories does contain enough evident seriousness, as well as humor, so that by the end even fledgling actors feel satisfied that indeed, something beneficial has emerged from the void.

一些经验不深不浅的一人一故事演员有时会抱怨某次回放活动很乏味。“这些故事不够深刻，”他们说。“那不过是些轶事罢了。”随着时间的推移，希望他们能学会“听出”任何故事——即便是看似最平淡无奇的故事——中蕴含的原型意象。不过，大多数情况下，一人一故事的连贯叙述中确实包含着足够的严肃性，同时也不乏幽默感，因此到最后，就连初出茅庐的演员也会感到满足，确信从那片虚无中确实涌现出了有益的东西。

Occasionally, however, what we might call a life-and-death story is told, someone's core experience. Then the ritual is most important. At such times the feeling of risk is palpable; there is fear that the teller may “flip out” or that a feeling of chaos may overwhelm everyone.

不过，偶尔也会讲述一些我们称之为“生与死的故事”的内容，即某人的核心经历。此时，仪式就显得尤为重要。在这种情况下，风险感触手可及；人们担心讲述者可能会“失控”，或者一种混乱的感觉会淹没所有人。

As an example, I would like to quote from Deborah Pearson, an Australian playback practitioner who has traveled widely. She tells about being guest conductor for a playback performance at a school in Finland when the children had only five minutes before learned about the death of a fellow student in a car accident. If playback is thought of as entertainment, or even as education, one might well argue that this was not the moment for it. Better cancel, and leave the school community to deal as best it can with its shock and grief. If, on the other hand, playback is thought of as a healing ritual, then perhaps it is *just* the moment for it, with one important caveat. It is essential that the company, and especially the conductor, be able to hold the emotions of the crowd and provide a safe place for public mourning.

举个例子，我想引用澳大利亚一人一故事实践者黛博拉·皮尔森的话，她曾游历四方。她讲述了自己在芬兰某所学校担任一人一故事演出客座领航时的经历：就在表演开始前五分钟，孩子们才得知一位同学因车祸去世的消息。如果将一人一故事视为娱乐，甚至视为教育，人们完全有理由认为此时并非进行一人一故事的合适时机。最好取消演出，让学校社群尽其所能地应对这场震惊与悲痛。反之，如果将一人一故事视为一种疗愈仪式，那么这或许*恰恰*是进行一人一故事的时机，但有一个重要的前提：剧团，尤其是领航员，必须能够承接人们的情绪，并为公开悼念提供一个安全的空间。

(Many playback actors know inside themselves that they are not yet ready to contain such strong events, and even if they have the artistic and social interactive skills, they will back away—as they should. And if any of the actors should worry why in their regular performances they are not getting deeper stories from their audiences, it is simply because the audiences sense that the actors are not yet ready to hold them.)

（许多一人一故事剧演员内心深处都清楚，自己尚未准备好承接如此强烈的事件；即使具备艺术和社交互动技能，他们也会退缩——这本是理所应当的。如果有些演员担心，为何在常规演出中无法从观众那里获得更深刻的故事，那仅仅是因为观众察觉到演员们尚未做好承接这些情感的准备。）

In this particular case, Deborah and her group of Finnish actors, responding to the need for a community healing ritual, did not retreat in the face of community crisis. They went ahead with the performance, ready to enact whatever difficult feelings anyone needed to tell. The young audience responded to the offer of the playback actors. They told about their feelings, and saw them acted out. There were two full stories told and performed. The ritual was accomplished. After the performance, Deborah writes: 

在这个特殊案例中，黛博拉和她的芬兰演员团队响应了社群对疗愈仪式的需求，面对社群危机并未退缩。他们坚持完成了演出，准备好演绎任何人需要倾诉的艰难情感。年轻的观众们回应了一人一故事演员们的邀请。他们讲述了自己的感受，并亲眼目睹了这些感受被演绎出来。共有两个完整的故事被讲述并演绎出来。仪式圆满完成。演出结束后，黛博拉写道：

> I noticed the heads were not hanging down so much as they left the room, and there was more energy. More of them had cried some tears during the performance. More of them were sitting closer to each other. I felt we had come at exactly the right time to look after them so they had more strength to continue with their grieving in the next few days and months to come.[6]
>
> 我注意到，当他们离开房间时，大家的脑袋没有像之前那样低垂，而且整体上显得更有活力了。更多人在演出过程中流下了眼泪。更多人彼此坐得更近了。我感到我们恰好在最恰当的时机来到这里照顾他们，使他们拥有更多力量，在接下来的几天乃至几个月里继续进行哀悼。[6]

There is another important aspect to this ritual holding function in playback. In the excitement of the spontaneous moment, audience members will fail to follow the “rules.” One will stand up and criticize the story. Another will want to jump onto the stage. A third will come to the teller's chair and try to manipulate the playback team. At such times the conductor must respond with speed and assurance, knowing when to be permissive and when to be a fierce keeper of the rules. To carry out this task requires not only knowledge and experience, but personal strength and even wisdom.

在一人一故事中，这种仪式性的承载功能还有另一个重要方面。在自发性时刻的兴奋中，观众往往会违反“规则”。有人会站起来批评故事内容；有人会想冲上舞台；还有人会走到讲述者的椅子旁，试图操控一人一故事团队。此时，领航员必须迅速而果断地作出回应，明辨何时该放任，何时该成为规则的坚定捍卫者。要完成这项任务，不仅需要知识和经验，更需要个人力量，甚至智慧。

## Shamanic Conducting 萨满式领航

One last point: it is important to observe that in order for discourse to take place and liveliness to be enhanced the keepers of the ritual not only need to hold the audience, but sometimes guide it as well. A strong, cohesive group, comprised of autonomous individuals, needs little guidance. They will create just the discourse they need, and find their own sources of liveliness during the course of the event. But in reality, few groups are so enlightened. Problematic individuals are often present, such as the man (it is usually a man) who has *many* comments, *many* feelings, *many* stories to tell.

最后一点：需要注意的是，为了促进对话的展开并增强现场活力，仪式主持者不仅需要吸引观众，有时还需要引导观众。一个由自主个体组成的强大而凝聚的群体，几乎不需要引导。他们会自行创造所需的对话，并在活动过程中找到属于自己的活力源泉。但现实中，鲜有如此开明（enlightened）的群体。往往会出现一些棘手的个体，例如那个（通常是男性）有*许多*评论、*许多*感受、*许多*故事要讲的人。

Sometimes the group as a whole is suffering from a belief or attitude that hinders the unfolding of the ritual experience. For example, they may be afraid of feelings, or they may feel that stories are only valid if they have a happy ending. Finally, it is often prevailing views of the society as a whole that can block the process. An example here might be the reluctance of those who consider themselves in any way isolated from the majority—such as an immigrant, a person of color, a very old person—to step up and tell.

有时，整个群体受某种信念或态度的困扰，从而阻碍了仪式体验的展开。例如，他们可能害怕面对情感，或者认为只有结局圆满的故事才具有价值。最后，往往是整个社会的主流观念阻碍了这一过程。一个例子是，那些自认为在某种程度上与主流社会隔绝的人——例如移民、有色人种、高龄老人——往往不愿站出来讲述。

In such situations, the playback performers need to have both courage and cleverness in steering around impediments. For example, in spite of playback's core dictum that “anyone can tell,” the conductor needs to *manage* (control) the man who wishes to tell too much, so that there will be space for others. The company needs to use all their artistry and humanity, as high practitioners of improvisational theatre and interactive social events, to charm and disarm any group into being open to feelings. They also need to withstand the need for resolution after a sad or painful story, knowing that inherent in their ritual process is the sure promise of transformation. Most importantly, they need always to keep a sharp nose out for prejudice and injustice, which often reveals itself as much from those who are silent as from the lips of those who speak. The ritual demands as much.

在这种情况下，一人一故事的表演者需要兼具勇气和智慧，才能克服这些障碍。例如，尽管一人一故事剧场的核心信条是“任何人都可以讲述”，但领航员仍需*管理*（控制）那些想讲得太多的人，以便为其他人留出空间。作为一人一故事剧场和互动社交活动的实践者，剧团需要运用全部的艺术造诣和人文关怀，以魅力打动并化解任何群体的戒备，使其敞开心扉接纳情感。他们还需抵御在悲伤或痛苦的故事结束后寻求“解决”的冲动，因为他们深知，转化的坚定承诺本就蕴含在这一仪式过程中。最重要的是，他们必须时刻保持敏锐的嗅觉，察觉偏见与不公——这些往往在沉默者身上显露得与在发言者口中同样明显。仪式本身就要求如此。

It is a commonplace in playback practice that the conductor and players must be “supportive” to the teller, but this idea of the performers as supportive can be clarified by looking at an example of social prejudice. In order to counteract an atmosphere of politeness, or even acceptance of a prejudiced statement or story from the audience, the team will need to exert a fierce allegiance to the idea of social equality. This will involve supporting those isolated or vulnerable *by being tough* with those who voice a prejudicial view. This does not mean casting aspersions; rather it means insisting on what is necessary to keep the ritual constructive.

在一人一故事实践中的一个常识是，领航员和表演者必须对讲述者“给予支持”，但通过一个社会偏见的例子，可以更清晰地阐明表演者作为支持者的这一理念。为了抵消观众中那种对带有偏见的言论或故事所表现出的客套态度甚至默许的态度，团队需要对社会平等的理念展现出坚定不移的忠诚。这将涉及*通过强硬态度*支持那些被孤立或处于弱势地位的人，同时对抗那些表达偏见观点的人。这并非指诋毁他人，而是坚持维护仪式建设性所必需的立场。

At a summertime performance on an American university campus, the conductor made immediate contact with a group of African-American teenagers who had arrived together by asking them who they were. Normally it might be thought of as too forward to single out a specific audience group, but in this case contact was preferable to silence and reinforcement of societal patterns of isolation and alienation (“Those black teenagers might cause trouble, let's just ignore them, what are they doing here, anyway.”) When one said they were part of a summer job program, the conductor, in preparation for a fluid sculpture, asked him how it was going. At this point, their counselor/supervisor broke in, saying it was going “Wonderfully.” Accepting this response, the conductor passed it on to the actors, and they acted out the counselor's “wonderful.” But it was important not to leave it at that, for the adult had answered for the (teenage) child. So the conductor repeated the question, saying, “Let's hear from one of the program participants.” The risk here on the artistic level was that the actors would get a second “wonderful,” and the improvisation might begin to feel boring. On the social level the risk was that *none* of the youngsters would want to speak publicly about how they felt.

在美国某大学校园的一次夏季演出中，领航员一见到一群结伴而来的非裔美国青少年，便立即主动与他们接触，询问他们的身份。通常情况下，单独点名特定观众群体可能会被视为过于唐突，但在这种情况下，主动接触总比保持沉默、从而强化社会中的孤立与疏离模式要好（“那些黑人青少年可能会惹麻烦，我们还是无视他们吧，他们到底来这里干什么？”）。当其中一人表示他们是暑期就业项目的一员时，领航员为了准备“流动塑像”环节，便询问他项目进展如何。此时，他们的辅导员/监督员插话道，项目进展“非常棒”。领航员接受了这一回答，并将它传达给演员们，演员们便演绎了辅导员所说的“非常棒”。但重要的是不能就此打住，因为是成年人代替（青少年）孩子作答了。于是领航员重复了问题，说道：“让我们听听项目参与者中的一位怎么说。”

What actually happened was that one boy did speak up. He said something interesting and full of feeling, which the actors then made into one of the high points of the show. The conductor's assertiveness paid off, and a prejudice concerning the rights of adults over children was not indulged.

实际上，确实有一个男孩站出来发言了。他说了一段既有趣又充满情感的话，演员们随后将这段话演绎成了演出中的一个高潮。领航员的果断态度取得了成效，那种认为成年人比儿童拥有更多权利的偏见也没有被纵容。

Another example of the shaman as guide took place during the Social Change workshop cited at the beginning of this essay. To recap the situation: there had been two stories told by whites about the role of blacks in their childhood lives, in which the tellers spoke from the perspective of embarrassed witnesses to prejudice. It was a strong theme. Undoubtedly more whites would come forward with stories that would help them assuage their sense of guilt. At this point, however, I intervened, deliberately asking for a story from one of the two blacks. Such intervention runs counter to the basic playback practice, emphasized earlier, of accepting stories from anyone at any time. This was also a request not without risk, for there was a distinct chance that the two singled out might refuse to tell. But the alternative was riskier—repeating an endemic prejudicial pattern of behavior that gave a voice to white citizens, but not blacks. Thus guidance was essential. In the event, an African-American told his story.

另一个关于“萨满”作为引导者的例子，发生在本文开头提到的“社会变革”工作坊中。回顾当时的情况：此前有两位白人讲述了黑人在他们童年生活中的角色，讲述者都是以目睹偏见却感到尴尬的旁观者身份发声的。这是一个强烈的主题。毫无疑问，会有更多白人站出来讲述故事，以此来缓解他们的内疚感。然而，就在此时，我介入其中，特意请两位黑人中的一位讲述故事。这种干预有悖于前文强调的一人一故事基本实践——即随时接受任何人的故事。这一请求也并非没有风险，因为被点名的两人很有可能拒绝讲述。但另一种选择风险更大——那将重蹈一种根深蒂固的、带有偏见的行为模式，即只给白人公民发声的机会，却排斥黑人。因此，引导是必不可少的。结果，一位非裔美国人讲述了他的故事。

The discourse continued in a constructive direction, towards the hearing of different voices, and vitality had a chance.

对话继续朝着建设性的方向发展，朝着倾听不同声音的方向迈进，生机由此得以展现。

## Conclusion 结语

For the second performance of our fledgling playback company, more than two decades ago, we made arrangements to visit the children's ward of a general hospital. The staff allowed us to come because they often welcomed volunteers of all sorts, and in their eyes we offered simply entertainment for the children. They had no idea of the ritual power of our form (or they might have had second thoughts). We wore clown-like overalls; we sang a children's song; we were funny and disarming. And yet… since our goal was something more than entertainment, we insisted on performing at visiting hour because we wanted parents to be present. We stuck to our form of inviting individual tellers, even if the bed had to be rolled forward for the child teller to be seen and heard. And we acted out stories—of the children, of the parents, and even the nursing staff. Two memories stand out for me of that day. First, the children told aspects of their experience one sensed they could not tell in ordinary conversation, such as the scary feeling of waiting in a holding room prior to an operation. Second, the experience transformed the atmosphere of the ward: prior to our performance, the children were lethargic and the parents worried behind their comforting smiles. But afterwards, there was eager conversation, and a much lighter mood prevailed that I would even call joyful. It was only our second playback performance, we hardly knew what we were doing, yet one could feel the power of the playback ritual working.

二十多年前，我们这个初创的一人一故事剧团准备进行第二次演出时，安排前往一家综合医院的儿科病房。医院工作人员允许我们进去，因为他们经常接待各类志愿者，在他们眼里，我们不过是为孩子们提供娱乐而已。他们完全不知道我们这种表演形式的仪式力量（否则他们可能会三思而后行）。我们穿着小丑般的连体工作服；唱着童谣；表现得既滑稽又平易近人。然而……由于我们的目标不仅仅是娱乐，我们坚持在探视时间演出，因为我们希望家长能在场。我们坚持邀请个别讲述者的形式，即使必须把病床推到前面，以便让小讲述者能被看到和听到。我们还演绎故事——关于孩子、关于家长，甚至关于护理人员的故事。那天有两段记忆让我印象深刻。首先，孩子们讲述了他们经历中的某些方面，能感觉到这些是他们在日常交谈中无法说出口的，比如在手术前于候诊室等待时那种令人恐惧的感觉。其次，这次体验改变了病房的气氛：演出前，孩子们无精打采，家长们在安慰性的微笑背后却满是忧虑。但演出结束后，大家热切地交谈着，整个病房弥漫着一种轻松得甚至可以说充满喜悦的氛围。那只是我们的第二次一人一故事剧场演出，我们几乎不知道自己在做什么，但人们能感受到一人一故事仪式所蕴含的力量正在发挥作用。

I came to playback theatre from the pursuit of experimental theatre in the period following the Vietnam War. I thought of myself as a theatre artist. In the course of playback's development, I studied psychodrama to learn the group process skills necessary to conduct interactive social events, recognizing how art and social interaction had to be skillfully blended in order to make playback work. Without fully realizing it, I strove during this time to teach my company, my students, and myself the demands of ritual. For it is the ritual component of playback theatre that takes it to our core being, helping us feel newly alive; and it is this ritual component that allows the kind of discourse necessary to transform a dysfunctional or outworn social order.

我是在越南战争结束后追求实验戏剧的过程中接触到一人一故事剧场的。我曾自视为一名戏剧艺术家。在一人一故事剧场的发展过程中，我研习了心理剧，以掌握开展互动性社会活动所需的群体过程技能，并认识到必须巧妙地将艺术与社会互动融合，才能让一人一故事剧场发挥作用。虽然当时并未完全意识到，但我在此期间一直努力向我的剧团、学生以及我自己传授仪式所要求的内涵。因为正是一人一故事剧场中的仪式成分，将其带入我们的内心深处，帮助我们重获新生；也正是这一仪式成分，促成了这一转化功能失调的或陈腐的社会秩序所必需的对话。

It happens through our stories. It happens through dance, image, and music. And it happens because of citizen actors who are willing to learn a challenging art on behalf of their communities.

这一切通过我们的故事实现，通过舞蹈、影像和音乐实现，更因为有那些愿意为社群学习这项充满挑战的艺术的“公民演员”而得以实现。
