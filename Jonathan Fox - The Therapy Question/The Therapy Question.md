# The Therapy Question 关于治疗的疑问

*By Jonathan Fox (2008)*

*乔纳森·福克斯 著（2008）*

Theatre fans look at us suspiciously and challenge, “Is this therapy?” while despite our statements to the contrary, psychodramatists and drama therapists claim us as their own.

戏剧爱好者们总是带着怀疑的目光审视我们，并质问道：“这算是一种治疗吗？”尽管我们一再否认，心理剧从业者和戏剧治疗师却仍将我们归入他们的范畴。

How to respond?

该如何回应？

I have always felt that playback theatre’s indefinability is positive. But it has stood in the way of many people, from audience-goers to funders, finding the right category for it.

我一直认为，一人一故事剧场的难以定义这一特点是积极的。但这也阻碍了许多人——从观众到资助者——为它找到合适的分类。

Playback theatre’s roots in the **oral tradition** is one cause of their difficulty. Oral traditional cultures tend to be holistic. The story the griot tells is a history lesson as well as an entertainment. The mask worn by the dancer is a tool to aid a healing process as well as an artistic creation.

一人一故事剧场植根于**口述传统**，这正是其难以界定的原因之一。口述传统文化往往具有整体性。格里奥（griot）讲述的故事既是一堂历史课，也是一种娱乐。舞者佩戴的面具既是一种辅助疗愈过程的工具，也是一种艺术创作。

Modern culture is more specialized. You practice art or you practice psychotherapy. If you do both, you certainly do them in different times and places.

现代文化则更加专业化。你要么从事艺术，要么从事心理治疗。如果你两者都做，肯定会在不同的时间和地点进行。

A second problem has to do with the **meaning of psychotherapy**, which is in many ways too narrow to accommodate playback theatre. Again, pt does not fit into the 50-minute, quiet-voiced, one-on-one model widely practiced in the northern world. It also does not fit into the medical model of cure to which most psychotherapy adheres: it does not aim to solve mental problems; nor is it private and protected.

第二个问题与**心理治疗的含义**有关，从许多方面来看，其定义过于狭隘，无法包容一人一故事剧场。同样，一人一故事剧场也不符合北方世界广泛采用的、时长50分钟、语调轻柔、一对一的模式。它也不符合大多数心理治疗所遵循的“治愈”式医学模式：它不以解决心理问题为目标；也不具有私密性和保护性。

Let me deal with two related questions - the connection between playback and psychodrama; and the therapeutic practice of playback theatre.

让我来探讨两个相关问题——一人一故事剧场与心理剧之间的联系；以及一人一故事剧场的治疗性实践。

Psychodrama informed and influenced playback theatre in its early development. But it does not define it. Moreno’s philosophy has much to offer, especially the concepts of spontaneity and sociometry. In some regions many pt practitioners are also trained in psychodrama (including myself). But this influence does not make playback a child of psychodrama.

心理剧在一人一故事剧场的早期发展中提供了启发并产生了影响，但它并不能给一人一故事剧场下定义。莫雷诺的哲学思想提供了许多宝贵见解，尤其是“自发性”和“社会计量法”的概念。在某些地区，许多一人一故事剧场的实践者也接受过心理剧的培训（包括我本人）。但这种影响并不意味着一人一故事剧场是心理剧的“子嗣”。

In some instances, psychodramatists, drama therapists, expressive therapists, and other psychotherapists make use of playback theatre as an adjunctive method in their clinical practice. This is a specialized application of playback that would rarely include performance and almost always involves some sort of adaptation to suit the particular therapeutic setting. In this usage, practiced by a therapist for therapeutic goals and in a therapeutic setting, it is fair to say that playback theatre is therapy.

在某些情况下，心理剧治疗师、戏剧治疗师、表达性治疗师及其他心理治疗师会将一人一故事剧场作为辅助方法应用于临床实践。这是一人一故事剧场的一种专业应用，通常很少包含表演环节，且几乎总是需要进行某种调整以适应特定的治疗环境。在此种由治疗师为实现治疗目标而在治疗环境中实施的用法中，可以说一人一故事剧场本身就是一种治疗。

By far and away the main use of playback theatre is in community settings. We speak of it as theatre; some also call it community cultural development; some, storytelling; some, education and training; some, communication. Most of us do not call it therapy.

迄今为止，一人一故事剧场的主要应用领域仍在社群环境中。我们称之为“剧场”；也有人称之为“社群文化发展”；有人称之为“故事讲述”；有人称之为“教育与培训”；还有人称之为“沟通”。我们大多数人并不将其称为“治疗”。

Yet playback theatre is broadly therapeutic. Of this there is no doubt. How so? This is how I see it:

然而，一人一故事剧场在广义上具有治疗作用。这一点毋庸置疑。为何如此？以下是我的看法：

**Identity-formation**. The need to tell one’s story is primal. Even more, it seems that telling our story helps greatly in defining ourselves to ourselves. This process is compatible with Boal’s concept of critical consciousness. The anthropologist Barbara Myerhoff talks about the value of “definitional ceremonies.” “Life histories,” she writes, “give people the opportunities to become visible and to enhance their reflexive consciousness.” So in being a teller, we become clearer about who we are; it is a vital act of affirmation.

**身份认同的形成**。讲述自身故事的渴望是与生俱来的。更重要的是，讲述自己的故事似乎极大地有助于我们向自己界定自我。这一过程与波瓦提出的“批判性意识”概念相契合。人类学家芭芭拉·迈尔霍夫（Barbara Myerhoff）谈到了“定义仪式”的价值。她写道：“生命史为人们提供了展现自我的机会，并能增强他们的反思意识。” 因此，作为讲述者，我们对“我是谁”有了更清晰的认识；这是一种至关重要的自我肯定行为。

**Dialogue and reconciliation**. In the sequence of stories told by any audience, a teller is always responding to what was told before. Tellers comment on previous stories in a complex pattern of alternative truth-telling (we call this linkage “the red thread” of a performance). Thus playback theatre turns out to be a good format for a group to share different perspectives on an issue. Moreover, the context of respectful listening that is so central to the pt process is a crucial condition for lessening conflict. During the playback theatre performance, we hear the narrative of the other.

**对话与和解**。在任何观众讲述的故事序列中，讲述者总是在回应之前讲述的内容。讲述者通过一种复杂的“替代性真相讲述（alternative truth-telling）”模式对先前故事进行评论（我们将这种关联称为表演中的“红线”）。因此，一人一故事剧场成为一个群体就某个议题（issue）分享不同视角的绝佳形式。此外，尊重倾听的氛围——这是一人一故事剧场过程的核心——是缓解冲突的关键条件。在一人一故事剧场表演中，我们倾听他人的叙述。

**Recovery**. Because of its gentleness, playback theatre is an effective way for an individual (and a group) to make the transition, to use the formulation of Judith Herman, from “traumatic memory” to “narrative memory”. Of course trauma is a psychotherapeutic problem. But it is also a social problem. In fact, civic leaders are often at a loss when they need powerful tools for healing a community-wide crisis, such as a natural disaster, or war. The fallback solution is too often to just get on with it and cope. However, I strongly suspect that without finding way to heal from the past, a community cannot creatively face its future.

**康复**。正因其温和的特质，借用朱迪思·赫尔曼的表述，一人一故事剧场是个人（及群体）实现从“创伤性记忆”向“叙事性记忆”过渡的有效途径。当然，创伤是一个心理治疗问题。但它同时也是一个社会问题。事实上，当社群领袖需要强有力的工具来疗愈整个社群的危机（如自然灾害或战争）时，往往束手无策。退而求其次的解决方案，往往只是继续前行并设法应对。然而，我强烈怀疑，如果找不到从过去中疗愈的方法，一个社群就无法富有创造性地面对未来。

**Moral imagination**. This concept, defined by peace building professor John Paul Lederach, involves “the capacity to imagine something rooted in the challenges of the real world yet capable of giving birth to that which does not yet exist.” In other words, we need moral imagination to imagine our future when the present seems full of problems and even hopeless. According to Viktor Frankl, for instance, it was the equivalent of moral imagination that enabled him and others to survive the WWII camps, while others, unable to see beyond the bleak and hopeless present, succumbed. It required moral imagination for blacks and whites in South Africa to plan for a peaceful transition from apartheid. Playback theatre, with its spontaneous telling and enactment, invites and inspires such envisioning.

**道德想象力**。这一概念由和平建设学教授约翰·保罗·莱德拉赫（John Paul Lederach）提出，指“一种既植根于现实世界的挑战，又能孕育出尚未存在之事物的想象能力”。换言之，当当下似乎问题重重甚至令人绝望时，我们需要道德想象力来构想我们的未来。例如，据维克多·弗兰克尔所述，正是这种类似于道德想象力的特质，使他和他人得以在二战集中营中幸存下来，而那些无法看穿阴郁绝望的当下、只顾眼前的人则最终屈服了。南非的黑人和白人要规划从种族隔离制度向和平过渡的进程，同样需要道德想象力。一人一故事剧场通过自发的讲述和演绎，激发并鼓舞着这种构想。

For the most part psychotherapy, focusing as it does on curing individuals, stays apolitical. However, when the focus is community-based, questions of social justice and historical oppression demand attention. Who has space to tell their story? Who listens? Who does not? The value pt places on providing access to anyone, even those traditionally silent, makes it a powerful tool for social change. One can say that playback theatre treats not (merely) the individual, but also the society.

大多数情况下，心理治疗因其专注于治愈个体，通常保持非政治性。然而，当关注点转向社群时，社会正义与历史压迫等问题便不容忽视。谁拥有讲述自己故事的空间？谁在倾听？谁又置若罔闻？PT 重视为任何人——甚至那些传统上被沉默的人——提供发声渠道，这使其成为推动社会变革的强大工具。可以说，一人一故事剧场治疗的对象不仅（仅仅）是个人，更是整个社会。

Thus while the framework of playback theatre is broader than psychotherapy, and it does not conform to some of its definitional guidelines, pt’s effectiveness at ego-building, resolving conflict, working with trauma, and helping people make positive decisions about their future suggests that it can accomplish many of the objectives of psychotherapy.

因此，尽管一人一故事剧场的框架比心理治疗更为宽广，且并不完全符合心理治疗的部分定义准则，但它在建立自我认同、化解冲突、处理创伤以及帮助人们为未来做出积极决策方面的有效性表明，它能够实现心理治疗的许多目标。

There is so much that we do not yet know about how playback works. So far most of the evidence is anecdotal. Merely to know in our hearts how effective it can be is not enough. We need to develop language suitable for an approach that provides a community healing ritual. We need rich descriptions and detailed analyses of a process that is highly dynamic and ephemeral.

关于一人一故事的运作机制，我们还有太多未知之处。迄今为止，大多数证据仅停留在轶事层面。仅仅在内心深处知道它有多么有效是不够的。我们需要发展一套适合这种提供社群疗愈仪式的表演方式的语言体系。我们需要对这一高度动态且转瞬即逝的过程进行丰富的描述和详细的分析。

What actually happens to the body of the teller seeing her story? What exactly are the processes of communication between audience members and performers? What kind of consciousness occurs among those present in a pt performance? What is the effect of witnessing the telling and enactment of personal stories? What role does the performance arc/structure/ritual/ceremony play in the creation of a lasting positive experience for the participants?

当讲述者目睹自己的故事时，她的身体究竟会发生什么变化？观众与表演者之间的沟通过程究竟是怎样的？在 PT 表演现场，在场者会产生怎样的意识状态？见证个人故事的讲述与演绎会产生什么影响？表演的弧线、结构、仪式或典礼在为参与者创造持久的积极体验方面发挥着什么作用？

The Israeli playback actor Uri Alon, who is also a professor of molecular biology and physics of complex systems, has an interest in investigating how and why playback theatre works. Other scholars are writing dissertations on the same question from different angles. This is an exciting development.

以色列一人一故事剧场演员乌里·阿隆（Uri Alon）——他同时也是一位分子生物学与复杂系统物理学教授——致力于探究一人一故事剧场如何运作以及为何有效。其他学者也正从不同角度就同一问题撰写学位论文。这是一项令人振奋的发展。

Meanwhile, we continue to argue that while playback heals, it is not therapy, and like the dance and drama of traditional societies, we defy the distinction between art and healing.

与此同时，我们继续主张：尽管一人一故事剧场具有疗愈作用，但它并非治疗；正如传统社会中的舞蹈与戏剧一样，我们拒绝将艺术与疗愈割裂开来。
