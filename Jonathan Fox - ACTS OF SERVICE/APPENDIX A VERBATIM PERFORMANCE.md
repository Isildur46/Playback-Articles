The performers in this account belong to the original Playback company. I acted as conductor. It was the second show of the day and took place in early evening. The audience nubered about 40 persons. It is remembered as a serviceable occasion by the company, neither among the worst nor the best.

Because of the importance of recognizing the oral characteristics of the playtext, I have “cleaned up” the transcription somewhat less than might normally be the case.

HUSBAND: (*Breathing with greater and greater difficulty.*)

MARY: The most important thing...

HUSBAND: (*Stuggling, MUSICIAN plays violin and sings wordless keening voice through to end of scene.*)

DAUGHTER: Daddy, I love you.

HUSBAND: I can't... I'm able... I'm able.. I... I....

MARY: We love you so much. That's the most important thing·

FRIENDS: You should have gotten some more tests. I told you it would happen. Why did you wait so long!

MARY: (Shouting) Shut up! You don't understand anything!

FRIENDS: I told you it would happen. You should have...

MARY: You don't understand anything.

DAUGHTER:(TO MARY) Don't listen. It never happened to them.(*MUSIC.*)

(*Silence. Then applause. Afer a quick look of TELLER acknowledgment, TELLER goes quickly to her seat inaudience.*)

CONDUCTOR: Has anyone else been in that kind of situation? (*Acknowledging one AUDIENCE MEMBER*) Yes? (*Acknowledging another*) Yah? (*ACTORS strike scene and sit on boxes, facing audience.*) Well, we're coming to the end... of our time to be together. (*MUSIC.*) I said at the beginning that we're going to tell stories and try to recreate theatre as it was in the olden days. And in the olden days, of course, the stories were all about heroes, and heroic deeds, and huge and wonderful events. Our stories are humbler. But they're for *our* time. And you tonight, who have shared your moments and your experiences, have shared your wisdom and your love and your own heroism. Thank you very much. (*Applause, as ACTORS join CONDUCTOR in bow.*)
