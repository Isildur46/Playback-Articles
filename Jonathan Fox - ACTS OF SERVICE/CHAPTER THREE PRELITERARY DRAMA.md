# CHAPTER THREE PRELITERARY DRAMA 第三章 前文字戏剧

A rtists see beyond their times, often pointing to the future by reaching “back.” Thus Picasso drew direct Linspiration from African masks; Stravinsky broke with classical tradition in his “Rites of Spring;” Artaud looked to Bali and Mexico for a reinvigoration of theatrical ideas. The intelligentsia, however, firmly wedded to a literary concept of art, lagged far behind. Astoundingly, it has been only in the 1980s that the clear connection between the French modernists and tribal art has been formally acknowledged and appreciated. Thus Gauguin's shift to Polynesia was much more than a flight *from*; it was also very much a voyage towards, and a large number of European modern artists, benefiting from exhibitions and private sales of tribal objects, joined him in spirit.[1] While the availability of tribal objects provided the painters and sculptors with tangible sources of inspiration, the problem has been more difficult for those in the performing arts. In music, jazz has provided a channel for contact with primal energies and ideas-and we can appreciate, in passing, the importance of improvisation in jazz--but for the most part the Doris Humphreys, the Arnold Schoenbergs, and the Jacques Copeaus have had to look into themselves, into that inner bridge, to help them find an alternative to the established cultural forms.

艺术家们能洞见其所处时代之外的事物，往往通过“回溯”来指明未来。因此，毕加索直接从非洲面具中汲取灵感；斯特拉文斯基在《春之祭》中打破了古典传统；阿尔托则从巴厘岛和墨西哥寻求戏剧理念的复兴。然而，知识界因固守“文学主义”的艺术观念，远远落后于时代。令人惊讶的是，直到20世纪80年代，法国现代主义者与部落艺术之间的明确联系才被正式承认和认可。因此，高更移居波利尼西亚绝不仅仅是一次*逃离*；它更是一次向往的旅程，而众多欧洲现代艺术家也通过部落艺术品的展览和私人交易从中受益，并在精神上与他同行。[1]虽然部落艺术品的广泛流通为画家和雕塑家提供了切实的灵感来源，但对于表演艺术工作者而言，这一问题却更为棘手。在音乐领域，爵士乐为接触原始能量与思想提供了一条渠道——顺便提一句，我们可以领略爵士乐中即兴创作的重要性——但就大多数情况而言，多丽丝·汉弗莱斯、阿诺德·勋伯格和雅克·科波等人不得不向内探寻，借助那座内在的桥梁，以帮助他们找到既定文化形式的替代方案。

[1] According to Small, the Paris. World Exhibition of 1889 featured performances, providing Europeans with an opportunity to experience preliterary music and dance (page 35). For an interesting account of Gauguin's mind-set, see Varnedoe, in Rubin (page 187): “Gauguin saw the strength and wholeness of primal life not only as the privilege of simpler folk and foreign societies but as a birthright recoverable within himself.”

[1] 据斯莫尔（Small）所述，1889年巴黎世界博览会设有表演环节，为欧洲人提供了体验前文学时期音乐与舞蹈的机会（第35页）。关于高更心态的有趣描述，参见瓦内多（Varnedoe）在鲁宾（Rubin）著作中的论述（第187页）： “高更认为，原始生活的力量与完整性不仅是质朴民众和异域社会的特权，更是他自身可以重拾的天赋权利。”

Of all the arts, theatre has perhaps been tardiest in turning back to oral values, perhaps because theatre, of its very nature, has always to some extent incorporated them. A more telling possibility is that theatre's special potential to transform its audience has made a loosening of the literary hammerlock an especially risky business. The situation is changing now. A keen interest in connecting with nonliterary theatrical experience motivates a number of directors, especially Peter Brook, and is an inspiration for Richard Schechner's latest book, *Between Theater and Anthropology*.

在所有艺术形式中，戏剧或许是最迟回归口述传统价值观念，这也许是因为戏剧以其本质而言，在某种程度上始终包含着这些价值。更有说服力的解释可能是：戏剧具有改变观众的特殊潜力，这使得摆脱文字的铁腕控制成为一项尤为冒险的尝试。如今，这种状况正在发生变化。对与非文字性戏剧体验建立联系的浓厚兴趣，激励着许多导演——尤其是彼得·布鲁克——并激发了理查德·谢克纳最新著作《在戏剧与人类学之间》的创作灵感。

Reporting on his research, Schechner knows the lesson Boas never understood. He is emphatic on the impossibility from our modernist perspective of capturing the nonliterary theatre past. For instance, he is interested in the dances of the Mudmen of Papua-New Guinea, but has to conclude that an understanding of the etiology of their theatrical inspiration is “not locatable.” Commenting on the 30,000-year-old Indian Agnicayana ritual, which is still performed and which has been filmed, he writes: “What I am saying is that no matter what textual documentation exists, we do not know what Agnicayana was.”[2]

在汇报其研究时，谢克纳领悟到了博阿斯从未理解的道理。他强调，从我们的现代主义视角来看，捕捉非文字性的戏剧过去是不可能的。例如，他对巴布亚新几内亚“泥人”的舞蹈很感兴趣，但不得不得出结论：对其戏剧灵感成因的理解“无法定位”。在评论那项已有3万年历史、至今仍在举行且已被拍摄下来的印度阿格尼卡亚纳（Agnicayana）仪式时，他写道：“我的意思是，无论存在何种文字记载，我们都不知道阿格尼卡亚纳究竟是什么。”[2]

[2] Schechner 1985,59, 77.

[2] 谢克纳 1985，第59、77页。

Accepting the ineluctable nature of our ignorance, it nevertheless feels useful to glean what we can about preliterary drama in order to clarify our understanding of contemporary scriptless theatre.

尽管我们不得不承认这种无知是不可避免的，但为了澄清我们对当代无剧本戏剧的理解，从前文字戏剧中汲取所能获取的信息仍具有现实意义。

## THE PRELITERARY DRAMA PARADIGM 前文字戏剧范式

Based on experience in Asia and my own reading, by no means exhaustive, I have adduced the following characteristics for preliterary drama (to what extent the examples cited below can be called *theatre* will be addressed in chapter 15):

基于我在亚洲的亲身经历以及我个人的阅读（虽远非详尽无遗），我总结出了前文字戏剧的以下特征（下文所举的例子在多大程度上可以被称为*戏剧*，将在第15章中讨论）：

*Trance-inducing*. Preliterary drama seems often to involve actors propelling themselves into an altered state, where, fuelled by adrenaline, they take on a transcendent power. Turner calls this condition “ergotropic behavior;” the great Sioux healer Black Elk refers to it when he describes his feeling of being “above the ground” after the enactment of a vision dance; the Balinese call it passing into “the other thought.”[3]

*诱发恍惚状态*。前文字戏剧似乎常常涉及演员将自己推入一种改变的状态，在那里，在肾上腺素的驱动下，他们获得了一种超然的力量。特纳将这种状态称为“能动性行为”；苏族伟大的治疗师黑麋鹿在描述他表演“幻视之舞”后感到“凌驾于大地之上”的感受时，也提到了这一点；巴厘岛人则称之为进入“另一种思维”[3]。

[3] See Turner 1981, 9; Neihardt, 147; de Zoete & Spies, 24.

[3] 参见特纳 1981，第 9 页；内哈特，第 147 页；德·佐特与斯皮斯，第 24 页。

An illustration of how this process works can be observed today among the Kung bush people of the Kalahari desert, who engage in all-night dances in which individuals, dancing around a fire, go into an ecstatic state called “kia.” Once in kia the Kung will do “inhuman” feats, such as throw themselves on the flames, without apparent harm. In kia they will also lay hands on the circle of watchers, making use of their temporary powers to draw out sickness and malcontent. While kia produces a deep catharsis, leading to a relaxed, pleasurable postdance state, attaining kia is never easy, for according to the Kung, the spiritual energy (“num”) needed causes great pain as it rises. Thus being a Kung healer requires courage, for the num is fearsome, and once kia is reached the individual becomes an intermediary between the community members and all-powerful cosmic powers.

关于这一过程如何运作的实例，今天可以在卡拉哈里沙漠的孔格丛林部落中观察到：他们进行通宵达旦的舞蹈，参与者围着篝火起舞，进入一种被称为“基亚”（kia）的狂喜状态。一旦进入基亚状态，孔格人便会做出“超乎常人”的壮举，例如扑向火焰，却似乎毫发无损。在“基亚”状态下，他们还会将手按在围观者的圈子上，利用自己暂时获得的力量将疾病和不满情绪驱除。虽然“基亚”能带来深刻的宣泄，使人进入一种放松、愉悦的舞后状态，但达到“基亚”绝非易事，因为据孔格人所言，所需的灵性能量（“努姆”）在升起时会带来巨大的痛苦。因此，成为一名孔氏治疗者需要勇气，因为“努姆”令人畏惧，一旦达到“基亚”状态，个人便成为社群成员与全能宇宙力量之间的中介。

The trance behavior of preliterary dancers often involves intense physical activity. It is highly athletic, requiring the performer to go to the limits of his or her physical powers.

前文字时期舞者的恍惚行为往往涉及剧烈的身体活动。这极具运动性，要求表演者将身体能力发挥到极限。

A corollary of this emphasis on trance is a de-emphasis on the spoken word--that is, on language as a primary medium. For example, the num of the Kung dancers is primed by a group of singers whose rhythm is vital (it has been timed at eight pulses per second) but whose words are nonsense. It is almost as if using sensible language in the singing might work against the attainment of a trance state. It is similar with Noh, Topeng, and other dramatic forms which utilize archaic or hieratic language that their audiences cannot understand.

这种对恍惚状态的强调，其必然结果便是对口头语言——即作为主要媒介的语言——的淡化。例如，孔舞者的“努姆”是由一群歌手激发出来的，他们的节奏至关重要（经测定为每秒八拍），但歌词却是无意义的。这几乎就像是在歌唱中使用通俗语言反而会阻碍进入恍惚状态。能剧、托彭（Topeng）及其他戏剧形式也是如此，它们使用观众无法理解的古语或神圣语言。

Obviously the comprehension of what is said or sung is not what the drama is about.[4]

显然，理解所说或所唱的内容并非戏剧的重点。[4]

[4] See Katz.

[4] 参见卡茨（Katz）。

To be sure, many forms of preliterary drama utilize language, but we can be sensitive to language's different purposes. In Africa today, writes Scott Kennedy in *In Search of African Theatre*, classical theatre is often “loose and improvisatory” and “repetition is both expected and desired,” confirming, as we might expect, that the objectives are not conciseness of word and scene as dictated by literary needs.[5] Repetitiveness will release words from their meaning and contribute to trance induction. We can also recall Walter Ong's emphasis in the last chapter on the phenomenological impact of the spoken word. Thus the great simplicity of language in popular forms such as Mummers Plays, crude to our educated ear, may catalyze altered states as much as prayers can.

诚然，许多前文学戏剧形式都运用了语言，但我们可以敏锐地察觉语言的不同目的。斯科特·肯尼迪在《寻觅非洲戏剧》中写道，当今非洲的古典戏剧往往“松散且即兴”，且“重复既是在预期内的，也是大家渴望的”，这正如我们所料，证实了其目的并非文字需求所要求的词语与场景的简洁性。[5]重复性将使语言摆脱其既定含义，从而有助于诱发恍惚状态。我们还可以回顾沃尔特·翁在上一章中对言语现象学影响的强调。因此，在“马默剧(Mummers Plays)”等通俗形式中，语言的极简风格——尽管在我们受过教育的听众耳中显得粗糙——其催化恍惚状态的能力不亚于祈祷。

[5] Kennedy, 43,47.

[5] 肯尼迪，第43、47页。

*Communal*. Above all, preliterary theatre is intimate. The Kung live in clans of 15 to 30 people, and everyone comes to the dances (if the mood is not right--for example, if there has been dissension in the camp, they will wait until the motivation for the dance has been restored), Peter Brook recounts an event he witnessed in the Near East:

*集体性*。最重要的是，前文字时期的戏剧具有亲密性。孔族人以15至30人的氏族为单位生活，每个人都会参加舞蹈（如果氛围不对——例如，营地内曾发生过分歧，他们就会等待，直到舞蹈的动机得以恢复），彼得·布鲁克讲述了他亲眼目睹的近东地区的一件事：

> On a certain day the carpenter and the village shopkeeper and the others go out into a field, and the whole village gathers around them and they play-sometimes for two hours, sometimes for three days or five days--a cycle of plays that have existed for several hundred years... I saw in a remote Iranian village one of the strangest things I have ever seen in theatre: A group of four hundred villagers, the entire population of the place, sitting under a tree and passing from laughter to outright sobbing-although they knew perfectly well the end of the story... and when [Hussein] was martyred the theatre form became a truth--there was no difference between past and present...[6]
>
> 某一天，木匠、村里的店主和其他人走到田野里，全村人围拢在他们周围，他们便开始表演——有时持续两小时，有时长达三日或五日——上演一套已流传数百年之久的戏剧循环…… 我在伊朗一个偏远的村庄里，目睹了戏剧史上最奇特的一幕：四百名村民——也就是全村人——坐在一棵树下，情绪从欢笑转为放声痛哭——尽管他们对故事的结局了如指掌……而当[侯赛因]殉难时，这种戏剧形式化作了现实——过去与现在之间再无界限…… [6]

[6] Brook, 1979, 52.

[6] 布鲁克，1979，第52页。

In such an intimate context, the circumstance of people who know each other well joining in an extraordinary occasion energizes the impact of the drama. The metalevel of community unites synergistically with the story level of the play, so that past and present become fused. As Black Elk comments after the enactment of one of his visions: “The experience was not a long one, but it had great meaning, because it made a picture of the relation between the people and the bison, and the power was in the meaning.”[7]

在如此亲密的氛围中，彼此熟识的人们共同参与这一非凡时刻，使戏剧的冲击力倍增。社群这一元层面与戏剧的故事层面产生了协同效应，从而使过去与现在融为一体。正如黑麋鹿在重现其一则幻视后所言：“这段体验虽短暂，却意义深远，因为它勾勒出了人类与野牛之间的关系图景，而力量正蕴藏于这一意义之中。”[7]

[7] Neihardt, 174.

[7] 内哈特，174页。

This intimacy, clearly, will to some extent be affected if not broken by the presence of a stranger. American clown Ron Jenkins, keenly feeling his outlander position during his year in a Balinese village as a student of Topeng, notes that his presence was enough to change the quality of a performance (as it may well have been for Peter Brook in Iran and Richard Katz, the psychologist who studied the Kung in South Africa).[8]

显然，这种亲密感在某种程度上会因陌生人的出现而受到影响，甚至被打破。美国小丑罗恩·詹金斯在巴厘岛的一个村庄作为托彭（Topeng）学员生活了一年，期间他敏锐地感受到自己作为外来者的处境，并指出他的存在足以改变表演的质感（这可能也适用于在伊朗的彼得·布鲁克，以及在南非研究孔族（Kung）的心理学家理查德·卡茨）。[8]

[8] Jenkins, 49.

[8] 詹金斯，第49页。

Secondly, preliterary theatre tends to be highly inclusive. Black Elk might ask anyone in the tribe to take part in an enactment; he himself was an actor for the first time as a youngster when he and five other boys were asked to be bears in a curing ceremony. In Indonesia, girls are featured performers without any training. Among the Oglala Sioux, anyone could become a healer once he or she had a vision. The situation among the Kung is similar. Katz writes: “The Kung wish all persons to have access to num, either by being healers, or being related to healers.” Apparently 60 per cent of the clan experience kia.[9]

其次，前文字时期的戏剧往往具有高度包容性。黑麋鹿可能会邀请部落中的任何人参与表演；他自己年轻时首次担任演员，当时他和另外五个男孩被邀请在驱邪仪式中扮演熊。在印度尼西亚，女孩们即使未经任何训练也能成为主要表演者。在奥格拉拉苏族中，任何人只要获得过神启，就能成为治疗师。孔族的情况与此类似。卡茨写道：“孔族希望所有人都能接触‘努姆’（num），无论是作为治疗师，还是与治疗师有亲属关系。”显然，该氏族中有60%的人经历过“基亚”（kia）。[9]

[9] Katz, 35.

[9] 卡茨，第35页。

*Redressive*. We have begun to use “healer” synonymously with “dancer” or “actor,” and for a good reason. The purpose of enactment in a preliterary culture always concerns matters vital to the well-being of the tribe, whether exorcising evil, vouchsafing crops, preparing for war, or celebrating a new season. Victor Turner has elaborated a thesis to explain the vital role of drama in social development which emphasizes drama's role in “redressing” breaches in the communal fabric. Ron Jenkins experienced one manifestation of this process first hand: “Eventually I came to understand that many village conflicts are aired with comic perspective in the temple dramas. It was an important mechanism for restoring equilibrium to the community.” It is important to keep in mind when considering this statement that the dramas Jenkins witnessed were always traditional; nevertheless, the intimacy of the context enabled performers to make very personal comments to their audience, either by choice of story, by adlibbed remarks, or even a pregnant glance at a particular person at a particular moment in the course of the performance. In such ways, the stories took on contemporaneity, and actors became instruments of community mental health.

*修复性*。我们已开始将“疗愈者”与“舞者”或“演员”视为同义词，这自有其道理。在前文字文化中，表演的目的始终关乎部落福祉的核心事务，无论是驱邪、祈求五谷丰登、备战，还是庆祝新季节的到来。维克多·特纳（Victor Turner）提出了一项论点，用以阐释戏剧在社会发展中的关键作用，该论点强调戏剧在“修复”社群纽带破裂方面的作用。罗恩·詹金斯（Ron Jenkins）亲身体验了这一过程的一种表现形式：“最终我明白，许多村庄冲突都会在神庙戏剧中以喜剧视角得以展现。这是恢复社群平衡的重要机制。” 在思考这一论述时，必须牢记詹金斯所见证的戏剧始终是传统的；尽管如此，这种亲密的情境使表演者能够向观众表达非常个人的见解——无论是通过故事的选择、即兴的台词，还是在表演过程中某个特定时刻对某个人投以意味深长的目光。通过这些方式，故事获得了共时性（contemporaneity），演员也成为了社群心理健康的促进者（instruments）。

Additionally, we can draw on our understanding of oral culture to appreciate that dramatic enactment was (and is) one way that the community communicated value. When the dancers forget how to go into trance, the actors lose touch with the story, the shaman turns to drink, or the griot gets a job, then the entire community risks disintegration.

此外，我们可以借助对口述文化的理解来认识到，戏剧表演曾是（且至今仍是）社群传递价值观的一种方式。当舞者忘记如何进入恍惚状态、演员与故事脱节、萨满沉溺于酒醉，或是格里奥（griot）去谋求一份工作时，整个社群便面临解体的风险。

*Attuned to the environment*. Finally, preliterary theatre was an outdoor experience, very much in nature rather than apart from it. The movement of the clouds across the sky, the sound of the wind in the leaves, the smell of dung and firewood-all were basic to a theatrical experience that aimed to make contact directly with cosmic forces and in which the performers were considered intermediaries. The actors' effort was not to surpass nature, nor to ignore it, but to find an intersection with its hidden powers. Thus, there was a value in adapting to existing circumstances, for aside from the practical necessity of using what space was available, whether village green or great lord's hall, a spiritual treasure might lie hidden in any rock or tree. One did not remove them lightly.

*与环境和谐共振*。最后，前文字时期的戏剧是一种户外体验，它深深植根于自然之中，而非与自然割裂。天空中流转的云彩、风拂过树叶的声响、粪便与柴火的气味——所有这些都是戏剧体验的基础，这种体验旨在直接接触宇宙力量，而表演者则被视为媒介。演员的努力既不是为了超越自然，也不是为了忽视自然，而是为了找到与自然隐秘力量的交汇点。因此，适应现有环境具有重要价值——除了利用现有空间（无论是村庄的绿地还是大领主的厅堂）这一实际必要性之外，任何岩石或树木中都可能隐藏着精神宝藏。人们不会轻易移走它们。

Related to this interest in attunement is a concern to connect with the local culture. Jenkins writes that when his Topeng troupe traveled, the performance was preceded by socializing which was not at all frivolous, for it was a time for the actors to “acclimate themselves to the village, the temple, and their hosts.”[10]

与这种对调和的关注相关的是对连接当地文化的重视。詹金斯写道，当他的托彭（Topeng）剧团巡演时，演出前会有社交活动，这绝非轻浮之举，因为这是演员们“适应村庄、寺庙及其东道主”的时机。[10]

[10] Jenkins, 53.

[10] 詹金斯，第53页。

We will utilize this four-cornered picture of preliterary drama, with its appreciation of trance, communal interaction, redressive outcome, and environmental adaptation, as a reference point for discussing today's oral theatre.

我们将以这种关于前文字戏剧的四个要点——即对恍惚状态、集体互动、修复性结局以及环境适应的重视——作为参考点，来探讨当今的口述戏剧。
