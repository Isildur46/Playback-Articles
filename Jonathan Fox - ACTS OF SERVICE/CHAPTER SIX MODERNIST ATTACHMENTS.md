# CHAPTER SIX MODERNIST ATTACHMENTS 第六章 现代主义的依附

A s awhole, the NST movement has been very much caught in the literary-minded idea of specialization, and nowhere is this more noticeable than in considering the concept of redressive purpose. Thus Schechner, writing about his workshop process in 1973, takes the trouble to insist that his highly subjective and interactive approach is not psychodrama. Similarly, actress Valerie Harper says about Viola Spolin: “Her work is for anybody, for everybody. It's therapeutic, though not therapy. She doesn't want it in any way compared to psychodrama.” Although Peter Brook mentions psychodrama positively (“A true image of necessary theatre-going I know is a psychodrama session in an asylum”), his allusion is closer to Sade and Charenton than Moreno and St. Elizabeths; the dramatic catalyst conjured up is an Artaudian “delirium” rather than a ritual of healing.[1] Thus it would appear that both in experimental and educational theatre circles, identification with therapy has been a threat. There have been practical reasons for this. For a theatre company's work to be called therapeutic may mean rejection from an arts council, or to be called psychological may get a teacher in trouble with authorities perennially afraid of political and even legal repercussions. I suspect, however, above and beyond a politic purpose in publicly disavowing therapy, there exists a deep-seated fear of its implications, especially with regard to *feelings*. We will say more about this in a moment.

总体而言，NST 运动在很大程度上陷入了文字化的专业化观念之中，这一点在探讨“修复性目的”这一概念时表现得尤为明显。因此，谢克纳在1973年撰文描述其工作坊过程时，特意强调他那种高度主观且互动性强的方法并非心理剧。同样，女演员瓦莱丽·哈珀在谈到维奥拉·斯波林时说：“她的工作面向任何人，面向所有人。它具有治疗性，但并非治疗。她不希望它以任何方式被与心理剧相提并论。”尽管彼得·布鲁克对心理剧持肯定态度（“我所知道的真正必要的观剧体验，是在精神病院参加一场心理剧疗程”），但他所暗指的更接近萨德和沙朗通，而非莫雷诺和圣伊丽莎白医院；其所唤起的戏剧性催化剂是一种阿尔托式的“狂乱”（delirium），而非疗愈仪式。[1]由此看来，无论在实验戏剧还是教育戏剧圈内，与心理治疗挂钩都曾是一种威胁。这背后存在实际原因。如果一个剧团的作品被称为“治疗性”的，可能会遭到艺术委员会的拒绝；而如果被称为“心理性”的，可能会让教师陷入麻烦，因为当局始终担心会引发政治甚至法律上的后果。不过，我怀疑，除了在公开场合否认与治疗有关的政治目的之外，还存在一种对其含义的深层次恐惧，尤其是关于*情感*的恐惧。我们稍后将对此进行更深入的探讨。

There has been an equivalent, though perhaps not as deep, mistrust of “art” on the part of certain sectors of the educational and community theatres. Owen Kelly, a socialist who sees “art” as a vehicle for appropriation, puts it this way: “The term art must therefore be removed, in our practice, from any imagined relationship with a polar opposite called nonart.” The educational theatre rejected early the artistic tradition's focus on the “talented” individual, and in turn, the converse pertained. In the artistic campShaw articulated a common viewpoint with his “those who can, do; those who can't, teach” adage; in fact I would say that many teachers, especially at the college level, buy into this attitude and think of themselves, ruefully, as artists manqué.[2]

教育戏剧和社群戏剧的某些领域对“艺术”也抱有类似的不信任，尽管程度或许没有那么深。奥文·凯利（Owen Kelly）是一位将“艺术”视为掠夺工具的社会主义者，他这样阐述道：“因此，在我们的实践中，‘艺术’这一术语必须摆脱与所谓‘非艺术’这一对立面之间任何想象中的联系。” 教育戏剧很早就摒弃了艺术传统中对“有才华”个体的关注，反之亦然。在艺术阵营中，肖（Shaw）通过“能者为之，不能者教之”这句格言阐明了普遍观点；事实上，我认为许多教师——尤其是大学层面的教师——认同这种态度，并带着一丝惆怅地将自己视为“未成名的艺术家”。[2]

Eric Bentley has argued that art, education, and therapy should be thought of not as opposed, but rather “overlapping,” and it would seem that the fear of contamination from other camps has been lessening in recent years. For example, Schechner's attitude toward the therapeutic is much more welcoming in *Between Theater and Anthropology* (1985)than in his previous books (could his own studies of preliterary drama have modified his viewpoint?),[3] Nevertheless, the thrust of modern attitudes, both on the part of practitioners and the milieus in which they work, is to draw lines and resist holistic definition.

## MISTRUST OF CATHARSIS 对宣泄的不信任

We have observed the extent to which theNST movement has rejected the texuality of the literary theatre, but avoiding plays is not the same as inducing trance, a prime feature of our preliterary drama model. We have already observed that in preliterary enactment, intense physical activity over many hours can cause ecstatic states which in turn produces a “moment of transcendence,” after which there is a pleasurable state of “unwinding and untying.”[5] This post-performance feeling was integrally connected with the redressive purpose of preliterary drama.

我们已经观察到，NST 运动在多大程度上拒绝了文字戏剧的文本性，但回避剧本并不等同于诱发恍惚状态——这正是我们“前文字戏剧”模型的核心特征。我们此前已指出，在前文字演绎中，持续数小时的剧烈身体活动会引发狂喜状态，进而产生“超然的瞬间”，随后便进入一种愉悦的“放松与解脱”状态。[5]这种演出后的感受与前文字戏剧的修复性目的密不可分。

[5] Katz, 97, 203.

Unfortunately, practitioners of NST have generally rejected catharsis-producing trance. In the experimental theatre, for example, Artaud's dictum, “Squeeze a man hard and you will always find something that is not human,” inspired many theatre companies, outraged at the complacencies of modern culture, to develop a theatre of confrontation which shocked, even outraged their audiences. The effect on spectators was assuredly emotional, but far from cathartic. For Artaud, who lived on the edge of madness, all freedom was “dark, and infallibly identified with sexual freedom.” Thus the “theatre of cruelty” underscored the violent, the ugly, the sexually perverse.

遗憾的是，NST 的实践者们普遍排斥那种能产生宣泄效果的恍惚状态。例如，在实验戏剧领域，阿尔托的那句名言——“用力挤压一个人，你总会发现其中非人的东西”，激励了许多对现代文化自满状态深感愤慨的剧团，促使他们发展出一种对抗性戏剧，这种戏剧令观众感到震惊，甚至愤慨。这种表演对观众产生的影响无疑是情感上的，但远非宣泄性的。对于生活在疯狂边缘的阿尔托而言，一切自由都是“黑暗的，且必然与性自由画上等号”。因此，“残酷戏剧”着重突出了暴力、丑陋和性变态。

Brecht took another tack. For him the theatre of catharsis was a means of robbing the spectator of an essential autonomy as a thinking person. His idea of *“verfremdung*,” a stylistic element of audience estrangement, was designed to encourage the viewer to reflect on, rather than identify with, the events on stage. Boal, who adopted this position, writes: ”Brecht wants the theatrical spectacle to be the beginning of action; the equilibrium should be sought by transforming society, and not by purging the individual of his just demands and needs.”[6] In this view catharsis is a manipulation, a means for the ruling elite to emasculate the oppressed populace. It is based on a concept of class in which the individuals watching the play are thought of as distinct from society, whereas in the oral tradition, society was hardly larger than the small band of spectators themselves.

布莱希特则采取了另一种策略。对他而言，宣泄戏剧是一种剥夺观众作为思考者所应有的基本自主性的手段。他提出的“间离”（verfremdung）——一种使观众产生疏离感的风格手法——旨在鼓励观众对舞台上的事件进行反思，而非与之产生认同。采纳这一立场的波瓦写道：“布莱希特希望戏剧表演成为行动的开端；应通过改造社会来寻求平衡，而不是通过剥夺个人正当的要求和需求来实现。”[6]从这一视角来看，宣泄是一种操纵，是统治精英阉割受压迫民众的手段。这种观点基于一种阶级观念，即认为观看戏剧的个体们与社会是割裂的，而在口述传统中，社会规模往往与那小群观众本身相差无几。

[6] See Esslin, 148-152; Boal, 106.

[6] 参见埃斯林，148-152页；波瓦，106页。

Clearly a stimulus for this point of view was the sentimentality of the legitimate theatre. It seems to me that many groups, impelled by a need to react to the literary theatre, embodied a rather confused sense of the place and purpose of emotion in their work. Certainly, to the extent to which strong feelings were stirred and allowed to find full release--and I am thinking of the celebrational happenings of the Living Theatre as an example--catharsis was achieved. In many respects, however, the best of the literary theatre, faithfully following Aristotle (who of course was describing practices with roots in the oral tradition), has paradoxically fulfilled more closely than the avant-garde the purpose of a theatre of trance.[7]

显然，这种观点的刺激源在于正统戏剧的感伤主义。在我看来，许多团体出于对文字戏剧作出反应的需要，在其作品中体现出一种关于情感在作品中的位置和目的的相当混乱的观念。当然，在强烈情感被激发并得以充分释放的程度上——我以“生活剧团”（Living Theatre）的庆典式即兴表演为例——净化效果确实实现了。然而，在许多方面，最优秀的文字戏剧——忠实地遵循亚里士多德（他当然是在描述植根于口述传统的实践）——悖论般地比先锋派更贴近地实现了“恍惚戏剧”的目的。[7]

[7] For an appreciation of the function of trance in literary theatre, see Cole,25, 26.

[7] 关于文字戏剧中恍惚状态功能的探讨，参见科尔，第25、26页。

Outside the experimental theatre, the resistance to catharsis has been more visceral than intellectual, stemming from a deep fear of the power of feelings. Thus in educational and community work, and even in therapeutic circles, there is more talk about safety and control than letting go. I think one reason why Moreno, despite his early appearance on the scene, always remained outside the establishment was his willingness to go all the way in the direction of purging feelings. The result, consistently, was high drama and a jolt to our habits of emotional distance and rationality.

在实验戏剧之外，对净化的抵制更多是本能的而非理性的，源于对情感力量的深深恐惧。因此，在教育和社群工作中，甚至在治疗领域，人们谈论安全和控制的频率远高于放手。我认为，莫雷诺尽管早年就活跃于戏剧界，却始终游离于主流之外，其中一个原因正是他愿意在情感宣泄的道路上走到底。其结果始终是戏剧性极强的场面，并给我们的情感疏离与理性习惯带来强烈冲击。

In my view the most truly cathartic groups are those like the Bread and Puppet Theatre, which are often able, with their stunning puppets, simple dialogue, and natural (outdoor) backgrounds, to create a feeling of awe not unlike what one imagines was the effect of a Medieval mystery play.

在我看来，最具真正宣泄效果的团体当属“面包与木偶剧团”（Bread and Puppet Theatre）这类团体，它们往往能凭借惊艳的木偶、简练的对话以及自然的（户外）背景，营造出一种令人敬畏的氛围，这与人们想象中中世纪神秘剧所产生的效果颇为相似。

## DENIGRATION OF PERFORMANCE 对表演的贬低

How else has NST departed from the preliterary drama paradigm? In oral culture, as we have seen, the performances matter because the community knows the culture's preservation is at stake. Black Elk speaks about his “compelling fear” once he had experienced his vision, knowing that to be useful it had to be enacted, and under his direction; it took him nine years to accept this destiny. The num of the Kung dancers, that boiling energy which will lift them into trance, causes great pain as it rises. In preliterary cultures, in short, performance is an essential *ordeal*.

NST 还在哪些方面偏离了前文字戏剧范式？正如我们所见，在口述文化中，表演之所以重要，是因为社群深知文化的存续系于此。黑麋鹿曾谈到，在经历了神启之后，他心中涌起一种“强烈的恐惧”，因为他明白，要让神启发挥作用，就必须将其演绎出来，并且必须由他亲自指导；他花了九年时间才接受这一命运。“孔舞”舞者体内那股将他们带入恍惚状态的“努姆”（num）——那种沸腾的能量——在升腾之际会带来巨大的痛苦。简而言之，在前文字文化中，表演是一种必不可少的*试炼*。

In literary culture, in contrast, the preservative function has diminished, and gone is the communal sense of there being a great deal at stake. The consequence is a perspective on theatre which values entertainment over risk taking, easy over arduous passages.

相比之下，在文字文化中，这种保存性功能已大为减弱，那种“事关重大”的集体意识也不复存在。其结果是，人们对戏剧的看法更重视娱乐而非冒险，更青睐轻松而非艰辛的历程。

The educational theatre, in particular, has failed to value this concept of performance as a trial. The reason is clear--reaction, again, to a literary culture which venerates cultural artifacts and pays scant heed to process. Doing theatre in this context, especially for young performers, can be a highly miseducative experience. It can become the wrong kind of ordeal. For this reason, educational theatre pioneer Brian Way is wary of performance, preferring what he terms a"sharing of work accomplished.

尤其是教育戏剧，未能重视“表演即试炼”这一概念。原因很明显——这再次是对文字文化的反应，后者崇尚文化的产物，却对创作的过程漠不关心。在此背景下进行戏剧创作，特别是对年轻演员而言，可能成为一种极具误导性的教育体验。它可能会演变成一种错误的试炼。正因如此，教育戏剧先驱布莱恩·韦伊对表演持谨慎态度，他更倾向于他所称的“已完成作品的分享”。

In the therapeutic theatre there exists a similar desire to protect the actors. Explorations in the workshop setting are the norm, and when performances are planned, the presence of the coordinator-teacher on stage provides a safety net. Often the intention of performance-for prisoners, say, or a troupe of senior citizens--is to provide anego-building success experience for actors. Thus the level of danger is carefully modulated. Such experiences can be and are immensely valuable for special population actors, who develop as persons as a result. But they do not fulfll the full potential of performance, which demands that no less than the whole community be saved.

在治疗性戏剧中，也存在着类似的保护演员的愿望。在工作坊环境中进行探索是常态，而当计划进行演出时，协调员-教师在舞台上的存在则提供了一张安全网。通常，演出——例如面向囚犯或老年团体——的初衷是为演员提供一种建立自尊的成功体验。因此，风险程度会被谨慎地控制。对于特殊群体的演员而言，此类体验具有巨大的价值，他们也确实因此获得了个人成长。但这并未充分发挥表演的全部潜力，因为真正的表演要求拯救的是整个社群，绝不是少数人。

The experimental theatre has come closest to embodying the spirit of ordeal, but the question of redemption remains problematic: Artaud's metaphor for ordeal was the Black Plague, a demonic image. Grotowski is more on the mark, with his concept of the actor as one who engages in a sacrificial act. Nevertheless, Grotowski's published writings give the reader little concrete sense of what this sacrifice is *for*. Grotowski may want a secular *sacrum*, but the sense of salvation is thin.[8]

实验戏剧最接近于体现“试炼”的精神，但救赎的问题依然棘手：阿尔托将“试炼”比作黑死病，这是一个充满魔性色彩的意象。格罗托夫斯基的观点则更为贴切，他将演员视为从事牺牲行为的人。尽管如此，格罗托夫斯基的已发表著作并未让读者具体感受到这种牺牲究竟是*为了*什么。格罗托夫斯基或许追求一种世俗的*神圣（sacrum）*，但其中的救赎感却十分淡薄。[8]

## CONNECTING WITH THE MAINSTREAM 与主流接轨

Arguably the most successful development in the NST in a worldly sense has been the comic-satiric theatre. Its brand of comedy has penetrated televisionand Hollywood. Its actors have been used in commercials. In fact, for many people the very word “improvisation” evokes this style of theatre. Improvisation, or “improv” as it is often termed, means funny, means cabaret, means zany audience suggestions.

从世俗角度来看，NST 最成功的成就当属喜剧讽刺剧。这种喜剧风格已渗透到电视和好莱坞，其演员也频繁出现在广告中。事实上，对许多人而言，“即兴”一词本身就让人联想到这种戏剧风格。即兴（Improvisation），或常被称为“即兴(improv)”，意味着幽默，意味着卡巴莱(cabaret)，意味着观众那些古灵精怪的提议。

A salient fact about this movement, and undoubtedly a significant reason for its impact, has been its commercial basis from the start. It is instructive to see how the commercial nature of the Compass Company cut through some of our oral paradigm qualities. For instance, the spirit of ensemble, strong at the start, diminished as the owners called for repeating what worked with the public. Professionals were brought in from New York. The unevenness of unrehearsed material resulted in decreased live improvisation, until improvisation became more a means of developing material in private rather than interacting in public. Shelley Berman came to the group attracted by the promise of collective creativity, but once he showed his monologue skills, he was sentenced to perform alone, just as Mike Nichols and Elaine May, once their brilliance as a team had been established, were told to perform as a duo.  What Bernard Sahlins, producer of Second City, dared call the “demotic, the burlesque, the theatre without heroes, the theatre of everyday life” became actually a highly refined vehicle for commercial success.

关于这一运动的一个显著事实——无疑也是其产生影响的重要原因——在于它从一开始就建立在商业基础上。观察“指南针剧团”（Compass Company）的商业性质如何打破我们某些“口述范式”的特质，颇具启发性。例如，最初十分强烈的集体精神随着剧团所有者要求重复那些受观众欢迎的段子而逐渐减弱。剧团还从纽约引进了专业演员。未经排练的素材质量参差不齐，导致现场即兴表演逐渐减少，直至即兴表演更多地成为在私下开发素材的手段，而非与公众互动的方式。谢利·伯曼（Shelley Berman）曾被集体创造力的前景所吸引而加入该团体，但一旦他展现出出色的独白能力，便被“判”为单独表演；正如迈克·尼科尔斯（Mike Nichols）和伊莱恩·梅（Elaine May）一样，在他们作为搭档的才华得到认可后，也被要求以双人组合的形式登台。 “第二城”（Second City）的制作人伯纳德·萨林斯（Bernard Sahlins）曾大胆称之为“通俗的、滑稽的、没有英雄的戏剧、日常生活中的戏剧”，实际上却成为了一种高度精炼的商业成功载体。

In terms of our discussion, we can say about the comic-satiric branch of NST that in its willingness to connect with established show business, with its tight, polished product, its stars, its modern marketing, it stands alone, an outstanding instance of dismissing the oral way. The rest of the NST movement, in contrast, has been strongly anti-commercial, for the most part establishing themselves on a not-for-profit basis(even the new circuses are nonprofit), and taking a potlatch stance towards the proliferation of their ideas and techniques.

就我们的讨论而言，关于 NST 中的喜剧讽刺的分支，可以说它愿意与成熟的演艺业接轨——凭借其紧凑、精良的作品、明星阵容以及现代营销手段——这使其独树一帜，成为摒弃口述传统的杰出范例。相比之下，NST 运动的其余部分则强烈反商业化，大多以非营利方式运作（就连新马戏团也是非营利的），并以“波特拉奇”（potlatch）式的态度对待其理念和技巧的传播。

## YEN FOR WORDS 对文字的渴求

If one looks at the differentNST schools from the point of view of how much its practitioners have written about their work, the experimental theatre is distinctly in the lead. This is hardly surprising, since proponents of experimental theatre are the most steeped in art and intellect. It is also interesting that in spite of their clear rejection of the theatre of plays, a significant number of experimental groups edged closer and closer over time to doing plays. They hired writers to be part of their developmental process, even eventually performed Beckett, Sam Shepard, and the plays of a new school of writers for the experimental theatre, such as Megan Terry and Jean-Claude Van Itallie. One can conclude that the excitement and challenge of literary theatre exerted a strong enough pull on these companies that they turned their backs on their NST beginnings.[9]

若从实践者撰写关于自身作品的文字数量这一角度审视各 NST 流派，实验戏剧显然遥遥领先。这并不令人意外，毕竟实验戏剧的倡导者们最深谙艺术与思想的精髓。同样耐人寻味的是，尽管他们明确拒绝“剧本戏剧”，但相当一部分实验剧团却随着时间的推移，逐渐向演出剧本剧靠拢。他们聘请剧作家参与创作过程，甚至最终上演了贝克特、萨姆·谢泼德的作品，以及梅根·特里和让-克劳德·范·伊塔利等实验戏剧新锐作家的剧作。由此可以得出结论：文字戏剧带来的兴奋与挑战对这些剧团产生了足够强大的吸引力，以至于他们背离了最初的 NST 立意。[9]

[9] At a performance by the Performance Group of Sam Shepard's The Tooth of Crime, which I believe marked a transition for that company towards scripted theatre, a revealing incident occurred: an audience member, seated on scaffolding in Jerry Rojo's environmental set, “crossed the line” by sounding a tambourine hanging next to her for use by an actor in a later scene. When she was quickly but quietly admonished and told not to touch, she turned rebellious, refusing to abandon the NST pattern of interactivity. When she continued to “participate,” the Performance Group had her expelled from the hall.

[9] 在“表演团体”上演山姆·谢泼德的《罪恶之牙》时——我认为这标志着该剧团向剧本戏剧的转型——发生了一起发人深省的事件：一位观众坐在杰里·罗霍设计的环境布景中的脚手架上，敲响了悬挂在她身旁、供演员在后续场景中使用的铃鼓，从而“越界”了。当她被迅速而轻声地劝阻并被告知不要触碰时，她变得叛逆起来，拒绝放弃 NST 那种互动模式。当她继续“参与”时，“表演小组”便将她驱逐出了剧场。

The same tendency is evident in educational theatre. The Theatre in Education (T.L.E.) concept, which comprises the presentation of a scripted play for a youth audience followed by a participatory workshop, has gained widespread acceptance. Participation and improvisational creativity are thus tempered with practice in aesthetic appreciation and play production.[10]

这种倾向在教育戏剧中同样显而易见。“教育戏剧”（T.L.E.）的概念——即先为青少年观众呈现一部剧本戏剧，随后开展参与式工作坊——已获得广泛认可。参与和即兴创造力因此与审美欣赏及戏剧制作的实践相融合。[10]

[10] For an account of the T.I.E. movement, see O'Toole.

[10] 关于T.I.E.运动的论述，参见奥图尔（O'Toole）。

In conclusion, the NST clearly shows strong affinity to the preliterary drama paradigm-valuing action over words, collectivity over individuality and hierarchy, simplicity and environmental involvement over technological grandeur. But at best it is a congruence rather than a conjunction. The questions of redressive purpose, catharsis, performance, commercialism, and use of plays remain areas in which NST, or certainly branches of the movement, are uneasy cousins with the past. 

总而言之，NST 显然与“前文字戏剧”范式有着强烈的亲和力——它重视行动胜于言语，集体性胜于个体性与等级制度，简约与环境融入胜于技术上的宏大。但这充其量是一种契合，而非融合。在修复性目的、宣泄、表演、商业化以及戏剧用途等问题上，NST（或至少是该运动的某些分支）与过去的关系依然如“尴尬的表亲”一般。

One is struck by the extent to which the motivation of the NST practitioners has been a reaction to the literary theatre- to its tyranny of language, its culture of performance, its social practices, its recreational purpose. Once the initial exhilaration of revolt had passed, though, it seems that many groups and teachers have lacked a sufficiently clear and positive philosophy to persevere, especially in a difficult social climate. It is almost as if after summoning all their energy to burst beyond the massive walls of the City of Culture, they have found themselves dazzled on the plain, not knowing precisely what to do, whether to turn back or forge ahead.

令人印象深刻的是，NST 实践者的动机在多大程度上是对文字戏剧的反应——针对其语言的专制、表演文化、社会惯例及其娱乐目的。然而，一旦最初反抗带来的兴奋感消退，许多团体和教师似乎便缺乏足够清晰且积极的哲学理念来坚持下去，尤其是在艰难的社会环境中。这几乎就像是，当他们耗尽全部精力冲破“文化之城”的厚重城墙后，却发现自己置身于平原上，被眼前的景象所迷惑，不知该如何是好，不知是该回头还是继续前进。

We have come to the end of the first section of the book, which has emphasized, by way of general background, the connection between today's nonscripted theatre and the ancient oral tradition, with its distinct stylistic needs and value system. The next section will highlight questions arising from the production of NST, such as the spontaneity of the actor, how one rehearses for improvisation, and what it means to be part of an NST “company.”

至此，本书第一部分已告一段落。本部分通过概述背景，着重阐释了当今无剧本戏剧与古代口述传统之间的联系，以及二者各自独特的风格需求和价值体系。下一部分将重点探讨 NST 创作过程中产生的问题，例如演员的自发性表、如何为即兴表演进行排练，以及成为 NST “剧团”一员的意义。
