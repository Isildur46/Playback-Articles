# CHAPTER FOUR PLAYBACK AS ORAL THEATRE 第四章 作为口述剧场的一人一故事

With the theory of oral composition behind us, we can now turn to modern incarnations of oral theatre. In this chapter, I will demonstrate their affinity with the preliterarytheatre paradigm by analyzing a performance of Playback Theatre.[1]

在梳理了口述创作理论之后，我们现在可以转向口述剧场的现代表现形式。在本章中，我将通过分析一场一人一故事剧场的演出，来阐明其与前文字戏剧范式的亲缘关系。[1]

[1] The full text of this performance, held at the Empire State Performing Arts Center in Albany, New York, May 1981, is included in the appendix. For further discussion of this performance, see Salas 1983, 19.

[1] 该演出于1981年5月在纽约州奥尔巴尼的帝国州立表演艺术中心举行，其全文收录于附录中。关于该演出的进一步讨论，参见萨拉斯（Salas）1983年著作第19页。

Before launching into the discussion, let me set the scene. We are in a theatre. The stage is bare, except for our wooden boxes, which the actors sit on in between stories  and use as props in the enactments. To their right is a prop tree hung with different kinds and colors of cloth, also used as props. Stage left sits the musician, an array of instruments before her. Downstage right are two chairs. One is for the conductor, which was my role on this occasion. The other is for the teller, who comes from the audience. The teller will relate a personal moment, then watch the story enacted. He or she will not leave the chair until it is time to return to the audience; the teller's reaction to the story as it is dramatized will be observed by the audience and is a dramatic element in the presentation.

在进入讨论之前，请允许我先描绘一下场景。我们身处一座剧院。舞台上空无一物，只有几只木箱——演员们在故事间隙坐在上面，并在表演中将其作为装置使用。木箱右侧有一棵道具树，树上悬挂着各种款式和颜色的彩布，同样作为装置使用。舞台左侧坐着一位乐师，她面前摆放着一排乐器。舞台右前方有两把椅子。一把是领航的座位，这次由我担任；另一把是讲述者的座位，讲述者从观众席中走上台来。讲述者将分享一段个人经历，随后观看故事的演绎。在回到观众席之前，他或她不会离开座位；观众将观察讲述者对戏剧化呈现的故事所作出的反应，这也是演出中一个戏剧性的元素。

The core of the performance is the sequence of six personal stories related by tellers and enacted by the company. The company also performs fluid sculptures, quick, dance-like pieces; sound sculptures, an improvisational collage in sound; and pairs, a comedic section in which the actors, arranged in twos, enact oppositional feelings and situations. There are two stories which audience volunteers enact. All the material comes from the personal experiences of audience members and is enacted spontaneously.

演出核心是由讲述者分享、剧团演绎的六则个人故事。剧团还呈现流动塑像——快速、宛如舞蹈的片段；声音塑像——即兴的声音拼贴；以及一对对——演员两人一组，演绎对立情绪与情境的喜剧环节。其中有两则故事由观众志愿者演员亲自演绎。所有素材均源自观众的亲身经历，并以自发的方式呈现。

The lighting is warm, and when the actors enter, they march through the audience, making music as they go. They position themselves on the boxes and allow the audience to regard them, a group of diverse individuals sitting as ordinary humans, chubby and thin, gray-bearded and barely-bearded, female and male. Their clothing is simple, long-sleeved tops and trousers in coordinated, gentle colors. Their expressions vary, but on the whole they give a friendly impression. At the same time there is a palpable tension in the theatre at this opening moment, for everyone knows that there is no play to be performed that afternoon. For actors and audience alike, what will happen is a great unknown, and the moment before the start is pregnant with that knowledge.

灯光温暖，演员们登场时穿行于观众席之间，边走边奏乐。他们坐在箱子上，任由观众打量——这是一群形形色色的个体，作为普通人端坐着：有胖有瘦，有满头银须的，也有刚长出几缕胡须的，有女性也有男性。他们的衣着简朴，穿着色调柔和、搭配协调的长袖上衣和长裤。表情各异，但总体给人以友好的印象。与此同时，在开场这一刻，剧场里弥漫着一种可感知的紧张气氛，因为大家都知道，那个下午并没有预定的剧目要上演。对于演员和观众而言，接下来会发生什么都是个巨大的未知数，而开场前的这一刻，正因这种认知而显得格外意味深长。

## LANGUAGE AND NONLANGUAGE 语言与非语言

To a significant extent, drama in Playback Theatre is not conveyed with words. In fact, the entire thrust of the performance is to take the verbal rendition of experience and translate it into not-so-verbal drama. In the fluid sculptures and the sound sculptures, words are rare, while in the pairs and the scenes, words often give way to nonverbal action.[2] The presence of the music, barely suggested in the playtext, also constitutes a strong nonverbal element which often carries the meaning of the story. Furthermore, each scene is initiated by a preparatory segment during which the musician plays as actors move into position. This nonverbal “setting-up” encloses the action of each story, whether humorous, mundane, or traumatic, within a consistent ritualistic framework.[3] Thus the dramatic part of the performance is permeated with nonlinguistic elements--so much so that Playback has often been described as a kind of mime.

在一人一故事剧场中，戏剧表现很大程度上并非通过语言来传达。事实上，整个表演的核心在于将对经历的语言叙述转化为非语言的戏剧表现。在流动塑像和声音塑像环节中，语言极为罕见；而在一对对和自由演绎环节中，语言往往让位于非语言动作。[2]演出实录中仅略有提及的音乐，也构成了一种强有力的非语言元素，常常承载着故事的意义。此外，每个场景的开始都有一段准备环节，在此期间，演员们进入各自位置的同时，乐师会进行演奏。这种非语言的“置景”将每个故事的行动——无论是幽默的、平淡的还是创伤性的——都置于一个连贯的仪式化框架之中。[3]因此，表演的戏剧部分浸透着非语言元素——以至于一人一故事常被描述为一种哑剧。

[2] 场景中的一个例子是玛丽的故事（II. 1215-1234）中那群给予建议的朋友们的合唱，其中台词是渐强声效与动作的一部分。

The process of this particular kind of story dramatization has a hypnotic effect on the audience; they are taken away from their normal rational-intellectual responses, as indicated by the effect of the conductor's suggestion during the period of audience introductions:“Introduce yourself to the people around you...and perhaps share with them one thing that happened to you in your house this week” (ll. 89-91). The first story (Ben's) took place just outside the house; the third (Tessa's) took place in a neighbor's house; the fifth (Judy's) in the garage next to the house; and the second (Gerald's), fourth (Barbara's) and sixth (Mary's) inside the house itself. I would contend that when offering stories, the tellers were not trying to follow a specific direction any more than the conductor was deliberately giving them one (during the actual invocation to stories [ll. 77-90] the instructions were deliberately open-ended); nevertheless, the phrase “in your house” exerted its subconscious power. Similarly, Ben's passing reference to a “dog license”(1. 186)probably influenced Tessa to tell her “dog” story. (ll.466-622)[4]

这种特殊形式的故事戏剧化过程对观众具有催眠般的效果；正如观众介绍环节中领航员的提示所显示的那样，观众被带离了他们通常的理性-智力反应：“向周围的人介绍一下自己……也许还可以和他们分享一件本周发生在你家里的事情”（第89-91行）。第一个故事（本的故事）发生在房子外；第三个（泰莎的故事）发生在邻居家；第五个（朱迪的故事）发生在房子旁的车库里；而第二个（杰拉尔德的故事）、第四个（芭芭拉的故事）和第六个（玛丽的故事）则发生在房子内部。我认为，在讲述故事时，讲述者们并非有意遵循特定指示，正如领航员也并未刻意给出具体要求（在实际征集故事的过程中[第77-90行]，指示是刻意保持开放式的）；尽管如此，“在你的家里”这一短语还是发挥了其潜移默化的作用。同样，本对“狗证”（1. 186）的顺带提及，很可能促使泰莎讲述了她的“狗”故事。（第466-622行）[4]

In the extraordinary atmosphere created during the performance, where many of the conventions both of social interaction and literary drama are suspended, audience members enter an unusual emotional state. An examination of audience laughter in the transcript reveals that not only is the audience laughing when one might expect--i.e, after a joke or a comic piece of business, but also at other, less explicable times. Two examples: when Ben, asked to choose a word that describes his (angry) older son, pauses, and the audience laughs; and when at the poignant climax to the story of giving away her dog, Tessa hugs Sir Benson, to audience laughter. One interpretation of such apparently inappropriateresponse is that although individuals in the audience are experiencing emotion, they are not comfortable or ready to acknowledge their feelings, a reaction similar to smiling after hearing tragic news.

在演出营造的非凡氛围中，社交互动与文字戏剧的许多惯例都被搁置，观众进入了一种不同寻常的情感状态。对演出实录中观众笑声的分析表明，观众不仅会在意料之中时发笑——即在笑话或滑稽桥段之后，还会在其他难以解释的时刻发笑。举两个例子：当本被要求选一个词来形容他那个（愤怒的）大儿子时，他停顿了一下，观众便笑了；以及在讲述送走爱犬的故事达到感人至深的高潮时，泰莎拥抱了本森爵士，观众也发出了笑声。对于这种看似不恰当的反应，一种解释是：尽管观众中的个体正在经历某种情感，但他们并不愿意或尚未准备好承认自己的感受，这种反应类似于听到悲惨消息后不自觉地微笑。

In the text overall, “O.K” is uttered 93 times. These excerpts only hint at the extent to which a Playback performance is full of both protracted silences and fleet overlapping utterances. While the speech between teller and conductor is more informal than the language of the scenes, nevertheless even there a “looseness” will be evident due to the uncertainties of improvisation. Certainly the audience-up portion (when audience members act), in this case two of the six scenes, is replete with interrupted and overlapping exchanges. The patterns of flow and pause, as evidenced here, are qute different from what is customary in the theatre of plays, where silence is eschewed and words carefully articulated.[6]

这些片段仅略微揭示了一人一故事演出中，冗长的沉默与飞快的交叠言语和如何交织的。尽管讲述者与领航员之间的对话比场景中的台词更为随意，但即便在此，由于即兴创作的不确定性，一种“松散感”依然显而易见。当然，观众参与环节（即观众上台表演的部分），在本例中占六场戏中的两场，充满了被打断和相互交叠的对话。如上所述，这种流动与停顿的模式与传统戏剧的惯例大相径庭——在传统戏剧中，沉默往往被摒弃，台词则被精心抒发。[6]

[6] Pinter might be considered an exception. However, his silences are carefully structured for ironic and other effects quite foreign to the oral traditional style.

[6] 品特或许可以被视为一个例外。然而，他的沉默是经过精心构思的，旨在营造讽刺等效果，这与口述传统风格截然不同。

We can also note the use of formulaic phrases in the conductor's language.  The conductor's formulas include: “And your name is?” (l. 114); “Where does your story take place?” (1. 116); “Pick someone to be you”(l. 121); “What happened at the end?” (l. 188); “Watch!” (1. 206); and “Did that fit in with the spirit, if not all the details, of what happened?” (1. 255). The formulas here are clearly not “building blocks” of narrative in the manner of the oral poets, but rather questions and instructions repeated at threshold points. As in oral narrative, however, formulaic variation is significant. For instance, in the final scene, which involved a vulnerable teller and a climactic story, the conductor makes a number of linguistic shifts. He softens his customary command at the end of the interview to:“O.K.? Watch.” (1. 1199). He also varies the “What happens in the end” formula: “Tell us just a little bit.  Whatever feels appropriate to you about those last hours, or... at the end, there.” (1. 1165-6).

我们还可以注意到领航员语言中固定话术的运用。领航员的固定话术包括：“你叫什么名字？”（第114行）；“你的故事发生在哪里？”（第116行）；“选一个人来扮演你”（第121行）；“最后发生了什么？”（第188行）； “请看！”（第1卷第206行）；以及“这虽然未必完全符合事件的细节，但是否契合了事件的精神？”（第1卷第255行）。此处的固定句式显然并非像口述诗人那样作为叙事的“构建模块”，而是出现在关键节点上反复出现的问题和指令。然而，与口述叙事一样，这些固定句式的变体具有重要意义。例如，在最后一场戏中——涉及一位情绪脆弱的讲述者和一个高潮迭起的故事——领航员进行了多次语言转换。他将访谈结尾惯常的命令语气软化为：“好吗？请看。”（第1卷第1199行）。他还对“最后发生了什么”这一固定句式进行了变通：“稍微讲一点吧。 关于最后那几个小时，或者……在结尾处，你觉得合适的部分，就讲一点吧。”（1. 1165-6）。

The story themes show characteristics of the “multiplicity” associated with the oral style. Thus in this particular performance *family* is a strong theme, especially sibling experience. So is *loss* (loss of pet, loss of husband), *being in the/a house*, and *prized objects* (Gerald's computer, Barbara's telephone). So is *telling* stories and *acting* (Judy's story, the pairs). The oral mode accommodates many themes at the same time, overlapping each other “layer upon layer,” as Walter Benjamin put it. It is quite a different treatment from the thematic selectivity and honing practiced by authors.[7]

故事主题展现了与口述风格相关的“多样性”特征。因此，在这场特定的表演中，*家庭*是一个强烈的主题，尤其是手足之间的经历。同样突出的还有*失去*（失去宠物、失去丈夫）、*身处屋内*以及*珍视之物*（杰拉尔德的电脑、芭芭拉的电话）。*讲述*故事和*表演*（朱迪的故事、一对对）也是如此。口述叙事模式能够同时容纳多种主题，正如瓦尔特·本雅明所言，这些主题“层层叠叠”地相互交织。这与作家们惯用的主题选择性和精炼手法截然不同。[7]

[7] Modern, nonliterary examples of some of these same communication elements can be found in the work of Milton Erickson, a seminal psychotherapist who practiced hypnosis and used many storytelling techniques. See Erickson 1944,31, 67-84. Erickson used confusion as a therapeutic tool; certain students of Erickson's method have found of interest the paradoxical commands of the Playback conductor: “What happens at the end?” for example, is a question which tells the teller to finish up while it is asking him or her to continue. In chapter 16, I discuss the purpose of confusing statements in the Playback Theatre conductor's language.

[7] 这些沟通元素中的一些在现代非文字范例中亦可见一斑，例如开创性心理治疗师米尔顿·埃里克森的作品——他擅长催眠疗法并运用了多种讲故事技巧。参见埃里克森 1944，第31页、第67-84页。埃里克森将“困惑”作为一种治疗工具；某些研究埃里克森方法的学者对一人一故事剧场领航员的悖论式指令颇感兴趣：例如，“最后发生了什么？”这一问题既在提示讲述者收尾，又在要求其继续讲述。在第16章中，我将探讨一人一故事剧场领航员语言中制造困惑的陈述所起的作用。

As for the structure of the show, it also has a piled-on quality. The enactments, in fact, are “repetitions” of the teller's stories, and one follows another, in apparent fortuitous fashion. Thus this particular performance consisted of three fluid sculptures, six scenes, two sound sculptures, and three pairs, The number of any of the elements is variable; in fact, they may not even all be included in a particular performance.

至于演出的结构，它也具有一种层层叠加的特点。事实上，这些演绎是讲述者故事的“重复”，一个接一个，看似偶然地展开。因此，这场特定的演出由三组流动塑像、六个自由演绎（scenes）、两组声音塑像和三个一对对组成。任何元素的数量都是可变的；事实上，在特定的演出中，它们甚至可能不会全部出现。

## COMMUNAL ELEMENTS 集体性元素

That the Playback form places a high value on inclusivity can be seen from the transcript in a number of ways. First, the various roles are not sacrosanct: individuals from the audience become tellers or actors--some even take on both roles; actors, in their turn, tell, and sit in the audience.

从剧本记录中可以从多个方面看出，一人一故事形式非常重视包容性。首先，各种角色并非不可动摇：观众中的个人会成为讲述者或演员——有些人甚至同时担任这两个角色；而演员们也会轮流讲述故事，并坐在观众席上。

Everyone is invited to participate. Furthermore, the actors speak to the audience as individuals, including the person at the light board from his booth at the rear of the hall; audience members speak from their seats; audience members communicate with each other. Anyone can speak up, and there is freedom to move about from one part of the space to another. This is not to say that there is not at the same time a formal element to the proceedings, but the feeling is not so much one of witnessing an untouchable mystery as taking part in a social gathering.

每个人都被邀请参与其中。此外，演员们会以个人的身份与观众交谈，包括坐在大厅后方灯光控制室里的灯光师；观众们在座位上发言；观众之间也会相互交流。任何人都可以发言，也可以自由地在空间的不同区域之间走动。这并非意味着整个过程完全缺乏形式感，但给人的感觉与其说是目睹某种不可触碰的神秘，不如说是参与了一场社交聚会。

A striking feature of the transcript is its emphasis on social interaction. A good deal of the performance, in truth, consists not of drama, but of talking around the drama.

该演出实录的一个显著特征在于其对社交互动的强调。事实上，演出的大部分内容并非戏剧本身，而是围绕戏剧展开的交谈。

In literary theatre, such relating--what the actors may whisper offstage (or even on), what audience members may say to each other *sotto voce*--is considered outside the framework of the performance, which remains inviolably fictive.[8]

在文字戏剧中，此类互动——无论是演员在台下（甚至台上）的低语，还是观众之间*sotto voce*的窃窃私语——都被视为超出演出框架之外，而演出本身则始终保持着不可侵犯的虚构性。[8]

[8] The existence of a clear boundary does not prevent a yearning to cross it. Hence the appeal of the gossip magazines and presentations such as the Academy Awards, which afford the spectator normally forbidden glimpses of their favorite actors in their private personae.

[8] 明确界限的存在并不能阻止人们跨越它的渴望。正因如此，八卦杂志和奥斯卡颁奖典礼等活动才具有如此大的吸引力——它们让观众得以一窥自己喜爱的演员在私下里的真实面貌，而这种窥探在平时是被禁止的。

Not so here. In fact, the entire performance can be regarded as an oscillation between dramatic and everyday reality. For example, at the moment of picking, a teller (and the rest of the audience) is relating to the actors as fellow citizens; in the next, as actors manifesting an imaginary world; and then, a few moments later, as citizens again. Even the stories themselves contribute to this oscillation in that they are fictive embodiments of real events. The performers have learned to skillfully manipulate this constant back-and-forth between the everyday and the dramatic, whereas the untrained audience performers, as can be seen in the audience-up segment, cannot help delaying the transitions by asking questions and making comments (II.741-789).[9]

但此处情况并非如此。事实上，整个演出可被视为戏剧现实与日常生活现实之间的摇摆。例如，在选角的瞬间，一位讲述者（以及其余观众）将演员视为同为公民；下一刻，又将他们视为展现虚构世界的演员；再过片刻，又重新视作公民。就连故事本身也助长了这种摇摆，因为它们是真实事件的虚构化身。表演者们已经学会了娴熟地驾驭日常与戏剧之间的这种持续往复，而未经训练的“观众表演者”——正如“观众上台”环节所显示的那样——却因提问和发表评论而不可避免地延迟了这种转换（II.741-789）。[9]

[9] What Schechner terms “performative” and “ordinary” and David Cole “imaginative truth” and “present truth,” I am distinguishing with the phrases dramatic or fictive reality vs. everyday reality, and the dramatic level vs. the level of social interaction . (Schechner, 127; Cole, 3)

[9] 对于谢克纳所称的“表演性”与“普通性”，以及大卫·科尔所称的“想象性真理”与“当下真理”，我将其区分开来，分别用“戏剧性或虚构现实”与“日常生活现实”，以及“戏剧层面”与“社会互动层面”来表述。（谢克纳，127；科尔，3）

There is another way in which the stories set up a vibration between the everyday and the dramatic: the themes of the anecdotes make central statements about the lives of the tellers sitting before us. For example, the first teller, Ben, was particularly quiet during the interview. He had trouble getting his words out, almost as if what he was about to reveal was too much for him. Yet the story, when it came, missed something at the core. Ben seemed, in short, inhibited, held in. His story had a similar theme, but there the “holding in” of his son was appropriate. Thus Ben's story and his manner of telling it commented on each other, and without knowing Ben personally, we can assume that the “*holding in-letting go*” theme is an important part of his personal story. To take one more example: Judy's story about playing Till Eulenspiegel is a comment on the meaning of dramatic action. The point of the story, she asserts to the conductor, referring to her father's misplaced rage, is that “it was just theatre”(ll. 936-7). That “just theatre” is a pregnant phrase. Judy is not an naive teller. She knew that this question “Is this theatre?” is always present in a Playback audience. But she never had told this story before. Furthermore, she respects the form sufficiently to use it to her own best purpose. Thus we can easily imagine what meaning this story might have had for Judy, who as an adult continued to playfully pretend to hang people. She constantly performed theatrical deeds her intellectual father would have despised were he still alive to see them--activities, moreover, which in her eyes (even as a child) were the opposite of cruel, or even frivolous--not “just” theatre, but theatre, a source of salvation for Judy and a boon to many others.

这些故事还通过另一种方式在日常与戏剧之间营造出共鸣：轶事中的主题直指坐我们面前的讲述者们的生活核心。例如，第一位讲述者本在访谈中格外沉默。他难以启齿，仿佛即将揭露的内容对他而言过于沉重。然而，当故事终于讲出来时，却遗漏了核心内容。简而言之，本似乎有所顾忌，有所隐忍。他的故事主题与此相似，但在故事中，他儿子那种“隐忍”的态度却是恰如其分的。因此，本的故事和他讲述的方式相互呼应；即使不认识本本人，我们也可以推断，“*隐忍与放手*”这一主题是他个人故事的重要组成部分。再举一个例子：朱迪讲述自己饰演蒂尔·欧伦斯皮格尔的故事，是对戏剧行动意义的一种评论。她向领航员强调，这个故事的要点——指的正是她父亲那无端的愤怒——在于“那不过是戏罢了”（第936-937行）。“不过是戏剧罢了”这一短语意味深长。朱迪并非天真的讲述者。她深知“这是戏吗？”这一问题始终萦绕在一人一故事剧场观众心中。但她此前从未讲述过这个故事。此外，她对这种形式怀有足够的敬意，并将其运用得恰到好处。因此，我们不难想象这个故事对朱迪而言可能具有何种意义——作为成年人，她依然会戏谑地假装绞死他人。她不断上演着那些戏剧性的举动——倘若她那位知识渊博的父亲尚在人世，定会对此嗤之以鼻；然而，在她眼中（即便在童年时期），这些活动绝非残酷，甚至谈不上轻浮——这并非“仅仅”是戏剧，而是真正的戏剧，既是朱迪的救赎之源，也是许多人的福音。

The repetitiveness and high number of interrupted and overlapping speech interactions in the transcript cannot be explained as indications of ironic intent or deep emotion as we are taught in literary analysis class. These elements can only be understood by appreciating the value placed on the interactivity of the participants. The emphasis is as much here on relation as it is on content. In each case there is a unique encounter, totally public in the Playback format, in which the pattern of conversation, including kinesthetic signals and other nonverbal cues, conveys a message that tells its own story.

正如我们在文学分析课上所学的那样，无法将记录中反复出现的、被打断的以及相互重叠的言语互动解释为讽刺意图或深切情感的表现。只有充分理解参与者对互动性的重视，才能真正理解这些元素。这里对关系的重视程度不亚于对内容的重视。在每种情况下，都存在一次独特的相遇——在一人一故事形式中，这种相遇完全公开——其中对话的模式，包括动觉信号和其他非语言线索，传达的信息本身就讲述了故事。

We are accustomed to the process working the other way- that is, for actors to take clear words formulated by a single author and give them the rough feeling of discourse, by intonation, pitch, look, and gesture. Here we see the drama of human interaction in an unrefined state. As Irving Goffman and others have appreciated, it is not crude and boring, as our conditioning for refinement would have us conclude, but rich with meaning.[10]

我们习惯于相反的过程——即演员将单一作者构思的清晰台词，通过语调、音高、眼神和手势，赋予其粗粝的对话感。而在这里，我们看到的是未经雕琢的人际互动戏剧。正如欧文·戈夫曼等人所指出的，这并非像我们受过“精致化”训练后所认为的那样粗糙乏味，而是蕴含着丰富的意义。[10]

[10] Goffman has distinguished himself by means of a dramaturgical approach to social analysis, as has Turner. Schechner also finds of interest “theme parks” and related developments on the American scene which seem to span boundaries between theatre and life. See Goffman, Turner 1981, and Schechner 1985 in References.

[10] 戈夫曼凭借其戏剧理论视角进行社会分析而独树一帜，特纳亦是如此。谢克纳还对美国社会中那些似乎跨越了戏剧与生活界限的“主题公园”及相关发展现象颇感兴趣。参见参考文献中的戈夫曼、特纳（1981）及谢克纳（1985）。

The Playback performance is intimate as well as inclusive. A modest-size audience does not in itself guarantee communal feeling, but it helps. In this case, the audience consisted of a few family groups, a few couples, and a few individuals. There was room for anyone who wanted to become actively involved, and nineteen individuals were actors or tellers of one sort or another, a good half of the audience. The Playback performers know that in groups of strangers, if the audience is too large, there is little likelihood of a story which really connects, whereas with a smaller audience there will be at least a possibility of strangers becoming comfortable enough with one another to use the form fully.

一人一故事演出既亲密又包容。观众规模适中本身并不能保证产生集体感，但确实有所帮助。在本次演出中，观众由几个家庭、几对情侣和几位个人组成。任何希望积极参与的人都有机会，共有十九人以各种形式担任演员或讲述者，约占观众总数的一半。一人一故事表演者深知，在由陌生人组成的群体中，若观众规模过大，故事很难真正产生共鸣；而观众规模较小的话，至少有可能让陌生人彼此感到足够自在，从而充分发挥这种表演形式的作用。

A good deal of special sharing takes place in the context of this kind of performance. While telling a story for all, an individual is also communicating to other members of his or her subgroup. For example, a father (Bob) and daughter (Tessa) each told about their own playground experience that day. In front of his wife and younger boy, Ben told a story in which the hot-tempered older son, perhaps the bad boy of the family, had been a good kid. Gerald told about his computer mania before his wife and daughter, perhaps as an indirect apology for his lack of attention to them (and the fact that his wife was one of the actors did not, in the Playback setting, make him an inappropriate teller). Tessa's story about her farewell to Sir Benson, and Barbara's about not being able to make a phone call in her own house (with her sister playing her), most certainly revealed aspects of the teller's experience that had not been previously expressed to family members who were present. The conductor does not try to conceal these connections between the dramatic and social, but to the contrary, points them out when he or she is aware of them.

在这类演出中，会发生许多特殊的分享。当一个人向所有人讲述故事时，其实也在与自己所属小群体的其他成员进行交流。例如，一位父亲（鲍勃）和女儿（泰莎）分别讲述了他们当天在游乐场各自的经历。本在妻子和小儿子面前讲述了一个故事，故事中那个脾气暴躁的大儿子——或许就是家里的“坏孩子”——其实是个好孩子。杰拉尔德在妻子和女儿面前讲述了自己沉迷电脑的经历，这或许是他对未能给予她们足够关注的一种间接道歉（而在一人一故事的语境下，他的妻子作为演员之一，并不影响他作为讲述者的适格性）。泰莎讲述了与本森爵士告别的故事，芭芭拉则讲述了在自己家中无法拨打电话的经历（由她的妹妹扮演她），这些故事无疑揭示了讲述者经历中那些此前未曾向在场的家人表达过的方面。领航员并不试图掩盖戏剧与社会之间的这些联系，相反，当他或她意识到这些联系时，会主动指出它们。

In addition to allowing family and other intimate connections to be part of the fabric of the occasion, the very idiosyncratic nature of the proceedings contributes to a sense of “we-ness.” The stories dramatized have never been rehearsed. They are immediate, unique. The conductor responds in kind-as in Tessa's story, for example, he can shift gears when he realizes that an apparently funny story is really sad, while the actor can often sense this and put into a scene details left unstated by the teller. Such elements give participants a sense of the singularity of their mutual experience, thus increasing their sense of community.

除了让家人及其他亲密关系成为活动的一部分外，整个流程极具个性的特质也增强了“我们”的归属感。这些被戏剧化的故事从未经过排练。它们是即兴的、独一无二的。领航员会相应地做出反应——例如在泰莎的故事中，当他意识到一个看似有趣的故事其实很悲伤时，他可以及时调整方向；而演员往往也能察觉到这一点，并在场景中加入讲述者未曾言明的情节细节。这些元素让参与者感受到彼此共同经历的独特性，从而增强了他们的社群感。

## REDRESSIVE ASPECTS 修复性层面

Performers and spectators, when asked about this performance six years later, remarked on the last scene as an outstanding feature: Mary's story about her defiant insistence on caring for her dying husband at home. It visibly mattered to Mary telling it--she was very moved and rushed back to her seat afterwards. It mattered to the actors enacting it-Scott, picked to play the husband, remembers being worried he would not be up to fulfilling the role. And it mattered to the audience. It was a story about death and courage; it contained its own wisdom. Often a Playback performance builds to this kind of end, a final scene which expresses something of particular importance for that particular audience on that particular occasion.

六年后，当被问及这场演出时，演员和观众都提到最后一场戏是该剧的一大亮点：玛丽讲述了她不屈不挠地坚持在家中照顾病危丈夫的故事。对玛丽来说，讲述这个故事显然意义重大——她当时非常感动，讲完后匆匆回到了座位上。对演绎这一段落的演员们来说，这同样意义重大——被选中饰演丈夫的斯科特回忆说，他曾担心自己无法胜任这个角色。对观众而言，这也意义非凡。这是一个关于死亡与勇气的故事，其中蕴含着独特的智慧。通常，一人一故事表演都会朝着这样的结局推进：最终场景会表达出在那个特定场合对那群特定观众而言尤为重要的内容。

The performance that day, as on other days, had its entertainment value. The audience actually laughed forty-nine times. But the purpose seemed to point to something more, a redressive element embodied in the final scene, which marked a culmination--not so much of the plot, as in a literary treatment, but of *that which should be remembered*.

那天的演出，和其他日子一样，具有娱乐价值。观众实际上笑了四十九次。但演出的目的似乎指向了更深层的意义，即最终场景中体现的某种修复性元素，它标志着一种高潮——与其说是情节的高潮（如同文学作品中的处理方式），不如说是*那些值得铭记的事物*的高潮。

To single out the final story, moreover, is not to diminish the value of the other stories, which have their own value. In fact, the act of telling one's experience and seeing it replayed is always an experience of criticalconsciousness, bringing objectivity to a circumstance that previously may have been inchoately personal.[11] Other audience members, seeing tellers experiencing this process, derive vicarious benefit, even though they may remain silent themselves.

此外，单独提及最后一个故事，并非要贬低其他故事的价值——它们自有其价值。事实上，讲述自身经历并见证其被重演这一行为，始终是一种批判性意识的体验，它为此前可能仅停留在模糊个人层面的情境注入了客观性。[11] 其他观众看到讲述者经历这一过程时，即使自己保持沉默，也能从中获得间接的益处。

[11] The phrase is Paolo Freire's, an educator who developed radical methods for teaching literacy (and who was an influence on Augusto Boal). For comment on the relevance of Freire's work to Playback Theatre, see Fox 1981,295-306.

[11] 该短语出自教育家保罗·弗莱雷之口，他开创了激进的扫盲教学方法（并对奥古斯都·波瓦产生了影响）。关于弗莱雷的著作与一人一故事剧场的相关性，参见福克斯 1981，295-306。

The open-ended quality of the invitation to tell, furthermore--the conductor emphasized that “any moment of our experience can have meaning for us and for others”(I.105)--conveys a liberating sense of validation. What is conveyed here is the value of honoring the *Eigenvelt*, our inner world of hopes and fears which often seem too private to risk communicating. Thus there is no attempt at solutions in Playback, for solutions reflect judgment and advice-giving. An example is Gerald's story, which presented a “problem,” if we look at it that way--that of husband ignoring family. However, the performers did not regard Gerald's story interpretatively; they simply sought to capture his experience as he related it.

此外，这种开放式的讲述邀请——领航员强调“我们经历中的任何时刻，对我们自己和他人而言都可能具有意义”（I.105）——传递出一种令人感到解放的被认可感。这里所传达的是尊重*Eigenvelt*的价值，即我们内心那个充满希望与恐惧的世界，这些情感往往因过于私密而不敢轻易表达。因此，一人一故事并不试图提供解决方案，因为解决方案往往意味着评判和说教。杰拉尔德的故事便是例证：若从这个角度看，他的故事确实呈现了一个“问题”——丈夫忽视家庭。然而，表演者并未对杰拉尔德的故事进行解读；他们只是试图如实呈现他讲述时的亲身经历。

Of course, there is no way that Gerald's story can be captured without a certain level of interpretation on the part of conductor and actors, all of whom are searching for meaning in the story.

当然，若没有领航员和演员们一定程度的解读，杰拉尔德的故事便无法被呈现出来——他们都在故事中寻求意义。

There is a definite educative element here--about validating experience, about telling, about acting, about inclusion, about taking risks, and so forth--both through and around the stories. This teaching, in which not only the performers but also others take part, contributes to a Playback performance's redressive impact.

这里确实存在明确的教育元素——关于经验的认可、关于讲述、关于表演、关于包容、关于勇于冒险等等——这些既体现在故事之中，也围绕着故事展开。这种教学不仅有表演者参与，还有其他人共同参与，从而增强了一人一故事表演的修复效应。

Finally, one of the constant themes of a Playback performance, and the source of considerable dramatic tension, is the question of meaningful experience itself. It can be summed up this way: Can we make of this show something worthwhile? Just as there are unlimited possibilities for stories worth remembering, there are in every instance unlimited possibilities for failure. The moment is fixed; we--performers and audience--are metaphorical acrobats on a bicycle on a high wire, balancing. Will we make it across without collapse? This demand for active participation can be unsettling to those used to a more passive theatre experience; it can also be exciting. But it always involves risk. Among the potentially threatening elements for the spectators on this occasion were the smallness of the audience (“no one else cared to come, they must be more knowledgeable than we; this must not be worth it”); the domination of children, especially Tessa (“this show is for kids; and the performers can't even control them”); with- holding (“I won't tell;” or “Well, I'll tell, but not my real core”); and alienation (“I don't belong here, so I won't take part. It's too in-groupy”). The actors, of course, have their own obstacles. We will discuss these in a later chapter, but just to mention one sort: on the day of this performance, a hunger striker had died in Northern Ireland, and at least one of the actors was strongly affected by this event; one of the company was absent because a teenager she had sheltered had been murdered by his brother, a shocking tragedy we all felt; and the friend's house where we had rested in between shows reminded those of us who knew her of his old girlfriend, who had committed suicide. For the actors, the line is always a fine one. If they are closed off to experience, their portrayals will be jejune. On the other hand, if they are too open, a portrayal of sibling hatred (Ben's story, Barbara's story), or dying (Mary's story), will turn formless and vapid due to the need for self-protection. (We will discuss further these important questions of actor's spontaneity in Section Two).

最后，一人一故事表演中一个贯穿始终的主题，也是相当大戏剧张力的来源，便是“有意义的体验”本身这一问题。它可以这样概括：我们能否让这场演出变得有价值？正如值得铭记的故事有无限的可能性一样，在每一种情况下，失败的可能性也是无限的。这一刻是固定的；我们——表演者和观众——如同在高空钢丝上骑自行车的隐喻中的杂技演员，正在保持平衡。我们能否不倒下地顺利穿越？这种对积极参与的要求，对于习惯了更被动戏剧体验的人来说可能会令人不安；但也可能令人兴奋。但这总是伴随着风险。此次对观众而言，潜在的威胁因素包括：观众人数稀少（“没人愿意来，他们肯定比我们更有见识；这演出肯定不值得”）；儿童占主导地位，尤其是泰莎（“这场演出是给小孩看的；而且表演者连他们都管不住”）；信息保留（“我不说”；或“好吧， 我会说，但不会透露我的真实想法”）；以及疏离感（“我不属于这里，所以不会参与。这太小圈子了”）。演员们当然也有自己的障碍。我们将在后面的章节中讨论这些，但仅举一例： 演出当天，北爱尔兰有一名绝食抗议者去世，至少有一位演员深受此事影响；剧团中有一人缺席，因为她曾收留的一名青少年被其兄长杀害，这是一场令我们所有人都深感震惊的悲剧；而我们在两场演出间隙休息的那位朋友的家，让认识她的人联想到他那位自杀身亡的前女友。对演员而言，这始终是一条微妙的界线。如果他们对体验封闭，他们的表演就会显得单薄乏味。另一方面，如果他们过于开放，那么对兄弟姐妹之间仇恨（本的故事、芭芭拉的故事）或死亡（玛丽的故事）的刻画，就会因自我保护的需要而变得形态不明、空洞无味。（我们将在第二部分进一步探讨演员自发性的这些重要问题。）

Thus for all who take part, the performance is a test similar to that presented in daily life--to achieve the positive despite a swarm of negativities, to find the creative answer to a tangled situation, to make meaning out of chaos. In this sense taking part in a successful Playback performance, as this was, is in itself a lesson for life, conveying a message best put by Mary as she refused assistance in mounting the stage: “I'm able!” (I.1109).

因此，对所有参与者而言，这场演出都是一场类似于日常生活的考验——在负面情绪的包围下仍能取得积极成果，在错综复杂的处境中找到富有创造力的解决方案，并在混乱中发掘意义。从这个意义上说，参与一场像这样成功的一人一故事演出，本身就是一堂人生课，传达的信息最好用玛丽在拒绝他人帮助登上舞台时的那句话来概括：“我能行！”（I.1109）。

## ENVIRONMENTAL ATTUNEMENT 环境的和谐共振

The Empire State Performing Arts Complex in Albany is the city's premier performance space, and the Playback Company had been selected as one of a number of companies from a multi-county area. The occasion was fairly low-key--the whole concept had been the result of an arts council's well-intentioned but underfunded scheme-yet we were pleased to have the exposure. However, the hall, even though we were put in the concert space rather than the main stage, was clearly too grandiose, especially when the audience consisted of fewer than 50 individuals. In addition, the stage was high and made it difficult for tellers to come to the teller's chair. The discomfort of performing Playback in so conventionally theatrical a space is expressed immediately by me (the conductor) in my opening speech; I cannot help conjuring up, by contrast, a space characterized by “cows and the clod coming through the crack.”

奥尔巴尼的帝国州表演艺术中心是该市首屈一指的演出场所，而一人一故事剧团是从多个县的众多剧团中脱颖而出、获选参演的之一。这次活动相当低调——整个构想源于艺术委员会一项初衷良好但资金不足的计划——尽管如此，我们仍为能借此机会展示自己而感到高兴。然而，尽管我们被安排在音乐厅而非主舞台演出，这个场馆显然过于宏伟，尤其是当观众人数不足50人时。此外，舞台过高，使得讲述者难以走到讲述者座椅旁。在如此传统戏剧化的空间里进行一人一故事表演所带来的不适感，我（作为领航员）在开场白中便立即表达了出来；我不禁联想到一个截然不同的空间——那里“牛群漫步，泥块从缝隙中渗入”。

We once were invited to do a family matinee in a church. Always pleased to perform in churches, I was surprised to find on entering that all the windows had been covered with black cloth. We were starting to remove the cloth when the organization's director arrived. She was as dismayed that we were letting the light in as I had been to see it shut out. Literary theatre leans towards a controlled, constructed environment; nonliterary theatre, towards letting nature in. She had obviously not understood the difference.

我们曾受邀在教堂举办一场家庭午场演出。我向来乐于在教堂表演，但一进门却惊讶地发现所有窗户都被彩布遮住了。正当我们开始揭开彩布时，该组织的负责人抵达了。她对我们放光进来感到沮丧，就像我看到光被挡在外面时感到的沮丧一样。文字戏剧倾向于受控、构建的环境；非文字戏剧则倾向于让自然融入其中。她显然没有理解其中的区别。

Despite the unsuitability of the Albany concert hall for Playback, we did our best to adapt to the space, inviting the audience to gather close (I.93-4). Often in Playback the necessary spirit of adaptation will involve the audience too, who will be invited to overcome unfavorable conditions and make the experience a good one. What was frustrating was an environment that gave so little back. Its architecture did not inspire; its acoustics did not amplify; most of the 400-odd seats were wasted. The environment impeded rather than abetted our particular kind of theatre.

尽管奥尔巴尼音乐厅并不适合一人一故事剧场表演，但我们还是尽最大努力适应这个空间，邀请观众聚拢过来（I.93-4）。在一人一故事剧场中，这种必要的适应精神往往也会将观众纳入其中，邀请他们共同克服不利条件，使体验变得美好。令人沮丧的是，这个环境几乎没有任何回馈。它的建筑设计无法激发灵感；其声学效果无法增强音效；400余个座位中大部分都被浪费了。这个环境阻碍了我们这种特殊形式的戏剧，而非助其一臂之力。

In reviewing this Playback Theatre performance from the point of view of the preliterary drama paradigm, we have remarked on a distinctly oral use of word (use of formulas, repetitiveness), theme (multiplicity), and structure (episodic). We have noted a tremendous emphasis on social interaction: the line between performers and spectators is fluid, and nondramatic interaction is a permissible part of the proceedings. We have observed here a premium on the concept of *valuable* story, as in oral culture, as well as a sense of the ever-present delicacy of *finding* it. The bard's opening prayer to the muse hints at this quality of uncertainty, for at any time the inspiration may be lacking, the listeners distracted, a storm may drive everyone away, or the shadows in the hall may haunt the tiny throng of celebrants, robbing them of joy.

从“前文字戏剧”范式视角审视一人一故事剧场演出时，我们注意到其语言运用（固定话术、重复）、主题（多样性）和结构（分段化）都呈现出鲜明的口述传统特征。我们还注意到其对社交互动的极度重视：表演者与观众之间的界限是流动的，非戏剧性的互动也是演出过程中被允许的一部分。我们在此观察到，正如口述文化中那样，*有价值*的故事这一概念备受推崇，同时人们也深切体会到*发掘*这类故事时始终存在的微妙性。吟游诗人向缪斯女神献上的开场祈祷便暗示了这种不确定性——因为灵感随时可能枯竭，听众可能分心，暴风雨可能驱散众人，或者大厅里的阴影可能笼罩着这群微小的仪式参与者，夺走他们的喜悦。
 
Of course, each element is not experienced discretely. For instance, the interconnectedness of the Playback approach contributes to the redressive purpose in that individual revelation spurs communal feeling. We are brought together through the tellers' stories.

当然，这些元素并非孤立存在。例如，一人一故事方法的相互关联性有助于实现修复性目的——个体的顿悟能激发集体情感。我们正是通过讲述者的故事而凝聚在一起。

As contemporary practitioners, we are not part of a preliterary oral tradition, but are instead engaged in an exercise of rediscovery. At best, we can show an affinity to preliterary drama. The extent to which we can develop a form which works and feels organic will depend on our understanding of the exigencies of oral style and our skill at adapting them to a postliterary age.

作为当代实践者，我们并非属于前文字时代的口述传统，而是致力于一场重新发现的探索。至多，我们能展现出与前文字戏剧的亲和力。我们能在多大程度上发展出一种既行之有效又感觉浑然天成的形式，将取决于我们对口述风格必要性的理解，以及我们将这些风格适应后文字时代的技巧。

Playback is nontraditional, an invention which attempts to provide a “new” oral form of theatre. After a decade, it seems like a child whom we can be confident will survive. At the same time, we can recognize its points of vulnerability. One is the use of language and the goal of trance. From a preliterary perspective, there is perhaps too much language, all that talking before and after scenes; whereas from a modernist perspective, the talking is not condensed enough and makes the show feel slow. Even though this performance benefited from a strong final story, the feeling was not fully cathartic. I would say the catharsis, to use T.E. Lawrence's term, was “burked” a bit, like a sneeze crushed in the mouth. Another point is the extent to which the Company achieved environmental attunement. In our pleasure over being accepted by the (literary)arts establishment in the form of a showcase invitation, did we forfeit our allegiance to the Playback form by agreeing to play in a 400- seat space? A final point is that the audience was not united by bonds of kinship or work, but only by a vague concept of location which these days has little to do with communitas. To deal with this alienated social circumstance requires much effort and is not always fruitful.

一人一故事剧场是非传统的，是一种试图提供“新”的口述戏剧形式的发明。经过十年的发展，它似乎已成长为一个我们确信能够存活下来的孩子。与此同时，我们也能察觉其脆弱之处。其一是语言的使用与恍惚状态的追求。从前文字时代的视角来看，语言或许过多——场景前后那些喋喋不休的对话；而从现代主义视角来看，对话又不够凝练，导致演出节奏显得拖沓。尽管这种演出得益于一个强有力的结局，但那种宣泄感并未完全释放。用T.E.劳伦斯的话来说，这种宣泄感可以说被“扼杀”了一点，就像一个被憋在嘴里的喷嚏。另一个问题是剧团在环境的和谐共振方面达到的程度。当我们因收到展示邀请而欣喜于被（文字）艺术界主流接纳时，是否因同意在400座的剧场演出，而背离了对一人一故事剧场形式的忠诚？最后一点是，观众之间并非由亲缘或工作纽带联结，而仅仅基于一种模糊的“地点”概念——如今这与“阈限共同体”（communitas）已几乎毫无关联。应对这种疏离的社会状况需要付出巨大努力，且未必总能取得成效。

Furthermore, in regard to this whole subject we must distinguish between the limitations of the affinity--obviously many elements of preliterary performances will just not work today--and the consequence of applying our critical judgment based upon an understanding of the oral style. There can be poor nonscripted theatre no less than there can be inferior play productions--a topic we will discuss further in chapter 16.

此外，关于这一整个议题，我们必须区分“亲和力”的局限性——显然，前文字时期表演的许多元素在今天根本行不通——与基于对口述风格的理解而运用批判性判断所产生的后果。正如存在劣质的戏剧制作一样，也存在质量低下的无剧本戏剧——这一话题我们将在第16章中进一步探讨。

What *is* important is to begin with an appreciation of this kind of improvisation as a valid form of theatrical performance with its own special qualities: its affinity for the natural, its moral earnestness, its sensitivity to the conjunction of dramatic and social, its ability to traverse the span between the mundane and the extraordinary. Aristotle wrote in the *Poetics* that tragedy and comedy began as “mere improvisation.”[12] Let us recognize the polemic tone of this “mere” and reevaluate. It is high time the siblings partial to oral theatre reclaim their seat at the table.

*真正重要的*是，首先要认识到这种即兴表演是一种具有自身独特品质的有效戏剧表现形式：它与自然的亲和力、其道德上的诚挚、对戏剧性与社会性交汇点的敏锐感知，以及跨越平凡与非凡之间鸿沟的能力。亚里士多德在《诗学》中写道，悲剧和喜剧最初“仅仅只是即兴表演”。[12]让我们认清“仅仅只是”一词所蕴含的论战语气，并重新评估。现在正是那些偏爱口述戏剧的“兄弟姐妹”们重返舞台中央的时候了。

[12] Aristotle, 57.
