# CHAPTER ONE THE NATURE OF ORALITY 第一章 口述的本质

This is a book about theatre without scripts--i.e., theatre that is not plays. But what is this, exactly? Do we mean the ceremonial dramas of nonliterate peoples? Or something modern, practiced in our society? And if so, what shape does this theatre take? What are its goals? What is its importance?

这是一本关于无剧本戏剧的书——即非戏剧作品的戏剧。但这究竟是什么？我们指的是不识字民族的仪式性戏剧吗？还是指我们社会中实践的某种现代形式？如果是后者，这种戏剧呈现何种形态？其目标是什么？又具有怎样的意义？

In the succeeding chapters I will seek answers to these questions, and in the process sketch the outline of a modern movement in the theatre which to date has received neither adequate nor coherent attention.

在接下来的章节中，我将寻求这些问题的答案，并在此过程中勾勒出一种现代戏剧运动的轮廓——迄今为止，这一运动既未得到充分的关注，也未获得连贯的探讨。

We will be talking throughout about oral theatre. The fact is, however, we are used to thinking of theatre as literary. Therefore it will be good to have an understanding at the start of the nature of oral composition. Some of the pioneering work in this field was accomplished through study of Homer's *Odyssey*. So we will begin there.

我们将始终围绕“口述戏剧”这一主题展开讨论。然而事实上，我们习惯于将戏剧视为文字性的。因此，在开篇之初，先对口述创作的本质有所了解是很有必要的。该领域的一些开创性成果正是通过对荷马的《奥德赛》的研究而取得的。因此，我们将从这里开始。

Surprisingly, it was not until well into the twentieth century that we possessed any concrete knowledge of how the Odyssey was created. While it was recognized that in many respects the ancient epics were different from modern works, it was generally assumed that they were literary achievements. As an example of the prevailing attitude, note the opinion of T.E. Lawrence, a.k.a. Lawrence of Arabia, who translated the *Odyssey*:

令人惊讶的是，直到二十世纪中叶，我们才对《奥德赛》的创作过程有了具体的了解。虽然人们认识到古代史诗在许多方面与现代作品不同，但普遍认为它们是文学成就。作为当时主流态度的例证，请注意 T.E.劳伦斯（即“阿拉伯的劳伦斯”）的观点，他曾翻译过《奥德赛》》：

> Crafty, exquisite, homogeneous--whatever great art may be, these are not its attributes. In this tale every big situation is burked and the writing is soft.
>
> 精巧、精致、浑然一体——无论伟大的艺术是什么，这些都不是它的特征。在这个故事中，每一个重大情节都被淡化处理，文笔也显得软弱无力。


Moreover:

此外：

> In four years of living with this novel I have tried to deduce the author from his self-betrayal in the work. I found a book worm, no longer young, living from home, a mainlander, city-bred and domestic...[1]
>
> 在与这部“小说”相伴的四年里，我曾试图从作者在作品中的自我暴露中推断其真实面目。我发现的是一位不再年轻、靠父母供养、来自内陆、在城市长大且安于家庭生活的书虫……[1]

[1] Steiner & Fagles, 153-4.

[1] 施泰纳与法格尔斯，第153-154页。

In this grumpy assessment, Lawrence could not have been more wrong. For we now know that Homer was not a “book worm,” nor “an author” in the usual sense of the term, and thus could not have written a “novel.”

在这番抱怨连连的评价中，劳伦斯的看法再错误不过了。因为我们现在知道，荷马既不是“书虫”，也不是通常意义上的“作家”，因此不可能写出“小说”。

In fact, Homer, it is now widely believed, did not “write” at all. He was illiterate. It is somewhat surprising that Lawrence, who had spent months in the vast Arabian spaces, where the cool of the evening brought stories out of bottles and life itself was drama, could have been so mistaken about Homer. But then everyone else of his generation was, too. Their literary lenses were so strong they could not recognize the great differences of style between oral and written composition. Nor did they appreciate that not only was a genre difference at stake, but a profound difference of lifestyle.[2]

事实上，如今普遍认为，荷马根本没有“写作”。他是个文盲。劳伦斯曾在广袤的阿拉伯大地逗留数月，那里傍晚的凉意会将故事从瓶中唤出，生活本身就是一出戏剧，然而他竟会对荷马产生如此严重的误解，这多少有些令人惊讶。但话说回来，他那一代的其他人也是如此。他们的文学视角过于狭隘，以至于无法辨识口述创作与书面创作在风格上的巨大差异。他们也没有意识到，这不仅涉及体裁的差异，更涉及生活方式的深刻差异。[2]

[2] Harvard professor of comparative literature Harry Levin makes this point in his preface to Lord, xi. The ensuing discussion relies extensively on Lord, who in addition to his own fieldwork had recourse to over 12,500 records of Yugoslav oral song in the Parry collection at Harvard.

[2] 哈佛大学比较文学教授哈里·莱文在洛德著作的序言中提出了这一观点（xi页）。下文的讨论大量依赖于洛德的研究，他除了自己的田野调查外，还参考了哈佛大学帕里收藏中超过12,500份南斯拉夫口述歌曲的记录。

## THE DISCOVERY OF ORAL COMPOSITION 口述创作的发现

The discovery that Homer was not a writer was made by Harvard classicist Milman Parry, who deduced it and then, with his student Albert Lord, proved his case by recording in Yugoslavia the stories of living illiterate bards.

荷马并非作家这一发现出自哈佛大学古典学家米尔曼·帕里之手。他首先推断出这一结论，随后与他的学生阿尔伯特·洛德一起，通过在南斯拉夫录制在世文盲吟游诗人的故事，证实了这一观点。

One of Parry's and Lord's most astounding discoveries in the Croatian mountains was that trained singerscould recite tales which were remarkably long, sometimes taking a whole night to recite. This phenomenon raised immediately the question of how they did it.

帕里和洛德在克罗地亚山区最令人惊叹的发现之一是，受过训练的吟游诗人能够背诵极其冗长的故事，有时甚至需要整整一夜才能背诵完毕。这一现象立即引发了一个问题：他们究竟是如何做到的？

It seemed that these storytellers used certain stylistic techniques to aid the power of their native memory. The first was the use of a rhythmic delivery structure, which included using a poetic line of a fixed length and stringed instrument to strum between lines to provide a pause for thought. The Yugoslav bards used a *gusle*, while Homer probably used a *cithara*, an early lyre. Already we begin to understand why Homer's original epics are in verse while each successive translation made in our literary epoch, not having the same “need” for metrical lines, has had to make a choice about a prose or verse form.(T.E. Lawrence, as one might guess, chose prose.)

这些说书人似乎运用了某些风格技巧来增强其天生的记忆力。首先是采用富有节奏的叙述结构，包括使用固定长度的诗行，并在诗行之间用弦乐器拨奏，以此提供思考的停顿。南斯拉夫的吟游诗人使用*gusle*，而荷马很可能使用的是*cithara*——一种早期的里拉琴。至此，我们已开始理解为何荷马的原始史诗采用诗体，而在我们这个文字时代，每部后续译作由于不再具有对格律诗行的同样“需求”，便不得不选择散文或诗体形式。（正如人们所料，T.E.劳伦斯选择了散文。）

The oral poets used a collection of small phrases, called “formulas” by Lord, as building blocks for their narrative. Famous formulas in Homer are “rosy-fingered dawn” and the “wine-dark sea.” Analysis showed, in fact, that tales sung by oral poets were in fact almost totally made up of repeated material (the figure for the *Odyssey* is about 90%).

口述诗人使用了一系列短语——洛德（Lord）称之为“固定句式”——作为叙事的构建模块。荷马作品中著名的固定句式有“玫瑰指尖的黎明”和“酒色的大海”。事实上，分析表明，口述诗人吟唱的故事几乎完全由重复的素材构成（《奥德赛》中的这一比例约为90%）。

Thus the oral poet did not need to memorize poems word for word, in the way we think of memorizing, because he had at his command a large number of stock phrases which were second-nature to him and which he used one after another. Lord identified a second unit of composition he called “themes,” which were of course made up of formulaic phrases and which, while contributing to the unfolding of the tale, also had an “energy” of their own. Examples of themes are “preparing for battle,” “ritual sacrifice,” “rigging ship,” “the competition,” and “the assembly.” Thus the poet knew a huge number of formulas and themes. His mnemonic knowledge of these units enabled him to retain and recite stories of great length. In fact, Lord found that certain Yugoslav singers could memorize a tale after hearing it only once by relying on their deep knowledge of formulaic expressions.

因此，口述诗人无需像我们通常理解的那样逐字背诵诗歌，因为他掌握着大量已内化为本能的固定短语，并能信手拈来地依次运用。洛德还识别出另一种创作单元，即他所称的“主题”，这些主题当然由固定短语构成，在推动故事发展的同时，也具有自身的“能量”。“主题”的例子包括“备战”、“祭祀”、“装船”、“竞赛”和“集会”。因此，吟游诗人熟知大量固定句式和主题。他对这些单元内容的记忆能力，使他能够保留并背诵篇幅极长的故事。事实上，洛德发现，某些南斯拉夫歌手仅凭对固定表达的深刻掌握，就能在听过一次后便将故事背诵下来。

By now it will be clear that when we are talking about a “tale” in the oral mode we are talking about a sung poem. What do we call the storyteller? Obviously “author” is not correct, for author connotes “the original writer of a book.” “Teller” ignores the singing; “bard” is antiquated. In fact, a precise term eludes our grasp, for modern English lacks the right word to describe such an artist.[3] We even lack a precise term for this kind of literature. “Literature” etymologically refers to reading, making the term “oral literature” a contradiction in terms.

至此，我们应当已经清楚，当我们在谈论口述传统中的“故事”时，所指的其实是吟唱的诗歌。那么，我们该如何称呼这种说书人呢？显然，“作者”这一称谓并不恰当，因为“作者”一词隐含着“书籍的原始撰写者”之意。“讲述者”忽略了吟唱这一环节；“吟游诗人”则已过时。事实上，我们难以找到一个精准的术语，因为现代英语中缺乏描述此类艺术家的恰当词汇。[3]我们甚至缺乏对这种文学形式的精准称谓。“文学”一词从词源上讲是指阅读，因此“口述文学”这一术语本身就自相矛盾。

[3] For a discussion of this problem of terminology, see Robert Fitzgerald's postscript to his translation of Homer, 488.

[3] 关于这一术语问题的讨论，参见罗伯特·菲茨杰拉德在其所译荷马作品后记第488页。

With a sense of the basic nature of oral recitation, let us look at some facets of the oral narrative style.

在理解了口述吟诵的基本性质之后，让我们来看看口述叙事风格的几个方面。


*Aggregation*. First, as we have seen, oral compositions are likely to be repetitious, or aggregative, not only in terms of repeated use of themes and phrases, but even in terms of sentence structure. A familiar example is the opening verses of the Bible.

*聚合*。首先，正如我们所见，口述创作往往具有重复性或聚合性，不仅体现在主题和短语的反复使用上，甚至在句子结构上也是如此。一个广为人知的例子就是《圣经》的开篇经文。

The repetitiveness helped the teller remember. But if we also try to recall *listening*, let us say, to the opening of Genesis, we know that there is dramatic power in those paralleled “ands” and in the repetition of certain phrases. Thus repetition has an effectiveness when heard that it often lacks when read.

这种重复性有助于讲述者记忆。但如果我们也试着回想*聆听*《创世记》开篇的情景，就会发现那些并列的“和”字以及某些短语的重复中蕴含着戏剧性的力量。因此，重复在聆听时具有一种阅读时往往缺乏的感染力。

The aggregative style of the oral poet extends to the structure of the story as well. We have seen how the storyteller thinks in terms of themes. The story is told by a “slow piling one on top of the other of thin, transparent layers.”[4] The storyteller's approach is episodic. To a reader schooled in literary composition, such as T.E. Lawrence, the big situations may indeed seem “burked.” For example, the climax of the *Odyssey* is Odysseus's return to Ithaca, with his defeat of the suitors and reunion with Penelope. Then follows a final book which ends inconclusively. Such an anti-climactic windup does not make sense from a literary perspective. The explanation is that Homer did not think in a literary way; he sang his tale until it ran out, or his voice ran out, or the people grew tired or restless. In fact, oral epics are known for beginning and ending “in medias res.”[5]

口述诗人的聚合式风格也延伸到了故事的结构之中。我们已经看到，讲故事的人是如何以主题为单位进行思考的。故事的讲述方式是“将纤薄透明的层层内容缓慢地堆叠在一起”[4]。讲故事的人采用的是情节片段式的叙事方法。对于受过文学创作训练的读者，例如 T.E.劳伦斯而言，这些宏大情境确实可能显得“被埋没了”。例如，《奥德赛》的高潮是奥德修斯重返伊萨卡，击败求婚者并与佩内洛普重逢。随后是最后一卷，却以未完待续的方式收尾。从文学角度来看，这种反高潮式的收尾是说不通的。原因在于荷马的思维方式并非文学式的；他只是将故事吟唱下去，直到故事讲完、嗓音沙哑，或是听众感到疲倦或坐立不安为止。事实上，口述史诗素以“从故事中间开始”[5]而著称。

[4] Benjamin, 93.

[4] 本雅明，第93页。

*Concrete narration*. A second important characteristic of the oral style is that it is narrative and deals in concrete images. A significant reason for this seems to be, again, the needs of memorization--i.e., it is easier to remember a sequence  of events than something abstract which is not rooted in a time and a place. In addition, preliterate cultures favor concrete ways of thinking. Even the metaphor requires a level of abstraction and compression unsuited to the oral poet, who uses many-worded “Homeric” similes instead.[6]

*具体叙事*。口述风格的第二个重要特征是其叙事性，并以具体意象为表现手段。这背后的一个重要原因似乎仍是记忆的需求——即，相较于那些没有具体时空根基的抽象概念，事件的顺序更容易被记住。此外，前文字文化更倾向于具体的思维方式。即便是隐喻，也需要一定程度的抽象和凝练，这并不适合口述诗人，他们通常使用词句冗长的“荷马式”明喻来代替。[6]

[6] On the significance of metaphor in the development of abstract reasoning, see Jaynes, 48-59.

[6] 关于隐喻在抽象推理发展中的意义，参见杰恩斯，第48-59页。

To give a nonliterary example: a child learns the “Alphabet Song” to remember the alphabet. It consists of monosyllabic words which refer to distinct letters the child has seen and perhaps even felt in a concrete form. Once the child comes to understand the meaning of the letters abstractly, and can, for example, select every other letter, then there is no longer need for a story-song to remember letters by.

举一个非文字的例子：孩子通过学习“字母歌”来记忆字母表。这首歌由单音节词组成，这些词分别对应孩子曾见过、甚至可能以具体形式触摸过的特定字母。一旦孩子开始抽象地理解字母的含义——例如能够选出每一个其他字母——那么就不再需要依靠这种故事歌来记忆字母了。

*Performance as ritual*. The memorization required to keep in mind and recite a tale in the oral mode demanded the regularity of the formulaic phrases and themes. Thus it was in the poet's interest to emphasize the sameness of the story from one telling to the next. Lord shows that oral poets adhere faithfully to the traditional telling of the story, consciously trying to avoid variations, for to depart from the inherited framework meant losing the means of memory.

*作为仪式的表演*。以口述形式记住并复述一个故事所需的记忆过程，要求固定句式和主题的规律性。因此，强调故事在每次讲述中的“一成不变”符合诗人的利益。洛德指出，口述诗人忠实地遵循传统的故事讲述方式，有意识地避免变体，因为偏离传承下来的框架就意味着失去记忆的手段。

There is a deeper reason why oral tales are “traditional” which demands that we imagine ourselves in a world with no books--no written information at all. In such a context, *remembrance* is essential, for if we forget, the story and its content will be gone forever, with no possibility of retrieval. Thus in oral culture there is a strong tribal reason for stressing a tradition of “what is worth remembering,” including the means to make it maximally easy to remember.  This is why the oral recitation is a performance with a familiar feel to it. It will be done the same way each time, and this sameness is important to the essence of the recitation, both for the singer and for the listeners, all of whom bear responsibility for remembrance.

口述故事之所以“传统”，还有更深层的原因，这要求我们设身处地想象一个没有书籍——完全没有书面信息——的世界。在这样的背景下，*记忆*至关重要，因为一旦遗忘，故事及其内容将永远消失，再无可能找回。因此，在口述文化中，强调“值得铭记之事”的传统存在强烈的部落动因，这包括采取一切手段使其尽可能易于记忆。这正是口述吟诵之所以具有熟悉感的表演形式的原因。每次的吟诵方式都一成不变，而这种一致性对吟诵的本质至关重要——无论对吟诵者还是听众而言，因为所有人都肩负着铭记的责任。

*Performance as improvisation*. Paradoxically, even though the singer endeavors to sing his tale the same way every time, it is impossible. This makes sense if we remember that the tale is not a “text” that has been memorized, but the experience of an auditory moment which cannot be objectively recalled or referred to. The precision of the rendition can never be absolutely verified by the singer. Only the technology and awareness of scholars such as Lord make verification possible, by pushing their tape recorders through the fence lines of history. This is why there is no such thing as a tale in oral culture. A particular story exists in all its variations as voiced by all its tellers in all their recitations. It is to this very unliterary characteristic Lord refers when discussing a tale's “multiformity.” In oral culture the story is constantly being recreated.

*即兴表演*。看似矛盾的是，尽管吟唱者竭力每次都以相同的方式吟唱他的故事，但这实际上是不可能的。若我们意识到，故事并非一段被背诵下来的“文本”，而是一个无法被客观回忆或援引的听觉瞬间的体验，这一点便不难理解。吟唱者永远无法绝对地验证其演绎的精准性。唯有像洛德（Lord）这样的学者凭借其技术手段与敏锐洞察力，才能将录音机伸过历史的围栏，从而使这种验证成为可能。这就是为什么在口述文化中根本不存在所谓“固定不变的故事”。一个特定的故事，以其所有变体形式，通过所有讲述者在每次讲述中的演绎而存在。洛德在讨论故事的“多形态性”时，所指的正是这种非文字性的特征。在口述文化中，故事正在不断被重塑。

There is a positive side to this inevitable variation from one telling to the next. Slight additions or deletions that slowly accrue from one telling to another enrich, improve, and add to the power of the tale.

这种从一次讲述到另一次讲述中不可避免的变化也有其积极的一面。从一次讲述到另一次讲述中逐渐积累的细微增删，丰富了故事，完善了故事，并增强了故事的力量。

Thus it is now thought that Homer was not a sui generis creator of stories, but one of a long line of singers who told the great stories of early Greek civilization. We do not know why his name is remembered. Perhaps he just happened to be singing the tales at that historical moment when a scribe undertook for the first time to write them down. Or perhaps Homer, the blind bard, is an imaginary figure representing that tradition which passed when people began to have to use their eyes to “read” stories. Or perhaps he was the best poet of his day, someone who simultaneously adhered faithfully to the traditional tale and improved it, so that its power was even greater and its language more memorable than ever before.

因此，如今人们认为荷马并非独一无二的故事创作者，而是讲述早期希腊文明伟大故事的漫长吟游诗人传统中的一员。我们不知道为何他的名字被后世铭记。也许他只是恰好在那个历史时刻吟唱着这些故事，而当时有位抄写员首次着手将它们记录下来。又或许，这位盲诗人荷马是一个虚构人物，象征着当人们开始不得不依靠双眼去“阅读”故事时，那段已然消逝的传统。再或许，他正是当时最杰出的诗人，既忠实遵循传统故事，又对其加以完善，使得故事的力量比以往更强大，语言也比以往更令人难忘。

It is interesting to speculate to what extent we know about this great bard from references to him in written documents, from oral communication passed down through generations of human beings from his time to ours. At one time or another, someone has mentioned Homer to you, as someone had mentioned Homer to that person... in such a way does a vibrant human chain stretch between you and people who lived in the ninth century before Christ. Its links are the “story of Homer.”

试着推测一下，我们对这位伟大吟游诗人的了解，究竟有多少来自书面文献中的提及，又有多少来自从他那个时代一直传承到我们这一代的口述交流，这确实很有意思。在某个时刻，有人曾向你提起过荷马，正如此前有人向那个人提起过荷马一样……正是通过这种方式，一条充满生机的人类链条将你与公元前九世纪的人们紧密相连。这条链条的每一环，便是“荷马的故事”。

What is important for us to remember when we discuss the theatre is the inherent connection between this “literature” of oral culture and improvisational performance. As Lord says, in the oral world “the poem is not composed *for* but *in* performance.”[7]

当我们讨论戏剧时，需要铭记的是口述文化中的这种“文学”与即兴表演之间固有的联系。正如洛德所言，在口述世界中，“诗歌并非*为了*表演而创作，而是*在*表演中创作的。”[7]

[7] Lord, 13.

[7] 洛德，第13页。

We have looked at the style of oral narrative. But what about its purpose? Of course a prime objective was to entertain. In pretechnological cultures, to pass the long nights telling and listening has been and still is a favorite pastime throughout the world. In the oral tradition, however, stories served as prime repositories for cultural knowledge, and thus were always more than entertainment. The *Odyssey*, for example, includes many passages that in another context would read like an instruction manual: we can learn how to dress for battle, conduct an animal sacrifice, navigate the Mediterranean. Such information would be possessed and transmitted by those members of the community who had special skills, master to apprentice. But in oral culture, the possession of hard-gained knowledge was fragile--if master and apprentice should die in an accident, the very survival of the clan might be in jeopardy. So the story-teller--and his audience--served as conservers of important information (to our literate “ear,” such information slows down the pace of the story and tends to bore us, since it is neither especially interesting nor important.).

然而，在口述传统中，故事是文化知识的主要载体，因此其意义远不止于娱乐。例如，《奥德赛》中包含许多在其他语境下读起来宛如操作手册的段落：我们可以从中学习如何穿戴战甲、如何进行动物牺牲仪式、如何在地中海航行。此类知识通常由社群中拥有特殊技能的成员掌握并传承，通过师徒相传的方式代代相授。但在口述文化中，这些来之不易的知识极为脆弱——倘若师傅和学徒因意外身亡，整个氏族的存续都可能岌岌可危。因此，讲述者及其听众便成为了重要信息的守护者（在我们这些识字者的“耳中”，此类信息会拖慢故事的节奏，往往令人感到乏味，因为它们既不特别有趣，也不特别重要）。

Furthermore, information was not all that the singer embodied in his tale. He also told the *history* of his people. Thus the genealogies he recited helped insure that each person would remember his or her past, and the heroic events of old were not just any triumphs and defeats, but the experiences of the forebears of the singer's audience.

此外，歌手在故事中所承载的远不止于信息。他还讲述着自己民族的*历史*。因此，他吟诵的族谱有助于确保每个人都能铭记自己的过去，而那些古老的英雄事迹也不仅仅是任意的胜败，更是歌手听众的先辈们亲身经历过的故事。

## THE MORAL IMPERATIVE 道德使命

Perhaps more importantly, the teller included in the tale those moral precepts most valued by the culture. Thus the storyteller in an oral culture assumed a special responsibility to tell the story which contained the teaching most important for the community to retain. In this sense he was a priest-a mediator between the people and higher elements--as well as a historian and yarn-spinner.

也许更重要的是，讲述者将该文化中最珍视的道德准则融入了故事之中。因此，在口述文化中，说书人肩负着特殊的责任，即讲述那些包含着社群最需要铭记的教诲的故事。从这个意义上说，他既是祭司——人民与更高力量之间的媒介——也是历史学家和故事讲述者。

In literary culture, of course, such functions are served by a variety of texts, organized according to subject. Thus we have, for instance, legal case books, history books, novels. The holistic approach which contains all such material in one “tale” is not necessarily believed.

当然，在文字文化中，这些功能由各类按主题分类的文本来承担。例如，我们有法律判例集、历史书籍和小说。将所有此类材料整合为一个“故事”的整体性方法，并不一定被人们所信奉。

In the oral world, however, the word has a sacral air.[9] Furthermore, this sensual, almost magical experience of hearing will be memorable insofar as the words themselves have value to the listeners: “The storyteller is a man who has counsel,” writes Walter Benjamin. It is because of this counseling function, adds Canadian storyteller Dan Yashinsky, that “the storyteller is one who stands at the very center of communal life.” And it is from a sense of words having *lost* value that Sebastian de Grazia writes of our own times: “Everyone reads today, but the bulk of what they read would not stand up literarily to the tales and songs of the storytellers and story singers of pre-literary times.”[10]

然而在口述传统中，语言却带有神圣的气息。[9]此外，这种感官的、近乎魔幻的聆听体验之所以令人难忘，正是因为言语本身对听众具有价值：“讲故事的人是一个能提供忠告的人，”瓦尔特·本雅明写道。加拿大讲故事人丹·亚辛斯基补充道，正是由于这种提供忠告的功能，“讲故事的人才站在社群生活的正中心。” 正是基于对词语已*丧失*价值的感知，塞巴斯蒂安·德·格拉齐亚才这样描述我们所处的时代：“如今人人都在阅读，但他们所读内容的大部分，在文字价值上根本无法与前文字时代说书人和吟游诗人的故事与歌谣相提并论。”[10]

[10] See Benjamin, 86; Yashinsky, 4; de Grazia, 343.

To sum up, then, illiterate bards sang tales which involved repetition, concrete narration, and performance that encompassed both the traditional and improvisational. Furthermore, while the experience was most definitely designed to be fun, there was a higher purpose involved, centering on the importance of remembrance and the transmission of a community's most important truths.

综上所述，不识字的吟游诗人所吟唱的故事包含重复、具体的叙述，以及兼具传统与即兴表演的呈现形式。此外，尽管这种体验无疑旨在带来乐趣，但其中还蕴含着更崇高的目的，核心在于铭记与传承社群最重要的真理。
