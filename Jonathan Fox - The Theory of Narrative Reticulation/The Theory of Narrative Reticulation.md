# The Theory of Narrative Reticulation 叙事网络理论

a brief description

简要说明

by Jonathan Fox

作者：乔纳森·福克斯

## Introduction 引言

The purpose of this paper is to provide a short explanation of the theory of narrative reticulation (NR). The theory is still in construction, and aspects may still be revised. In order to keep the paper brief, I will for the most part omit examples (please provide illustrations from your own experience). The theory of narrative reticulation, conceived as a comprehensive theory for playback theatre, describes the dynamic balance of four attributes--story, atmosphere, spontaneity, and guidance—allowing a flow of interconnected stories.

本文旨在简要阐述叙事网络理论（NR）。该理论目前仍处于构建阶段，部分内容可能还会进行修订。为使文章简明扼要，我将基本省略具体事例（请结合自身经验进行说明）。叙事网络理论被构想为一种关于一人一故事剧场的综合理论，它描述了故事、氛围、自发性与引导这四个属性之间的动态平衡，从而使相互关联的故事得以流畅展开。

The word “reticulation” comes from biology and can be used to describe the veins of a leaf or the lines of the palm. In our context the name evokes the interrelationship of the personal stories told by a community group in playback theatre. Narrative reticulation constitutes a spontaneous, face-to-face mode of communication that stands in contrast to reason-based, planned approaches. It is a tool for assessing effectiveness in playback theatre work, and it may also explain a general approach to reducing social alienation in modern life.  The four attributes do not exist in hierarchy, but I will begin with story.

“网状结构”（reticulation）一词源自生物学，可用于描述叶脉或手掌的纹路。在我们的语境中，这一名称唤起了一人一故事剧场中社群团体所讲述的个人故事之间的相互关系。“叙事网络”构成了一种自发的、面对面的沟通模式，与基于理性、经过规划的方法形成鲜明对比。它是评估一人一故事剧场工作成效的工具，同时也可能阐释一种缓解现代生活中社会疏离感的普遍方法。这四个属性之间不存在等级关系，但我将从“故事”开始阐述。

## Story

We all know what a story is, whether it is a “true story,” a “newspaper story,” a “short story” in a magazine, a novel, or an actual lie, as in, “don’t tell me another made-up story now.” However, the kind of story we ask for in playback theatre is different from all of these everyday uses of the word. We seek a personal true experience articulated on the spot. This kind of story appears within a spectrum of conscious awareness, bounded by an obscure unknowing at one extreme (“I have no story”) and a stubborn rigidity on the other (“I need seven characters and three scenes in my story.”) We are hoping that the teller’s story will emerge from a zone of discoverable truth that lies within this spectrum. 

我们都知道什么是故事，无论是“真实故事”、“新闻报道”、杂志上的“短篇小说”、长篇小说，还是彻头彻尾的谎言——比如“别再跟我讲那些编造的故事了”。然而，我们在一人一故事剧场中所寻求的故事，与这些日常语境中的用法截然不同。我们寻求的是当场表达出的个人真实经历。这类故事出现在意识觉知的光谱之中，一端是模糊的无知（“我没有故事”），另一端是顽固的僵化（“我的故事里必须有七个角色和三个场景”）。我们希望讲述者的故事能从这个光谱内可被发掘的真相区域中浮现出来。

The teller’s story may be rough, its details unclear, the meaning not at once graspable. But if it is authentically emergent, then most of the time the actors will have something juicy to work with, and most important, the story will say something important about the teller. Even everyday stories about ordinary events will contain an intended meaning that reveals who that teller is. In this sense, our stories are key to our identity. Because the playback performing team are “completing” the story, giving it clarity and meaning, they need basic skills in the quick apprehension and dramatic portrayal of stories, including fleshing them out thematically, in space, in character, and in plot (these are abilities we do not learn in school and university, where even the study of literature is determinedly cerebral and far from the sensualized experience of live narrative).

讲述者的故事可能粗糙，细节模糊，含义难以立即把握。但如果它是真实涌现的，那么大多数时候演员们都会有值得探索的东西，最重要的是，这个故事会揭示讲述者的某些重要特质。即使是关于平凡事件的日常故事，也蕴含着一种有意传达的含义，揭示了讲述者是谁。从这个意义上说，我们的故事是我们身份认同的关键。由于一人一故事表演团队是在“完成”故事，赋予其清晰度与意义，因此他们需要掌握快速把握故事并进行戏剧化演绎的基本技能，包括在主题、空间、人物和情节层面丰富故事内涵（这些能力并非在学校和大学里能学到的——在那里，即便是文学研究也刻意追求理性化，与现场叙事的感官体验相去甚远）。

What is true about an individual teller is also true about a community (I am using the word “community” here to mean the people present, the audience). When the stories are truly emergent, and the narrative reticulation is strong, one teller’s story sparks another through a web of association, and there are complex connections between narratives. The sequence of stories, considered as a whole, will reveal important aspects of the identity of a community. If the NR is weak, on the other hand, then the connections will be shallow, the meanings thin. A strong NR leads to augmentation of meaning—a deepening of the stories as the event proceeds—and a vigorous exchange of various perspectives. For instance, a story of triumph will be followed by one that does not end happily, as if the second teller is responding by saying, “Life is not always this way;” the story by a parent will be followed by a story from the perspective of a child.  In this way, NR promotes dialog.

适用于个体讲述者的道理同样适用于一个社群（此处我使用“社群”一词指代在场的人群，即观众）。当故事真正具有自发性，且叙事网络足够紧密时，一位讲述者的故事会通过联想之网激发另一位讲述者的故事，叙事之间便会形成复杂的联系。将故事序列视为一个整体，便能揭示社区身份的重要方面。反之，如果叙事网络（NR）薄弱，那么这些联系就会流于表面，意义也会显得单薄。强大的叙事网络（NR）会增强意义——随着活动的进行，故事愈发深刻——并促进各种视角的活跃交流。例如，一个关于胜利的故事之后，往往会紧接着一个结局并不圆满的故事，仿佛第二位讲述者正在回应道：“生活并非总是如此”；一位家长讲述的故事之后，又会接续一个从孩子视角出发的故事。通过这种方式，叙事网络促进了对话。

It is a very dynamic process. For the most part, the community responds unconsciously, and the performers focus on the challenges of enactment story by story. (“Reticulation” as a name for the theory is a compromise, for while it highlights the connections between stories, the name does not suggest the importance of an emergent, energetic process.)

这是一个充满动态的过程。大多数情况下，社区成员的回应是无意识的，而表演者则专注于逐个故事的演绎挑战。（将该理论命名为“网状”是一种折中，因为虽然它突出了故事之间的联系，但这个名称并未体现出这种自发涌现、充满活力的过程的重要性。）

In the early days of playback theatre we often redid a story, if the teller was not satisfied. We called these additional scenes “corrections.” However, an intuitive appreciation of NR at work led us to drop corrections; we learned to trust that what was uncaptured or unexpressed in any particular story would come to the fore in the next one. In other words, the NR creates a kind of tapestry with a unified design and a sense of the whole, and it is not necessary to provide a sense of completion for each element. Of course, even though we rarely redo a scene these days, we always give the teller the last word about her story. 

在一人一故事剧场的早期，如果讲述者对某个故事不满意，我们常常会重演该故事。我们将这些额外的场景称为“修正”。然而，对 NR 运作机制的直观理解促使我们放弃了“修正”的做法；我们学会了相信，任何特定故事中未被捕捉或未被表达的内容，都将在下一个故事中浮现出来。换言之，叙事网络（NR）编织出一幅设计统一、整体感鲜明的“挂毯”，因此无需为每个元素都提供完整的结局。当然，尽管如今我们很少重演某个场景，但我们始终让讲述者对自己的故事拥有最终发言权。

The same awareness helps us relax about a sequence of grim or heavy stories. We learned to trust that the NR, with its tendency to produce different points of view, will produce a teller with a story of resilience and hope. It is only a conductor ignorant of NR who will ask the community, “Let’s have a happy story now.” Of course, the opposite is also true. Communities often need to balance positive experiences with a more complicated version of the truth, one that includes suffering and disappointment.

这种认知也帮助我们以更从容的心态面对一系列阴郁或沉重的故事。我们学会了相信，叙事网络凭借其激发多元视角的潜能，终将邀请出一个呈现充满韧性和希望的故事的讲述者。只有不了解叙事网络的领航员，才会对社群说：“现在讲个快乐的故事吧。”当然，反之亦然。社区往往需要在积极的体验与更复杂的真相之间取得平衡——这种真相包含着痛苦与失望。

## Atmosphere 氛围

One distinguishing feature about a playback theatre event is attention paid to atmosphere. First, the performers will do their best to create a feeling of neighborliness in the room. As much as possible, we want the community to relax and be ready to open up in front of each other. There is no one way to promote neighborliness, and of course each setting is different. The conductor’s manner is so important, as well as the presence of the actors and musician. The team usually introduce themselves and at some point invite individuals in the audience to introduce themselves to their neighbors. There is a distinct warm up phase to the performance, beginning with the most nonthreatening questions from the conductor, such as “How was your day today?” or “How do you feel about this weather?” If the event is a workshop, then this process is drawn out and may involve a prolonged sequence of steps.

一人一故事剧场活动的一个显著特征是对氛围的重视。首先，表演者会竭尽全力在现场营造一种邻里般的亲切感。我们希望社群成员尽可能放松，并做好在彼此面前敞开心扉的准备。促进这种亲切感没有固定模式，当然每个场合也各不相同。领航员的举止至关重要，演员和乐师的临在感同样重要。团队通常会先进行自我介绍，随后邀请观众中的个别成员向邻座自我介绍。演出有一个明显的“热身”阶段，由领航员提出最不具威胁性的问题开始，例如“你今天过得怎么样？”或“你觉得这天气怎么样？”如果活动是工作坊形式，那么这个过程会拉长，可能包含一系列较长的步骤。

What is essential is that a community, who will often come to the event stressed from the pressures of daily life, be skillfully led up to and across a threshold of engagement, so that they will find themselves ready to share personal experiences. Community members need to let go of their critical minds, both toward others and themselves, sufficiently to dare to raise their hands. For if no one reaches this state of readiness, then there will be no performance.

关键在于，对于那些往往因日常生活压力而倍感焦虑的社群成员，需要通过娴熟的引导，帮助他们跨越参与的门槛，从而做好分享个人经历的准备。社群成员需要放下对他人和自身的批判心态，才能有足够的勇气举手发言。因为如果没有人达到这种准备状态，那么演出就无法进行。

Playback theatre depends on the revelation of individual experience in a public setting. If the group exists in a milieu where it is impossible to create a mood of neighborliness—if there is such mistrust or fear that no one will want to expose themselves in front of the others—then playback theatre cannot function as designed. Thus a playback leader must make a careful assessment of the milieu and often negotiate conditions before an event.

一人一故事剧场依赖于在公共场合揭示个人经历。如果群体所处的环境无法营造出邻里般的亲切氛围——如果存在如此深重的猜疑或恐惧，以致无人愿意在他人面前展露自我——那么一人一故事剧场就无法按设计正常运作。因此，一人一故事剧场带领者必须仔细评估环境，并通常在活动前协商相关条件。

Relaxation is a crucial element. The playback team wants the community to begin to open up their minds, thus deepening their field of associations. For the more active the memory and imagination of the community, the greater is the potential for a strong narrative reticulation. That the environment feel protected from harsh judgment is also important; otherwise tellers will not dare to expose the subjective truth that is the key to their identity narrative. It is easier to achieve this kind of space with a small community, rather than a large one; thus playback audiences will most often be intimate ones.

放松是至关重要的要素。一人一故事团队希望社群成员开始敞开心扉，从而深化他们的联想领域。因为社群成员的记忆和想象力越活跃，形成强有力叙事网络的潜力就越大。营造一个免受严厉评判的保护性环境也至关重要；否则，讲述者将不敢揭示作为其身份叙事关键的主观真相。在小型社群中营造这种空间比在大型社群更容易；因此，一人一故事剧场的观众通常是亲密的小群体。

A second aspect of atmosphere contrasts with the first. A distinguishing feature of playback is the theatrical retelling of each story. In this way, the experience becomes a heightened dramatic event that, like all theatre, is inherently exciting. In fact while a neighborly atmosphere may help someone feel sufficiently at ease to raise their hand, the dramatic atmosphere will, paradoxically, also inspire them to do so. The movement, the music, the colors, the lights, and the dramatizations elevate the community into a kind of trance state in which individuals are likely to surprise themselves and jump into the fire, as it were, perhaps telling a story they have not thought about for years or ever dared to voice aloud.

氛围的第二个方面与第一个形成对比。一人一故事剧场的一个显著特征是每个故事都以戏剧化的方式重新讲述。通过这种方式，体验成为一种高度戏剧化的事件，如同所有戏剧一样，本身就充满激动人心的魅力。事实上，虽然邻里般的氛围可能让人感到足够放松而愿意举手发言，但戏剧化的氛围却会——看似矛盾——同样激励人们这样做。肢体动作、音乐、色彩、灯光以及戏剧化的呈现，将社群带入一种恍惚状态，在此状态下，个体往往会出乎意料地“跳入火海”，或许讲述一个他们多年未曾想起，甚至从未敢大声说出的故事。

The dramatic intensity of the atmosphere helps with another aspect: what we familiarly term the ritual—a structure for the event that envelopes all the action and provides the community with the security that each revelation is containable and will be handled in the same fashion. A teller will be invited to the chair; there will be a short interview, at the end of which the conductor will say, “Let’s watch!”; music will mark a brief scene-setting phase; then follows the enactment, ended by the actors’ look of acknowledgment to the teller; finally, the conductor invites the teller to have the final word. The ritual also includes other elements, including structured moments of music and silence.

这种戏剧性的氛围强度还助益于另一个方面：我们通常所说的“仪式”——一种包裹着所有行动的活动框架，它为社群提供了安全感，确保每一次心声的吐露都能被妥善接纳，并以相同的方式予以处理。讲述者会被邀请到椅子上；随后进行简短的访谈，结束时领航员会说：“请看！”；音乐开始进入一个简短的阶段——舞台设置阶段；随后是演绎阶段，以演员们向讲述者投以致意的目光作为结束；最后，领航员邀请讲述者作最后分享。这一仪式还包含其他元素，包括结构化的音乐与静默时刻。

The demands on the conductor will vary. At one moment, she will want to be warm, friendly, and good humored to put people at their ease, while at another she will turn shamanistic, even fierce, in her determination to establish and hold the ritual framework.

对领航员的要求会因情况而异。有时，她需要表现得热情、友善且风趣幽默，让人们感到放松；而有时，为了确立并维持仪式框架，她又会展现出萨满般的特质，甚至显得凛然。

A core value of playback theatre is the belief that everyone has the right to tell their story. Unfortunately, however, our societies rarely share this conviction, even if modern concepts of democracy and human rights pay lip service to it. The reality is that in many contexts some people have great access to public attention, while others are silenced. In this way the practice of playback theatre becomes an act of social change, as we encourage communities to be willing to listen to everybody.  Inclusivity relates to the challenge of building a positive atmosphere, because we all feel it when one person, or type of person, dominates the narrative; such bias negatively affects the narrative reticulation.

一人一故事剧场的核心价值在于相信每个人都有权讲述自己的故事。然而遗憾的是，尽管现代民主和人权理念口头上支持这一信念，但我们的社会却很少真正认同这一点。现实情况是，在许多场合，有些人能轻易获得公众关注，而另一些人却被压制了声音。正因如此，一人一故事剧场的实践成为一种社会变革的行动——我们鼓励社群愿意倾听每个人的声音。包容性与营造积极氛围的挑战息息相关，因为当某个人或某类人主导叙事时，我们都能感受到这种氛围；这种偏见会对叙事网络产生负面影响。

Where inclusivity prevails, however, and there is a sense among the community that anyone is welcomed to speak up, there occasionally occurs a state of resonance among the community, where standard dualisms, such as self and other, fade, replaced by sense of oneness that is akin to spiritual experience. While playback theatre may rarely achieve this state of resonance, most practitioners will recognize it. Playback theatre, caught up in the energy of NR, moves toward resonance.

然而，当包容性占据主导地位，且社群中形成“任何人都可以畅所欲言”的氛围时，社群中偶尔会产生一种共鸣状态——此时，诸如“自我”与“他者”之类的标准二元对立逐渐消退，取而代之的是一种类似于灵性体验的同一感。尽管一人一故事剧场很少能达到这种共鸣状态，但大多数从业者都会对此有所体会。一人一故事剧场在 NR 能量的推动下，正朝着共鸣的方向迈进。

In a traditional society that lacks the benefits of modern technology, cultural events occur in attunement with nature. The dance cannot be danced if the wind is too strong and kicking up too much dust, for example, forcing either a change of venue or postponement. In a similar way, the playback theatre team is making constant adjustments, depending on the “weather” of the community, which at different times will be reluctant, or eager. One moment may call for good-humored tomfoolery, another for inviting a particularly quiet member to be teller, another for an unplanned moment of music, and another for strict adherence to the ritual. Because it is spontaneous, we might say that the playback theatre process follows natural rhythms. This is very different from literary theatre, which takes place in an artificially constructed environment.

在缺乏现代科技便利的传统社会中，文化活动总是与自然和谐共生。例如，如果风力过大、扬起太多尘土，舞蹈便无法进行，只能被迫更换场地或推迟演出。同样地，一人一故事剧场团队也会根据社群的“天气”不断调整——社群在不同时刻，时而显得不情愿，时而充满热情。有时需要轻松幽默的嬉闹，有时需要邀请一位特别安静的成员担任讲述者，有时需要即兴的音乐时刻，有时则需要严格遵循仪式流程。正因其自发性，可以说一人一故事剧场的过程遵循着自然的节奏。这与在人为构建的环境中进行的文学戏剧截然不同。

The overall goal is a relaxed community, listening closely to their fellow citizens, open to deep associations of memory, and ready to raise a hand to tell a story.

总体目标是营造一个轻松的社群氛围，让成员们仔细倾听同伴的心声，敞开心扉去感受记忆的深层联结，并随时准备举手讲述自己的故事。

## Spontaneity 自发性

Without script or audience plants, playback is an uncompromising form of improvisation. Spontaneity is its catchword. The term embraces several meanings, however.

没有剧本，也没有安排好的“内应”，一人一故事是一种毫不妥协的即兴表演形式。“自发性”是它的关键词。然而，这一术语包含着多重含义。

The first is sheer playfulness and joy. It is no accident that the word for acting and playing in many languages is the same. Even serious scenes are created upon a bedrock of improvisational play based on give and take between actors. They use the unplanned interaction of their bodies, their emotion, and their language to create vivid and meaningful action. It would appear that as human beings we are made to enjoy such play. It brings us joy.

首先是纯粹的玩乐心与喜悦。许多语言中，“表演”和“玩耍”使用同一个词绝非偶然。即便是严肃的场景，也是建立在演员之间相互配合的即兴游戏基础之上。他们利用身体、情感和语言之间未预先规划的互动，创造出生动而富有意义的行动。似乎作为人类，我们天生就乐于享受这种游戏，它给我们带来喜悦。

The second is the ability to react on the spot to sense information. We need super alive receptors to take in all aspects of the teller’s narrative. Thinking too much gets in the way (there is an aspect of spontaneity that relates to thinking; we will discuss that in the next section.)

其次是能够对感官信息做出即时反应。我们需要极其敏锐的感知，才能捕捉讲述者叙事中的方方面面。过度思考会成为障碍（自发性中确实存在与思考相关的一面；我们将在下一节中讨论这一点）。

The third is the ability to draw on creativity and imagination. A story that may be incomplete or unclear needs to be shaped and given all the attributes of drama. The performers let impulses for action lead them to the stage—impulses that often come from preconscious sources that consist of an image, a shape, an emotion, a thought—and then meld their different “ideas” into one coherent scene. In this practice, summoning the imagination, finding action that expresses it, and shaping inspiration into meaning, all in the unspoken moment, proficiency can take years to acquire. The performers’ gradual increase in capacity, and the satisfaction that comes from it, is one reason members of playback theatre groups often stay involved for a decade or more. 

第三点是调动创造力和想象力的能力。一个可能尚不完整或不够清晰的故事，需要被塑造成形，并赋予戏剧应有的所有特质。表演者让行动的冲动引领他们走上舞台——这些冲动往往源自前意识层面，由一个意象、一种形态、一种情感或一个念头构成——随后将各自不同的“想法”熔铸成一个连贯的场景。在这项实践中，调动想象力、寻找能表达想象力的动作，以及将灵感塑造成意义——所有这些都在无言的瞬间完成——要掌握娴熟的技巧可能需要数年时间。表演者能力的逐步提升，以及由此带来的满足感，正是一人一故事剧场成员往往能坚持参与十年或更久的原因之一。

A key area for training is movement, which unlocks the imaginative mind. Again, the difficulty of developing the necessary performing skills for this kind of spontaneous story enactment is made greater by the lack of training available in general education; we are not taught to pretend (be imaginative), to dance, to collaborate. In fact, we are often taught to drop the natural play and role play of our early years for sedentary, rational, distinctly antiplay, narrowly imaginative learning.

训练的关键领域是肢体动作，它能开启想象力的大门。同样，由于普通教育中缺乏相关训练，培养这种即兴故事演绎所需的表演技能变得更加困难；我们从未被教导过如何假装（发挥想象力）、跳舞或协作。事实上，我们往往被教导要摒弃童年时期自然的玩耍和角色扮演，转而接受那种静坐式、理性化、明显反玩耍且想象力狭隘的学习方式。

The fourth is role flexibility, the ability of an actor, for example, to play whatever role is called for in the story presented, whether it be a fierce employer at one instance and a cute puppy dog, the next. Most of us find some roles much easier to play than others, and in the sense of expanding our role flexibility, spontaneity can be trained. We practice by enacting our own stories. We also perform a wide variety of exercises designed to expand our readiness and range, so that we are ready to fulfill any story that comes up.

第四点是角色灵活性，例如，演员有能力在呈现的故事中扮演任何所需的角色，无论下一刻是扮演凶狠的雇主，还是可爱的小狗。我们大多数人发现某些角色比其他角色更容易扮演，从拓展角色灵活性的角度来看，自发性是可以通过训练培养的。我们通过演绎自己的故事来进行练习。我们还会进行各种各样的练习，旨在拓展我们的准备状态和表现范围，以便随时准备演绎任何出现的故事情节。

A fifth is collaboration. A playback theatre group, like a sports team, learns to cooperate with each other to a high degree. The task of creating improvisational scenes before audiences demands it. Communities are as often impressed by the teamwork of the performers as by the stories themselves. The members of a playback group must remain in positive relationship with each other, or else it will be prohibitively difficult, even impossible, to achieve spontaneous teamwork on the stage. This is a tall order, requiring that groups take valuable time away from skill-building in order to talk things out with each other. 

第五点是协作。一人一故事剧场就像一支运动队伍，学会高度地相互配合。在观众面前即兴创作场景的任务要求如此。观众往往对表演者的团队合作印象深刻，不亚于对故事本身的印象。一人一故事剧团的成员必须保持积极的相互关系，否则要在舞台上实现即兴的团队合作将难如登天，甚至根本无法实现。这是一项艰巨的任务，要求剧团从技能训练中抽出宝贵的时间，用于彼此沟通交流。

An interesting experiment has been conducted by Lior Noy and colleagues at the Weizmann Institute in Israel, which has featured its own playback theatre team. Noy created a device he calls the Mirror Game, in which the movements of two players are precisely measured by sliding a counter along a two-dimensional axis as they try to synchronize with each other. The scientists found that random individuals lead and follow each other in jerky motions, while practiced improvisors, by seeming to be able to anticipate each other’s movements, can actually achieve a “no-leader” state, creating a smooth line on the graph[ See Noy, Lior. The Mirror Game: A Natural Science Study of Togetherness. In Performance Studies in Motion: International Perspectives and Practices in the Twenty-First Century. Editors: Atay, Citron, D. Bloomsbury Methuen Drama, 2014 PP. 416. Also see the Theater Lab of Noy’s colleague and collaborator at the Weizmann Institute, Uri Alon: https://www.weizmann.ac.il/mcb/UriAlon/research/theatre-lab.].

以色列魏茨曼科学研究所的利奥尔·诺伊（Lior Noy）及其同事进行了一项有趣的实验，该研究所拥有自己的一人一故事剧场。诺伊发明了一种名为“镜像游戏”（Mirror Game）的装置：当两名演员试图相互同步时，通过沿二维轴滑动计数器，精确测量他们的动作。科学家们发现，随机配对的参与者之间以生硬的动作相互引领和跟随，而训练有素的即兴表演者似乎能够预判彼此的动作，实际上可以达到一种“无带领者”的状态，在图表上形成一条平滑的曲线[参见诺伊，利奥尔。《镜像游戏：关于共同性的自然科学研究》。载于《动态表演研究：21世纪的国际视角与实践》。编辑：阿泰（Atay）、西特龙（Citron）、D. 布鲁姆斯伯里·梅休恩戏剧出版社，2014年，第416页。另见诺伊在魏茨曼研究所的同事兼合作者乌里·阿隆（Uri Alon）的“剧场实验室”：https://www.weizmann.ac.il/mcb/UriAlon/research/theatre-lab。]

Inherent in the idea of spontaneity is a core of integrity. A performer needs to have a sense of her own boundaries—not so much in a psychological sense, although this also is true, but in an imaginative sense. Otherwise, how can she know that an image for embodiment is inspired by the teller’s story rather than her own? In other words, meaningfully “retelling” a story means not being triggered by it, keeping one’s artistic distance from it, adding to and subtracting from it—in short, making art from it. For this reason, an individual who has a very incomplete or fuzzy sense of her- or himself will find it very difficult to perform playback theatre, because it will often be impossible to evaluate impulses. It may seem paradoxical, but a story can only be successfully “retold” by performers who find their own unique manner of embodying the teller’s intended meaning. This license to be creative is what makes playback theatre so full of vitality and brings depth and beauty to an often commonplace-seeming story.

“自发性”这一概念内含着核心的完整性。表演者需要具备对自身边界的感知——这并非主要指心理层面的边界（尽管这一点也成立），而是指想象层面的边界。否则，她如何能分辨出，某个需要具象化的意象究竟是源自讲述者的故事，还是源自她自身？换言之，有意义地“重述”一个故事，意味着不被故事所牵动，与之保持艺术上的距离，对其进行增减——简而言之，就是将其转化为艺术。正因如此，那些对自己认知非常不完整或模糊的人，会发现表演一人一故事剧场非常困难，因为他们往往无法评估自己的冲动。这看似自相矛盾，但只有当表演者找到自己独特的方式来诠释讲述者所传达的意图时，才能成功地“重述”一个故事。正是这种发挥创造力的自由，使一人一故事剧场充满活力，并为那些看似平淡无奇的故事赋予了深度与美感。

Spontaneity lies at the heart of the art in playback theatre. While clearly the playback approach does not fit in to the conventional definition of art as “product,” nor its common “art for art’s sake” disregard for the welfare of both audience and performers, nevertheless the challenge of reenacting personal narratives on the spot, drawing on movement, spoken language, music, and other theatrical elements, allows for impressive artistic achievement. Narrative reticulation allows for playback theatre to be effective at many levels, including a high artistic standard. Some would argue that of course the highest artistic standard represents the pinnacle of potential achievement in playback theatre. I am not sure about that. While it stands to reason that the most artistic performers, so long as they are also skilled in the other attributes of narrative reticulation, will be able to succeed in the widest number of milieus, I am convinced, however, that the allegiance of playback theatre to spontaneity allows for all sorts of practitioners to draw on the artist in themselves and find necessary sources of creativity.

自发性是一人一故事剧场艺术的核心。虽然一人一故事剧场的方法显然不符合将艺术定义为“产品”的传统观念，也不符合那种忽视观众和表演者福祉的“为艺术而艺术”的常见观念，但通过运用肢体动作、语言、音乐及其他戏剧元素，当场重现个人叙事的挑战，却能成就令人印象深刻的艺术成果。叙事网络结构使一人一故事剧场在多个层面都具有成效，包括达到较高的艺术水准。有人可能会认为，当然，最高的艺术水准代表了一人一故事剧场所能达到的巅峰。对此我并不确定。虽然从道理上讲，最具艺术造诣的表演者——只要他们同时也精通叙事隐喻法的其他属性——确实能在最广泛的环境中取得成功，但我坚信，一人一故事剧场对自发性的坚持，使各类从业者都能激发内心的艺术家潜质，并找到必要的创造力源泉。

## Guidance 引导

The four attributes that make up the cornerstones of narrative reticulation pertain to every aspect of the playback theatre process, but for reasons of brevity, this essay has focused on the teller in discussing story, the group in discussing atmosphere, and the performers in discussing spontaneity. In the same vein, this section, on the last attribute, guidance, will primarily concentrate on the role of the conductor.

构成叙事网络的基石的四大要素涉及一人一故事剧场过程的方方面面，但出于篇幅考虑，本文在讨论“故事”时侧重于讲述者，在讨论“氛围”时侧重于团体，在讨论“自发性”时侧重于表演者。同理，本节将围绕最后一个要素——“引导”——主要聚焦于领航员的角色。

The conductor leads the audience throughout the playback performance as an emcee. She welcomes them at the start and explains the nature of the event. She prompts the audience for narratives by asking questions. She interviews tellers. Most of the time, she chooses what specific forms the actors use in their enactments. She monitors the arc of the performance, deciding when is just the right time to invite the first story, and when will be the last. She conducts the ritual, making clear what are its rules and keeping them honored. Finally, she is responsible for choosing the next teller, encouraging the audience when no hand is raised and selecting when there are many raised at once. These leadership tasks take a variety of skills, ensuring that the conductor is a complex role that takes considerable time to learn.

领航员作为司仪，在整个一人一故事表演过程中引领观众。她在开场时欢迎观众并解释活动的性质；通过提问引导观众讲述故事；与讲述者进行访谈；大多数时候，她会决定演员在表演中采用何种具体形式；她把控着表演的走向，决定何时是邀请第一个故事的恰当时机，以及何时该结束。她领航这一仪式，阐明规则并确保其得到遵守。最后，她负责挑选下一位讲述者，在无人举手时鼓励观众，在多人同时举手时进行筛选。这些领导任务需要多种技能，因此“领航员”是一个复杂的角色，需要花费相当长的时间来掌握。

In the unfolding of a playback event, there are many moments when the attributes converge. The emergence of just the right story for the moment represents a convergence of story and atmosphere. Or a particularly satisfying enactment, for instance, will be the result of story and spontaneity coming together in a synergistic fashion.

在一人一故事活动的展开过程中，有许多时刻这些特质会汇聚在一起。恰好出现一个与当下时刻完美契合的故事，代表着故事与氛围的交汇。又或者，一次特别令人满意的演绎，往往是故事与自发性以协同方式融合的结果。

Often, however, the attributes will not converge, but will pull in opposition to each other. At such moments it will be the conductor, more than anyone, who must make an intervention. A typical example is the long-winded teller. The demands of spontaneity will argue to let the person’s story unfold naturally, while the demands of atmosphere will argue for an interruption to prevent the community from growing bored. Another example occurs when interviewing a very sad teller, who might find herself wordless with grief during the narrative. The demands of atmosphere call for the conductor to provide comfort to the teller, but again, the demands of story will be to hear the details of what happened. The conductor in this situation will need to encourage the perhaps weeping teller to get the words out, while in the previous example, the conductor needs to contain the flood of words. In these and many such situations the conductor must decide exactly when and how to intervene (or not). 

然而，这些要素往往不会自然交汇，反而会相互抵触。在这样的时刻，领航员比任何人都更需要介入。一个典型的例子就是喋喋不休的讲述者。自发性的要求主张让讲述者的故事自然展开，而氛围的要求则主张进行打断，以防止群体感到厌倦。另一个例子是采访一位非常悲伤的讲述者，她在叙述过程中可能会因悲痛而语塞。氛围的要求需要领航员给予讲述者安慰，但故事的要求则是听清事情的细节。在此情境下，领航员需要鼓励可能正在哭泣的讲述者说出心声；而在前一个例子中，领航员则需要控制住滔滔不绝的言语。在这些以及许多类似情况下，领航员必须准确判断何时以及如何介入（或不介入）。

It is important to note that the actors will also need to make such discriminations. For instance, in the case of a story containing violence experienced by the teller, the actors need to weigh the demands of story, which may involve cataclysmic events, with those of atmosphere, since they know that if they retraumatize the teller in front of the community (by enacting the story too literally), the flow of the narrative reticulation  may suffer irreparable harm.
Thus even though the playback event unfolds in an unplanned manner, the team is constantly making on-the-spot decisions, guiding the progress of the event in response to challenges presented by opposing attribute demands. In one sense this control is the opposite of spontaneity. But on another level, this thinking constitutes another order of spontaneity. 

需要注意的是，演员们也需要做出此类判断。例如，当故事涉及讲述者亲历的暴力事件时，演员们需要权衡故事需求（可能包含灾难性事件）与氛围营造的需求，因为他们深知，若在社群众人面前（因过于字面化地演绎故事）使讲述者再次遭受心理创伤，叙事网络的流动可能会遭受无法挽回的损害。

Balance is key. In the unfolding of the playback theatre event, the interplay of the attributes often becomes unbalanced (or threatens to become unbalanced). It is the job of the team, especially the conductor, to sense this and make an intervention that will restore an equilibrium that fosters the flow of stories.

平衡是关键。在一人一故事剧场活动的展开过程中，各种属性的相互作用往往会失衡（或有失衡之虞）。团队——尤其是领航员——的职责就是察觉这一点，并进行干预以恢复能促进故事流畅发展的平衡状态。

There is an ethical imperative that underpins playback theatre, and maintaining it constitutes another major area of guidance. The conductor must see that no one gets harmed during the event; this includes other community members and actors, as well as tellers. In listening to a narrative, the conductor must balance respect for the teller with fairness to others. For instance, the conductor should not allow a teller to tell a malicious story about another person present; the conductor should not allow a teller to insult an actor. This precept has its distinct complications, such as in instances where the teller’s story contains clear prejudice of some kind.

一人一故事剧场以一种伦理准则为根基，维护这一准则构成了指导工作的另一个重要方面。领航员必须确保活动过程中无人受到伤害；这包括其他社群成员、演员以及讲述者。在倾听讲述时，领航员必须在尊重讲述者与对他人公平之间取得平衡。例如，领航员不应允许讲述者讲述关于在场他人的恶意故事；领航员也不应允许讲述者侮辱演员。这一准则存在其独特的复杂性，例如当讲述者的故事中包含某种明显的偏见时。

That part of atmosphere which calls for inclusivity will need guidance to make the ideal a reality, often consisting of a direct invitation to a specific community member, framed in just the right manner and occurring at just the right moment. 

那种呼吁包容性的氛围需要引导才能将理想变为现实，这通常表现为以恰当的方式、在恰当的时机，直接邀请特定的社群成员参与。

Guidance is an essential component to every playback theatre event. However, when the event has a special purpose, such as education (a private company introducing a new management concept), healing (a performance for residents of a women’s shelter) or dialog (a village community divided by participation in civil war), then the conductor will need to exercise additional guidance to make sure that event keeps to its intended purpose.

引导是每场一人一故事剧场活动不可或缺的组成部分。然而，当活动具有特殊目的时——例如教育（私营企业介绍新的管理理念）、疗愈（为妇女庇护所的居民举办的演出）或对话（因参与内战而分裂的乡村社群）——领航员就需要施加额外的引导，以确保活动始终符合其预期目的。

Human beings seem to have a primal urge to relate stories to each other, and I imagine that it is not particularly difficult to achieve some degree of narrative reticulation in any social group, provided the members get a chance to talk openly and leisurely with one another. Nevertheless, years of experience have shown that the presence of performers as artistic retellers can make a huge difference to the resonance of the stories shared, and guidance can provide a major catalyst to an increased level of narrative reticulation.

人类似乎有着相互讲述故事的原始冲动，我认为只要成员们有机会彼此坦诚而从容地交谈，在任何社交群体中实现一定程度的叙事网络并不难。然而，多年的经验表明，表演者作为艺术性的讲述者参与其中，会对共享故事的共鸣产生巨大影响，而引导则能成为提升叙事网络水平的重要催化剂。

## Discussion 讨论

Narrative reticulation theory is designed to elaborate both the red thread concept and the three circle theory that have been useful for playback theatre practitioners over many years. The four attributes of narrative reticulation operate in a similar way to a complex system (like the human body). Each attribute is a very different world of its own, but they ultimately must work in synergy for narrative reticulation to take place. Many factors bear on this, including the skill of the performers, the openness of the audience, and the milieu.

叙事网络理论旨在深入阐释“红线”概念和“三圈理论”——这两者多年来一直对一人一故事剧场实践者大有裨益。叙事网络的四个属性运作方式类似于一个复杂系统（如人体）。每个属性本身都是一个截然不同的世界，但最终必须协同作用，叙事网络才能形成。许多因素都会影响这一点，包括表演者的技巧、观众的开放程度以及环境氛围。

Let’s look briefly at negative outcomes. If the conductor or actors mishear or fail to understand the intended meaning of the teller, or if they fail to have an adequate grasp of how stories work, then the theme of the enactments will head off in an unintended direction, and the community may feel uneasy, since they sense that a key element about the narrated experience was missed. We could call this an example of misplace guidance, since the team takes the story off on a tangent not intended by the teller. If the conductor is too unassertive, failing to intervene when needed, the ritual may be loosened to a fatal extent; or in the opposite case, if the conductor is manipulative, the atmosphere will be poisoned by unempathic leadership. If the conductor sticks too rigidly to a pre-event plan of which forms to use when and cannot adjust to the circumstances as they unfold, then the performance will feel over-structured and the necessary dynamism of the NR process will be throttled.  

让我们简要探讨一下负面结果。如果领航员或演员听错或未能理解讲述者的本意，或者他们未能充分掌握故事的运作机制，那么表演的主题就会偏离预期的方向，社群成员可能会感到不安，因为他们察觉到叙述经历中的某个关键要素被遗漏了。我们可以将此称为“引导失当”的例子，因为团队将故事带向了讲述者未曾预期的方向。如果领航员过于缺乏主见，在需要时未能介入，仪式可能会松散到致命的程度；反之，如果领航员过于操控，缺乏同理心的带领方式将毒化整个氛围。如果领航员过于僵化地拘泥于活动前制定的“何时使用何种形式”的计划，而无法根据事态发展灵活调整，那么表演就会显得结构过于僵化，NR 过程所必需的活力也将被扼杀。

When the integrity of an individual attribute is not maintained (some PT groups are too unskilled in one area or another), or the interplay of the four attributes become unbalanced, then the narrative reticulation will be weak. Tellers will hesitate to come forward. The energy will diminish. The emotions will be flat. The overall meaning will feel superficial.

当某个属性的完整性无法维持（某些 PT 小组在某个领域技能不足），或者四大属性的相互作用失衡时，叙事网络就会变得薄弱。讲述者会犹豫不决，不敢站出来。能量会减弱。情感会变得平淡。整体意义会显得肤浅。

When each of the four attributes thrives, in contrast, and the playback team nimbly maintains them in balance with each other, then the energy will grow as the event proceeds. Hands will start to rise as tellers feel compelled to share. Emotions will be strong, including a sense of connection among the community. The narrative reticulation—the connection between the stories themselves—will be deep and complex.

相反，当这四个属性蓬勃发展，且一人一故事团队能灵活地保持它们之间的平衡时，随着活动的推进，能量将不断增强。讲述者会感到迫切想要分享，于是举手的人会越来越多。情感将十分强烈，包括社群成员之间的联结感。叙事网络——即故事本身之间的联系——将变得深邃而复杂。

## Outcomes 成果

Finally, let’s look briefly at how narrative reticulation theory might be useful. For playback theatre practitioners, the theory can be applied to structuring training and rehearsals, as well as assessing performance. What is an actor’s and conductor’s strong and weak points in terms of the theory? What areas need strengthening? Where did the performance become unbalanced? At what junctures? What other action might have kept the NR strong?

最后，让我们简要探讨一下叙事网络理论可能带来的实用价值。对于一人一故事剧场的实践者而言，该理论可应用于培训和排练的结构设计，以及衡量演出。从该理论的角度来看，演员和领航员的优缺点分别是什么？哪些方面需要加强？表演在何处失去了平衡？在哪些节点失去平衡？采取哪些其他措施本可以保持叙事网络的健壮？

It might also be possible that the theory has relevance outside the world of playback theatre. NR provides a rationale for dialog through personal story. It emphasizes the power of listening and community engagement. It charts a method of on-the-spot decision-making that may be useful for other areas of work. Finally, it presents a concept of modulated consciousness that is an alternative to logic-driven concepts of thinking.

该理论或许在一人一故事剧场领域之外也具有相关性。叙事网络为通过个人故事开展对话提供了理论依据。它强调倾听与社群参与的力量。它勾勒出一种即时决策的方法，这可能对其他工作领域有所裨益。最后，它提出了一种“调制意识”的概念，为逻辑驱动的思维模式提供了另一种选择。

Narrative reticulation means connection, perceived meaning, energy for communication. More of it in our social contexts will lead, one can hope, to more cohesive and civil communities.

叙事网络意味着联结、感知到的意义以及沟通的能量。我们有理由期待，在社会语境中更多地运用这一理念，将有助于构建更加凝聚且文明的社群。
