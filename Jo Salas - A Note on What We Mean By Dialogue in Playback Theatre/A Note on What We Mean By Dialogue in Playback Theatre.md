# A Note on What We Mean By “Dialogue” in Playback Theatre

Jo Salas (2006)

These days we often describe Playback Theatre as community dialogue, but without further explanation it may be a misleading description. The kind of dialogue that we are referring to is not a cognitive dialogue. It is not the same as hearing someone speak their thoughts and feelings and reasons, then responding with your own thoughts, feelings, andreasons. It is not a process of discussion, of thought and logic.

如今，我们经常将一人一故事剧场描述为社群对话，但如果不作进一步解释，这种描述可能会产生误导。我们所说的对话不是认知上的对话。它不同于听别人讲述自己的想法、感受和理由，然后用自己的想法、感受和理由做出回应。这不是一个讨论、思考和逻辑的过程。

Instead, it is a dialogue in the realm of story, image, emotion, and physical action; an embodied, imaginative dialogue. We call it a dialogue because, in the unfolding of ashow, the stories are summoned and prompted by each other. No one knows ahead of time which voices will be heard, which stories enacted. An audience member hears a story and watches it enacted, then finds herself recalling an event from her own life. It may or may not have any apparent connection with the story she’s just seen. She remembers a moment from her life, and thinks yes, she’d like to tell about it. The conductor asks for another story and she raises her hand.

相反，它是在故事、图像、情感和肢体动作领域的对话；是一种无形的、富有想象力的对话。我们称其为对话，是因为在演出的展开过程中，故事是由彼此召唤和推动的。没有人知道哪些声音会被听到，哪些故事会被演绎。观众听到故事，看到故事被演绎，然后发现自己回忆起自己生活中的某个事件。这件事可能与她刚刚看到的故事有明显的联系，也可能没有。她想起了自己生活中的某一时刻，并想是的，她想讲述这个时刻。领航员要求她再讲一个故事，她举起了手。

Whether the connection is apparent or not, her story is most likely a response to what has just happened, or perhaps to something that happened earlier in the show. But it is not a logical or dialectical response. In Playback Theatre, every statement, every story is conveyed, comprehended, and responded to on kinesthetic and imaginative levels. The dialogue proceeds through association and inspiration in relation to any aspect of what the teller has said and how the performers have brought it to life on stage.

无论其中的联系是否明显，她的故事都很可能是对刚刚发生的事情的回应，或许是对演出中早些时候发生的事情的回应。但这并不是逻辑或辩证的反应。在一人一故事剧场中，每一个陈述、每一个故事都是在动觉和想象的层面上传达、理解和回应的。对话是通过联想和灵感进行的，联想和灵感涉及讲述者所讲述的任何方面，以及表演者如何在舞台上将其栩栩如生地展现出来。

After a Playback event—particularly one which has addressed a social issue—in which different voices have been heard and their stories enacted, audience members, including tellers, are affected, in a lasting way, by what they have witnessed. It’s not that opinions have necessarily been changed. It’s that each person’s comprehension of the shared reality has been altered and expanded, however subtly.

在 Playback 活动（尤其是涉及社会问题的 Playback 活动）中，不同的声音被听到，他们的故事被演绎出来，观众（包括讲述者）会被他们所目睹的一切持久地影响。这并不是说观点一定会改变。而是每个人对共同现实的理解都得到了改变和扩展，无论多么微妙。

In a non-Playback gathering dealing with a pressing and controversial topic, a more traditional dialogue may take place where many people speak their minds, often in direct response to others. Arguments are presented and either supported or refuted. The conversation moves fast. Many words are spoken.

在非一故事的集会中，如果讨论的是一个紧迫的、有争议的话题，可能会进行更传统的对话，许多人都会畅所欲言，通常是对他人的直接回应。人们提出论点，或支持或反驳。对话进展迅速。说的话很多。

The limitation of this kind of dialogue—even when it is facilitated with care for maximum access and involvement—is that it is easy to resist in any way being changed by what you hear. It is easy to simply reiterate, louder and louder, what you knew and felt before you came into the room.

这种对话的局限性在于，人们很容易抵制因听到的内容而发生任何改变。你很容易用更大的声音重复你在进入房间之前所知道和感受到的东西。

In Playback it’s the opposite. The process moves slowly. Words alternate with action. Fewer voices may be heard, though they are likely—if the conductor is attentive and skilful—to represent all the diversity in the room. Audience members’ points of view are expressed through highly personal testimony and narrative, and transformed into theatre, witnessed by the rest of the audience without any demand to comment, judge, analyze, orapprove. A teller’s point of view may be unwelcome, even anathema, to others listening, but after hearing the human voice of the teller and seeing the story, there is a little more space inside any audience member (and performer) to accommodate the humanity of that person and his or her opinions. And there is the immediate opportunity to tell one’s own story or feelings in response.

而 Playback 却恰恰相反。这个过程进展缓慢。语言与动作交替进行。虽然听到的声音可能较少，但如果指挥者细心娴熟，这些声音很可能代表了房间里的所有不同声音。观众的观点通过高度个人化的证词和叙事表达出来，并转化为戏剧，由其他观众见证，无需评论、评判、分析或批准。讲述者的观点可能不受欢迎，甚至是其他人听后的厌恶，但在听到讲述者的人声和看到故事之后，任何观众（和表演者）的内心都会有更多的空间来容纳讲述者的人性和他或她的观点。同时，观众（和表演者）也有机会讲述自己的故事或感受。

A Playback show which addresses a contentious topic (whether intentionally or because audience members raise it) is unlikely to result in “reconciliation” in the sense of agreements and understanding reached—though it could well be part of a longer, more complex process in that direction. But it is likely to build a realm of openness in which people become less walled-off by their views and experiences, more receptive to each other as people. This more expansive place is an essential to any kind of long-termmutual learning or reconciliation.

Playback 表演如果涉及有争议的话题（无论是有意为之，还是因为观众提出），就不太可能达成 "和解"，即达成一致和理解--尽管它很可能是朝此方向发展的更长期、更复杂过程的一部分。但它很可能会建立一个开放的领域，在这个领域中，人们的观点和经历不再那么封闭，而是更容易接受彼此。这种更广阔的空间对于任何形式的长期相互学习或和解都是必不可少的。

In 2004, prior to the US election, Hudson River Playback Theatre carried out a series of shows, mostly on college campuses, called “Play Back the Vote.” Prompted by the lowvoter turnouts in this country especially among 18-24 year olds, we wanted to explorevoting itself—why people vote or don’t—and hopefully encourage some nonvoters to register (voter registration was available after each show). One of the shows took place at a local community college where the student body includes a wide range of age, class, and race. I taught basic Playback skills in a drama class and several students joined our team for the performance.

2004 年，在美国大选之前，哈德逊河一人一故事剧场（Hudson River Playback Theatre）开展了一系列名为 "一人一故事投票 "的演出，主要在大学校园进行。由于美国的投票率很低，尤其是 18-24 岁的年轻人，我们希望探讨投票本身--人们为什么投票或不投票--并希望鼓励一些非选民进行登记（每场演出后都提供选民登记服务）。其中一场演出在当地社区大学举行，那里的学生年龄、阶级和种族差异很大。我在戏剧课上教授了基本的 Playback 技巧，有几名学生加入了我们的演出团队。

At the show we heard markedly different and often heated points of view. Although our explicit focus was voting itself, many spoke about the candidates and the issues as well. Our tellers included a student who was vehemently pro-Bush, pro-Bible, and anti-gay; a faculty member who said she would vote “to stop the killing” in Iraq; another student who was fed up with the main parties and wanted to vote for Nader; someone else who spoke of alternating between hope and disgust; another who was committed to voting as a responsibility of citizenship; a student who refused to vote because she found the wholeprocess meaningless; a teacher who was incensed by reports of voters being intimidated by election officials who assumed they would vote the “wrong” way. Hands flew up each time we asked a question or invited the next teller: the audience of about 120 seemed thoroughly engaged.

在节目中，我们听到了明显不同的观点，而且往往是激烈的观点。虽然我们明确关注的焦点是投票本身，但许多人也谈到了候选人和问题。我们的讲述者包括一位强烈支持布什、支持圣经、反对同性恋的学生；一位说她将投票 "阻止 "伊拉克杀戮的教职员工；另一位对主要政党感到厌倦、想投纳德一票的学生；还有一位在希望和厌恶之间交替转换的学生； 还有一名学生认为整个投票过程毫无意义，因此拒绝投票；一名教师对选民受到选举官员恐吓的报道感到愤怒，因为选举官员认为他们会投 "错误 "的票。每当我们提出一个问题或邀请下一位讲述者时，观众们的手都举得高高的：大约 120 名观众似乎都非常投入。

As the performers stood together in a circle backstage after the show, a young man—who’d spoken up several times during the performance—approached us to tell us that in his opinion Playback Theatre is not a good vehicle for dialogue, because we have to make everything so simple. He said that it became like television, just sound bites.

演出结束后，当表演者在后台围成一圈站在一起时，一位年轻人走过来告诉我们，在他看来，一人一故事剧场不是对话的好载体，因为我们必须把一切都变得如此简单。他说，这变得像电视一样，只有声音段落。

It was not the right moment to engage in a critical discussion, especially for our young guest actors. We just thanked him and went back to our own sharing. But afterwards I thought a lot about his critique. I think he was reacting in a valid way to the word “dialogue” which I’d used in opening the show. As I said above, a Playback show is not a dialogue, or not an effective dialogue, in the way we usually use the word. But what he was overlooking was the other level of dialogue where people were exchanging their deeply-felt statements and stories; the fact that so many voices and opinions were indeed heard aloud and honored by being transformed into theatre; the fact that everyone, audience and performers alike, heard and felt a range of feelings and points of view much broader than the range most of us are likely to expose ourselves to in our chosen circles. Through the medium of theatre, we could listen to those otherwise-uncomfortable opinions without inwardly clenching and then quickly dismissing them.

现在不是进行批判性讨论的时候，尤其是对我们的年轻演员来说。我们只是向他表示感谢，然后继续自己的分享。但事后我对他的批评想了很多。我认为他对我在开场时使用的 "对话 "一词的反应是有道理的。正如我在上文所说，按照我们通常的用法，Playback 表演不是对话，或者说不是有效的对话。但是，他忽略了另一个层面的对话，即人们在交流他们深入内心的叙述和故事；事实上，这么多的声音和观点通过转变成戏剧而被大声听到并得到尊重；事实上，每个人，无论是观众还是表演者，都听到并感受到了比我们大多数人在自己选择的圈子里可能接触到的范围更广的情感和观点。通过戏剧这一媒介，我们可以倾听那些让人不舒服的观点，而不会在内心紧绷，然后迅速将其否定。

I myself usually feel so wounded and enraged by extreme right-wing views, or by the apathy and ignorance that to me is the scourge of this country, that I insulate myself from hearing those voices. I turn off the radio, I put down the newspaper, I avoid talking politics except with those whose views are like mine. It’s just too personally painful to do anything else. But from the Playback stage I can find room in myself for any points of view. I can listen to them, engage with the teller, take care that we respect and capture this person’s experience, without shutting down or becoming distressed. And even after the show is over, I notice a difference within myself, a space that has expanded. It becomes easier for me not to lose sight of the fact that people with radically different points of view are still fellow- beings, not utterly alien from myself. They are people with stories.

我自己通常会被极端右翼的观点所伤害和激怒，或者被我认为是这个国家祸害的冷漠和无知所伤害和激怒，从而使自己远离这些声音。我关掉收音机，放下报纸，避免谈论政治，除非是与我观点相同的人。这对我个人来说太痛苦了，我什么都做不了。但在一人一故事的舞台上，我可以为任何观点找到自己的空间。我可以倾听他们的观点，与讲述者互动，注意尊重和捕捉这个人的经历，而不会关闭或变得苦恼。即使在演出结束后，我也会注意到自己内心的变化，空间扩大了。我越来越容易不忽视这样一个事实，即观点截然不同的人仍然是同类，而不是与我完全格格不入。他们是有故事的人。

If we can’t bear to listen to each other, no connection, no dialogue, no reconciliation is possible. It is only from this expanded inner space that change of any kind can come. This is the kind of dialogue that Playback Theatre can achieve.

如果我们无法承受去倾听对方，就不可能有联系、对话与和解。只有从这一扩大的内在空间中才能产生某种形式的变化。这就是一人一故事剧场所能实现的对话。
