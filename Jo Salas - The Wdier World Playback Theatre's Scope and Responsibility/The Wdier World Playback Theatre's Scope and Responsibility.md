# THE WIDER WORLD: PLAYBACK THEATRE'S SCOPE AND RESPONSIBILITY¹ 更广阔的世界：一人一故事剧场的视野与责任¹

Jo Salas (2003)

乔·萨拉斯（2003）

SEVERAL YEARS AGO I began to feel restless with the exclusively personal focus of most Playback Theatre stories, especially in public performances. I felt sure that our personal stories were connected to much larger stories, including the huge tides of history. And I knew that I myself was often deeply affected by events that were not part of my own life. I wanted very much for our Playback stage to widen—to become a place where we could collectively acknowledge these connections between the personal and the political.

几年前，我开始对大多数一人一故事剧场故事——尤其是公开演出中——那种仅关注个人层面的倾向感到不安。我确信，我们的个人故事与更宏大的叙事息息相关，包括历史的汹涌浪潮。我也深知，自己常常会受到那些与自身生活无关的事件的深刻影响。我非常希望我们的一人一故事舞台能够拓展——成为一个能够让我们集体承认个人与政治之间这些联系的场所。

In public shows I began to invite stories that acknowledged these links. But no matter what invitation or suggestion I made, the stories remained purely individualistic—often wonderful, of course, memorable, revelatory, and so on—but focused purely on what happened within the scope of our audience members' individual experience.

在公开演出中，我开始邀请那些能体现这些联系的故事。但无论我发出怎样的邀请或建议，故事依然纯粹是个人化的——当然，这些故事往往精彩绝伦、令人难忘、发人深省等等——但其焦点始终仅限于观众个人经历的范畴之内。

It was clear that it was going to take more than a gentle invitation. We—my company, Hudson River Playback Theatre, and I—decided to create a Playback Theatre version of the Living Newspaper. (The tradition of the Living Newspaper goes back at least as far as revolutionary Russia, when news was brought to illiterate populations by actors who enacted the pressing stories of the day. Later, J.L. Moreno experimented with a psychodramatic version.²)

显然，仅靠温和的邀请是远远不够的。我们——我的剧团“哈德逊河一人一故事剧场”和我本人——决定创作一部一人一故事剧场版本的“活报剧”。（“活报剧”的传统至少可以追溯到革命时期的俄罗斯，当时演员们通过演绎当天的紧迫新闻，将消息传递给不识字的民众。后来，J.L.莫雷诺尝试了心理剧版本。²）

We quickly ran up against a number of problems. Some were technical—it seemed that to act out news stories effectively we needed to plan and rehearse them, at least minimally. Some were philosophical—should we, the company, choose the stories, or let the audience suggest them? What if someone chose a story we were not willing to enact? The most challenging problems were to do with our own process. Our own political differences came to the surface—and, more fundamentally, our different views about the importance of bringing the political/social world into our work. Sometimes a news story was vetoed by company members who didn't know about it and didn't want to know about it. "Our audiences come to enjoy themselves, not to be dragged down by depressing stuff like this," one person said.

我们很快就遇到了许多问题。有些是技术性的——似乎要有效地演绎新闻故事，我们需要进行至少最基本的策划和排练。有些是哲学层面的——我们剧团应该自行选择故事，还是让观众提议？如果有人选了一个我们不愿演绎的故事，又该怎么办？最具挑战性的问题则与我们自身的创作过程有关。我们内部的政治分歧浮出水面——更根本的是，我们对将政治/社会议题融入作品的重要性持有不同看法。有时，某则新闻报道会被那些对此一无所知且不愿了解的剧团成员否决。“我们的观众是来享受娱乐的，而不是被这种令人沮丧的内容拖累，”有人说道。

In spite of all these hurdles, we performed a Living Newspaper in a number of shows. And we found that when the wider world was presented so strongly, it enlarged the parameters of the stories. Tellers still told about love and loss and family and work and nature. But they also remembered times when their lives confronted them with social injustice, when they were challenged to be courageous or altruistic, often as witnesses: when they were directly aware of the vast, messy business of people struggling in a post-colonial, capitalist, racist society.

尽管面临所有这些障碍，我们还是在多场演出中呈现了“活报剧”。我们发现，当更广阔的世界被如此鲜明地呈现出来时，故事的维度便随之拓展。讲述者依然讲述着爱情、失去、家庭、工作和自然。但他们同时也回想起那些生活中直面社会不公的时刻，那些被要求展现勇气或无私精神的时刻——往往是以见证者的身份：当他们切身感受到，在后殖民、资本主义、种族主义社会中，人们正为生存而挣扎的这幅浩瀚而纷乱的图景。

We pulled back from the Living Newspaper, failing to find a format that really worked in the middle of a Playback show. But we had launched ourselves on a path of engagement and discovery—a path of slow but steady growth in the direction of taking responsibility, paying attention, acknowledging our own connections to history and social reality, facing our own identity in the world which is a part of every story we tell and hear, whether we know it or not.

我们逐渐淡出了“活报剧”的形式，因为未能找到一种真正适合融入一人一故事演出中的表现形式。但我们已踏上一条参与与探索的道路——这是一条缓慢而稳步成长的道路，引领我们走向承担责任、保持关注、承认自身与历史及社会现实的联系，并直面我们在世界中的身份认同。无论我们是否意识到，这种身份认同都是我们讲述和聆听的每一个故事中不可或缺的一部分。

The exploring we have now done together—through our own stories, through much discussion, through searching and sometimes conflict—has brought us collectively to a place where, although we are not political clones, we share an awareness of right and wrong in the world. And we know that stories of the wider world belong on our Playback stage as much as stories of the inner life.

我们迄今共同进行的探索——通过自身的故事、通过大量讨论、通过寻求答案以及有时出现的冲突——已将我们集体带到这样一个境地：尽管我们并非政治上的克隆人，但我们对世间的是非对错有着共同的认知。而且我们深知，广阔世界的故事与内心世界的故事一样，都理应出现在我们的一人一故事舞台上。

The discoveries we have made together enable us to enlarge the picture easily and organically. Broadening the frame of reference at the outset of a show leads to a broader and deeper pool for the stories that will follow. We often mention what's happening in the world in our personal introductions at the opening of a show. Our warm-up questions always include a question such as "What's something in the news recently that's had an impact on you?" People respond. The conductor may make reference to a social or political reality relevant to the event, like the public performance we did in an area much celebrated in our upstate region for its Huguenot history. I happened to know some of the untold stories of this famous street: the stories of the slaves who lived and died in each one of the picturesque old houses. My brief mention of this overlooked history made it possible for a teller (he told me later) to come forward with a very strong story about tragic discrimination in his own Asian culture.

我们共同的发现使我们能够轻松而自然地拓宽视野。在演出伊始就拓宽参照框架，将为后续的故事提供更广阔、更深厚的源泉。我们在演出开场时的个人介绍中，常常提及世间正在发生的事情。我们的热身问题中总会包含诸如“最近有什么新闻对你产生了影响？”之类的问题。观众们会积极回应。领航员可能会提及与该事件相关的社会或政治现实，比如我们曾在纽约州北部一个因胡格诺派历史而广受赞誉的地区进行过公开演出。我恰好知道这条著名街道上一些不为人知的故事：那些在每一栋风景如画的老房子里生活并逝去的奴隶的故事。我对这段被忽视的历史的简短提及，促使一位讲述者（他后来告诉我）站出来，讲述了一个关于他自身亚裔文化中悲惨歧视经历的极具冲击力的故事。

## Prejudiced tellers 带有偏见的讲述者

With or without such an invitation, stories sometimes bring in the wider world in ways that are uncomfortable for the performers and the audience. Playback is built for dialogue: what better way to listen to each other?³ But what happens, playbackers often ask, if someone tells a story that embodies an offensive point of view? What if you have a racist teller, for example? It's essential for the Playback team—not just the conductor—to be alert to any kind of intolerance or disrespect and to find a way to address it without sacrificing the teller. Not easy: such moments seem to pit the necessity to uphold our values squarely against the commitment to honor the teller. But we must do it, for our own integrity, for those in the audience who are offended by the teller's ignorance or prejudice, and so that the teller has a chance to learn from seeing his story.

无论是否有这样的邀请，故事有时会以一种让表演者和观众都感到不适的方式，将更广阔的世界引入其中。一人一故事旨在促进对话：还有什么比这更好的方式来倾听彼此呢？³但一人一故事的参与者们常会问：如果有人讲述的故事体现了冒犯性的观点，该怎么办？例如，如果讲述者是种族主义者呢？一人一故事团队——而不仅仅是领航员——必须对任何形式的不宽容或不尊重保持警惕，并找到一种既能处理问题又不损害讲述者尊严的方式。这并不容易：此类时刻似乎将捍卫我们价值观的必要性，与尊重讲述者的承诺直接对立起来。但我们必须这样做——为了我们自身的正直，为了那些因讲述者的无知或偏见而感到冒犯的观众，也为了让讲述者有机会通过审视自己的故事而获得成长。

The performing team has several options. Firstly, the conductor can—and should—find a way to distance herself from what is problematic in the story, making it clear that she does not share the biased assumption that the teller is expressing. ("A big Black guy came over and I was terrified." "Why were you terrified?" says the conductor, refusing to be complicit in the stereotype that all Black men are dangerous.) Secondly, the actors can make sure that their enactment brings out the humanity of the victims of the teller's prejudice. Another opportunity comes after the enactment, while the actors are still in position on the stage. In that brief, but important moment of checking in with the teller, the conductor can allow space for an expression of insight or realization. She might even invite the teller to look at the actor playing the target of their bias: "I wonder how that person might have felt inside."

表演团队有几种选择。首先，领航员可以——也应该——设法与故事中存在问题的内容保持距离，明确表示她并不认同讲述者所表达的偏见。（“一个高大的黑人走过来，我吓坏了。” “你为什么会感到害怕？”领航员问道，拒绝助长‘所有黑人男性都很危险’这种刻板印象。）其次，演员们可以确保他们的表演凸显出讲述者偏见所针对的受害者的人性。另一个契机出现在表演结束后，当演员们仍留在舞台上的位置时。在那个与讲述者进行简短但重要的交流时刻，领航员可以留出空间，让讲述者表达自己的见解或领悟。她甚至可以邀请讲述者看看扮演其偏见对象的演员： “我不知道那个人当时内心是怎样的感受。”

After the teller returns to the audience, and depending on the impact of the story and the vulnerability of the teller, you might use short forms to reflect other audience members' feelings about seeing the story. Or you can be alert to—or invite—a subsequent story that may address the issues of the earlier one. The dialogue continues.

当讲述者回到观众席后，根据故事产生的影响以及讲述者的脆弱程度，你可以采用简短的形式来反映其他观众对这个故事的感受。或者，你可以留意——或邀请——后续的故事来探讨前一个故事涉及的问题。对话仍在继续。

Lastly, the ending of a show can provide a fertile opportunity to revisit a hurtful story. Hudson River Playback Theatre ends its shows with a collage of moments inspired by the stories and short forms, focusing not on memorable moments but on the stories' untold aspects. We try to shine a light on the story from a different angle, using our empathy and imagination. After a story that embodied injustice, whether recognized by the teller or not, the object can become the subject. The Arab store owner, irrationally feared by the teller after 9/11, expresses his own fear. The homeless woman who refused the wealthy teller's gift of a worn-out sweater tells her side of the story.

最后，一场演出的结尾可以提供一个绝佳的机会，让我们重新审视那些令人心痛的故事。哈德逊河一人一故事剧场在演出结束时，会呈现一段由故事和短剧片段启发的剪辑集锦，其重点不在于那些令人难忘的瞬间，而在于故事中未曾讲述的层面。我们试图运用同理心和想象力，从不同的角度为故事照亮一盏明灯。在讲述了一个体现不公的故事之后——无论讲述者是否意识到这一点——客体可以成为主体。那位在“9·11”事件后被讲述者无端恐惧的阿拉伯店主，表达了他内心的恐惧；那位拒绝了富裕讲述者赠送的一件旧毛衣的无家可归妇女，则讲述了她自己的故事。

We have learned in this way that we can embrace any story, giving it its due attention and respect, knowing that we are still upholding the ideals of awareness and justice.⁴

通过这种方式，我们领悟到：我们可以接纳任何故事，给予它应有的关注与尊重，同时深知我们依然恪守着觉醒与正义的理想。⁴

⁴ In the most extreme, and extremely rare, situation, a performer or team has the right to decline to enact a story. Ultimately, we are human beings with values and integrity.

FROM THE AUDIENCE, I watched my company do a public show four days after the beginning of the US invasion of Iraq, to which all of us were passionately opposed. In the previous rehearsal, the performers had told their own stories in preparation: we knew that the war was likely to emerge as the main theme of the show. They also prepared themselves to be open to pro-war stories. In the show, some of their opening statements conveyed anguish about what was happening. But the conductor's welcome made it clear that stories from any point of view were welcome. We are here to listen to each other, she said. In that particular audience there was only one audience comment not clearly opposed to the invasion: a man talked about his trust that the government knew more than the rest of us. The actors reflected his feelings, different from their own, knowing the capacity of Playback to hold varying points of view and deepen understanding for everyone.

作为观众，我目睹了我的剧团在美国入侵伊拉克四天后进行的一场公开演出——我们所有人都对此表示强烈反对。在之前的排练中，表演者们为了做好准备，讲述了各自的故事：我们知道战争很可能成为演出的主要主题。他们同时也做好了准备，以开放的心态接纳支持战争的故事。演出中，部分演员的开场陈述表达了对时局发展的痛心。但领航员的致辞明确表示，无论持何种观点的故事都受到欢迎。“我们聚在这里是为了倾听彼此，”她说。在那场演出中，全场观众中只有一位观众的发言并未明确反对入侵：一位男士表示，他相信政府比我们其他人掌握更多信息。演员们反映了他的感受——尽管这与他们自身的观点不同——因为他们深知一人一故事这种形式能够包容不同的观点，并加深所有人的理解。

Playback Theatre has an important role to play in our communities during this time of war where truth, as they say, is the first casualty. In this situation, as well as in the perennial struggles for justice on any front, it is our responsibility to fulfill this role by examining our own participation in the social/political world, and educating ourselves about our society. When we, the Playback team, are prepared and unified in this goal, our audiences will embrace this much-needed opportunity to tell stories about the wider world and how they are part of it.

在战争时期——正如人们所说，“真相是战争中的第一牺牲品”——一人一故事剧场在我们的社群中发挥着重要作用。在此情境下，以及在任何领域为正义而进行的持久斗争中，我们都有责任通过审视自己在社会/政治领域的参与，并加深对社会的认识，来履行这一职责。当我们——即一人一故事剧团团队——为此目标做好准备并团结一致时，我们的观众将抓住这一亟需的机会，讲述关于更广阔世界以及他们如何融入其中的故事。
